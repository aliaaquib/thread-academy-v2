## What does "solving" mean?

To **solve** an equation is to find the value of the unknown that makes it true. In `x + 5 = 12`, the solution is `x = 7`, because `7 + 5 = 12`.

<Definition term="linear equation">
  A **linear equation** is an equation where the unknown appears only to the power 1 — no `x²`, no `1/x`.
  It always graphs as a straight line, which is where the name comes from.
</Definition>

Think of an equation as a perfectly balanced scale. The golden rule of solving is:

<Formula label="The golden rule">
  Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
</Formula>

## One-step equations

Solve `x + 5 = 12`. The `+ 5` is in the way, so undo it by subtracting 5 from **both** sides:

```maths
x + 5 = 12
x + 5 − 5 = 12 − 5
x = 7
```

**Check:** substitute back — `7 + 5 = 12` ✓. Checking catches arithmetic slips and takes ten seconds. Always do it.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: division">
  Solve `x/3 = 9`.

  The `÷ 3` is undone by multiplying both sides by 3:

  ```maths
  x/3 = 9
  x = 9 × 3
  x = 27
  ```

  Check: `27/3 = 9` ✓
</WorkedExample>

## Two-step equations

Solve `3x + 2 = 14`. The unknown is wrapped in two layers: `× 3`, then `+ 2`. Peel them off from the **outside in** — undo addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division:

```maths
3x + 2 = 14        subtract 2 from both sides
3x = 12            divide both sides by 3
x = 4

Check: 3(4) + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14 ✓
```

<ImportantNote title="Undo in reverse">
  Think of the equation as wrapping `x` in layers. The last operation applied to `x` is the first one you undo.
  In `3x + 2`, the `+ 2` happened last, so the `− 2` happens first.
</ImportantNote>

## Unknowns on both sides

First collect all the `x`-terms on one side (usually the left), then solve as normal:

```maths
5x − 3 = 2x + 9     subtract 2x from both sides
3x − 3 = 9          add 3 to both sides
3x = 12             divide by 3
x = 4

Check: 5(4) − 3 = 17 and 2(4) + 9 = 17 ✓
```

If subtracting gives you a negative `x`-term, don't panic — just keep going, or collect the terms on the other side instead. `7 − 2x = 1` becomes `−2x = −6`, so `x = 3`. A negative divided by a negative is positive.

## Brackets and fractions

**Brackets:** expand first, then solve.

```maths
4(x − 3) = 2x + 6
4x − 12 = 2x + 6      expand the bracket
2x − 12 = 6           subtract 2x from both sides
2x = 18               add 12 to both sides
x = 9
```

**Fractions:** multiply every term by the denominator to clear it.

```maths
x/4 + 5 = 12          multiply everything by 4
x + 20 = 48           subtract 20
x = 28
```

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Doing it to one side only.** `3x + 2 = 14` → `3x = 14` (forgot to subtract 2 from the right too). The scale must stay balanced.
  - **Sign errors when expanding.** `−2(x − 5)` is `−2x + 10`, not `−2x − 10`. A minus outside flips every sign inside.
  - **Dividing only part of a side.** In `(3x + 6)/3`, the 3 divides *everything*: `x + 2`, not `3x + 2`.
  - **Skipping the check.** Substitution is the cheapest error-detector in mathematics. Use it.
</ImportantNote>

<Summary>
  - Do the same operation to both sides to keep the equation balanced.
  - Undo operations in reverse order: addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division.
  - With unknowns on both sides, collect the x-terms together before isolating x.
  - Expand brackets first; clear fractions by multiplying through.
  - Always check by substituting your answer into the original equation.
</Summary>
