## Rivers at work

A river does three jobs with the material (load) it carries:

1. **Erosion** — wearing away rock and soil.
2. **Transportation** — carrying the eroded material downstream.
3. **Deposition** — dropping material when the river loses energy.

<Definition term="erosion">
  **Erosion** is the wearing away of rock and soil by a moving force — here, flowing water —
  followed by removal of the material. (Weathering breaks rock up; erosion carries it away.)
</Definition>

## Four types of erosion

- **Hydraulic action** — the sheer force of water crashing into banks, compressing air into cracks until rock breaks off.
- **Abrasion** — sand and pebbles carried by the river scour the bed and banks like sandpaper. The main erosive force.
- **Attrition** — rocks carried by the river smash into each other and wear down, becoming smaller and rounder downstream.
- **Solution** — acidic river water dissolves soluble rocks like limestone.

## Landforms: erosion in action

**Waterfalls** form where a river flows over hard rock onto soft rock. The soft rock erodes faster (differential erosion), creating a step; the overhanging hard rock eventually collapses, and the waterfall **retreats** upstream, leaving a gorge.

**Meanders** form downstream where the river swings side to side. Water flows fastest on the **outside** of the bend, eroding it into a steep **river cliff**; slowest on the **inside**, depositing material as a gentle **slip-off slope**.

<ImportantNote title="Link process to landform">
  Top answers always connect the two: don't just *describe* a meander — *explain* it with
  erosion on the outside bend and deposition on the inside. Process explains pattern.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the four types of river erosion and briefly describe each." hint="Hydraulic…, abrasion, attrition, solution.">
    Hydraulic action (force of water), abrasion (scouring by load), attrition (rocks wearing each other down), solution (dissolving soluble rock).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain how a waterfall retreats upstream." hint="Think hard rock over soft rock, and undercutting.">
    Soft rock erodes faster, undercutting the hard cap rock; the overhang collapses, moving the waterfall's edge upstream and leaving a gorge.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is one bank of a meander steep and the other gentle?" hint="Where is the water fastest?">
    Fastest water on the outside bend erodes a steep river cliff; slowest water on the inside bend deposits a gentle slip-off slope.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Rivers erode, transport and deposit — dominating in the upper, middle and lower course respectively.
  - Four erosion types: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution.
  - Waterfalls form by differential erosion and retreat upstream.
  - Meanders: erosion outside the bend (river cliff), deposition inside (slip-off slope).
</Summary>
