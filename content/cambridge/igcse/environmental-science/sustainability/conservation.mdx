## Conservation

Species are going extinct far faster than the natural background rate. Conservation asks: what do we save, and how?

<Definition term="Biodiversity">
  **Biodiversity** is the variety of life — genetic diversity within species, diversity of species, and diversity of ecosystems. More diverse systems are more resilient.
</Definition>

## Key points

- In-situ conservation protects species in their habitat: national parks, marine reserves.
- Ex-situ conservation works outside habitats: seed banks, zoos, captive breeding.
- Habitat loss is the biggest driver of extinction.
- Success story: the Arabian oryx, extinct in the wild by 1972, now numbers over 1,000 after reintroduction.

<Example title="The Svalbard Seed Vault">
  Deep in Arctic permafrost, over a million seed samples are stored as a backup for world agriculture — ex-situ conservation against crop failure, war and climate change.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish in-situ and ex-situ conservation, giving an example of each." hint="In = in place; ex = out of place.">
    In-situ: protecting species in their habitat, e.g. a national park. Ex-situ: outside the habitat, e.g. a seed bank or captive breeding programme.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does biodiversity loss matter to humans?" hint="Think food, medicine, resilience.">
    We depend on ecosystems for food, clean water, medicines and climate stability; diverse systems recover better from shocks.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Biodiversity underpins resilient ecosystems and human wellbeing.
  - In-situ = protect habitats; ex-situ = protect species outside habitats.
  - Habitat loss drives most extinctions.
  - Conservation works — oryx, condors and whales prove it.
</Summary>
