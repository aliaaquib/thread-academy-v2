## Bonjour — and when to use it

**Bonjour** works almost everywhere during the day — shops, school, strangers. In the evening switch to **bonsoir**. At night, leaving: **bonne nuit**.

- **Bonjour** — Hello / good day
- **Bonsoir** — Good evening
- **Salut** — Hi (informal, friends only)
- **Au revoir** — Goodbye
- **À bientôt** — See you soon

<ImportantNote title="Bonjour is a ritual">
  In France you greet people — entering a shop without "bonjour" is genuinely rude.
  One word buys you goodwill everywhere.
</ImportantNote>

## Je m'appelle… — introductions

- **Je m'appelle…** — My name is… (literally "I call myself…")
- **Comment tu t'appelles ?** — What's your name? (informal)
- **Comment vous appelez-vous ?** — What's your name? (formal)
- **Je suis…** — I am…
- **Comment ça va ?** — How's it going? → **Ça va bien, merci** — I'm well, thanks.
- **Enchanté(e)** — Nice to meet you.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: a first conversation">
  ```text
  A: Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Léa. Comment tu t'appelles ?
  B: Je m'appelle Marc. Enchanté.
  A: Comment ça va ?
  B: Ça va bien, merci !
  ```
</WorkedExample>

## Tu vs vous — the two "yous"

French has two ways to say "you": **tu** (informal — friends, family, classmates) and **vous** (formal — adults you don't know, teachers, officials). Using *tu* with a stranger can offend; using *vous* with a friend sounds cold. When in doubt, start with **vous**.
