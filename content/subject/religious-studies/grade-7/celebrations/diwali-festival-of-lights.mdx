Every religion has special days when people stop ordinary work, dress up, share food and remember what matters most. From the lamps of Diwali to the shared meals of Eid, festivals turn beliefs into something you can see, hear and taste. In this chapter you will explore how celebrations bring communities together and what they teach us about each faith.

This lesson focuses on Diwali, Festival of Lights: why Hindus, Jains and Sikhs light lamps for Diwali and what the lights symbolise.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Light, food and togetherness

Many festivals use the same simple ingredients: lights or candles to show hope, special foods to share, and gatherings of family and friends. Diwali lamps, Christmas candles and Hanukkah candles all use light as a picture of goodness pushing back darkness. Sharing food, from Eid sweets to Christmas dinner, turns celebration into kindness.

### Festivals mark what matters most

Religions set aside special days to remember turning points in their story - a birth, a rescue, a new beginning. Celebrating together reminds everyone, especially children, what the community believes. That is why festivals repeat every year: the repetition keeps the story alive.

**Key term — tradition:** A belief or way of doing things passed down from older to younger generations, such as lighting candles every year at the same festival.

<WorkedExample title="What does Diwali celebrate?">
  Your friend says Diwali is just 'Hindu fireworks night'. Use what you know to explain what the festival really means.

  Step 1: Name who celebrates - Hindus, Jains and Sikhs all keep Diwali, each for slightly different reasons.
  Step 2: Name what they do - homes are cleaned and decorated, small oil lamps called diyas are lit, and families share sweets and gifts.
  Step 3: Explain what the lamps mean - for many Hindus they recall Prince Rama returning home after rescuing Sita, with light standing for good defeating evil.
  Step 4: Add the other traditions - Jains remember Mahavira reaching liberation, and Sikhs mark Bandi Chhor Divas, when Guru Hargobind was freed from prison.
  Step 5: Conclude - Diwali is not only fireworks; it is a festival of light, new beginnings and hope shared by three religions.

  **Answer:** Diwali is a festival of light celebrated by Hindus, Jains and Sikhs. The lamps stand for good overcoming evil and hope overcoming darkness, and each tradition links the festival to its own sacred story.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Diwali is only a Hindu festival** Jains and Sikhs also celebrate at Diwali time - Jains remember Mahavira's liberation and Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas.
  - **Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are the same festival** Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan fasting, while Eid al-Adha honours Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice and falls during the Hajj season.
</ImportantNote>
