How do people show love for God or the sacred? Through prayer whispered alone, songs sung together, rituals repeated for centuries and journeys to holy places. Worship turns inner belief into outward action. This chapter visits the prayers, places and practices through which faith comes alive.

This lesson focuses on How People Pray: how and why people pray in different traditions.

## Key ideas

### Prayer takes many forms

Prayer is not only asking for things. It includes praise, thanks, confession of wrongs and quiet meditation. Muslims pray five set prayers (salat) facing Mecca each day; Christians may use the Lord's Prayer or pray freely; Buddhists meditate rather than praying to a god. The form fits the belief.

### Sacred space shapes worship

Places of worship are designed to turn minds towards the sacred. A mosque's mihrab shows the direction of Mecca; a church's altar recalls Christ's sacrifice; a gurdwara's open doors welcome everyone to worship and eat together; a Hindu temple houses the murti, the image through which devotees focus on the deity. Architecture teaches theology.

**Key term — worship:** Acts that honour God or the sacred, including prayer, singing, rituals and festivals.

<WorkedExample title="Analysing the Hajj">
  The Hajj is the annual Muslim pilgrimage to Mecca. Explain why it counts as worship, using three features of the pilgrimage.

  Step 1: Identify what happens - millions of Muslims travel to Mecca, circle the Kaaba, stand at Arafat and perform set rituals over several days.
  Step 2: Link to belief - the Hajj is the fifth Pillar of Islam, commanded by God, retracing the actions of Abraham and Muhammad.
  Step 3: Feature one - ihram: pilgrims wear simple white cloth, showing equality before God.
  Step 4: Feature two - circling the Kaaba (tawaf): a physical act of devotion centring life on God.
  Step 5: Feature three - standing at Arafat: a day of prayer and repentance seeking forgiveness.
  Step 6: Conclude - the Hajj is worship because every action expresses devotion, obedience and unity with Muslims worldwide.

  **Answer:** The Hajj counts as worship because it is commanded by God as a Pillar of Islam and every ritual - the simple dress of ihram, circling the Kaaba and prayer at Arafat - expresses devotion, repentance and the equality and unity of all Muslims.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Prayer is only asking God for things** Prayer also includes praise, thanksgiving, confession and silent meditation - asking is only one form.
  - **All places of worship work the same way** Each is designed around its faith's beliefs: a mosque faces Mecca, a synagogue centres on the Torah scroll, a gurdwara welcomes all to eat together.
</ImportantNote>

    Praise, thanksgiving, confession and meditation (or intercession for others).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the mihrab in a mosque?" hint="It shows a direction.">

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How many times a day do Muslims perform salat, and which direction do they face?" hint="Five...">

  </PracticeItem>

  </PracticeItem>
