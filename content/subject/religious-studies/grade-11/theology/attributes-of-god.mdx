Theology is the disciplined study of God - arguing carefully about what can be said of the divine and how. From the paradoxes of omnipotence to the mystery of the Trinity and the limits of human language, theologians have built some of philosophy's most precise arguments. This chapter introduces their methods and their hardest questions.

This lesson focuses on Attributes of God: omnipotence, omniscience and perfect goodness - what can they mean?

## Key ideas

### The coherence of the divine attributes

Classical theism holds that God is omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good and eternal - but each attribute raises puzzles. Can an omnipotent God create a stone too heavy to lift? Aquinas replied that omnipotence means power over all that is logically possible, and a self-contradictory 'unliftable stone' describes nothing at all. Can God know the future without destroying human freedom? Boethius argued that God's eternity means God sees all time at once, as present, rather than foreseeing it. Theology proceeds by refining definitions, not abandoning them.

### The Trinity: unity and distinction

The doctrine of the Trinity, defined at the Council of Nicaea in 325 CE, holds that God is one being in three persons - Father, Son and Holy Spirit. It is not tritheism (three gods) and not modalism (one God wearing three masks): the persons are really distinct yet fully share the one divine nature. Theologians call this a mystery - not a puzzle to be solved, but a truth to be explored - and use analogies (such as Augustine's mind, with memory, understanding and will) while admitting every analogy breaks down.

**Key term — omnipotence:** All-powerfulness; classically understood as the power to do anything that is logically possible.

<WorkedExample title="The stone paradox">
  'Can God create a stone so heavy that he cannot lift it?' Show step by step why most theologians think this does not refute omnipotence.

  Step 1: State the dilemma - if God can create such a stone, there is something he cannot lift; if he cannot create it, there is something he cannot create. Either way, something seems beyond his power.
  Step 2: Clarify omnipotence - following Aquinas, omnipotence is the power to do all that is logically possible, not the power to do the self-contradictory.
  Step 3: Analyse the phrase - 'a stone too heavy for an all-powerful being to lift' is a contradiction in terms, like a square circle; it describes nothing.
  Step 4: Apply the definition - being unable to actualise a contradiction is not a limit on power, any more than being unable to draw a square circle is a limit on artistry.
  Step 5: Conclude - the paradox trades on an inflated definition of omnipotence that no classical theologian held.

  **Answer:** The paradox fails because omnipotence means the power to do the logically possible. 'A stone an omnipotent being cannot lift' is self-contradictory - it names nothing - so God's inability to create it is no more a limit than inability to make a square circle.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Omnipotence means God can do absolutely anything, including the logically impossible** Classical theologians from Aquinas onwards define omnipotence as power to do all that is logically possible; contradictions describe nothing and are not 'things' to be done.
  - **The Trinity means Christians believe in three gods** The doctrine affirms one God in three persons - tritheism is explicitly rejected as heresy; the persons share one divine nature.
</ImportantNote>
