The world is full of amazing places — deserts, cities, mountains and seas. This chapter is your first tour of planet Earth: you will find out about its continents and oceans, meet some of its countries and flags, and learn to read simple maps like a real explorer.

This lesson focuses on Continents and Oceans: learn the names and locations of the seven continents and five oceans on a world map.

## Key ideas

### Maps are pictures of places

A map is a flat drawing that shows where places are. It cannot show everything, so it uses colours, labels and symbols to keep things clear. When you read a map, always start by looking at the title and the key, which explains what the colours and symbols mean.

### North, south, east and west

Compass directions help you describe where things are on a map. North is usually at the top of a map. You can also use words like 'near', 'next to' and 'between' to describe places in relation to one another.

**Key term — Ocean:** A huge area of salt water covering most of the Earth's surface. The five oceans are the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic oceans.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Continents and Oceans">
  How many continents are there on Earth?

  Seven.

  **Answer:** Seven.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Mixing up continents and countries** A continent is a huge land mass, while a country is a much smaller area inside it with its own government. France is a country; Europe is the continent it is in.
  - **Thinking the equator is a real line painted on the Earth** The equator is an imaginary line drawn around the middle of the Earth on maps and globes. It helps people describe location, but you cannot see it on the ground.
</ImportantNote>
