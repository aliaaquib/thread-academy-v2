## Key ideas

### Some problems need every country at once

Climate change cannot be solved by one country cutting emissions while others increase theirs — greenhouse gases mix in one shared atmosphere. This is a collective action problem: everyone benefits if all cooperate, but each is tempted to let others do the work. International agreements, such as the Paris Agreement on climate, are attempts to solve it through shared commitments.

### Citizenship can be active at many scales

Being a global citizen is not only about grand gestures. It includes informed voting, ethical consumption, fundraising, campaigning, volunteering and simply staying informed about world events. Evaluation matters: effective action is targeted, evidence-based and sustained, whereas one-off gestures may feel good but achieve little.

**Key term — Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** A set of 17 global goals adopted by the United Nations in 2015, aiming to end poverty, protect the planet and ensure peace and prosperity for all by 2030. Examples include quality education, clean water and climate action.

<WorkedExample title="Evaluating a climate argument">
  A student argues: 'My country is small, so its emissions do not matter — big countries should act first.' Evaluate this argument in three steps.

  Step 1: Identify the claim — that small emitters have no responsibility to act.
  Step 2: Test the logic — if every small country reasoned this way, a large share of global emissions would be excused, and the collective action problem would never be solved.
  Step 3: Consider fairness and leadership — small countries can still lead by example, and many climate agreements ask all countries to contribute according to their means.

  **Answer:** The argument is weak: climate change is a collective action problem, so excusing every small emitter would leave a large share of emissions unaddressed. All countries share responsibility, even if their contributions differ in size.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming awareness alone solves problems** Sharing a post raises awareness, but real change usually needs sustained, targeted action — funding, policy change or long-term volunteering. Always ask what the action achieves, not just how it feels.
  - **Thinking global citizenship replaces national citizenship** Global citizenship is an outlook and a set of responsibilities, not a passport. People remain citizens of their countries while also acting as members of a wider human community.
</ImportantNote>
