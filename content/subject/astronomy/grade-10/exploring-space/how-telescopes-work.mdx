## Key ideas

### Telescopes are light buckets

A telescope's power comes mainly from the size of its mirror or lens: bigger means more light collected and fainter objects visible. Space telescopes like Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope sit above Earth's blurring atmosphere, giving far sharper views. Webb observes infrared light, letting it see the first galaxies and peer inside dusty star-forming clouds.

### Light carries messages

Splitting starlight into a spectrum shows which elements a star contains, because each element absorbs specific colours. The overall shift of the spectrum reveals motion: redshift means moving away. This single technique proved the universe is expanding and lets astronomers measure the composition of distant stars.

**Key term — Telescope:** An instrument that collects light (or other radiation) from distant objects and magnifies the image. Larger mirrors collect more light, revealing fainter objects.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: How Telescopes Work">
  Why do astronomers build telescopes with very large mirrors?

  Larger mirrors collect more light, so fainter and more distant objects become visible, and detail improves.

  **Answer:** Larger mirrors collect more light, so fainter and more distant objects become visible, and detail improves.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming the James Webb Space Telescope takes ordinary photos** Webb observes mainly infrared light, invisible to our eyes; its famous images are translated into visible colours so we can see them.
  - **Thinking telescopes mainly magnify** A telescope's most important job is collecting light; magnification without light-gathering just makes a dim blur bigger.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage of putting a telescope in space rather than on the ground." hint="Think about what sits between a ground telescope and the stars.">
    It sits above Earth's atmosphere, which blurs and absorbs light, so images are much sharper.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does redshift tell an astronomer about a galaxy?" hint="It relates to motion and the expanding universe.">
    Its light is stretched towards the red, showing the galaxy is moving away from us; greater redshift means greater distance.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How can a spectrum reveal what a star is made of?" hint="Each element leaves a fingerprint.">
    Each chemical element absorbs specific colours, producing dark lines at fixed positions in the spectrum that identify the elements present.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two robotic missions and what they explored." hint="Think of one at Saturn and rovers on another planet.">
    Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years; the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers are exploring the surface of Mars.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
