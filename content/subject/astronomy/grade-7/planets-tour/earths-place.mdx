This lesson focuses on Earth's Special Place: why Earth, neither too hot nor too cold, can support life.

<Definition term="Earth's Special Place">
  Why Earth, neither too hot nor too cold, can support life.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Earth is just right

Earth orbits in the habitable zone, where temperatures allow liquid water. It has a protective atmosphere and a magnetic field that shields it from harmful radiation. As far as we know, Earth is the only planet with life.

### Eight planets, two kinds

The four inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars) are small and rocky. The four outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune) are huge giants made mostly of gas and liquid. The dividing line is the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

**Key term — Rotation:** A planet spinning on its own axis. One full rotation gives the planet its day; Earth rotates once every 24 hours.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Earth's Special Place">
  Why is Earth able to support life? Give two reasons.

  Earth orbits in the habitable zone so liquid water can exist, and it has an atmosphere and magnetic field that protect living things.

  **Answer:** Earth orbits in the habitable zone so liquid water can exist, and it has an atmosphere and magnetic field that protect living things.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Counting Pluto as the ninth planet** Pluto is a dwarf planet, not one of the eight planets. It was reclassified in 2006 because it is too small to have cleared its orbit.
  - **Thinking Saturn is the only planet with rings** Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune all have rings too; Saturn's are simply the brightest and easiest to see.
</ImportantNote>
