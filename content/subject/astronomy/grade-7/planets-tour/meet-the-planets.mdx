Our solar system has eight planets, each one a world with its own weather, moons and landscapes. From scorched Mercury to icy Neptune, the planets show just how varied a star's family can be. This tour takes you to each of them in turn, starting with the one closest to the Sun.

This lesson focuses on Meet the Planets: the eight planets in order from the Sun and what makes each special.

<Definition term="Meet the Planets">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Eight planets, two kinds

The four inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars) are small and rocky. The four outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune) are huge giants made mostly of gas and liquid. The dividing line is the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

### Order from the Sun

The planets in order are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Many people remember the order with a sentence whose first letters match: My Very Excellent Mother Just Served Us Noodles. Neptune is the farthest, orbiting about 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth.

**Key term — Orbit:** The curved path a planet follows around the Sun, pulled by the Sun's gravity. A planet's year is the time it takes to complete one orbit.

<WorkedExample title="Ordering the planets">
  Write the eight planets in order from the Sun, and say which one is the largest.

  Start with the four small rocky planets nearest the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.
  Then list the four giant planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  Check the order against the memory sentence: My Very Excellent Mother Just Served Us Noodles.
  The largest planet is Jupiter: it is so big that all the other seven planets could fit inside it.

  **Answer:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Jupiter is the largest planet.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Counting Pluto as the ninth planet** Pluto is a dwarf planet, not one of the eight planets. It was reclassified in 2006 because it is too small to have cleared its orbit.
  - **Assuming planets are the only things orbiting the Sun** Asteroids, comets and dwarf planets also orbit the Sun. The planets are just the largest members of the family.
</ImportantNote>
