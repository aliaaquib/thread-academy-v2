## Key ideas

### Why stars seem to move

Stars appear to move across the sky during the night, but it is actually Earth that is turning. Earth spins once every 24 hours, so the whole sky appears to rotate around a point near the pole star. This is the same reason the Sun rises and sets.

### Stars come in colours

Look closely and you will notice some stars are blue-white while others are orange or red. Colour tells you the star's surface temperature: blue-white stars are the hottest, orange and red stars the coolest. This is one of the first things astronomers notice about a star.

**Key term — Constellation:** A group of stars that form a recognisable pattern when viewed from Earth. The stars in a constellation are usually not close to each other in space; they only look grouped because we see them from one direction.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Finding Constellations">
  How can the Plough help you find north?

  The two pointer stars at the front of the bowl point towards Polaris, the North Star, which marks north.

  **Answer:** The two pointer stars at the front of the bowl point towards Polaris, the North Star, which marks north.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking stars in a constellation are close together in space** The stars in one constellation can be hundreds of light years apart; the pattern is a trick of perspective from Earth.
  - **Expecting to see the night sky clearly straight away** Your eyes need about 20 minutes in the dark to adjust. Looking at a phone screen resets the process, so put devices away while stargazing.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="Name two things, other than stars, that you can see in the night sky." hint="Think of the brightest object after the Sun, and the bright dots that move slowly over weeks.">
    The Moon and planets such as Venus, Mars and Jupiter are easy to see with the naked eye.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why stars appear to move across the sky during the night." hint="It is Earth doing the moving, not the stars.">
    Earth spins on its axis once every 24 hours, so the sky appears to rotate overhead while the stars stay fixed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How long does it take for the Moon to go through all its phases once?" hint="Slightly less than one calendar month.">
    About 29.5 days, roughly one month.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do astronomers use red torches instead of white ones?" hint="Think about what bright light does to your night vision.">
    White light destroys night vision, which takes about 20 minutes to build up, while dim red light preserves it.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
