## Key ideas

### Why stars seem to move

Stars appear to move across the sky during the night, but it is actually Earth that is turning. Earth spins once every 24 hours, so the whole sky appears to rotate around a point near the pole star. This is the same reason the Sun rises and sets.

### The Moon changes shape

The Moon does not make its own light; we see it because sunlight bounces off it. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half, from a thin crescent to a full disc. The full cycle of phases takes just under a month.

**Key term — Night vision:** Your eyes' ability to see in the dark, which takes around 20 minutes to fully develop. Bright white light destroys it, which is why astronomers use dim red torches.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Sky Watching Safely">
  Why do astronomers use red torches instead of white ones?

  White light destroys night vision, which takes about 20 minutes to build up, while dim red light preserves it.

  **Answer:** White light destroys night vision, which takes about 20 minutes to build up, while dim red light preserves it.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Expecting to see the night sky clearly straight away** Your eyes need about 20 minutes in the dark to adjust. Looking at a phone screen resets the process, so put devices away while stargazing.
  - **Saying the Moon changes shape because of Earth's shadow** The phases of the Moon are caused by the viewing angle as the Moon orbits Earth, not by Earth's shadow. Earth's shadow only falls on the Moon during a rare lunar eclipse.
</ImportantNote>
