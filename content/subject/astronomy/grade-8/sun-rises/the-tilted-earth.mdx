This lesson focuses on The Tilted Earth: how Earth's tilt creates the seasons as we orbit the Sun.

<Definition term="The Tilted Earth">
  How Earth's tilt creates the seasons as we orbit the Sun.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Why we have seasons

Earth's axis is tilted, so as Earth orbits the Sun, first one hemisphere then the other leans towards the Sun. The hemisphere leaning towards the Sun gets stronger sunlight and longer days: summer. The tilt, not Earth's distance from the Sun, causes the seasons.

### Day and night

Earth rotates once every 24 hours. When your part of Earth turns to face the Sun, you have daytime; when it turns away, you have night. The Sun only appears to rise and set because we are moving with Earth.

**Key term — Revolution:** Earth's journey around the Sun, taking about 365 days, or one year. Revolution combined with Earth's tilt causes the seasons.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Tilted Earth">
  What causes the seasons: Earth's distance from the Sun or its tilt?

  Earth's tilt. The tilted axis means each hemisphere takes turns leaning towards the Sun through the year.

  **Answer:** Earth's tilt. The tilted axis means each hemisphere takes turns leaning towards the Sun through the year.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying summer happens because Earth is closer to the Sun** Earth is actually slightly closer to the Sun in January. Seasons are caused by Earth's tilt, not by distance.
  - **Thinking the Sun moves around Earth each day** The Sun appears to move because Earth rotates; the Sun itself stays still while Earth spins.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How long does Earth take to spin around once?" hint="This is the length of one day.">
    24 hours.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why the Sun appears to rise in the east." hint="Think about which way Earth spins.">
    Earth rotates from west to east, so the eastern horizon turns towards the Sun first, making the Sun appear to rise in the east.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do the north and south poles have months of daylight followed by months of darkness?" hint="Think about how the tilt affects the poles during Earth's orbit.">
    The tilt means each pole spends half the year leaning towards the Sun (continuous daylight) and half leaning away (continuous night).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A sundial's shadow moves during the day. What makes it move?" hint="It is not the Sun moving.">
    Earth's rotation carries the sundial around, so the Sun appears to move across the sky and the shadow shifts with it.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
