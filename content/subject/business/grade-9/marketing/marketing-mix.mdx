This lesson focuses on The Marketing Mix: master product, price, place and promotion.

<Definition term="The Marketing Mix">
  Master product, price, place and promotion.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### The 4Ps in action

Product decisions cover quality, design and packaging; price must cover costs yet attract buyers; place is about distribution channels; promotion includes advertising, social media and sales promotions. The mix must be consistent — a luxury brand cannot sell through discount stores.

### The customer is king

Marketing starts from customer needs, not from the product: identify the target market, understand its wants, then design the offer around them. Businesses that ignore customers — making what they want to make — usually fail.

**Key term — marketing mix:** The blend of product, price, place and promotion decisions — the 4Ps — used to meet customer needs profitably.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Marketing Mix">
  Name the 4Ps of the marketing mix.

  Product, price, place, promotion.

  **Answer:** Product, price, place, promotion.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking marketing is just advertising** Advertising is one promotion method — the 4Ps are far wider, and product and price decisions matter more.
  - **Starting with the product, not the customer** Build what customers want, not what you want to make — research first.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example of 'place' in the marketing mix." hint="Where customers buy.">
    Any one of: shops, supermarkets, online store, market stall, vending machines.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does a strong brand add value?" hint="Think about price.">
    Loyal customers pay premium prices, so the brand lets the firm charge more than production cost alone would justify.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A firm targets wealthy over-50s with a luxury watch. Suggest a suitable 'promotion' method." hint="Think about where they look.">
    Any suitable method, e.g. adverts in quality newspapers or sponsorship of golf events — it must reach wealthy over-50s.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between primary and secondary research?" hint="New data vs existing data.">
    Primary research collects new data first-hand (surveys, interviews); secondary research uses existing data (statistics, reports).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
