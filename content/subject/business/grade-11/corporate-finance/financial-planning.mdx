## Key ideas

### Mergers: motives and risks

Firms merge for economies of scale, market power, diversification or to acquire brands and talent. Yet many mergers destroy shareholder value through overpayment, culture clashes and integration problems — due diligence is essential.

### Appraising investment

Payback is quick and intuitive but ignores the time value of money and cash flows after payback. ARR uses average accounting profit but also ignores timing. NPV discounts future cash flows — theoretically best — but depends on the discount rate chosen. Firms often use several methods together.

**Key term — net present value:** The value today of a project's future cash flows minus the initial investment, discounted for time. A positive NPV means accept. Often shortened to NPV.

<WorkedExample title="An NPV decision">
  A project costs £100,000 now and returns £40,000 a year for 3 years. The discount rate is 10%. Should it proceed?

  Discount each inflow: year 1 £40,000 × 0.909 = £36,364; year 2 × 0.826 = £33,058; year 3 × 0.751 = £30,053.
  Total present value: £36,364 + £33,058 + £30,053 = £99,475.
  NPV = £99,475 − £100,000 = about −£530.
  NPV is negative, so reject on financial grounds.

  **Answer:** NPV is about −£530 — negative, so the project should be rejected (or re-examined at a lower discount rate).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Accepting projects on payback alone** Payback ignores cash flows after payback and the time value of money — always consider NPV too.
  - **Thinking debt is always bad** Moderate debt is cheap finance and magnifies returns; the danger is excessive gearing.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A project costs £50,000 and returns £20,000 a year. What is the payback period?" hint="Divide.">
    2.5 years (£50,000 ÷ £20,000).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What NPV rule should a firm follow?" hint="Positive means…">
    Accept projects with positive NPV; reject those with negative NPV.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage of retained profit as a source of finance." hint="Think about cost and risk.">
    Any one of: no interest, no repayment, no loss of control, no issue costs.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does high gearing mean?" hint="Debt vs equity.">
    A high proportion of capital from debt — higher risk but higher potential returns to shareholders.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
