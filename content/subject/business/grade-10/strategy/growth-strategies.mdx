## Key ideas

### Ansoff and growth risk

Market penetration (more of existing products to existing markets) is safest; diversification (new products, new markets) is riskiest. Firms balance organic growth against mergers and takeovers, which are fast but bring integration risk.

### Porter's generic strategies

Porter argues firms win through cost leadership (being the cheapest, like Aldi), differentiation (unique products commanding premiums, like Apple), or focus (serving a niche superbly). Being 'stuck in the middle' — neither cheapest nor different — is dangerous.

**Key term — Ansoff's matrix:** A growth framework: market penetration, market development, product development and diversification, in rising order of risk.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Growth Strategies">
  What is the riskiest option on Ansoff's matrix?

  Diversification.

  **Answer:** Diversification.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pursuing growth for its own sake** Growth that destroys profit or overstretches resources (overtrading) harms shareholders.
  - **Copying rivals' strategies** Advantage must be hard to copy and fit your resources — imitation invites direct retaliation.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name Porter's three generic strategies." hint="Cheap, different, niche.">
    Cost leadership, differentiation, focus.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A strength is internal or external in SWOT?" hint="Inside the firm.">
    Internal — strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one benefit of economies of scale." hint="Think unit costs.">
    Lower average costs, allowing lower prices or higher margins.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one risk of expanding abroad." hint="Think currency or culture.">
    Any one of: exchange-rate movements, cultural misunderstandings, political instability, unfamiliar regulation.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
