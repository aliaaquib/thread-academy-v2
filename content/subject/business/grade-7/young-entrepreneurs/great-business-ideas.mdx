You are never too young to think like an entrepreneur. This chapter shows where business ideas come from, how to plan a tiny venture like a bake sale or a car-washing round, and how to tell whether it actually makes a profit.

This lesson focuses on Great Business Ideas: learn where business ideas come from.

## Key ideas

### Spotting opportunities

Good ideas solve problems: classmates forget pencils, neighbours' cars get dirty, parents want birthday cakes. Entrepreneurs notice frustrations and imagine a product or service that fixes them — then check whether people will actually pay.

### Planning simply

Even a lemonade stand needs a plan: what will you sell, what will it cost to make, what price will you charge, and where will you sell it? Writing the plan down helps you spot problems before you spend any money.

**Key term — entrepreneur:** Someone who spots an opportunity and starts a business, taking the risk in the hope of reward. Entrepreneurs organise resources and accept uncertainty.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Great Business Ideas">
  What is the difference between revenue and profit?

  Revenue is total sales income; profit is revenue minus costs.

  **Answer:** Revenue is total sales income; profit is revenue minus costs.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming friends are customers** Friends may buy once from kindness; a real business needs strangers willing to pay repeatedly.
  - **Forgetting hidden costs** Your time, travel and packaging are real costs — include them or your profit is exaggerated.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is a customer more important than a good product?" hint="No buyers means…">
    Because without customers buying, even a brilliant product earns no revenue and the business fails.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Tom sells 10 cakes at £1.50 each. Ingredients cost £5. What is his profit?" hint="Revenue first, then subtract.">
    Revenue £15, profit £15 − £5 = £10.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one quality of a good entrepreneur." hint="Think about spotting chances.">
    Any one of: spots opportunities, works hard, takes calculated risks, is creative, persists after setbacks.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one hidden cost of running a bake sale." hint="Beyond ingredients.">
    Any one of: your time, travel, packaging, electricity for the oven.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
