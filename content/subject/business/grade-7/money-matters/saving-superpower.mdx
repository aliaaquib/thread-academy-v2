<Definition term="The Saving Superpower">
  Learn why saving first beats spending first.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Pay yourself first

Most people spend first and save what is left — which is usually nothing. The golden habit is reversed: move savings aside the moment money arrives, then spend the rest guilt-free. Even £1 a week builds the habit and the pot.

### Spending smart

Smart spenders compare prices, wait a day before buying wants, and ask three questions: do I need it, will I use it, can I afford it? Adverts are designed to make you want things; recognising tricks like celebrity endorsement and 'limited-time offers' weakens their power.

**Key term — budget:** A plan dividing your income between saving and different kinds of spending, so your money lasts.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Saving Superpower">
  What does 'pay yourself first' mean?

  Move money into savings the moment income arrives, before spending anything.

  **Answer:** Move money into savings the moment income arrives, before spending anything.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saving 'what is left'** There is rarely anything left — move savings first, the moment money arrives.
  - **Trusting every advert** Adverts exist to sell, not to inform; the 'sale' price may never have been the real price.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is a budget useful?" hint="Think about control.">
    It plans where money goes, so you save enough, avoid overspending and stay in control.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one way a child could earn money." hint="Think chores or neighbours.">
    Any one of: chores, dog walking, car washing, selling crafts, paper round.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is an impulse buy?" hint="Unplanned…">
    An unplanned purchase made on sudden desire, without thinking it through.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You want £30 trainers and get £5 a week. If you save it all, how many weeks?" hint="Divide.">
    6 weeks (£30 ÷ £5).
  </PracticeItem>
