## Key ideas

### Disagreements are normal

Teams disagree because people see things differently — that is healthy. Unhealthy conflict comes from personal attacks or refusing to listen. Fair teams focus on the problem, let everyone speak, and compromise or vote to decide.

### Why teams beat individuals

Teams combine different skills and ideas: one person is creative, another organised, a third good with numbers. Working together also shares the workload and catches mistakes — but only if everyone contributes and respects each other.

**Key term — team:** A group of people working together towards a shared goal, each contributing different strengths.

<WorkedExample title="The science fair team">
  Four pupils must build a volcano model in one week. Allocate roles and a simple plan.

  List the tasks: research, building, painting, presenting.
  Match strengths: the artistic pupil paints, the confident speaker presents.
  Set mini-deadlines: research by Tuesday, build by Thursday.
  Agree disputes are settled by majority vote.

  **Answer:** Each pupil has a role matched to their strengths, with mini-deadlines and a fair voting rule for disagreements.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Letting one person do everything** That is not teamwork — share tasks fairly so everyone learns and no one burns out.
  - **Interrupting instead of listening** Good team members listen fully before responding; interrupting loses ideas and causes resentment.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a group and a team?" hint="Think about shared goals.">
    A team works together towards a shared goal with coordinated roles; a group may just be people together.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three useful team roles." hint="Leader…">
    Any three of: leader, recorder, timekeeper, researcher, presenter.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a compromise?" hint="Everyone gives up something.">
    A solution where each side gives up something so the team can move forward.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one sign of poor communication in a team." hint="Think interrupting or silence.">
    Any one of: interrupting, not listening, people not knowing their tasks, important news not shared.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
