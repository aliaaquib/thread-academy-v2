## Key ideas

### Sectors of industry

Primary businesses extract raw materials (farming, mining), secondary businesses manufacture goods, and tertiary businesses provide services. The UK economy is dominated by the tertiary sector, and many firms span sectors — a bakery buys primary wheat and sells tertiary café service.

### Ownership and liability

A sole trader is simple to set up but the owner is personally liable for debts — their house could be at risk. A private limited company (Ltd) is a separate legal person, so shareholders' liability is limited to their investment, making it easier to raise finance but costlier to administer.

**Key term — sole trader:** A business owned and run by one person, who keeps all the profit but faces unlimited liability for debts.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Sectors of Industry">
  A farm extracts wheat. Which sector is it in?

  The primary sector.

  **Answer:** The primary sector.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking profit is the only objective** Survival, growth and social aims often dominate — especially for new firms and social enterprises.
  - **Confusing revenue with profit** Revenue is total sales; profit is what remains after costs — a firm can have huge revenue and still make a loss.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is unlimited liability?" hint="Think about personal assets.">
    The owner is personally responsible for all business debts, so personal assets like a house are at risk.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage of a sole trader." hint="Think simplicity.">
    Any one of: simple to set up, full control, keeps all profit, cheap to run.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two stakeholders of a supermarket." hint="Think beyond owners.">
    Any two of: employees, customers, suppliers, local community, government.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might a new business prioritise survival over profit?" hint="Think about the first year.">
    Because most new firms fail early — staying alive long enough to establish itself matters more than maximising short-term profit.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
