## Solving problems for profit

A **business** is an organisation that produces goods or services to meet people's needs and wants — usually aiming to make a **profit**. Behind every business is **enterprise**: someone spotting an opportunity and taking the risk to pursue it.

<Definition term="business">
  A **business** combines resources (land, labour, capital) through **enterprise** to produce
  goods or services that meet needs and wants, aiming to earn profit.
</Definition>

## Needs vs wants

- **Needs** are essentials for survival: food, water, shelter, clothing.
- **Wants** are desires beyond survival: the latest phone, branded trainers, holidays.

The line moves with wealth and culture — but the distinction matters: businesses selling *needs* (bread, medicine) face steadier demand than those selling *wants* (luxury watches), which suffer first in a downturn.

<ImportantNote title="Why it matters">
  "Is this a need or a want?" is the first question behind pricing, marketing and survival.
  Needs sell themselves; wants must be *sold*.
</ImportantNote>

## The four factors of production

Every business combines four ingredients:

1. **Land** — natural resources: the site, raw materials, water, sunlight.
2. **Labour** — human effort and skill: workers, managers, designers.
3. **Capital** — manufactured resources used in production: machinery, buildings, vehicles, software.
4. **Enterprise** — the risk-taking organiser who brings the other three together. Without enterprise, the rest sits idle.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: a bakery">
  **Land:** the shop premises, wheat, water. **Labour:** bakers and the cashier.
  **Capital:** ovens, mixers, the delivery van. **Enterprise:** the founder who risked
  savings on the idea that this street needed better bread.
</WorkedExample>

Each factor earns a **reward**: land earns *rent*, labour earns *wages*, capital earns *interest*, enterprise earns *profit* (or suffers loss — the risk).

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Classify each factor for a taxi firm: the car, the driver, the founder's idea, the fuel." hint="Capital, labour, enterprise, land.">
    Car → capital. Driver → labour. Founder's idea/risk → enterprise. Fuel (oil) → land (natural resource).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why wants are riskier to sell than needs." hint="What happens to each in a recession?">
    In hard times people cut wants first but keep buying needs, so demand for wants is less stable — riskier for the business.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
