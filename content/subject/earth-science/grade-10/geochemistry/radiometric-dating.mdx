## Key ideas

### Radioactive decay is a geological clock

Each radioactive isotope decays at a fixed rate described by its half-life, unaffected by heat, pressure or chemistry. By measuring the ratio of parent to daughter isotopes in a mineral, geologists calculate how many half-lives have passed since the crystal formed. Carbon-14, with a short half-life, dates organic material back tens of thousands of years, while potassium-40 dates ancient rocks billions of years old.

### Cooling magma sorts minerals by temperature

Bowen's reaction series has two branches: the discontinuous branch runs from olivine through pyroxene and amphibole to biotite, while the continuous branch runs from calcium-rich to sodium-rich plagioclase feldspar. Both branches converge on potassium feldspar, muscovite and finally quartz. Early-formed minerals react with the remaining melt as cooling continues, which is why fractional crystallisation can produce very different rocks from one magma.

**Key term — Half-life:** A half-life is the time taken for half the atoms of a radioactive parent isotope in a sample to decay into the daughter isotope.

<WorkedExample title="Dating a rock with potassium-40">
  A mineral crystal contains one part potassium-40 for every seven parts of its daughter product, argon-40. How many half-lives have passed, and what does that tell you?

  Work out the fraction of parent isotope remaining: 1 part parent out of 8 parts total means one-eighth remains.
  Each half-life halves the parent: after one half-life one-half remains, after two one-quarter, after three one-eighth.
  One-eighth remaining means three half-lives have passed.
  Because potassium-40 has a very long half-life, three half-lives means the crystal formed billions of years ago, in the very ancient Earth.

  **Answer:** Three half-lives have passed, since (1/2)³ = 1/8. The rock is extremely ancient, dating from billions of years ago.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking half-life means half the atoms vanish** The atoms do not vanish: each parent atom transforms into a daughter atom. The total number of atoms stays the same; only the ratio changes.
  - **Using carbon-14 to date dinosaurs** Carbon-14's short half-life makes it useless beyond about 50,000 years. Dinosaur fossils, tens of millions of years old, need long-lived systems such as potassium-argon.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Write a word equation for the oxidation of iron in rocks." hint="Iron plus oxygen plus water gives...">
    Iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron oxide (rust).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why can carbon-14 dating not be used on a 100-million-year-old fossil?" hint="Think about how much parent would remain.">
    After so many half-lives essentially no carbon-14 remains; its short half-life only allows dating back tens of thousands of years.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why fractional crystallisation can make one magma produce both basalt and granite." hint="Think about early crystals being removed.">
    Early-formed minerals such as olivine are rich in iron and magnesium; if they settle out and are removed, the remaining melt becomes richer in silica, eventually crystallising silica-rich rocks like granite.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is quartz harder and more resistant to weathering than olivine?" hint="Think about how the tetrahedra are linked.">
    Quartz shares every oxygen in a strong three-dimensional framework, while olivine has isolated tetrahedra with weaker ionic bonds, so olivine breaks down more easily.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
