## Key ideas

### Rivers change from source to mouth

In the upper course, steep gradients give fast water that cuts V-shaped valleys and waterfalls. In the middle course the valley widens and the river begins to meander, eroding the outer bends and depositing on the inner bends. In the lower course the gentle river builds wide floodplains and deltas, dropping the last of its load where it meets the sea.

### Waves both destroy and build coasts

Erosion processes such as hydraulic action and abrasion cut cliffs back, carving out caves, arches, stacks and stumps in sequence. Where the coast is sheltered, deposition takes over: longshore drift carries sediment along the shore and drops it to form beaches and spits. Whether a coast erodes or grows depends on the balance between these forces.

**Key term — Tributary:** A tributary is a smaller stream or river that flows into a larger river. The point where they join is called a confluence.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: River Landscapes">
  Describe two landforms found in the upper course of a river and explain how each forms.

  V-shaped valleys, cut by fast vertical erosion, and waterfalls, where a hard rock band resists erosion while softer rock below is worn away, creating a plunge pool.

  **Answer:** V-shaped valleys, cut by fast vertical erosion, and waterfalls, where a hard rock band resists erosion while softer rock below is worn away, creating a plunge pool.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking deltas form wherever a river meets the sea** Deltas form only where the river drops lots of sediment and the sea is calm enough not to wash it away. On exposed coasts, longshore drift carries the sediment off.
  - **Mixing up hydraulic action and abrasion** Hydraulic action is the sheer force of water compressing air into cracks; abrasion is rock particles carried by the water scraping the cliff like sandpaper. One is water power, the other is grinding.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a watershed?" hint="Think about high ground dividing rivers.">
    The boundary, usually along high ground, that separates two drainage basins so rainfall drains into different river systems.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Put these coastal landforms in the order they form: stump, cave, arch, stack." hint="Start with the hole in the cliff.">
    Cave, arch, stack, stump.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is groundwater in aquifers an important water source, and what is one risk of overusing it?" hint="Think about what happens when extraction exceeds recharge.">
    Aquifers store large volumes of clean, naturally filtered water; over-extraction lowers the water table and can cause wells to run dry or allow seawater into coastal aquifers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain how longshore drift moves sediment along a beach." hint="Think about waves arriving at an angle.">
    Waves approach the shore at an angle and push sediment up the beach diagonally; the backwash pulls it straight down, so sediment moves in a zigzag along the coast.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
