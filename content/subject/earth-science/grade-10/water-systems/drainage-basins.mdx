## Key ideas

### Rivers change from source to mouth

In the upper course, steep gradients give fast water that cuts V-shaped valleys and waterfalls. In the middle course the valley widens and the river begins to meander, eroding the outer bends and depositing on the inner bends. In the lower course the gentle river builds wide floodplains and deltas, dropping the last of its load where it meets the sea.

### Water must be managed, not just used

Dams store water and generate hydroelectric power but flood valleys and trap sediment. Desalination turns seawater into drinking water but needs large amounts of energy. Transfer schemes move water from wet regions to dry ones but can spark disputes between regions. Sustainable management means matching supply to demand while protecting ecosystems.

**Key term — Watershed:** A watershed is the boundary, usually along high ground, separating two drainage basins. Rain falling on either side drains into different river systems.

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking deltas form wherever a river meets the sea** Deltas form only where the river drops lots of sediment and the sea is calm enough not to wash it away. On exposed coasts, longshore drift carries the sediment off.
  - **Mixing up hydraulic action and abrasion** Hydraulic action is the sheer force of water compressing air into cracks; abrasion is rock particles carried by the water scraping the cliff like sandpaper. One is water power, the other is grinding.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Describe two landforms found in the upper course of a river and explain how each forms." hint="Think steep and fast.">
    V-shaped valleys, cut by fast vertical erosion, and waterfalls, where a hard rock band resists erosion while softer rock below is worn away, creating a plunge pool.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain how longshore drift moves sediment along a beach." hint="Think about waves arriving at an angle.">
    Waves approach the shore at an angle and push sediment up the beach diagonally; the backwash pulls it straight down, so sediment moves in a zigzag along the coast.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Put these coastal landforms in the order they form: stump, cave, arch, stack." hint="Start with the hole in the cliff.">
    Cave, arch, stack, stump.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage and one disadvantage of building a dam." hint="Think power versus flooding.">
    Advantage: stores water and can generate hydroelectric power. Disadvantage: floods valleys, displaces people and traps sediment.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
