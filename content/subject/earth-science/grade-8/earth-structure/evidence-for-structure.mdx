## Key ideas

### Earthquake waves X-ray the planet

When an earthquake happens, seismic waves travel through the whole Earth and are recorded by seismometers worldwide. P-waves slow down and S-waves stop completely on paths through a deep liquid layer, which is how we know the outer core is liquid iron and nickel. Changes in wave speed mark the boundaries between the crust, mantle and core.

### Three kinds of plate margin

At constructive margins plates pull apart and magma rises to build new crust, as along mid-ocean ridges. At destructive margins one plate subducts beneath another, causing deep earthquakes and volcanoes, as around the Pacific. At conservative margins plates slide sideways past each other, building up stress released as earthquakes, as along California's San Andreas Fault.

**Key term — Seismic waves:** Seismic waves are vibrations that travel through the Earth after an earthquake. How they speed up, slow down or disappear reveals what the rock inside the Earth is like.

<WorkedExample title="Reading the shadow zone">
  After a large earthquake, seismometers on the opposite side of Earth record P-waves but no S-waves. What do the missing S-waves tell us about the outer core?

  Recall that S-waves are shear waves: they can travel through solids but not through liquids.
  P-waves are recorded, so waves did reach the far side; only the S-waves are missing.
  Something on the path must have blocked the S-waves while letting P-waves through.
  The only layer on that path that is liquid is the outer core, so the missing S-waves are evidence that the outer core is liquid.

  **Answer:** The missing S-waves show that the waves passed through a liquid, because S-waves cannot travel through liquids. This is evidence that the outer core is liquid.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Mixing up P-waves and S-waves** P-waves are the faster primary waves and travel through solids and liquids; S-waves are slower secondary waves and travel only through solids. Remember: P for primary and first to arrive.
  - **Saying the mantle is completely liquid** Most of the mantle is solid rock that flows extremely slowly, like warm toffee. Only the outer core is fully liquid.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is subduction, and what hazards does it cause?" hint="Think about one plate sinking.">
    Subduction is one plate sinking beneath another at a destructive margin; it causes deep earthquakes and volcanoes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the four main layers of the Earth from the surface down." hint="Start with the thin rocky skin.">
    Crust, mantle, outer core, inner core.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Earthquakes happen along the San Andreas Fault, but there are no volcanoes there. What type of margin is it, and why are there no volcanoes?" hint="How do the plates move there?">
    It is a conservative margin: the plates slide sideways past each other, so no crust is created or destroyed and no magma rises, hence earthquakes but no volcanoes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why can S-waves not travel through the outer core?" hint="Think about what S-waves need.">
    S-waves are shear waves that only travel through solids, and the outer core is liquid.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
