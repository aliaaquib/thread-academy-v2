## Key ideas

### The continents were once joined

The coastlines of South America and Africa fit together like jigsaw pieces, and identical fossils and rock types appear on both sides of the Atlantic. Alfred Wegener argued the continents had once formed one supercontinent and later drifted apart. His idea was rejected at first because he could not explain what moved the continents, until sea-floor spreading supplied the mechanism.

### Earthquake waves X-ray the planet

When an earthquake happens, seismic waves travel through the whole Earth and are recorded by seismometers worldwide. P-waves slow down and S-waves stop completely on paths through a deep liquid layer, which is how we know the outer core is liquid iron and nickel. Changes in wave speed mark the boundaries between the crust, mantle and core.

**Key term — Continental drift:** Continental drift is the slow movement of continents across Earth's surface over millions of years, driven by the motion of tectonic plates.

  **Answer:** Any two of: the jigsaw fit of continents such as South America and Africa; identical fossils found on continents now separated by oceans; matching rock types and mountain ranges across the Atlantic.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying the mantle is completely liquid** Most of the mantle is solid rock that flows extremely slowly, like warm toffee. Only the outer core is fully liquid.
  - **Mixing up P-waves and S-waves** P-waves are the faster primary waves and travel through solids and liquids; S-waves are slower secondary waves and travel only through solids. Remember: P for primary and first to arrive.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the four main layers of the Earth from the surface down." hint="Start with the thin rocky skin.">
    Crust, mantle, outer core, inner core.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why can S-waves not travel through the outer core?" hint="Think about what S-waves need.">
    S-waves are shear waves that only travel through solids, and the outer core is liquid.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What happens at a mid-ocean ridge?" hint="Think about plates moving apart.">
    Two plates pull apart and magma rises to cool and form new oceanic crust: sea-floor spreading.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is subduction, and what hazards does it cause?" hint="Think about one plate sinking.">
    Subduction is one plate sinking beneath another at a destructive margin; it causes deep earthquakes and volcanoes.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
