Every day the Sun rises and sets, the Moon changes shape, and through the year the seasons turn. In this chapter you will discover the simple spinning and tilting movements of Earth that cause day and night, the Moon's phases and the seasons.

This lesson focuses on Our Star, the Sun: learn what the Sun is and why its light and heat make life on Earth possible.

## Key ideas

### The Moon's changing shape is a trick of light

The Moon does not make its own light; we see it because it reflects sunlight. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half, which we call phases, from a thin crescent to a full Moon. The Moon itself is always a round ball.

### Day and night come from spinning

Earth spins on its axis once every 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has daytime while the opposite side faces away into night. It is Earth's spin, not the Sun moving, that makes the Sun appear to rise in the east and set in the west.

**Key term — Star:** A star is a huge ball of glowing gas that makes its own light and heat. The Sun is the star at the centre of our Solar System.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Our Star, the Sun">

  **Answer:** The Sun is a star: a huge ball of glowing gas that makes its own light and heat.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking the Sun moves around Earth** The Sun only appears to move across the sky. It is Earth spinning on its axis that causes sunrise and sunset.
  - **Thinking summer happens when Earth is nearer the Sun** Earth's distance from the Sun barely changes through the year. Seasons happen because Earth's tilt points each half of the planet towards or away from the Sun.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How long does Earth take to spin once on its axis?" hint="Think about the length of a day.">
    About 24 hours, which is one day.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do we have night?" hint="Think about which side of Earth you are on.">
    We have night when our part of Earth has spun to face away from the Sun.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does the Moon seem to change shape?" hint="Think about sunlight and what we can see.">
    We see different amounts of the Moon's sunlit half as it orbits Earth; these changing views are called phases.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What causes the seasons?" hint="It is about tilt, not distance.">
    Earth's tilted axis means each half of the planet leans towards the Sun for part of the year (summer) and away for the rest (winter).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
