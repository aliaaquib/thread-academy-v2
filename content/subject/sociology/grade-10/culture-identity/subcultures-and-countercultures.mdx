## Key ideas

### Deviance is a label

Howard Becker argued in Outsiders (1963) that deviance is not a quality of an act but the result of social groups making rules and applying them: a person becomes deviant by being successfully labelled as such. His labelling theory, building on Chicago School studies of urban life, shows how being called a criminal or a troublemaker can push people towards the very behaviour condemned. The theory's power is showing that reactions create deviance; its limit is saying less about why some acts are harmful in the first place.

### Life is a theatre

In The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1959), Erving Goffman used dramaturgy, the language of theatre, to describe social life: we manage impressions from our front stage, like a classroom, while relaxing into a truer self back stage, like a bedroom. Gender, ethnicity and class shape the roles we are offered and how audiences judge our performances. The idea reveals identity as something we do, not just something we have.

**Key term — Counterculture:** A subculture whose values deliberately oppose mainstream society, such as the hippies of the 1960s with their rejection of consumerism and conventional careers.

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming subcultures are criminal gangs** Most subcultures are harmless style or interest groups; researchers study them for what they reveal about mainstream values, not because members are criminals.
  - **Thinking globalisation makes everyone the same** Global contact produces hybridity, new mixed cultures, and people also actively resist and adapt global influences rather than just copying them.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="State Becker's labelling theory in one sentence." hint="Who creates deviance: the actor or the audience?">
    Deviance is created when social groups make rules and successfully label rule-breakers as outsiders.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Using Goffman's dramaturgy, explain why you might act differently in a job interview and at home with friends." hint="Front stage versus back stage.">
    The interview is front stage: you perform a polished self to manage the interviewer's impression. Home is back stage: you drop the performance among a trusted audience.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one real example of cultural hybridity and explain the mix." hint="Think of food or music that blends two traditions.">
    Any accurate example, e.g. bhangra music blending Punjabi folk traditions with Western pop and hip-hop in Britain.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Summarise Adorno's criticism of the culture industry." hint="Standardised products, passive audiences.">
    Adorno argued that mass entertainment is standardised by profit-driven industries, keeping audiences passive, conformist and distracted from real inequality.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
