</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Society as a living body

Functionalists, following Durkheim, compare society to a body: just as organs keep a body alive, institutions like the family, schools and religion each perform functions that keep society stable. Durkheim argued that even apparently private acts are shaped by social facts, forces outside the individual. The strength of functionalism is explaining social order; its weakness is that it can overlook conflict, inequality and social change.

### Society as a battleground

Conflict theorists, following Marx, see society as divided between those who own wealth and power and those who do not. Marx argued that the bourgeoisie, who own the means of production, exploit the proletariat, who must sell their labour to survive, and that this class conflict drives history. The strength of conflict theory is exposing inequality; critics say it exaggerates conflict and reduces everything to economics.

**Key term — Conflict theory:** The perspective, associated with Karl Marx, that sees society as divided by competition between groups with unequal power and resources.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Three Core Perspectives">
  Name the three core sociological perspectives and the thinker most associated with each.

  Functionalism, Durkheim; conflict theory, Marx; symbolic interactionism, Mead and Goffman.

  **Answer:** Functionalism, Durkheim; conflict theory, Marx; symbolic interactionism, Mead and Goffman.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Mixing up structural and action perspectives** Functionalism and conflict theory are structural, macro perspectives about big forces; symbolic interactionism is an action, micro perspective about individuals. Keep the levels straight.
  - **Treating one perspective as the proven right answer** Perspectives are lenses for interpreting evidence, not facts. Strong answers compare their strengths and weaknesses.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Apply conflict theory to the fact that private-school pupils are over-represented in top universities." hint="Who has the resources, and whose interests does the system serve?">
    Conflict theorists would argue that wealthy families convert economic power into educational advantage, so the system reproduces class inequality rather than rewarding pure merit.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Durkheim studied suicide rates and found they varied with levels of social integration. Which perspective does this classic study support, and why?" hint="It links a private act to a social force.">
    Functionalism: it shows that even suicide, seemingly the most individual act, is shaped by social facts such as integration, supporting the view of society as a constraining system.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a functionalist mean by a social function?" hint="Think of organs in a body.">
    The contribution an institution makes to keeping society stable and orderly, like the family socialising children.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain the structure versus agency debate in your own words." hint="How much are we shaped, and how much do we choose?">
    The debate over whether people's behaviour is mainly determined by social structures such as class and institutions, or by individuals' own choices and actions.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
