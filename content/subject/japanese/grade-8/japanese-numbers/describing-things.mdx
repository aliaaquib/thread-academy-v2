## Key ideas

### Colours become adjectives

Before a noun, most colour words take an い ending: あか → あかい りんご (akai ringo, a red apple), あお → あおい そら (aoi sora, a blue sky). Saying あか りんご is a classic learner mistake. A few colours like みどり keep their noun form with の: みどりの かばん (midori no kaban, a green bag).

### Two ways to say some numbers

The numbers 4, 7 and 9 each have two readings: し/よん (shi/yon), しち/なな (shichi/nana), きゅう/く (kyū/ku). し sounds like the word for death, so よん is often preferred in everyday counting. In set phrases the traditional reading wins — July is しちがつ (shichigatsu). When in doubt while counting, use よん, なな, きゅう.

**Key term — いち (ichi):** One. The starting point of counting; the numbers 1–10 are いち, に, さん, し/よん, ご, ろく, しち/なな, はち, きゅう/く, じゅう.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Describing Things">
  Read this number word: はち.

  'hachi' — eight.

  **Answer:** 'hachi' — eight.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Putting あか directly before a noun** あか りんご ✗ → あかい りんご ✓ (akai ringo, a red apple). Colour words need their い adjective ending before nouns.
  - **Reading きいろ without the long vowel** きいろ (kiiro, yellow) has a long 'ii'. Shortening it to きろ changes the word entirely — stretch that vowel.
</ImportantNote>
