## Key ideas

### The etiquette of eating

いただきます before the meal and ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita, thank you for the meal) after it frame eating as gratitude. Never stick chopsticks upright in rice (たてばし, tatebashi) — it recalls funeral offerings. Pass food with the thick ends of your chopsticks, not the ends you eat with, and lift small bowls to your mouth rather than leaning over the table.

### A year of festivals

正月 (しょうがつ, shōgatsu, New Year) is the year's biggest holiday, spent with family and first shrine visits (初詣, はつもうで, hatsumōde). Spring brings 花見 (はなみ, hanami) under the blossom; summer brings 盆 (ぼん, bon), honouring ancestors; July brings 七夕 (たなばた, tanabata), the star festival. Festivals blend Shinto and Buddhist roots with community celebration — learn their foods, greetings and customs as a set.

**Key term — いただきます (itadakimasu):** 'I humbly receive' — said before eating to thank everyone involved in bringing the meal to the table.

<WorkedExample title="What to say before a meal">
  You are a guest at a Japanese home and dinner is served. What do you say, and why?

  Recognise the moment: the meal is about to begin.
  Recall the phrase of gratitude said before eating: いただきます.
  Say it with your hands together, before touching your chopsticks.
  Understand the meaning: 'I humbly receive' — thanks to the cook, the farmers, and everyone who made the meal possible.

  **Answer:** Say いただきます (itadakimasu). It expresses gratitude to everyone involved in bringing the food to the table.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying いただきます after the meal** いただきます opens the meal; ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita) closes it. Swapping them confuses your hosts.
  - **Sticking chopsticks upright in rice** たてばし (tatebashi) mirrors offerings made at funerals — it is one of Japan's gravest table taboos. Lay chopsticks across your bowl or on the rest instead.
</ImportantNote>
