Culture is the context that makes language make sense — why people bow, what いただきます really thanks, and when the whole country picnics under cherry blossom. This chapter tours daily customs, the festival calendar, food etiquette and modern society. Understanding these turns textbook Japanese into lived Japanese.

This lesson focuses on Customs of Daily Life: bowing, shoes-off homes and the small rituals of everyday respect.

## Key ideas

### Bowing and the genkan

Bows replace handshakes: deeper and longer means more respect, and you bow lower to those senior to you. At home, the 玄関 (げんかん, genkan) entryway marks the line between outside and inside — outdoor shoes come off there and slippers go on. Keeping the outside out is both hygiene and respect for the home.

### A year of festivals

正月 (しょうがつ, shōgatsu, New Year) is the year's biggest holiday, spent with family and first shrine visits (初詣, はつもうで, hatsumōde). Spring brings 花見 (はなみ, hanami) under the blossom; summer brings 盆 (ぼん, bon), honouring ancestors; July brings 七夕 (たなばた, tanabata), the star festival. Festivals blend Shinto and Buddhist roots with community celebration — learn their foods, greetings and customs as a set.

**Key term — おじぎ (ojigi):** Bowing. The depth and duration of a bow express the degree of respect, from a nod to a deep 45-degree bow.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Customs of Daily Life">
  Where do you remove your outdoor shoes in a Japanese home?

  At the 玄関 (げんかん, genkan).

  **Answer:** At the 玄関 (げんかん, genkan).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Wearing outdoor shoes indoors** Shoes come off at the 玄関 (genkan) — stepping inside with them on is unhygienic and disrespectful. Change into the slippers provided.
  - **Saying いただきます after the meal** いただきます opens the meal; ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita) closes it. Swapping them confuses your hosts.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="What do you say before eating in Japan?" hint="'I humbly receive'.">
    いただきます (itadakimasu).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What do you say after finishing a meal?" hint="It thanks the cook for the feast.">
    ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which spring festival involves picnicking under cherry blossom?" hint="はなみ.">
    花見 (はなみ, hanami) — cherry-blossom viewing.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you never stick chopsticks upright in rice?" hint="Think of funeral customs.">
    It is called たてばし (tatebashi) and resembles offerings to the dead — a serious taboo.
  </PracticeItem>
