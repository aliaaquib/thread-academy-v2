## Key ideas

### Four ways to say 'if'

〜と states general truths and inevitable results: 春になると、花が咲く (haru ni naru to, hana ga saku — when spring comes, flowers bloom). 〜ば gives hypothetical conditions: 安ければ買う (yasukereba kau — if it's cheap, I'll buy it). 〜たら covers 'if/when' with a completed condition: 着いたら電話する (tsuitara denwa suru — when I arrive, I'll call). 〜なら sets up a topic-based condition: 行くなら早くしろ (iku nara hayaku shiro — if you're going, hurry up). Choosing well is a hallmark of advanced writing.

### Forming the passive

Group 2 (ichidan) verbs: drop る, add られる — たべる → たべられる (taberareru, to be eaten). Group 1 (godan) verbs: change the final -u sound to -a and add れる — かく → かかれる (kakareru, to be written), のむ → のまれる (nomareru, to be drunk). The agent takes に: 母にしかられた (haha ni shikarareta, I was scolded by my mother). Japanese also has the 'suffering passive' for unwelcome events: 雨に降られた (ame ni furareta, I was rained on — and suffered for it).

**Key term — たら (tara):** The 〜たら conditional — 'if/when'. 雨が降ったら、行きません (ame ga futtara, ikimasen — if it rains, I won't go).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Four Conditionals">
  Which conditional suits a general truth like 'when spring comes, flowers bloom'?

  〜と: 春になると、花が咲く.

  **Answer:** 〜と: 春になると、花が咲く.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forming the Group 1 passive like Group 2** かく → かくられる ✗. Change the final -u to -a first: かく → かかれる ✓ (kakareru, to be written).
  - **Using ら抜き言葉 (ranuki)** たべれる ✗ is non-standard slang for the potential form. In exams write たべられる ✓ (taberareru, can eat).
</ImportantNote>

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give the causative form of たべる." hint="Group 2: drop る, add させる.">
