## Key ideas

### Reading a haiku: 5-7-5

Haiku count sounds (morae), not English syllables. Bashō's most famous haiku: 古池や (ふるいけや, furuike ya — 5) / 蛙飛び込む (かわずとびこむ, kawazu tobikomu — 7) / 水の音 (みずのおと, mizu no oto — 5). 'An old pond — a frog jumps in — the sound of water.' The や (ya) is a cutting word (kireji) that slices the poem into two images, and the frog is the kigo placing it in spring.

### Sōseki and the modern novel

夏目漱石's こころ (Kokoro, 1914) follows a student and his enigmatic mentor 'Sensei' through guilt, friendship and Japan's shift from Meiji tradition to modernity. Its famous opening line — 私はその人を常に先生と呼んでいた (watashi wa sono hito wo tsune ni sensei to yonde ita, 'I always called him Sensei') — establishes the intimate, confessional tone. For essays, discuss themes (個人主義, kojinshugi, individualism; 罪, tsumi, guilt) with textual evidence, not plot summary alone.

**Key term — はいく (haiku):** A 17-sound poem in a 5-7-5 pattern. It captures a single moment, usually with a season word.

<WorkedExample title="Counting the sounds in a haiku line">
  Does みずのおと fit the final 5-sound line of a haiku?

  Split into morae: み・ず・の・お・と.
  Count them: 1-2-3-4-5.
  Compare with the 5-7-5 pattern's final line, which needs exactly 5.
  Note that each kana counts as one mora — the お is a single sound.

  **Answer:** Yes — みずのおと (mizu no oto, 'the sound of water') has exactly 5 morae, fitting the final line of Bashō's haiku.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Counting English syllables instead of Japanese morae** Haiku is 5-7-5 sounds in Japanese, not syllables in translation. 'An old pond' is 4 English syllables but 古池や is 5 Japanese morae — always count the Japanese.
  - **Writing essays in です・ます style** です・ます is for speech. A-level essays demand the literary である style: 大切です ✗ → 大切である ✓ (taisetsu de aru, it is important).
</ImportantNote>
