## Key ideas

### Sōseki and the modern novel

夏目漱石's こころ (Kokoro, 1914) follows a student and his enigmatic mentor 'Sensei' through guilt, friendship and Japan's shift from Meiji tradition to modernity. Its famous opening line — 私はその人を常に先生と呼んでいた (watashi wa sono hito wo tsune ni sensei to yonde ita, 'I always called him Sensei') — establishes the intimate, confessional tone. For essays, discuss themes (個人主義, kojinshugi, individualism; 罪, tsumi, guilt) with textual evidence, not plot summary alone.

### Literary language: the である style

Essays and novels use the plain literary copula である instead of です: 彼は学生である (kare wa gakusei de aru, he is a student). Literary Japanese is kanji-dense and avoids conversational fillers. At A-level, write your essays in である style — examiners expect it — and quote key terms in kanji to show precision.

**Key term — しょうせつ (shōsetsu):** Novel. The modern Japanese novel emerged in the Meiji era, with 夏目漱石 (Natsume Sōseki) as its founding figure.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Modern Japanese Fiction">
  Who wrote the novel こころ (Kokoro)?

  夏目漱石 (Natsume Sōseki), published 1914.

  **Answer:** 夏目漱石 (Natsume Sōseki), published 1914.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Summarising plot instead of analysing** Examiners reward discussion of theme, language and context — for Kokoro, guilt and Meiji modernisation — supported by short quotations, not retellings of the story.
  - **Counting English syllables instead of Japanese morae** Haiku is 5-7-5 sounds in Japanese, not syllables in translation. 'An old pond' is 4 English syllables but 古池や is 5 Japanese morae — always count the Japanese.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="What is a kigo?" hint="It anchors the poem to a time of year.">
    A season word, e.g. かわず (frog) for spring.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Rewrite in literary style: これは大切です." hint="Replace です with the literary copula.">
    これは大切である。(Kore wa taisetsu de aru.).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does えいが mean? Name one film-studies term." hint="Think cinema.">
    'Eiga' means film; e.g. かんとく (kantoku, director) or あらすじ (arasuji, plot summary).
  </PracticeItem>
