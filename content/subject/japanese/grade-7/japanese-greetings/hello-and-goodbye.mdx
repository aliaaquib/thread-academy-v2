In Japan, greetings do heavy lifting: they open conversations, show respect and set the tone for everything after. This chapter gives you the essential phrases for hello, goodbye, introductions and thanks. Practise them aloud — Japanese greetings sound best with a smile and a small bow.

This lesson focuses on Hello and Goodbye: greet people at any time of day and take your leave politely.

<Definition term="Hello and Goodbye">
  Greet people at any time of day and take your leave politely.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Greetings change with the time of day

Japanese greets the morning with おはよう (ohayō), the day with こんにちは (konnichiwa) and the evening with こんばんは (konbanwa, good evening). Using こんにちは at night sounds as odd as saying 'good morning' at midnight. Listen for which greeting Japanese speakers use and copy them.

### Bowing and politeness

Greetings in Japan usually come with a small bow, and longer, deeper bows show more respect. Pairing your words with a bow matters as much as the words themselves. In polite speech, ありがとうございます beats a plain ありがとう with teachers and strangers.

**Key term — こんにちは (konnichiwa):** Hello / good afternoon. The all-purpose daytime greeting, used from late morning until evening.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Hello and Goodbye">
  What do you say when meeting someone for the very first time?

  はじめまして (hajimemashite).

  **Answer:** はじめまして (hajimemashite).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying さようなら for a quick goodbye** さようなら sounds final, like leaving for a long time. For 'see you later', use またね (mata ne) or じゃあね (jā ne) with friends.
  - **Using こんにちは in the evening** After dark, switch to こんばんは (konbanwa, good evening). Time-matching your greeting is a quick way to sound natural.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say 'good evening' in Japanese?" hint="It starts with こん, like こんにちは.">
    こんばんは (konbanwa).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does ありがとう mean?" hint="You say it when someone gives you something.">
    Thank you.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Translate into Japanese: 'I am Mia.'" hint="Use わたしは…です. Mia in katakana is ミア.">
    わたしは ミア です。(Watashi wa Mia desu.).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your friend is leaving school and you will see them tomorrow. Should you say さようなら?" hint="さようなら is for long farewells.">
    No — use またね (mata ne) or またあした (mata ashita, see you tomorrow).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
