## Key ideas

### Polite vs plain style

です and ます mark the polite style used with teachers, strangers and anyone older. The plain style — だ instead of です, たべる instead of たべます — is for close friends and family. Mixing them in one sentence is a common error. Pick one style per conversation and stick to it.

### Word order with particles

Japanese is subject–object–verb: わたしは りんごを たべます (watashi wa ringo wo tabemasu, I eat apples). The particle は (pronounced wa) marks the topic and を (pronounced wo) marks the object. Particles, not word order, show who does what — so learn each particle's job.

**Key term — です (desu):** The polite copula — like 'is/am/are' in polite speech. がくせいです (gakusei desu) means 'I am a student'.

<WorkedExample title="Saying 'This is a book'">
  How do you say 'This is a book' in polite Japanese?

  'This' (near me) is これ.
  Mark it as the topic with は — pronounced 'wa'.
  'Book' is ほん (hon).
  Finish with the polite copula です.

  **Answer:** これは ほんです。(Kore wa hon desu.) — This is a book.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Asking questions without か** Rising intonation alone does not make a Japanese question in writing or polite speech. これは ほんです ✗ (statement) → これは ほんですか ✓ (question).
  - **Pronouncing the particle は as 'ha'** As a particle, は is always 'wa': わたしは = watashi wa. Save 'ha' for inside words like はは (haha, mother).
</ImportantNote>
