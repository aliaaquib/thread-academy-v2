## Key ideas

### い-adjectives and な-adjectives

い-adjectives like たのしい (tanoshii, fun) end in い and describe nouns directly: たのしい ゲーム (tanoshii gēmu, a fun game). な-adjectives like すき need な before a noun: すきな ほん (suki na hon, a book I like). Spot which type an adjective is and you will place it correctly every time.

### Saying what you like

好き works differently from the English verb 'like': the liked thing is marked with が, not を. サッカーが すきです (sakkā ga suki desu) is literally 'football is likeable to me' → 'I like football'. Remember が with すき and きらい (kirai, dislike), and the pattern will serve you for years.

**Key term — がっこう (gakkō):** School. Note the small っ doubles the k-sound: ga-k-kō, not ga-ko.

<WorkedExample title="Saying 'I like football'">
  How do you say 'I like football' in polite Japanese?

  'Football' is the loanword サッカー (sakkā) — katakana, because it is borrowed.
  'Like' uses すき (suki), which takes the particle が, not を.
  Add the polite copula です.
  Put it together: サッカー + が + すき + です.

  **Answer:** サッカーが すきです。(Sakkā ga suki desu.) — I like football.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using を with すき** サッカーを すきです ✗ → サッカーが すきです ✓. The liked thing always takes が.
  - **Calling your own mother おかあさん to outsiders** Use the humble はは (haha) for your own family when speaking to others; save おかあさん for other people's mothers.
</ImportantNote>
