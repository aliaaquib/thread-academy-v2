## Key ideas

### い-adjectives and な-adjectives

い-adjectives like たのしい (tanoshii, fun) end in い and describe nouns directly: たのしい ゲーム (tanoshii gēmu, a fun game). な-adjectives like すき need な before a noun: すきな ほん (suki na hon, a book I like). Spot which type an adjective is and you will place it correctly every time.

### Two sets of family words

Japanese has humble words for your own family and respectful words for others'. Your mother is はは (haha) when you talk about her, but someone else's mother is おかあさん (okāsan). Mixing them up — calling your own mother おかあさん to a stranger — is a classic error that instantly marks you as a learner.

**Key term — かぞく (kazoku):** Family. When speaking about your own family to outsiders, use humble terms: ちち (chichi, my father), はは (haha, my mother).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Describing People">
  Translate: 'my friend'.

  わたしの ともだち (watashi no tomodachi).

  **Answer:** わたしの ともだち (watashi no tomodachi).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling your own mother おかあさん to outsiders** Use the humble はは (haha) for your own family when speaking to others; save おかあさん for other people's mothers.
  - **Using を with すき** サッカーを すきです ✗ → サッカーが すきです ✓. The liked thing always takes が.
</ImportantNote>
