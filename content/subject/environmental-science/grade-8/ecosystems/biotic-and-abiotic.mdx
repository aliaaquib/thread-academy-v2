</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Ecosystems combine living and non-living parts

The biotic community of producers, consumers and decomposers depends on abiotic factors such as light, temperature and water. Change an abiotic factor, for example by shading a pond, and the whole community shifts because the organisms are adapted to particular conditions.

### Ecosystems develop through succession

Bare ground is colonised first by hardy pioneer species, which change the soil and shelter so other species can follow. Over time a stable climax community forms, such as woodland. If a forest burns, secondary succession restarts the process from the surviving soil.

**Key term — Abiotic factor:** A non-living influence on an ecosystem, such as temperature, light intensity, water availability, soil pH or wind.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Biotic and Abiotic">
  Sort these into biotic and abiotic: rainfall, oak trees, soil pH, fungi, wind, caterpillars.

  Biotic: oak trees, fungi, caterpillars. Abiotic: rainfall, soil pH, wind.

  **Answer:** Biotic: oak trees, fungi, caterpillars. Abiotic: rainfall, soil pH, wind.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking energy is recycled like nutrients** Nutrients cycle through decomposers, but energy flows one way and is lost as heat. Ecosystems need a constant input of light energy.
  - **Confusing habitat with niche** A habitat is where an organism lives; a niche is what it does there. Two species can share a habitat but occupy different niches.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Define an ecosystem in one or two sentences." hint="Mention both the living community and the non-living surroundings.">
    An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with each other and with the non-living parts of their environment, such as water, soil and climate.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are there always fewer top predators than producers in an ecosystem?" hint="Think about what happens to energy at each feeding step.">
    Only about one tenth of energy passes to each next trophic level, with the rest lost as heat and waste. Little energy remains to support large predators, so fewer of them can survive.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A lake becomes shaded by new buildings. Predict one likely effect on its ecosystem and explain why." hint="Light is an abiotic factor that producers depend on.">
    Less light means less photosynthesis by pondweed and algae, so producers decline. With less food and oxygen, the animals that depend on them would decline too.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Two bird species eat the same insects in the same wood. What ecological problem might they face?" hint="Think about what happens when niches overlap.">
    They occupy very similar niches and will compete for the same food. One may outcompete the other unless their niches differ in some way, such as feeding at different heights.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
