<Definition term="The Water Cycle">
  Follow water as it journeys between sky, land and sea.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Water is always on the move

The Sun heats seas and lakes, water evaporates and rises, cools into clouds, and falls as rain. Rivers carry it back to the sea and the cycle begins again. No new water is being made, so we share the same water the dinosaurs drank.

### Most water is not drinkable

About 97 percent of Earth's water is salty seawater, and much of the fresh water is locked in ice. Only a tiny share is easily available in rivers and lakes, which is why clean drinking water is precious and must not be wasted.

**Key term — Water cycle:** The continuous journey of water between the oceans, the air and the land, driven by the Sun's heat. The same water is reused again and again.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Water Cycle">
  Name the four main stages of the water cycle in order.

  Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection as rivers carry water back to the sea.

  **Answer:** Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection as rivers carry water back to the sea.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking water is used up when we drink it** Water is never used up; it moves through the water cycle and is reused endlessly. What matters is keeping it clean and not wasting the clean supply.
  - **Believing all water is safe to drink** Only treated tap water is reliably safe. River, pond and sea water can contain germs and pollution, so never drink them.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Where does your tap water come from? Describe its journey in two or three steps." hint="Start with rain and end at your tap.">
    Rain falls on hills and gathers in rivers or reservoirs. It is piped to a treatment works where it is cleaned, then travels through underground pipes to homes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is evaporation?" hint="Think about what happens to a puddle on a sunny day.">
    Evaporation is when liquid water is warmed, usually by the Sun, and turns into invisible water vapour that rises into the air.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is only a small share of Earth's water available for us to drink?" hint="Think about what most of Earth's water is like.">
    About 97 percent is salty seawater, and much fresh water is frozen in ice. Only a tiny share sits in rivers and lakes where we can easily reach it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give three ways your family could use less water." hint="Think about the bathroom, the kitchen and the garden.">
    Examples: take shorter showers, turn off the tap while brushing teeth, fix dripping taps, water plants with a watering can, and only run full washing loads.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
