## Key ideas

### Biodiversity works at three levels

Genetic diversity lets a species adapt to disease and change; species diversity keeps food webs functioning; ecosystem diversity provides varied habitats and services. Losing any level weakens the others: a species reduced to a few inbred individuals may survive today but cannot adapt tomorrow.

### Good reserve design matters as much as size

Large, connected protected areas support more species than small isolated ones, because small reserves lose species faster and cut off breeding. Wildlife corridors linking reserves let animals migrate and genes flow. Buffer zones around core protected areas reduce conflict with neighbouring farmland.

**Key term — Biodiversity:** The variety of life at three levels: genetic diversity within species, species diversity in an area, and ecosystem diversity across landscapes.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Restoration Ecology">
  A wetland drained for farming is to be restored. Suggest two restoration steps and explain each.

  Block the drainage channels to rewet the soil, restoring the water levels wetland plants and birds need. Replant native reeds and sedges to rebuild habitat structure and food sources, which then attract insects, fish and birds back.

  **Answer:** Block the drainage channels to rewet the soil, restoring the water levels wetland plants and birds need. Replant native reeds and sedges to rebuild habitat structure and food sources, which then attract insects, fish and birds back.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking conservation means keeping nature untouched** Many habitats, such as meadows and heathland, depend on traditional management like grazing. Conservation often means active management, not abandonment.
  - **Believing extinction is always natural and unstoppable** Background extinction is natural, but the current rate is far higher and driven by human causes that conservation action can address.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are endemic species especially vulnerable to extinction?" hint="Think about what happens if their one home is damaged.">
    They live in only one place, so if that habitat is destroyed or altered they have no other wild population to fall back on.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the three levels of biodiversity and give an example of each." hint="Think genes, species and ecosystems.">
    Genetic diversity: different varieties of rice. Species diversity: the number of bird species in a forest. Ecosystem diversity: the range of habitats such as wetlands, grasslands and forests in a region.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why wildlife corridors improve a network of small reserves." hint="Think about what isolated populations cannot do.">
    Corridors let animals move between reserves to feed, breed and migrate, and allow genes to flow. This prevents inbreeding and lets species recolonise if one reserve suffers a disaster.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="List the five main threats to biodiversity." hint="Think habitat, harvesting, invaders, pollution and climate.">
    Habitat destruction, overexploitation, invasive species, pollution, and climate change.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
