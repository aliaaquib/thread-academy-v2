## Key ideas

### The greenhouse effect is natural, but humans have strengthened it

Greenhouse gases trap some of the Sun's heat, keeping Earth warm enough for life. By burning fossil fuels and cutting forests, humans have added extra carbon dioxide and methane, trapping more heat and raising global temperatures.

### Pollution takes three main forms

Air pollution comes from vehicles, power stations and industry; water pollution from sewage, farm fertilisers and oil spills; land pollution from litter, landfill and chemicals. All three can travel far from their source: winds carry smog across countries and rivers carry waste to the sea.

**Key term — Acid rain:** Rain made acidic by gases such as sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from burning fossil fuels. It damages trees, lakes and stone buildings.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Acid Rain and Smog">
  How does acid rain form, and what damage does it cause?

  Burning fossil fuels releases sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which dissolve in rainwater making it acidic. Acid rain damages tree leaves, acidifies lakes killing fish, and erodes stone buildings.

  **Answer:** Burning fossil fuels releases sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which dissolve in rainwater making it acidic. Acid rain damages tree leaves, acidifies lakes killing fish, and erodes stone buildings.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking pollution only harms the place it is released** Pollutants travel. Acid rain can fall far from the power station that caused it, and ocean currents spread plastic worldwide.
  - **Assuming climate change only means warmer weather** A warmer planet also brings heavier rainfall, stronger storms, rising seas and shifting seasons, not just higher temperatures.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two greenhouse gases and one human source of each." hint="One comes from burning fuels; the other from farming and waste.">
    Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels such as coal and petrol. Methane from cattle farming, rice paddies and rotting waste in landfill.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example each of air, water and land pollution, naming the source." hint="Think of a vehicle, a farm and a rubbish tip.">
    Air: exhaust fumes from vehicles. Water: fertiliser runoff from farms. Land: chemicals leaking from landfill sites.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why the greenhouse effect itself is not the problem." hint="Think about what Earth would be like without any greenhouse gases.">
    The natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth warm enough for life. The problem is that human activities have added extra greenhouse gases, strengthening the effect and warming the planet beyond its natural balance.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is habitat destruction especially dangerous when combined with pollution?" hint="Think about how a species copes when it faces two threats at once.">
    Habitat loss shrinks populations and pollution weakens the survivors. A small, stressed population is far less able to recover, so the two threats multiply each other's harm.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
