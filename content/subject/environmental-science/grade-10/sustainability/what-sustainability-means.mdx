## Key ideas

### Sustainability rests on three pillars

A truly sustainable choice protects the environment, supports society and makes economic sense. Cheap coal power may help the economy but fails the environmental pillar, while banning all farming would protect nature but fail the social pillar. Good decisions balance all three.

### Renewable does not mean unlimited

Timber is renewable only if trees are replanted and felling stays within regrowth rates; overfished seas collapse because fish are taken faster than they breed. Fossil fuels, by contrast, took millions of years to form and are non-renewable on any human timescale.

**Key term — Sustainability:** Meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It balances environmental, social and economic goals.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Sustainability Means">
  Give one example of a sustainable choice for each of the three pillars.

  Environmental: buying certified sustainable timber. Social: fair wages for farm workers. Economic: a repair business that creates local jobs while cutting waste.

  **Answer:** Environmental: buying certified sustainable timber. Social: fair wages for farm workers. Economic: a repair business that creates local jobs while cutting waste.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking sustainability is only about the environment** Sustainability has three pillars: environmental, social and economic. A solution must work for people and livelihoods as well as nature.
  - **Calling all natural resources renewable** Fossil fuels and minerals do not renew on human timescales. Only resources that regrow or refill, and are harvested within those rates, count as renewable.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Define sustainability in your own words." hint="Think about the needs of people living a hundred years from now.">
    Meeting our own needs today without damaging the ability of future generations to meet theirs, balancing environmental, social and economic goals.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three actions that shrink a personal carbon footprint." hint="Think about travel, food and home energy.">
    Examples: walk, cycle or use public transport instead of driving; eat less meat and waste less food; switch to a green electricity tariff and insulate the home.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why fish stocks can be renewable yet still collapse." hint="Compare the rate of catching with the rate of breeding.">
    Fish breed and replace themselves, so they are renewable. But if fishing removes them faster than they breed, the stock shrinks each year and can collapse.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does the circular economy differ from the linear economy?" hint="Follow a product from raw material to the end of its life in each model.">
    The linear economy takes resources, makes products and throws them away. The circular economy designs products for repair, reuse and recycling so materials keep circulating and waste is designed out.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
