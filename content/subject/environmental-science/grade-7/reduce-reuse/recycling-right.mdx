## Key ideas

### Rubbish does not disappear

When the bin lorry drives away, waste is usually taken to landfill, where it is buried and compacted. Many materials, such as plastic bottles, take hundreds of years to break down, so the rubbish we make today can still be in the ground when we are grandparents.

### Reduce comes before recycle

The three Rs work best in order: reduce, reuse, then recycle. Recycling still uses energy and lorries, while reducing avoids the waste entirely. Refusing an extra plastic bag or choosing a product with less packaging beats recycling the bag afterwards.

**Key term — Waste:** Anything we throw away because we no longer want or need it. Waste includes food scraps, packaging, old clothes and broken objects.

<WorkedExample title="Sorting a packed lunch">
  Sort this lunch waste into the best option: an apple core, a plastic sandwich bag, a banana skin, a drinks can, a reusable lunchbox, an unopened spare biscuit.

  Apple core and banana skin: food waste, which can be composted rather than landfilled.
  Drinks can: rinse and recycle, since metal cans are widely recycled.
  Plastic sandwich bag: could have been avoided with the reusable lunchbox, so this is a reduce lesson for tomorrow.
  Reusable lunchbox: already the best choice, wash and use again.
  Unopened spare biscuit: save it for tomorrow instead of binning it, which is reducing waste.

  **Answer:** Compost: apple core, banana skin. Recycle: drinks can. Reuse: lunchbox. Reduce next time: use the lunchbox instead of the plastic bag, and keep the spare biscuit for tomorrow.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking recycling fixes everything** Recycling uses energy and some materials can only be recycled a few times. Reducing waste in the first place is always better.
  - **Putting the wrong things in the recycling bin** Food-stained card, crisp packets and many soft plastics cannot go in most household recycling. Wrong items can spoil a whole load.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Put the three Rs in order of importance and explain why the first one wins." hint="Which R stops waste from existing at all?">
    Reduce, reuse, recycle. Reducing wins because it avoids creating waste entirely, while reusing and recycling still use energy and transport.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is reusing a glass jar better than recycling it?" hint="Compare the energy each option needs.">
    Reusing needs no melting or remaking, so it uses far less energy than recycling the jar into new glass.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your old bike is too small for you but still works. What is the best thing to do with it?" hint="Someone else could still ride it.">
    Pass it on: give it to a sibling, donate it or sell it second-hand. Reusing it avoids waste and saves the resources needed to make a new bike.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a landfill?" hint="Think about where the bin lorry takes your rubbish.">
    A landfill is a site where rubbish is buried in the ground. Waste there can take hundreds of years to break down.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
