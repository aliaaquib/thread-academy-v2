Look closely at your garden, park or school grounds and you will find a whole world of living things. From tiny ants under a stone to tall trees full of birds, nature is everywhere. In this chapter you will learn to notice, name and understand the wildlife around you.

This lesson focuses on Habitats Nearby: discover the different places where living things make their homes around you.

<Definition term="Habitats Nearby">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Habitats provide everything needed

Every habitat supplies its residents with food, water, shelter and space. A robin needs insects or berries to eat, water to drink, and bushes to nest in. If one of these is missing, the animal cannot live there, which is why different animals are found in different places.

### Living things change with the seasons

In spring many plants grow new leaves and animals have their young. In autumn trees drop their leaves and some birds fly to warmer countries. In winter many minibeasts hide away and some mammals sleep through the cold. These changes are responses to temperature and the length of daylight.

**Key term — Organism:** Any living thing, from a mushroom to a mouse. All organisms need food and water, and they respond to changes around them.

<WorkedExample title="Sorting living and non-living">
  Decide which of these are living things: a daisy, a stone, a robin, a puddle, a mushroom, a fallen leaf.

  Ask whether it grows or once grew: the daisy, robin, mushroom and leaf all grew at some point.
  Ask whether it needs food or water: the daisy, robin and mushroom do; the stone and puddle do not.
  Ask whether it can reproduce: the daisy, robin and mushroom can make more of themselves.
  Conclude that the daisy, robin and mushroom are living, the stone and puddle are non-living, and the fallen leaf is the remains of something that was once living.

  **Answer:** Living: daisy, robin, mushroom. Non-living: stone, puddle. The fallen leaf came from a living plant but is no longer alive itself.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking plants are not alive** Plants grow, need water, make food and respond to light, so they are living things just like animals.
  - **Calling every small creature an insect** Spiders, woodlice and worms are not insects. Insects have six legs and three body parts, while spiders have eight legs.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three things every habitat must provide for the animals that live there." hint="Think about what an animal needs every single day to stay alive.">
    Food, water and shelter, plus enough space. A habitat must supply all of these.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A woodlouse is found under a damp log. What does this tell you about its habitat needs?" hint="Think about what the log provides that the open path does not.">
    The woodlouse needs damp, dark shelter. The log gives it moisture, protection from drying out, and hiding places from predators.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do green plants need sunlight?" hint="It is linked to how plants make their food.">
    Plants use sunlight, water and air to make their own food in photosynthesis. Without light they cannot feed themselves and will weaken or die.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example of an adaptation and explain how it helps the organism." hint="Pick an animal you know and think about one of its body parts.">
    Example: a duck's webbed feet. They act like paddles and help the duck swim to find food and escape danger.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
