## Key ideas

### Talking to boys and girls

Arabic often changes its endings for males and females. 'How are you?' is كيف حالك؟ (kayfa ḥāluk?) to a boy but كيف حالكِ؟ (kayfa ḥāluki?) to a girl. The same happens with 'what is your name?': ما اسمك؟ (mā ismuk?) for a boy, ما اسمكِ؟ (mā ismuki?) for a girl. Matching the ending shows real care.

### Greetings come with good wishes

Arabic greetings love to wish people well. أهلا وسهلا (ahlan wa sahlan) means 'you have come to family and easy ground' — a warm welcome for guests. When someone asks how you are, answer بخير، الحمد لله (bi-khayr, al-ḥamdu li-llāh — well, praise be to God). These little phrases make every hello feel like a hug.

**Key term — اسمي (ismī) — my name is:** Made of اسم (ism, name) plus ـي, the ending that means 'my'. Say اسمي سارة (ismī Sāra) to mean 'my name is Sara'.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: My Name Is">
  How do you ask a girl 'what is your name?'.

  ما اسمكِ؟ (mā ismuki?).

  **Answer:** ما اسمكِ؟ (mā ismuki?).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Replying to السلام عليكم with السلام عليكم** The matching reply is وعليكم السلام (wa ʿalaykum as-salām) — 'and peace be upon you too'. Repeating the greeting back sounds unfinished.
  - **Using كيف حالكَ to a girl** The ـكَ ending is for males; for females use ـكِ: كيف حالكِ؟ (kayfa ḥāluki?).
</ImportantNote>
