Arabic has 28 letters, and they love to join hands! They march from right to left across the page, the opposite way to English. In this chapter you will meet the letters, learn their sounds and read your very first Arabic words.

This lesson focuses on Meet the Letters: say hello to the 28 letters of the Arabic alphabet and learn which way they march.

## Key ideas

### Letters change their shape

Most Arabic letters change shape depending on where they sit in a word: at the start, in the middle, at the end, or all alone. The letter ع, for example, is a neat loop on its own, grows a tooth at the start of a word, stretches both ways in the middle and curls off at the end, as in علم (ʿilm, knowledge). It is always the same letter with the same sound — only its outfit changes. Learn the four shapes and every word becomes readable.

### Six letters never join forwards

Six letters — ا د ذ ر ز و — are the rebels of the alphabet: they join to the letter before them but never to the letter after them. That is why باب (bāb, door) has a little gap before its last letter: the ا refuses to hold the next ب's hand. Whenever you spot a gap inside a word, look for one of these six.

**Key term — ا (ʾalif) — alif:** The first letter of the Arabic alphabet. It is a tall straight line and makes a long 'aa' sound, as in باب (bāb, door).

  **Answer:** 28 letters.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Writing Arabic words from left to right** Always start on the right-hand side of the page. The first letter of باب sits on the right, not the left.
  - **Mixing up ب ت ث** These three letters share one shape — only the dots differ. ب has one dot below, ت has two dots above and ث has three dots above. Count the dots every time.
</ImportantNote>
