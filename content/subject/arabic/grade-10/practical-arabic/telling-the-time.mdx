## Key ideas

### Telling the time with ordinals

Arabic tells the time with ordinal numbers, and because الساعة is feminine, the ordinals are feminine too: الساعة الواحدة (one o'clock), الساعة الثانية (two o'clock), الساعة الثالثة والنصف (half past three). For minutes past, add و (and): الساعة الخامسة والربع (quarter past five). For minutes to, use إلا (except): الساعة السادسة إلا الربع (quarter to six). The pattern is mechanical — learn it once and every time works.

### Numbers behave differently after ten

From 11 upwards, the counted noun stays singular: أحد عشر كتابا (aḥada ʿashara kitāban, eleven books) — literally 'eleven book'. Numbers 3 to 10 also show 'polarity', taking the opposite gender's form: ثلاثة كتب (thalāthu kutub, three books) but ثلاث سيارات (thalāthu sayyārāt, three cars). For GCSE, remember the golden rule: 3–10 take a plural noun, 11 and above take a singular one.

**Key term — الساعة (as-sāʿa) — hour / o'clock:** The key to telling time. الساعة الثالثة (as-sāʿa ath-thālitha) means 'three o'clock' — note the feminine ordinal because ساعة is feminine.

<WorkedExample title="Saying the time: 3:45">
  It is 3:45. Ask 'what time is it?' and answer in Arabic.

  The question is كم الساعة؟ (kam as-sāʿa?) — literally 'how much is the hour?', i.e. 'what time is it?'.
  3:45 is a quarter to four, so count up to the fourth hour: الساعة الرابعة.
  Use إلا (except) for 'to': الساعة الرابعة إلا الربع.
  Remember الرابعة is feminine to match الساعة.

  **Answer:** كم الساعة؟ الساعة الرابعة إلا الربع. (What time is it? It is quarter to four.).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying الساعة الواحد** ساعة is feminine, so one o'clock needs the feminine ordinal: الساعة الواحدة (as-sāʿa al-wāḥida).
  - **Using و instead of إلا for 'to'** الساعة الخامسة والربع is quarter past five; الساعة السادسة إلا الربع is quarter to six. Swap them and you are half an hour wrong.
</ImportantNote>
