## Key ideas

### Question words are conversation keys

Six question words open almost any conversation: ما (mā, what), من (man, who), أين (ʾayna, where), متى (matā, when), كيف (kayfa, how) and كم (kam, how many / how much). أين تسكن؟ (ʾayna taskun?) means 'where do you live?' and كم عمرك؟ (kam ʿumruk?) means 'how old are you?'. Learn these six and you can interview anyone.

### Match your endings to your listener

Arabic marks gender on the word 'you'. To a boy: اسمك (ismuk), عمرك (ʿumruk), من فضلك (min faḍlik). To a girl: اسمكِ (ismuki), عمركِ (ʿumurki), من فضلكِ (min faḍliki). Examiners love this detail — using the right ending shows genuine control of the language.

**Key term — شكرا (shukran) — thank you:** The essential polite word. Add جزيلا (jazīlan) for extra warmth: شكرا جزيلا (shukran jazīlan, thank you very much).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Everyday Phrases">
  How do you say 'please' to a boy?

  من فضلك (min faḍlik).

  **Answer:** من فضلك (min faḍlik).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Answering شكرا with شكرا** The natural reply to 'thank you' is عفوا (ʿafwan, you're welcome) — echoing شكرا back sounds odd.
  - **Saying ليس أذهب** ليس never goes before a verb. For 'I do not go', use لا: لا أذهب (lā adhhab).
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Your friend thanks you. Reply politely." hint="One word meaning 'you're welcome'.">
    عفوا (ʿafwan).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the Arabic for 'sorry' (said by a boy)?" hint="It starts with آ.">
    آسف (ʾāsif); a girl says آسفة (ʾāsifa).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does أين تسكن؟ mean?" hint="أين = where; تسكن = you live.">
    'Where do you live?' (ʾayna taskun?).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Translate 'I do not speak Arabic' into Arabic." hint="Use لا before the present-tense verb أتكلم.">
    لا أتكلم العربية (lā atakallamu al-ʿarabiyya).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
