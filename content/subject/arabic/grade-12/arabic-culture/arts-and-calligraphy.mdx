## Key ideas

### One language, many voices

The Arab world spans the 22 member states of the Arab League, from Morocco to Oman, and its Arabic is diglossic: Modern Standard Arabic rules news, education and formal writing, while everyday life runs on regional dialects — Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf and Maghrebi among them. Dialects can differ so much that speakers from opposite ends of the region may switch to MSA to understand each other. Learning MSA gives you the key to all of them; learning a dialect gives you a home in one.

### The guest is sacred

Hospitality (ḍiyāfa) is a point of honour: a guest is offered Arabic coffee (قهوة عربية) in small cups, with sweet dates, before any business is discussed — refusing outright can offend. Receive and give with the right hand; the left is considered unclean. Greetings are physical and warm: handshakes, and among friends a cheek-to-cheek greeting. These rituals are not extras — they are the grammar of social life.

**Key term — خط عربي (khaṭṭ ʿarabī) — Arabic calligraphy:** The art of beautiful writing, developed over centuries into styles such as نسخ (naskh), ثلث (thuluth) and كوفي (kūfī). Because figurative art was limited in religious settings, calligraphy became Islam's supreme visual art.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Arts and Calligraphy">
  Name two styles of Arabic calligraphy.

  Any two of: نسخ (naskh), ثلث (thuluth), ديواني (dīwānī), كوفي (kūfī).

  **Answer:** Any two of: نسخ (naskh), ثلث (thuluth), ديواني (dīwānī), كوفي (kūfī).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying رمضان كريم on Eid day** رمضان كريم belongs to Ramadan itself. On Eid, say عيد مبارك.
  - **Offering or taking with the left hand** Always use the right hand for giving, receiving and eating — the left hand is considered unclean in Arab etiquette.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How many member states does the Arab League have?" hint="More than twenty.">
    22 member states.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between MSA and a dialect?" hint="Think newsreader versus home.">
    MSA is the formal, shared standard used in news, school and literature; dialects are the regional spoken varieties used in daily life.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="During Ramadan, between which two daily markers do Muslims fast?" hint="Dawn to sunset.">
    From dawn (الفجر, al-fajr) to sunset (المغرب, al-maghrib).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What do you say to someone at the start of Ramadan?" hint="It means 'generous Ramadan'.">
    رمضان كريم (Ramaḍān karīm).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
