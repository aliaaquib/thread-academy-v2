## Key ideas

### Five factors shape a climate

Latitude decides how directly the Sun's rays hit; altitude cools the air as you climb; distance from the sea brings milder, wetter weather near coasts and more extreme temperatures inland; prevailing winds carry warm or cold, wet or dry air; and ocean currents, like the warm North Atlantic Drift, can heat or cool coastlines.

### The greenhouse effect is natural — humans are strengthening it

Gases such as carbon dioxide trap some of the Sun's heat, keeping Earth warm enough for life — without them the planet would freeze. Burning fossil fuels and cutting down forests add extra greenhouse gases, trapping more heat and warming the climate. That is why scientists distinguish the natural greenhouse effect from the enhanced one.

**Key term — latitude:** How far north or south of the equator a place is, measured in degrees — it is the biggest control on temperature.

<WorkedExample title="Reading a climate graph">
  A climate graph shows temperatures around 27°C all year and heavy rainfall in every month, peaking mid-year. What climate is this, and which biome would you expect?

  Read the temperature line first: it stays near 27°C all year, so there is no real winter — this is a low-latitude, tropical location.
  Read the rainfall bars: every month is wet, so there is no dry season.
  Hot all year plus year-round heavy rain matches a tropical rainforest climate.
  Conclude the biome: dense evergreen rainforest, like the Amazon or Congo basins.

  **Answer:** A tropical rainforest climate — constantly hot with heavy rainfall in every month, supporting dense evergreen forest.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Reading rainfall bars as temperature** On a climate graph, the bars are monthly rainfall and the line is temperature — mixing them up flips the whole interpretation.
  - **Saying 'today is cold, so climate change is wrong'** One cold day is weather; climate change is about long-term global averages measured over decades. Local cold snaps still happen in a warming world.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A town on the coast has milder winters than a town far inland at the same latitude. Why?" hint="Water heats up and cools down more slowly than land.">
    The sea moderates temperatures, warming the coastal town in winter and cooling it in summer; inland places have more extreme continental climates.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="On a climate graph, what do the bars and the line show?" hint="One is measured in millimetres, the other in degrees.">
    Bars show monthly rainfall (mm); the line shows mean monthly temperature (°C).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is it colder at the top of a mountain than at its base?" hint="Think about what happens to air as you climb.">
    Temperature falls with altitude because the air is thinner and under less pressure.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one natural and one human cause of climate change." hint="Natural causes include the Sun and volcanoes; human causes involve fossil fuels.">
    Natural: changes in solar output or volcanic eruptions. Human: burning fossil fuels, deforestation.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
