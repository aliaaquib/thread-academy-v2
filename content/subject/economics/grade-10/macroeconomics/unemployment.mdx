## Key ideas

### Growth and the business cycle

Real GDP growth means the economy produces more, which can raise living standards. Economies move through a business cycle of boom and recession; a recession is commonly defined as two consecutive quarters of falling GDP, bringing falling incomes and rising unemployment.

### Inflation: causes and costs

Demand-pull inflation comes from too much spending chasing too few goods; cost-push inflation comes from rising costs like wages or oil. Moderate inflation is normal, but high inflation erodes savings, confuses price signals and can trigger a wage-price spiral.

**Key term — unemployment rate:** The percentage of the labour force without a job but actively seeking work.

<WorkedExample title="Calculating inflation">
  A basket of everyday goods costs £100 in 2024 and £104 in 2025. Calculate the inflation rate and compare it with the Bank of England's 2% target.

  Find the rise: £104 − £100 = £4.
  Divide by the original: £4 ÷ £100 = 0.04.
  Convert to a percentage: 0.04 × 100 = 4%.
  Compare: 4% is double the 2% target — inflation is too high.

  **Answer:** Inflation is 4%, double the Bank of England's 2% target, so the Bank might consider raising interest rates.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing one price rise with inflation** Inflation is a rise in the general price level — one product getting dearer while others stay flat is a relative price change, not inflation.
  - **Thinking GDP measures happiness** GDP counts output, not wellbeing: it ignores unpaid work, the environment and how income is shared.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one cause of demand-pull and one of cost-push inflation." hint="Too much spending vs rising costs.">
    Demand-pull: excessive consumer spending. Cost-push: rising oil or wage costs.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one cost of unemployment to society." hint="Think about lost output or public money.">
    Any one of: lost output, higher benefit payments, lower tax revenue, social problems such as ill health.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does GDP stand for and what does it measure?" hint="Gross…">
    Gross domestic product — the total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Prices rise from £200 to £210. What is the inflation rate?" hint="Rise ÷ original × 100.">
    5% (£10 ÷ £200 × 100).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
