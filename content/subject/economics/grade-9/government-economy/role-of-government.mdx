Governments shape the economy every day: they build roads, run schools, collect taxes and support people in need. This chapter explains what governments do, where their money comes from, and how they decide what to spend it on.

This lesson focuses on What Governments Do: learn the main economic jobs of a government.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Why governments intervene

Some things markets provide badly: street lights benefit everyone whether they pay or not, so private firms would not supply enough. Governments also protect the vulnerable, keep competition fair and manage the whole economy through fiscal policy.

### Spending choices and trade-offs

Government budgets are limited, so spending more on hospitals means less for something else — the same opportunity-cost idea households face. Borrowing can fund investment today but means interest payments tomorrow.

**Key term — tax:** A compulsory payment to the government, such as income tax on earnings or VAT on shopping. Taxes fund public services.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Governments Do">
  A government spends £500 billion and raises £450 billion in tax. What is the deficit?

  £50 billion (£500bn − £450bn).

  **Answer:** £50 billion (£500bn − £450bn).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming governments can spend without limits** Borrowing must be repaid with interest, so today's spending can mean tomorrow's tax rises or spending cuts.
  - **Thinking taxes disappear** Tax revenue is spent on public services — it is transferred to schools, hospitals and roads, not destroyed.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two public services funded by taxes." hint="Think school and hospital.">
    Any two of: schools, hospitals, roads, police, fire service, street lighting.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is VAT?" hint="It is added at the till.">
    Value Added Tax — a tax added to the price of most goods and services when you buy them.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why would a private firm not provide street lighting?" hint="Think about who benefits and who pays.">
    Because everyone benefits whether they pay or not, so the firm could not charge enough users to cover its costs.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is income tax progressive or regressive? Explain." hint="Think about tax rates rising with income.">
    Progressive — higher earners pay a bigger share of their income in tax.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
