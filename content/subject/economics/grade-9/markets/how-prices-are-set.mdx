## Key ideas

### Prices emerge from interaction

If many buyers chase few goods, sellers can raise prices; if shelves are full of unsold goods, sellers cut prices to attract buyers. Haggling in a bazaar and pricing algorithms on a shopping website are both versions of this price discovery.

### Competition protects consumers

When several firms compete, each must offer better value to win customers, which lowers prices and improves quality and choice. A monopoly — one seller with no rivals — can charge high prices because customers have nowhere else to go.

**Key term — competition:** Rivalry between sellers for customers. It tends to push prices down and quality up.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: How Prices Are Set">
  Why does competition tend to lower prices?

  Rival firms undercut each other to attract buyers, driving prices down.

  **Answer:** Rival firms undercut each other to attract buyers, driving prices down.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming the seller always sets the price** Sellers propose prices, but buyers decide whether to pay — price is the outcome of both sides interacting.
  - **Believing competition always lowers quality** Competition usually raises quality, because firms must attract customers; it is monopolies that can afford to be sloppy.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is collusion between rival firms harmful?" hint="Think about the 80p lemonade.">
    It removes competition, keeping prices high and harming consumers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="If demand for a good rises while supply stays the same, what happens to price?" hint="More buyers, same goods.">
    Price rises, as buyers compete for limited supply.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A new phone is scarce at launch and many people want one. What happens to its price?" hint="Shortage means…">
    Sellers can charge a high price because demand exceeds supply.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two examples of markets, one physical and one digital." hint="Think street and screen.">
    Any physical market (e.g. a farmers' market) and any digital one (e.g. an auction website or stock exchange).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
