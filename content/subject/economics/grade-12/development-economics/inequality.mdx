## Key ideas

### Beyond GDP

GDP per capita misses health, education and the environment. The HDI combines income, schooling and life expectancy; critics note it still hides inequality and environmental damage. Sen's capabilities approach goes further, asking what people are actually free to do and be.

### Why poverty persists

Debated causes include colonial legacies, weak institutions and corruption, geography and disease burden, rapid population growth, and poverty traps where low income means low saving, low investment and low growth. There is no single cause — and no single cure.

**Key term — Gini coefficient:** A 0-to-1 measure of income inequality, where 0 is perfect equality and 1 means one person has everything.

<WorkedExample title="Reading the HDI">
  Country X has GDP per capita of $8,000 and HDI 0.72; Country Y has GDP per capita $6,000 and HDI 0.78. Which is more 'developed'?

  On GDP per capita alone, X looks more developed.
  On the broader HDI measure, Y scores higher.
  The HDI includes health and education, not just output.
  Y converts its income into wellbeing more effectively.

  **Answer:** On the broader HDI measure, Y is more developed despite lower income — development is about more than output.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating all inequality as purely bad** Some inequality can reward enterprise, but extreme inequality harms health, mobility and social cohesion — the level and causes matter.
  - **Equating growth with development** Growth raises output; development improves lives — growth without schools, clinics or rights may leave most people behind.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the three components of the HDI." hint="Money, health, learning.">
    Income (GNI per capita), life expectancy and education.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a Gini coefficient of 0 mean?" hint="Perfect…">
    Perfect income equality.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one limitation of GDP per capita as a development measure." hint="Think about what it ignores.">
    Any one of: ignores health and education, ignores inequality, ignores the environment and unpaid work.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a poverty trap?" hint="Low income → low saving → ?">
    A cycle where low income means low saving and investment, which keeps income low.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
