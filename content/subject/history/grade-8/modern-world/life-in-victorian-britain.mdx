## Key ideas

### Steam changed everything

The steam engine powered factories, mines, ships and railways. Raw cotton could be spun in Manchester, carried by train, and shipped worldwide. Steam made Britain the 'workshop of the world', but it also tied the country's wealth to coal, colonies and harsh factory labour.

### Empire and industry fed each other

Factories needed raw materials such as cotton, and colonies supplied them cheaply; colonies then bought Britain's finished goods. This trade made Britain rich, but it was built on unequal power — and, in earlier centuries, on the transatlantic slave trade, which Britain abolished in 1807 and 1833.

**Key term — Industrialisation:** The growth of factories and machine-made goods, replacing hand work. Britain industrialised first, from about 1760.

<WorkedExample title="Why Did Towns Grow So Fast?">
  Manchester's population exploded in the early 1800s. Using what you know about industrialisation, explain why.

  Identify the push: machines did farm work faster and enclosures reduced farm jobs, so workers left the countryside.
  Identify the pull: new cotton mills needed thousands of workers and paid wages in cash.
  Add transport: canals and then railways let mills bring coal in and send goods out, so factories clustered in towns.
  Link the factors: jobs drew people in, and more people meant more shops, houses and services — so the town grew even faster.

  **Answer:** Manchester grew because factories offered waged work that drew people from the countryside, while canals and railways made the town a perfect centre for industry; each new factory and railway pulled in more workers, and the growing population itself created more jobs.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying the Industrial Revolution happened everywhere at once** It began in Britain from about 1760 and spread to other countries over many decades. Even within Britain, some regions industrialised much earlier than others.
  - **Claiming empire was only about trade** Trade mattered, but empire also meant political control, military power and the belief that British rule was superior — ideas many colonised peoples resisted.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two reasons people moved from the countryside to industrial towns." hint="Think push and pull.">
    Fewer farm jobs pushed them out; factory wages and work pulled them in.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain one way the British Empire was unequal." hint="Who made the decisions?">
    Britain held the political and military power while colonised peoples had little say in how they were governed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="In which year did women win equal voting rights with men in Britain?" hint="After 1918, one more step was needed.">
    1928, when women over 21 could vote on the same terms as men.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How did colonies help British industry?" hint="Factories need raw materials and customers.">
    Colonies supplied cheap raw materials like cotton and bought Britain's manufactured goods.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
