## Key ideas

### Explorers opened up the world

Voyages across the Atlantic and around Africa connected continents that had been unknown to each other. These journeys brought new foods, ideas and maps — but they also led to suffering for the peoples explorers met. Great events can have both bright and dark sides.

### One event can change everything

Before 1666, London was a crowded city of wooden houses. After the Great Fire burned most of it down, the city was rebuilt in brick and stone with wider streets. A single event, lasting just four days, reshaped the capital for centuries.

**Key term — Explorer:** Someone who travels to places unknown to their own people. Explorers crossed oceans in small wooden ships.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Explorers and Voyages">
  Why is it wrong to say explorers 'discovered' empty lands?

  Because peoples already lived in those lands; the explorers were simply new arrivals to them.

  **Answer:** Because peoples already lived in those lands; the explorers were simply new arrivals to them.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing explorers found empty lands** The lands explorers reached already had peoples living in them. 'Discovery' meant new to the explorers, not empty.
  - **Thinking the fire was the end of London** London was rebuilt quickly in brick and stone, and the new buildings, including St Paul's Cathedral, made it grander than before.
</ImportantNote>
