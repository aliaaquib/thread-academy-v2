## Key ideas

### Arguments can be fixed

Even the best friends disagree sometimes. What matters is how you handle it: use calm words, listen to the other side, and look for a compromise. Saying sorry when you were wrong is not weakness — it is one of the bravest parts of friendship.

### Good friends are kind on purpose

Kindness is not just a feeling; it is something you do. Good friends listen when you talk, tell the truth gently, and cheer you on. The good news is that every one of these habits can be practised, starting today.

**Key term — compromise:** When everyone gives a little so that a disagreement can be sorted fairly for all sides.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Fixing Arguments">
  True or false: good friends never argue.

  False. Friends do argue; what matters is fixing arguments kindly.

  **Answer:** False. Friends do argue; what matters is fixing arguments kindly.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Winning an argument means you are right** Shouting loudest does not make you right; listening and finding a fair compromise does.
  - **Real friends never disagree** All friends disagree sometimes. A strong friendship is not one with no arguments, but one where arguments get fixed kindly.
</ImportantNote>
