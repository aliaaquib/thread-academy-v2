## Key ideas

### Different parts do different jobs

The cerebrum, the big folded part on top, handles thinking, memory and planning. Tucked underneath at the back, the cerebellum coordinates movement and balance — it is why practice makes your football kick smoother. The brain stem, connecting the brain to the spinal cord, runs the automatic essentials: breathing, heartbeat and staying awake.

### Neurons are the brain's wiring

Each neuron has branchy dendrites that collect signals, a cell body that processes them, and a long axon that fires the message onward. When the signal reaches the end of the axon, neurotransmitter chemicals carry it across the synapse to the next neuron. Everything you think, feel and do is built from patterns of these tiny handoffs, happening millions of times a second.

**Key term — neuron:** A nerve cell that carries messages around your nervous system; your brain contains around 86 billion of them.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Parts of the Brain">
  Name the three main parts of the brain and one job each does.

  Cerebrum — thinking and memory; cerebellum — movement and balance; brain stem — automatic jobs like breathing and heartbeat.

  **Answer:** Cerebrum — thinking and memory; cerebellum — movement and balance; brain stem — automatic jobs like breathing and heartbeat.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **We only use 10% of our brains** Brain scans show we use virtually all of our brain, just not all at once — different parts switch on for different jobs. The 10% claim is a myth.
  - **The brain stops changing after childhood** The brain keeps rewiring itself throughout life as you learn — that is how adults can still learn languages and musical instruments.
</ImportantNote>
