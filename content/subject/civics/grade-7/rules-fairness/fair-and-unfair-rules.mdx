This lesson focuses on Fair and Unfair Rules: learn what makes a rule fair and why fairness matters.

<Definition term="Fair and Unfair Rules">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Fair rules treat everyone the same

A rule is fair when it applies to everyone equally and has a good reason behind it. Queuing for your turn is fair because everyone waits. A rule that let one child always go first would be unfair. Fairness means everyone gets an equal chance, not that everyone gets whatever they want.

### Rules keep people safe

Rules tell us what to do in shared spaces so nobody gets hurt. Looking both ways before crossing, walking in corridors and taking turns on equipment all prevent accidents. When everyone follows safety rules, the whole group is protected.

**Key term — rule:** An instruction that tells people what they should or should not do in a shared space. Rules help groups stay safe and work well together.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Fair and Unfair Rules">
  Why do we need rules?

  Rules keep people safe and make sure everyone is treated fairly. Without rules, stronger or louder people might take everything for themselves.

  **Answer:** Rules keep people safe and make sure everyone is treated fairly. Without rules, stronger or louder people might take everything for themselves.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing fair means everyone gets exactly the same** Fairness sometimes means giving extra help to those who need it. A child with a broken leg may need to sit near the door; that is fair because it meets a real need. Treating everyone exactly the same can actually be unfair.
  - **Thinking small rule-breaking does not matter** Small broken rules add up. If everyone dropped one piece of litter, the playground would be filthy. Following rules even when nobody is watching shows respect for others.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What makes a rule fair? Give an example." hint="Think about equal treatment and good reasons.">
    It applies to everyone equally and has a sensible reason. Queuing for lunch is fair because everyone waits their turn.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You think a classroom rule is unfair. What should you do?" hint="How can you disagree politely?">
    Listen calmly, explain your view politely, and accept the decision or suggest a better rule respectfully. Shouting or breaking the rule is not fair.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you follow a rule even when no teacher is watching?" hint="Who benefits when everyone follows the rules?">
    Because rules protect everyone, including you. Following them shows respect and keeps shared spaces safe for all.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do schools have a rule about walking in corridors?" hint="What could happen if everyone ran indoors?">
    Walking in corridors stops people bumping into each other and getting hurt. It keeps a busy school safe.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
