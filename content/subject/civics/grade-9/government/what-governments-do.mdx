## Key ideas

### Government organises collective life

Governments make and enforce laws, raise and spend money through taxation, and provide services such as schools, roads and healthcare. They also represent the country abroad and manage defence. Without these functions, large societies could not coordinate the shared systems everyone relies on.

### Power is divided to prevent abuse

Many democracies separate the legislature, which makes laws, from the executive, which carries them out, and the judiciary, which interprets them. Each branch can check the others: courts can review government actions, and legislatures can scrutinise ministers. The United Kingdom blends these branches more than the United States does, but scrutiny by Parliament and independent courts still constrains power.

**Key term — legislature:** The branch of government that makes laws, such as the UK Parliament with its House of Commons and House of Lords. It also scrutinises the executive and approves taxation and spending.

  **Answer:** The system of institutions that makes and enforces laws, raises revenue and provides public services for a country or area. It includes the legislature, executive, judiciary and, in many systems, local authorities.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking the government is just one person** The prime minister or president leads, but government includes ministers, civil servants, councils and courts. Laws are made by legislatures, carried out by executives and interpreted by judges. Focusing on one personality misses the institutions that actually do the work.
  - **Believing courts make the laws** In a democracy, elected legislatures make laws while courts interpret and apply them. Judges can strike down unlawful government action, but they do not write statutes. Confusing the two misunderstands the separation of powers.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Describe the roles of the House of Commons, the House of Lords and the monarch." hint="Elected chamber, revising chamber, ceremonial head.">
    The House of Commons is the elected chamber that makes laws and holds the government to account; the House of Lords is the revising chamber that scrutinises legislation. The monarch's role is ceremonial, giving Royal Assent as a formality.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give three responsibilities of local government and explain why it matters." hint="List daily-life services, then explain closeness to citizens.">
    Councils manage schools, housing, planning, local roads, rubbish collection and local services. They matter because these decisions affect everyday life directly and are closer for citizens to influence.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish the legislature, executive and judiciary." hint="Make, carry out, interpret.">
    The legislature makes laws, the executive carries out laws and runs public services, and the judiciary interprets laws and settles disputes. Dividing them stops too much power gathering in one place and lets each branch check the others.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain the purpose of the separation of powers." hint="Think about preventing the concentration of power.">
    It divides state power among branches so each can limit the others, for example courts reviewing executive action and legislatures scrutinising ministers. This protects liberty by making it harder for any one group to dominate.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
