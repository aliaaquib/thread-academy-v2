<Definition term="Building an Argument">
  Structure claims, evidence and counterarguments convincingly.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Concede to conquer

Strong arguments acknowledge the other side. A counterargument — critics argue X, but… — shows fairness and lets you demolish objections on your own terms. Ignoring opposition looks weak; answering it looks commanding.

### The rhetorical triangle

Every persuasive act balances speaker, audience and message. Ethos convinces the audience to trust you, pathos makes them feel, and logos makes them think. The most powerful persuasion blends all three: evidence alone rarely moves people, and emotion alone rarely convinces them.

**Key term — logos:** Persuasion through logic and evidence. Statistics, reasoning and clear structure appeal to the rational mind.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Building an Argument">
  What is a counterargument?

  Acknowledging and answering an opposing view to strengthen your own position.

  **Answer:** Acknowledging and answering an opposing view to strengthen your own position.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pathos without logos** Emotion moves audiences but evidence convinces them. Even the most stirring speech needs facts beneath the feeling.
  - **Piling on rhetorical questions** One well-placed rhetorical question is powerful; five in a row sound hectoring. Vary your devices.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name Aristotle's three modes of persuasion." hint="Ethos...">
    Ethos, pathos and logos.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a tricolon?" hint="Three-part...">
    A series of three parallel items, e.g. sign the petition, join the protest, save our library.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does anaphora persuade?" hint="Think rhythm.">
    Repetition at the start of clauses builds rhythm and emotional momentum, making ideas memorable.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which mode of persuasion does a personal story mainly use?" hint="Feelings.">
    Pathos.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
