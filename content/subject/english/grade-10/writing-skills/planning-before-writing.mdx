GCSE writing rewards control: controlled planning, controlled paragraphs and controlled language. Whether you are crafting an essay, a description or an argument, the same foundations apply — think first, structure always, and choose every word on purpose.

This lesson focuses on Planning Before Writing: turn a question into a plan with clear, ordered points.

<Definition term="Planning Before Writing">
  Turn a question into a plan with clear, ordered points.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Plan every answer

Spend five minutes planning before writing a single sentence. For an essay, turn the question into three or four points; for a story, map the beginning, middle and end. A plan prevents rambling and guarantees you answer the actual question.

### Show, don't just tell

In descriptive writing, she was sad tells; tears slid down her cheeks as she stared at the empty chair shows. Use the five senses — what can be seen, heard, smelled, touched and tasted — and choose precise nouns and verbs rather than piling on adjectives.

**Key term — discourse marker:** A connective that organises writing: however, furthermore, consequently, on the other hand. They guide the reader through your argument.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Planning Before Writing">
  Why should you plan before writing an essay?

  To organise your points and make sure you answer the actual question.

  **Answer:** To organise your points and make sure you answer the actual question.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Writing everything you know about a text** Only include points that answer the question. Irrelevant knowledge, however accurate, earns no marks.
  - **Repeating the same discourse marker** Vary however, furthermore and in contrast. Repeating also in every paragraph makes writing sound immature.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

  <PracticeItem question="Name three of the five senses to use in descriptive writing." hint="See, hear...">
    Any three of: sight, hearing, smell, touch, taste.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does the final D in PEED stand for?" hint="Beyond explanation.">
    Develop.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is stronger: the room was scary or shadows crawled across the walls?" hint="Show, don't tell.">
    Shadows crawled across the walls — it shows the fear through imagery.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a thesis?" hint="It appears in the introduction.">
    The central argument of an essay, which every paragraph supports.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
