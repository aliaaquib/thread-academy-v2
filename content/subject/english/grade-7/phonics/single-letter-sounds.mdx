Phonics is the code that unlocks reading. Every word is made of sounds, and every sound can be written with letters. Once you crack the code, you can read almost any word you meet.

This lesson focuses on Single Letter Sounds: learn the sound each letter of the alphabet makes, from a to z.

<Definition term="Single Letter Sounds">
  Learn the sound each letter of the alphabet makes, from a to z.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Sounds before letter names

Always learn the sound a letter makes, not its name. The letter s says sss, not ess. Sounds can be pushed together into words; letter names get in the way of blending.

### Blend from left to right

Point under each letter and say the sounds in order, then say them faster until they join into the word. Stretching sss-aaa-t until it becomes sat is the skill that turns decoding into real reading.

**Key term — grapheme:** The letter or letters that spell a sound. In ship, the letters sh are a single grapheme.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Single Letter Sounds">
  How many sounds are in the word fish?

  3 — the letters sh make one sound.

  **Answer:** 3 — the letters sh make one sound.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying letter names instead of sounds** Say sss, a, t — not ess, ay, tee. Letter names cannot be blended into words.
  - **Guessing from the first letter** Always read every sound in the word. Guessing from the first letter causes mistakes like reading horse as house.
</ImportantNote>
