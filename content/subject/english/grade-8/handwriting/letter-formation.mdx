## Key ideas

### Start at the top

Almost every letter starts at the top and travels down or around. Starting letters in the right place keeps them neat and makes joining them later much easier.

### Letters belong in families

Letters that share shapes are easier to learn together. Round letters like c, a, d and g all start the same way. Practising one family at a time means fewer shapes to remember.

**Key term — baseline:** The line that letters sit on. Round letters like a, c and o rest on the baseline.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Forming Letters">
  Which of these letters have ascenders: b, a, h, g?

  b and h.

  **Answer:** b and h.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Starting letters at the bottom** Nearly all letters start at the top. Bottom-up letters are slow and hard to join later.
  - **Gripping the pencil too tightly** A tight grip makes hands ache and letters shaky. Hold the pencil gently, as if holding a baby bird.
</ImportantNote>
