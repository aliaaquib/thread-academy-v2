Handwriting is how your ideas travel from your head to the page. When letters are formed clearly, anyone can read your brilliant thinking. With daily practice your hand will learn the shapes almost by itself.

This lesson focuses on Grip and Posture: hold your pencil correctly and sit comfortably so writing feels easy.

<Definition term="Grip and Posture">
  Hold your pencil correctly and sit comfortably so writing feels easy.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Spaces matter as much as letters

Leave a finger-width space between each word so your writing can be read easily. Words that run together are hard to read, even when every letter is perfect.

### Letters belong in families

Letters that share shapes are easier to learn together. Round letters like c, a, d and g all start the same way. Practising one family at a time means fewer shapes to remember.

**Key term — tripod grip:** Holding the pencil between the thumb and first two fingers. This grip gives the best control and stops hands getting tired.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Grip and Posture">
  What is a tripod grip?

  Holding the pencil with the thumb and first two fingers.

  **Answer:** Holding the pencil with the thumb and first two fingers.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Gripping the pencil too tightly** A tight grip makes hands ache and letters shaky. Hold the pencil gently, as if holding a baby bird.
  - **Writing letters in different sizes** Use the lines to guide you: tall letters reach the top line, small letters stay between the lines.
</ImportantNote>
