This lesson focuses on Finding Information: locate key facts and quotations quickly and accurately in a text.

<Definition term="Finding Information">
  Locate key facts and quotations quickly and accurately in a text.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Retrieval before inference

Answer what the text actually says before guessing what it means. Find the sentence that holds the fact, then quote it. Examiners reward answers rooted in the text, not in memory or imagination.

### Structure shapes meaning

Writers order their ideas on purpose. A text might open with a shocking image to grab attention, then zoom out to explain it. Noticing where a text begins, shifts and ends lets you comment on structure — a skill GCSE examiners specifically reward.

**Key term — inference:** A conclusion drawn from clues in the text rather than stated facts. If a character slams the door, we infer they are angry.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Finding Information">
  What is an inference?

  A conclusion drawn from clues in the text, not stated directly.

  **Answer:** A conclusion drawn from clues in the text, not stated directly.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Quoting whole sentences** Short quotations of a few words are easier to analyse. Long quotations suggest you have not chosen the most important words.
  - **Feature-spotting without explaining** Naming a simile earns little; explaining its effect on the reader earns the marks. Always ask: what does this make me feel or think?
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is better evidence: the whole sentence 'the dark, gloomy, frightening night' or the single word 'gloomy'?" hint="Shorter is usually stronger.">
    The single word 'gloomy' — short quotations let you focus your analysis.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might a writer open a text with a short, shocking sentence?" hint="Think about the reader.">
    To grab the reader's attention and create tension from the start.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What do the three letters of PEE stand for?" hint="Point...">
    Point, Evidence, Explanation.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does home connote that house does not?" hint="Think feelings.">
    Warmth, comfort and safety.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
