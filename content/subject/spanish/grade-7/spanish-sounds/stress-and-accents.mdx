## Key ideas

### Stress follows rules

Words ending in a vowel, n or s stress the second-to-last syllable (casa, hablan). Words ending in other consonants stress the last syllable (hotel, ciudad). A written accent breaks the rule and always marks the stress, as in teléfono and lápiz.

### Five vowels, always the same

Spanish vowels never change their sound: a as in 'father', e as in 'bed', i as in 'machine', o as in 'hot', u as in 'flute'. So casa is CA-sa and mesa is ME-sa. Practise them slowly and your pronunciation will instantly sound more Spanish.

**Key term — tilde:** The written accent mark, as in música. It always marks the stressed syllable and can distinguish words, like sí (yes) from si (if).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Stress and Accents">
  Why does 'teléfono' need a written accent?

  The stress falls on the third-to-last syllable (te-LÉ-fo-no), breaking the normal rule — the accent marks it.

  **Answer:** The stress falls on the third-to-last syllable (te-LÉ-fo-no), breaking the normal rule — the accent marks it.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Ignoring the accent** Papa (potato) and papá (dad) differ only in stress — the written accent changes the meaning.
  - **Pronouncing vowels like English** Never say 'cay-sa' for casa — Spanish a is always 'ah', so it is CA-sa.
</ImportantNote>
