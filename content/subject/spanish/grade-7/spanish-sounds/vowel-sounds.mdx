## Key ideas

### Five vowels, always the same

Spanish vowels never change their sound: a as in 'father', e as in 'bed', i as in 'machine', o as in 'hot', u as in 'flute'. So casa is CA-sa and mesa is ME-sa. Practise them slowly and your pronunciation will instantly sound more Spanish.

### Consonants that catch learners out

The j sounds like a strong English h (jamón = ha-MON). In most of Spain, z (and c before e or i) sounds like 'th' in 'think', while in Latin America it sounds like 's'. The double r needs a trill — flutter your tongue against the ridge behind your teeth; it comes with practice.

**Key term — vocales:** The five vowels a, e, i, o, u. Each has one pure sound that never changes, unlike English vowels which shift constantly.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Vowel Sounds">
  How many vowel sounds does Spanish have, and do they change?

  Five vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u), and each one always sounds the same.

  **Answer:** Five vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u), and each one always sounds the same.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pronouncing vowels like English** Never say 'cay-sa' for casa — Spanish a is always 'ah', so it is CA-sa.
  - **Ignoring the accent** Papa (potato) and papá (dad) differ only in stress — the written accent changes the meaning.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How is the letter j pronounced in 'jamón'?" hint="A strong throat sound, like English 'h'.">
    Like a strong 'h', so jamón sounds like 'ha-MON'.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you pronounce ñ?" hint="Think of the middle of 'canyon'.">
    Like 'ny', as in niño (NEE-nyo).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Where is the stress in 'mesa'?" hint="It ends in a vowel.">
    On the second-to-last syllable: ME-sa.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between 'pero' and 'perro'?" hint="Listen to the r.">
    'Pero' (tapped r) means 'but'; 'perro' (trilled r) means 'dog'.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
