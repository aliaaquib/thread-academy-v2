## Key ideas

### Politeness opens doors

Por favor (please), gracias (thank you), perdón (sorry / excuse me) and de nada (you're welcome) cost nothing and earn goodwill. Spanish speakers use tú with friends and usted with strangers or elders — with unfamiliar adults, usted is the safer choice.

### Greetings change with the day

Use buenos días in the morning, buenas tardes in the afternoon, and buenas noches in the evening — note that noches covers both 'evening' and 'night' as a greeting. ¿Qué tal? (How's it going?) and ¿Cómo estás? (How are you?) keep conversations friendly.

**Key term — gracias:** Thank you. Add 'muchas gracias' (thank you very much) for extra warmth; 'de nada' means 'you're welcome'.

<WorkedExample title="Ana meets Pablo">
  Write a short dialogue in which Ana (10) meets Pablo and introduces herself.

  Greet: ¡Hola!
  Ask how he is: ¿Cómo estás?
  Give her name: Me llamo Ana.
  Give her age: Tengo diez años.
  Close politely: ¡Encantada de conocerte! (Pleased to meet you!) — 'encantada' with an 'a' because Ana is female.

  **Answer:** ¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás? Me llamo Ana. Tengo diez años. ¡Encantada de conocerte!
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting 'por favor'** Requests without por favor sound rude — always add it.
  - **Saying 'yo soy diez años'** Age uses tener, not ser: Tengo diez años (I have ten years).
</ImportantNote>
