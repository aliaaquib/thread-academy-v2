Advanced Spanish means expressing nuance: doubt, hypothesis, politeness and complex argument. This chapter tackles the subjunctive, the conditional, impersonal structures and the connectors that make your Spanish flow.

This lesson focuses on The Subjunctive Mood: express wishes, doubts and emotions.

<Definition term="The Subjunctive Mood">
  Express wishes, doubts and emotions.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### The conditional and hypotheses

Form with the infinitive + -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían, learning the twelve irregular stems (tendr-, podr-, dir-). Si + imperfect subjunctive + conditional expresses unreal conditions: Si tuviera tiempo, viajaría (If I had time, I would travel).

### When the subjunctive appears

Use it after querer que, dudar que, es importante que, para que, antes de que and ojalá. Formation: take the 'yo' present form, drop the -o, add the opposite endings (hablar → hable). Two clauses with different subjects trigger it: quiero que estudies.

**Key term — el subjuntivo:** The subjunctive mood — used after expressions of wishing, doubt or emotion: quiero que vengas (I want you to come).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Subjunctive Mood">
  Which mood follows 'dudo que'?

  The subjunctive: dudo que venga.

  **Answer:** The subjunctive: dudo que venga.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Overusing the subjunctive** After creo que (I think that) use the indicative — the subjunctive follows doubt, not certainty.
  - **Using the indicative after 'quiero que'** Two subjects plus wanting = subjunctive: quiero que vengas, not 'quiero que vienes'.
</ImportantNote>
