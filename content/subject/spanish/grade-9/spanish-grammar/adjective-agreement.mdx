<Definition term="Adjective Agreement">
  Make adjectives match in gender and number.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Gender: patterns and traps

Most -o nouns are masculine and most -a nouns feminine — but el problema, el tema and el día break the rule. Learn nouns with their article: la mano (hand), el mapa (map). Adjectives must agree: un día bonito, una noche bonita.

### The three conjugations

-ar (hablar), -er (comer) and -ir (vivir) verbs share most endings; only the 'we' and 'you plural' forms differ. Learn one verb of each type and you can conjugate hundreds. Stem-changing verbs (pensar → pienso, pedir → pido) change in every form except nosotros and vosotros.

**Key term — el / la / los / las:** The definite articles — masculine/feminine, singular/plural. Every Spanish noun has a gender, so learn each noun with its article.

<WorkedExample title="Using 'tener que'">
  Translate: 'I have to do my homework today.'.

  'I have to' = tengo que.
  'Do' = hacer.
  'My homework' = mis deberes.
  'Today' = hoy.
  Assemble: Tengo que hacer mis deberes hoy.

  **Answer:** Tengo que hacer mis deberes hoy.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying 'la problema'** Problema is masculine despite the -a ending: el problema.
  - **Forgetting stem changes** Pensar becomes pienso, not 'penso' — the e → ie change hits every form except nosotros and vosotros.
</ImportantNote>
