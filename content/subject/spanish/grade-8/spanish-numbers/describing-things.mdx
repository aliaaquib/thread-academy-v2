This lesson focuses on Describing Things: put colours with nouns: el coche rojo.

<Definition term="Describing Things">
  Put colours with nouns: el coche rojo.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Colour agreement

Spanish adjectives match the noun: el coche rojo (the red car), la casa roja (the red house), los coches rojos, las casas rojas. Colours like azul and verde add only -es in the plural: las flores azules.

### Word order

Unlike English, colours usually come after the noun: un perro negro (a black dog). The natural rhythm is number + noun + colour: tres gatos blancos (three white cats).

**Key term — rojo / roja:** Red. Colours ending in -o change to -a for feminine nouns: el coche rojo, la casa roja.

<WorkedExample title="Describing 'two red apples'">
  Translate 'two red apples' into Spanish.

  'Two' = dos.
  'Apples' = manzanas (feminine plural).
  'Red' must agree: feminine plural → rojas.
  The colour follows the noun: dos manzanas rojas.

  **Answer:** Dos manzanas rojas.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Putting the colour first** Say el coche rojo, not 'el rojo coche' — colours follow the noun.
  - **Saying 'uno libro'** Uno shortens to un before masculine nouns: un libro.
</ImportantNote>
