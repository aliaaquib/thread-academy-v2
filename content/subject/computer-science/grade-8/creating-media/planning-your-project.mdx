Computers are brilliant tools for making things: posters, slideshows, animations and recordings. But great digital media does not happen by accident — it is planned, built step by step, and saved carefully. In this chapter you will plan, create and share your own media like a young designer.

This lesson focuses on Planning Your Project: sketch a simple storyboard before you start making anything.

<Definition term="Planning Your Project">
  Sketch a simple storyboard before you start making anything.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Plan before you make

A storyboard saves time and arguments: sketch each slide or scene, write what the words will say, and agree who does what. Changing a sketch takes seconds; rebuilding a finished slideshow takes ages. Professionals in film and games always storyboard first.

### Respect other people's work

Pictures and music you find online belong to someone. Only use images you made yourself, or ones your teacher says are free to use — and always say where a borrowed image came from. This habit is called giving credit, and it is the law as well as good manners.

**Key term — storyboard:** A set of small sketches showing each scene of your project in order, like a comic strip of your plan.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Planning Your Project">
  What is a storyboard, and why do film-makers use one?

  A storyboard is a set of sketches showing each scene in order; it lets film-makers plan and fix problems on paper before expensive filming starts.

  **Answer:** A storyboard is a set of sketches showing each scene in order; it lets film-makers plan and fix problems on paper before expensive filming starts.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Starting on the computer with no plan** Correction: spend five minutes on a storyboard first — it makes the building stage twice as fast.
  - **Using a picture from the internet with no credit** Correction: use your own drawings or teacher-approved images, and write down where any borrowed image came from.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is the better file name: 'untitled2' or 'dolphin-poster-final'? Why?" hint="Imagine finding it again in a month.">
    'dolphin-poster-final' — it describes the file's contents, so you can find it without opening it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You want music for your slideshow. What is the safe choice?" hint="Remember who owns music you find online.">
    Use music your teacher says is free to use, or record your own — and always credit the creator.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does an animation make still pictures look as if they move?" hint="Think about flipping the corner of a notebook.">
    It shows many slightly different pictures one after another very quickly, and your eyes blend them into smooth movement.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your slideshow file will not open and you last saved it an hour ago. What habit would have saved your work?" hint="Think about the save habit.">
    Saving often — every few minutes — so only a few minutes of work could ever be lost.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
