A **network** is two or more devices connected so they can share data and resources — files, printers, internet connections. Networks are classified mainly by how much ground they cover.

<Definition term="Network">
  Two or more computing devices connected together to share data, software and hardware resources.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Three sizes: PAN, LAN, WAN

- **PAN (Personal Area Network)** — the smallest: your phone talking to your watch or earbuds over Bluetooth, within a few metres.
- **LAN (Local Area Network)** — one site: a school, office or home. The organisation owns the equipment; devices share printers, files and one internet connection.
- **WAN (Wide Area Network)** — large distances: cities, countries. Usually several LANs joined together. The **internet** is the biggest WAN — a worldwide network of networks.

### Wired vs wireless

Networks connect devices by cable or by radio:

- **Wired** (Ethernet cables, fibre optic) — faster, more reliable, harder to intercept; but cables cost and you cannot roam.
- **Wireless** (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth) — convenient, mobile; but slower at distance, easier to intercept, and walls get in the way.

Most real networks mix both: cables between the important machines, Wi-Fi for everything that moves.

### The hardware that makes it work

- **Router** — directs data between networks (e.g. your home LAN and your internet provider's network); the box your Wi-Fi usually comes from.
- **Switch** — connects devices *within* a LAN and sends data only to the intended device.
- **Wireless access point** — broadcasts the Wi-Fi signal devices join.

<WorkedExample title="Classify the networks">
  Three setups: (a) wireless earbuds paired to a phone, (b) 40 computers in a school sharing one printer, (c) a company linking its Bishkek and Osh offices.

  (a) A few metres, personal devices → PAN (Bluetooth). (b) One site, organisation-owned, shared resources → LAN. (c) Two cities, LANs joined over distance → WAN.

  **Answer:** (a) PAN, (b) LAN, (c) WAN — classified by geographic area, exactly how the book defines them.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **"The internet is a LAN"** Correction: the internet spans the planet — it is the largest WAN, made of countless joined networks.
  - **Wi-Fi = internet** Correction: Wi-Fi is just the wireless link to your router; the internet is the global WAN beyond it.
  - **Router vs switch** Correction: a switch connects devices within one LAN; a router connects networks to each other.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example each of a PAN, a LAN and a WAN.">
    PAN: phone + smartwatch over Bluetooth. LAN: a school's computers and printers. WAN: the internet.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why would a school choose wired connections for its main computers but Wi-Fi for tablets?">
    Wired is faster and more reliable for fixed machines; tablets move around, so they need wireless convenience.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a router do that a switch does not?">
    A router directs data between different networks (e.g. home LAN to the internet); a switch only connects devices within one LAN.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is Bluetooth a PAN or LAN technology? Explain.">
    PAN — it connects personal devices over a few metres, which is the PAN's defining scale.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Network: connected devices sharing data and resources; classified by area into PAN, LAN and WAN.
  - PAN: personal, metres (Bluetooth). LAN: one site, organisation-owned. WAN: large distances; the internet is the largest.
  - Wired is fast and reliable; wireless is convenient and mobile — real networks mix both.
  - Router connects networks together; switch connects devices within a LAN.
</Summary>
