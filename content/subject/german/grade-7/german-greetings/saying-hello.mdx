Every conversation starts with a greeting, and German gives you wonderful ones to choose from. In this chapter you will learn to say hello and goodbye, ask how someone is, and introduce yourself with confidence.

This lesson focuses on Saying Hello: learn Guten Morgen, Guten Tag, Guten Abend and Hallo and when to use each one.

<Definition term="Saying Hello">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Time-of-day greetings

Match your greeting to the time: Guten Morgen (morning), Guten Tag (daytime), Guten Abend (evening). Gute Nacht is only said when someone is going to sleep, not as an evening hello. Hallo works any time and is friendly and informal.

### Du and Sie

German has two words for you: du for friends, family and children, and Sie for adults you do not know well and in formal situations. Use Wie geht es dir? with friends and Wie geht es Ihnen? formally. Getting this right is one of the most important social skills in German.

**Key term — Guten Morgen / Guten Tag / Guten Abend:** The time-of-day greetings: good morning, good day (hello) and good evening. Hallo is the all-purpose informal hello.

<WorkedExample title="Anna introduces herself">
  Anna is 11 and from London. Write her four-sentence self-introduction: a hello, her name, her age, where she is from, then a goodbye.

  Step 1: Start with Hallo! for a friendly hello.
  Step 2: Say her name with Ich heiße Anna.
  Step 3: Give her age with Ich bin elf Jahre alt — use bin, never habe, for age.
  Step 4: Say where she is from with Ich komme aus London, then finish with Tschüss!

  **Answer:** Hallo! Ich heiße Anna. Ich bin elf Jahre alt. Ich komme aus London. Tschüss!
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying Gute Nacht as an evening greeting** Gute Nacht means good night when going to sleep. In the evening arriving somewhere, say Guten Abend.
  - **Saying Ich habe 11 Jahre** Age uses sein, not haben: say Ich bin elf Jahre alt (I am eleven years old).
</ImportantNote>
