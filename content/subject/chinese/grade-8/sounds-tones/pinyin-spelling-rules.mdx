## Key ideas

### Pinyin spelling rules

The dots on ü disappear after j, q, x and y: write 去 qù, never qǜ. The finals iou, uei and uen shorten to iu, ui and un: 六 liù, 对 duì, 们 men. Use an apostrophe when a syllable starting with a, o or e follows another syllable: 西安 xī'ān is not the same word as 先 xiān.

### Third-tone sandhi

When two third tones meet, the first becomes a second tone: 你 (nǐ) plus 好 (hǎo) is pronounced ní hǎo. In a longer string, every third tone except the last one changes: 我很好 (wǒ hěn hǎo) is said wó hén hǎo. The tone marks never change on paper — this is a pronunciation rule only.

**Key term — 变调 (biàndiào):** Tone sandhi: a tone that changes because of its neighbours. The written tone mark stays the same; only the pronunciation shifts.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Pinyin Spelling Rules">
  Write to go (去) in pinyin.

  qù.

  **Answer:** qù.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Writing qǜ with the umlaut dots** After j, q, x and y the dots vanish: 去 is qù and 学 is xué. Only n and l keep them: 女 nǚ, 绿 lǜ.
  - **Writing ní hǎo with a second-tone mark** Tone marks record the original tone: always write 你好 as nǐ hǎo, even though you say ní hǎo.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does 西安 need an apostrophe?" hint="Without it you would read xiān.">
    xī'ān shows two syllables, xī plus ān; without the apostrophe, xian would be read as the single syllable xiān.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you pronounce 你好, and how do you write it?" hint="Marks stay, sound changes.">
    Written nǐ hǎo, pronounced ní hǎo.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give the spoken form of 一 in 一个 and 一天." hint="Before a 4th tone it becomes 2nd; before a 1st tone it becomes 4th.">
    一个 yí gè; 一天 yìtiān.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you pronounce 不是?" hint="不 before a fourth tone.">
    búshì.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
