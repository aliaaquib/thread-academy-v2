## Key ideas

### Reading 静夜思

李白 (Lǐ Bái, 701–762), the great Tang poet, wrote: 床前明月光，疑是地上霜。举头望明月，低头思故乡。(Chuáng qián míng yuè guāng, yí shì dì shang shuāng. Jǔ tóu wàng míng yuè, dī tóu sī gù xiāng.) In four short lines the poet moves from moonlight mistaken for frost to looking up at the moon and bowing his head in longing for home — the whole poem turns on the contrast of 举头 (raising the head) and 低头 (lowering it).

### Modern fiction: Lu Xun

鲁迅 (Lǔ Xùn, 1881–1936) is regarded as the founder of modern Chinese literature. His 1918 story 《狂人日记》 (Kuángrén Rìjì, Diary of a Madman), written in the vernacular rather than classical Chinese, attacked old traditions and opened the door for the modern short story.

**Key term — 诗 (shī):** A poem. Classical poems such as Tang verse follow strict line lengths and rhythms, quite unlike free modern verse.

<WorkedExample title="Unpack a line of Li Bai">
  Explain the imagery in 床前明月光，疑是地上霜.

  Translate literally: Before my bed, bright moonlight; I wonder if it is frost on the ground.
  Spot the 比喻: moonlight is compared to frost — both pale, cold and spread thinly.
  Notice 疑是 (I suspect it is): the poet is half-asleep, unsure what he sees, which makes the image dreamlike and sets up the homesickness that follows.

  **Answer:** The line compares moonlight pooling by the bed to frost on the ground (a 比喻 simile), and the hesitant 疑是 gives the scene a drowsy, uncertain mood. In two images Li Bai fixes the quiet loneliness of a night far from home — the theme the poem's title promises.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Ignoring the form** Classical poems balance their lines — note the parallel pair 举头 and 低头. Comment on structure, not just vocabulary.
  - **Translating a poem word for word** A literal gloss is the first step, not the answer: reorganise into natural English that keeps the image and mood.
</ImportantNote>
