## Key ideas

### Family and etiquette

Respect for elders (尊敬长辈 zūnjìng zhǎngbèi) shapes daily life: greet the eldest first, pour their tea first, and accept things with both hands. Never stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice — it resembles incense offered to the dead. Direct refusal can cost face, so a vague I will think about it often means no.

### Festivals through the year

春节 (Chūnjié) brings the 年夜饭 (niányèfàn, reunion dinner), 春联 (chūnlián, spring couplets) pasted on doors, and 红包 for children. 中秋节 (Zhōngqiūjié, Mid-Autumn Festival) celebrates 团圆 (tuányuán, family reunion) with 月饼 (yuèbǐng, mooncakes) under the full moon. 端午节 (Duānwǔjié, Dragon Boat Festival) honours the poet 屈原 (Qū Yuán) with dragon-boat races and 粽子 (zòngzi, sticky-rice dumplings).

**Key term — 面子 (miànzi):** Face: dignity and social standing. Giving face (给面子 gěi miànzi) means showing respect; losing it means public embarrassment.

<WorkedExample title="Describe three Spring Festival customs">
  In Chinese, explain three things people do at Spring Festival.

  Name the festival and setting: 过年时 (guònián shí, at New Year time).
  List three customs with verbs: 吃年夜饭 (eat the reunion dinner), 发红包 (give red envelopes), 贴春联 (paste spring couplets).
  Join them in one flowing sentence.

  **Answer:** 过年时，人们吃年夜饭，长辈给孩子发红包，家家户户贴春联。(Guònián shí, rénmen chī niányèfàn, zhǎngbèi gěi háizi fā hóngbāo, jiājiāhùhù tiē chūnlián.) — At New Year, people eat the reunion dinner, elders give children red envelopes, and every household pastes up spring couplets.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Wearing white to a celebration** White is the colour of mourning in China; red means luck and joy. Choose red envelopes, not white ones, for festive gifts.
  - **Calling every festival Chinese New Year** Only 春节 is New Year. 中秋节 is the Mid-Autumn Festival and 端午节 is the Dragon Boat Festival — each has its own food and story.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="What does 没面子 mean?" hint="面子 means face.">
    To have lost face — to be embarrassed or shamed publicly.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three Chinese festivals and one food for each." hint="春节， 中秋节， 端午节.">
    春节 — dumplings and reunion-dinner dishes; 中秋节 — 月饼 (yuèbǐng) mooncakes; 端午节 — 粽子 (zòngzi) sticky-rice dumplings.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a 红包, and when is it given?" hint="Red envelope.">
    A red envelope containing money, given at Spring Festival and weddings for luck.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you not stick chopsticks upright in rice?" hint="It resembles something offered to the dead.">
    It looks like incense sticks offered to the dead — deeply unlucky at the table.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
