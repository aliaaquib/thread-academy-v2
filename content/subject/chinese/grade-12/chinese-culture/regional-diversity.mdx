## Key ideas

### Regions and modern life

China is not one culture: the south eats rice while the north eats wheat noodles and dumplings (南米北面), Guangdong and Hong Kong speak Cantonese, and Sichuan is famous for fiery food. Modern cities run on 高铁 (gāotiě, high-speed rail) and 移动支付 (yídòng zhīfù, mobile payment) — cash is now rare among young people. Tradition and technology sit side by side: grandparents video-call into the family reunion dinner.

### Festivals through the year

春节 (Chūnjié) brings the 年夜饭 (niányèfàn, reunion dinner), 春联 (chūnlián, spring couplets) pasted on doors, and 红包 for children. 中秋节 (Zhōngqiūjié, Mid-Autumn Festival) celebrates 团圆 (tuányuán, family reunion) with 月饼 (yuèbǐng, mooncakes) under the full moon. 端午节 (Duānwǔjié, Dragon Boat Festival) honours the poet 屈原 (Qū Yuán) with dragon-boat races and 粽子 (zòngzi, sticky-rice dumplings).

**Key term — 方言 (fāngyán):** A regional variety of speech, such as Cantonese (粤语 Yuèyǔ) or Shanghainese (上海话 Shànghǎihuà).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Regional Diversity">
  Explain 南米北面 in English.

  South rice, north noodles: the south traditionally eats rice while the north eats wheat-based noodles and dumplings.

  **Answer:** South rice, north noodles: the south traditionally eats rice while the north eats wheat-based noodles and dumplings.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming everyone speaks Mandarin** Mandarin (普通话 pǔtōnghuà) is the national standard, but millions speak Cantonese, Shanghainese and other 方言 as their mother tongue.
  - **Calling every festival Chinese New Year** Only 春节 is New Year. 中秋节 is the Mid-Autumn Festival and 端午节 is the Dragon Boat Festival — each has its own food and story.
</ImportantNote>

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a 红包, and when is it given?" hint="Red envelope.">

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you not stick chopsticks upright in rice?" hint="It resembles something offered to the dead.">
    It looks like incense sticks offered to the dead — deeply unlucky at the table.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say high-speed rail in Chinese?" hint="高铁.">
    高铁 (gāotiě).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
