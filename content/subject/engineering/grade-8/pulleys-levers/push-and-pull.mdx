Long before engines existed, people moved huge stones and lifted heavy loads using nothing but cleverly shaped sticks, ropes and wheels. These simple machines do not add energy, but they change how force is applied so a small push can do a big job. In this chapter you will experiment with levers and pulleys and find out how they make work easier.

This lesson focuses on Push and Pull: a lever is a stiff bar that turns on a pivot to lift heavy loads.

<Definition term="Push and Pull">
  A lever is a stiff bar that turns on a pivot to lift heavy loads.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Where you push matters

On a lever, the farther your push is from the fulcrum, the easier the lift becomes. That is why a long spanner loosens a stiff nut more easily than a short one, and why a door opens most easily when you push the edge farthest from the hinges.

### A lever trades distance for force

Push down on the long end of a seesaw and the short end rises with a much bigger force. Your hand travels a long way while the load moves only a little, so the machine multiplies your force by making you move farther. Nothing is free: the total work stays the same.

**Key term — Lever:** A stiff bar that turns around a fixed point to lift or move a load.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Push and Pull">
  A wheelbarrow is a lever. Where is its fulcrum?

  The wheel axle: the barrow pivots around the wheel when you lift the handles.

  **Answer:** The wheel axle: the barrow pivots around the wheel when you lift the handles.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pushing close to the fulcrum and wondering why it is hard** Effort near the pivot has little turning effect. Move your push farther from the fulcrum to make it easier.
  - **Thinking a pulley makes the load weigh less** A single fixed pulley changes the direction of the pull but the load weighs exactly the same. Only extra pulleys sharing the rope reduce the effort.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a single fixed pulley change about lifting a bucket from a well?" hint="Does the bucket get lighter?">
    Only the direction: you pull down instead of lifting up. The effort needed stays the same.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is it easier to open a door by pushing the handle than by pushing near the hinges?" hint="Which push is farther from the pivot?">
    The handle is farther from the hinges, so the same push has a bigger turning effect.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A crowbar lifts a 600 N rock with an effort of 150 N. How many times has the force been multiplied?" hint="Divide the load by the effort.">
    4 times, because 600 divided by 150 = 4.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 400 N load sits 0.5 m left of a fulcrum. What weight placed 1 m to the right balances it?" hint="Match weight times distance on both sides.">
    200 N, because 400 times 0.5 = 200, and 200 times 1 = 200.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
