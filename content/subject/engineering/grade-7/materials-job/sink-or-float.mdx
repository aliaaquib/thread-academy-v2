<Definition term="Sink or Float">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Properties describe materials

Every material has properties: how hard it is, whether it bends, stretches, soaks up water or lets light through. Engineers describe these properties carefully so they can compare materials fairly. A fair test changes only one thing at a time, such as dropping the same ball onto different surfaces to compare bounciness.

### Match the material to the job

An umbrella needs to be waterproof and light, so coated fabric beats steel. A frying pan needs to survive heat, so metal beats plastic. Engineers list what the object must do first, then choose the material whose properties fit best.

**Key term — Property:** A quality of a material that can be observed or measured, such as hardness, flexibility or waterproofing.

<WorkedExample title="Choosing a material for a lunchbox">
  Pick the best material for a lunchbox from wood, cardboard, glass and plastic. The box must keep food dry, survive being dropped, and be light to carry.

  List the needs: waterproof, tough enough to survive drops, and light.
  Test each material: drip water on all four, drop each from table height, and lift each.
  Score them: wood is tough but heavy and not naturally waterproof; cardboard is light but soaks and tears; glass is waterproof but heavy and shatters; plastic is waterproof, tough and light.
  Choose the winner and explain why using your test results.

  **Answer:** Plastic wins because it is the only material that is waterproof, tough enough to survive a drop, and light to carry. Wood and cardboard fail the waterproof test, and glass fails the drop test.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Testing materials unfairly** If you drop one sample from higher up, the test is unfair. Change only one thing at a time so the comparison is honest.
  - **Choosing a material because it looks nice** Looks do not keep rain out or survive a drop. Test the properties the job actually needs first.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which property does a raincoat need most?" hint="What must a raincoat stop?">
    It must be waterproof so rain cannot soak through.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are windows made of glass rather than wood?" hint="What do we want windows to do with light?">
    Glass is transparent, so daylight passes through, while wood blocks it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is glass a poor choice for a football?" hint="Think about what happens when a ball is kicked and headed.">
    Glass is not tough: it would shatter on the first hard kick, and it is heavy and dangerous.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A wooden spoon and a metal spoon are left in a hot pan. Which handle stays cooler, and why?" hint="Which material lets heat travel through it faster?">
    The wooden handle, because wood conducts heat much more slowly than metal.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
