## Key ideas

### Reading film as text

Sergei Eisenstein's montage — монтаж (montázh) — cuts images together to create ideas neither shot contains alone; see the Odessa Steps sequence in Battleship Potemkin. Andrei Tarkovsky does the opposite: long, slow takes that make time itself visible. Both directors ask the same question as the novelists: how does art show truth?

### The Onegin stanza

Eugene Onegin is written in a unique 14-line stanza rhyming ABABCCDD EFFEGG, which Pushkin uses for everything from landscape to irony to direct addresses to the reader. The stanza's conversational ease lets Pushkin slip between narrator and character — a technique to name in essays as authorial intrusion.

**Key term — роман (román) — novel:** The long prose form. The great Russian романы — Война и мир (Voyná i mir — War and Peace), Преступление и наказание (Prestupléniye i nakazániye — Crime and Punishment) — are as much philosophy as story.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Russian Film Classics">
  Contrast Eisenstein and Tarkovsky in one sentence each.

  Eisenstein builds meaning through rapid montage (montázh) collisions of images; Tarkovsky reveals truth through long, contemplative takes.

  **Answer:** Eisenstein builds meaning through rapid montage (montázh) collisions of images; Tarkovsky reveals truth through long, contemplative takes.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating film as illustration of the book** Eisenstein's montage (montázh) makes its own arguments — analyse the editing itself rather than using the film as a picture-book of the novel.
  - **Summarising Chekhov's plots instead of analysing** Examiners want the подтекст (podtékst — subtext): what is not said. Writing only what happens in 'The Lady with the Dog' earns description marks, not analysis marks.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is подтекст in Chekhov?" hint="It is what the characters do not say.">
    Подтекст (podtékst) — subtext: the real meaning carried by pauses, trivial remarks and omissions.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two great Russian романы and their authors." hint="Think Tolstoy and Dostoevsky.">
    Война и мир (Voyná i mir — War and Peace) by Tolstoy (Толстой (Tolstóy)); Преступление и наказание (Prestupléniye i nakazániye — Crime and Punishment) by Dostoevsky (Достоевский (Dostoyévskiy)).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the rhyme scheme of the Onegin stanza?" hint="14 lines: ABABCCDD…">
    ABABCCDD EFFEGG.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Translate лишний человек and explain the term." hint="It describes Onegin and his literary descendants.">
    Лишний человек (líshniy chelovék) — 'the superfluous man': a clever, bored aristocrat unable to act meaningfully.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
