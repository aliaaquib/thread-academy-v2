This lesson focuses on Magic Polite Words: say спасибо (spasíbo — thank you), пожалуйста (pozháluysta — please) and извините (izviníte — excuse me) to sound kind and polite.

<Definition term="Magic Polite Words">
  Say спасибо (spasíbo — thank you), пожалуйста (pozháluysta — please) and извините (izviníte — excuse me) to sound kind and polite.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Asking 'how are you?'

After hello, Russians often ask Как дела? (kak delá — how are things?). You can answer Хорошо, спасибо! (khoroshó, spasíbo — good, thank you!), Нормально (normál'no — okay) or Отлично! (otlíchnо — excellent!). Adding А у тебя? (a u tebyá — and how about you?) keeps the conversation going.

### Formal and informal greetings

Russian has two levels of politeness. Use здравствуйте (zdrávstvuyte — hello) and до свидания (do svidániya — goodbye) with adults and strangers, and привет (privét — hi) and пока (paká — bye) with friends. Choosing the right one shows respect, which matters a great deal in Russian culture.

**Key term — спасибо (spasíbo) — thank you:** The word for 'thank you', used just as often as in English. Pair it with пожалуйста (pozháluysta — please / you're welcome) to sound extra polite.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Magic Polite Words">
  Translate 'thank you' into Russian.

  Спасибо (spasíbo).

  **Answer:** Спасибо (spasíbo).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting to answer Как дела?** When someone asks Как дела? (kak delá — how are you?), always answer — Хорошо, спасибо! (khoroshó, spasíbo — good, thank you!) — and ask back with А у тебя? (a u tebyá — and you?).
  - **Saying привет to a teacher** Привет (privét — hi) is only for friends. To a teacher or a stranger, say Здравствуйте (zdrávstvuyte — hello).
</ImportantNote>
