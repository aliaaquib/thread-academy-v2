Bonjour opens every door in France — politeness is taken seriously. This chapter covers greetings for every moment, confident introductions, and the essential tu/vous distinction.

This lesson focuses on Saying Hello: learn bonjour, salut and time-based greetings.

<Definition term="Saying Hello">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Bonjour and beyond

Bonjour works until evening, when bonsoir takes over — bonsoir covers both 'good evening' and 'good night' as a greeting. Salut is for peers; bonjour is the safe default with adults. Add Comment ça va? (How's it going?) to sound natural.

### Introducing yourself

Je m'appelle… gives your name, J'ai… ans gives your age — note French uses 'I have' for age: J'ai dix ans (I am ten). J'habite à… says where you live: J'habite à Londres. Enchanté(e) means 'pleased to meet you' — add an extra e if you are female: enchantée.

**Key term — bonjour:** Hello / good morning — used all day until evening. Always say it when entering a shop in France.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Saying Hello">
  When do you switch from 'bonjour' to 'bonsoir'?

  In the evening — bonsoir is both 'good evening' and 'good night' as a greeting.

  **Answer:** In the evening — bonsoir is both 'good evening' and 'good night' as a greeting.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using 'salut' with a teacher** Salut is informal — say Bonjour, madame / monsieur to adults.
  - **Saying 'je suis neuf ans'** Age uses avoir: J'ai neuf ans (I have nine years).
</ImportantNote>
