## Key ideas

### Silent letters and liaisons

Final consonants are usually silent: petit, beaucoup, Paris (the s is silent!). But c, r, f and l are often pronounced — remember 'CaReFuL'. In liaisons, a silent consonant wakes up before a vowel: deux‿enfants.

### The four nasal vowels

On (bon), an/en (vent), in (matin) and un (brun) are single sounds made through the nose — do not pronounce the n! In southern France un and in have merged, but standard French keeps all four distinct.

**Key term — voyelle nasale:** Nasal vowel — air flows through the nose: on (as in bon), an/en (enfant), in (vin). Never pronounce the n itself.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Silent Letters">
  Which letters are often pronounced at the end of French words?

  C, R, F and L — as in sac, fleur, neuf, bal.

  **Answer:** C, R, F and L — as in sac, fleur, neuf, bal.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pronouncing the final s in 'Paris'** It is 'pa-REE' — final consonants are usually silent in French.
  - **Saying nasal vowels as vowel + n** Bon is one nasal sound, not 'bone' — keep the air in your nose and skip the n.
</ImportantNote>
