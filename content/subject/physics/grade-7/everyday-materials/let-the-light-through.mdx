</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Some materials let water or light through, others block them

A sponge soaks up water while a plastic bag keeps it out — one is absorbent and the other is waterproof. Clear glass is transparent, frosted glass is translucent, and wood is opaque. These blocking and letting-through properties decide how we use each material.

### We choose materials to suit the job

Wellies are made of rubber because rubber is waterproof and bendy. Windows are made of glass because it is transparent and hard. Matching the property to the purpose is the key idea behind materials science, and engineers do it for everything from bridges to lunchboxes.

**Key term — transparent:** Lets nearly all light through so you can see clearly through it, like clean glass.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Let the Light Through">
  Name one transparent material and one opaque material.

  Transparent: glass or clear plastic. Opaque: wood, metal or brick.

  **Answer:** Transparent: glass or clear plastic. Opaque: wood, metal or brick.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking glass is always transparent** Frosted or stained glass is translucent — it lets some light through but you cannot see clearly through it.
  - **Assuming shiny means waterproof** Shininess is about light bouncing off the surface; a shiny sponge still soaks up water.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="Sort these into natural and man-made materials: wood, plastic, cotton, glass." hint="Natural materials come from plants, animals or rocks.">

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is a wooden spoon hard or soft? How could you test it?" hint="Try scratching or bending it.">
    Hard — you cannot dent it with a fingernail or bend it easily.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are umbrellas not made of paper?" hint="What happens to paper in rain?">
    Paper is not waterproof — it soaks up water and tears, so it would fail as an umbrella.
