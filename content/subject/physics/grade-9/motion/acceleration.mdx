This lesson focuses on What Is Acceleration: working out how quickly velocity changes using a = (v − u) ÷ t.

<Definition term="What Is Acceleration">
  Working out how quickly velocity changes using a = (v − u) ÷ t.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Graphs turn journeys into pictures

On a distance–time graph, the gradient (slope) equals the speed — steeper means faster, flat means stationary. On a velocity–time graph the gradient gives acceleration, and the area under the line gives the distance travelled. One graph can replace a whole table of data.

### Acceleration measures changing velocity

Acceleration = (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time. A car going from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 s accelerates at 4 m/s². Because velocity includes direction, turning a corner at constant speed still counts as acceleration.

**Key term — acceleration:** The rate of change of velocity, in m/s². Positive means speeding up, negative means slowing down.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Is Acceleration">
  A van accelerates from 10 m/s to 22 m/s in 6 s. Find the acceleration.

  (22 − 10) ÷ 6 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 m/s².

  **Answer:** (22 − 10) ÷ 6 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 m/s².
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Reading the gradient of a velocity–time graph as speed** The gradient of a velocity–time graph is acceleration; speed is read straight off the vertical axis.
  - **Mixing up distance and displacement** Distance is the total path travelled; displacement is the straight line from start to end — they match only on a straight one-way trip.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How is distance found from a velocity–time graph?" hint="Think of the shape under the line.">
    It equals the area under the graph, between the line and the time axis.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A train rounds a bend at a constant 40 m/s. Is it accelerating? Explain." hint="Velocity includes direction.">
    Yes — its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing, which means it is accelerating even though its speed is constant.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A runner covers 100 m in 12.5 s. Calculate the average speed." hint="Divide distance by time.">
    100 ÷ 12.5 = 8 m/s.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A car travels at a steady 25 m/s for 40 s. How far does it go?" hint="Rearrange: distance = speed × time.">
    25 × 40 = 1000 m.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
