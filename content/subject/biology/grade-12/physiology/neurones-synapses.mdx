## Key ideas

### Neurones and synapses

A neurone carries an electrical impulse along its axon, insulated by a myelin sheath that speeds transmission. At the synapse, the impulse triggers release of neurotransmitter, which diffuses across the gap and binds to receptors on the next neurone, starting a new impulse. Because transmission is one-way and chemical, synapses introduce a tiny delay but allow impulses to be directed and integrated.

### The reflex arc

A spinal reflex has five stages: receptor detects the stimulus, sensory neurone carries the impulse to the spinal cord, a relay neurone passes it across, a motor neurone carries the response out, and the effector (muscle) responds. Bypassing the brain makes the response faster, which protects the body — you snatch your hand from a hot surface before you consciously feel pain.

**Key term — synapse:** The junction between two neurones, where the impulse is transmitted by diffusion of neurotransmitter across a tiny gap.

  **Answer:** The arriving impulse triggers release of neurotransmitter into the synaptic gap; it diffuses across and binds to receptors on the next neurone, triggering a new impulse there.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying impulses jump the synapse electrically** The impulse travels electrically along the axon but crosses the synapse chemically, by diffusion of neurotransmitter. The gap cannot be jumped electrically.
  - **Claiming reflexes involve the brain thinking** Spinal reflexes are coordinated by relay neurones in the spinal cord; the brain is informed afterwards, which is why you feel the pain after you have already moved.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Compare nervous and hormonal control on speed and duration." hint="Wires versus blood.">
    Nervous responses are very fast and short-lived, travelling as electrical impulses along neurones to specific targets. Hormonal responses are slower and longer-lasting, travelling in the blood to widespread targets.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why a reflex action is faster than a conscious response." hint="What does the impulse bypass?">
    The impulse travels only to the spinal cord via short relay neurones and does not travel up to the brain for conscious processing, so the pathway is shorter and the response quicker.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="State the five stages of a spinal reflex arc in order." hint="Start with the receptor.">
    Receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (in the spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector (muscle).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain how glucagon raises blood glucose." hint="What does the liver do with glycogen?">
    When blood glucose falls, the pancreas releases glucagon, which stimulates the liver to convert stored glycogen back into glucose and release it into the blood.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
