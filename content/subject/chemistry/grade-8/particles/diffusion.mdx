## Key ideas

### Particles are always moving

In the particle model, every substance is made of tiny particles with empty space between them, and the particles are in constant motion. The hotter the substance, the faster its particles move — heating does not make new particles, it makes the existing ones move more. This one idea explains expansion, diffusion and changes of state.

### Arrangement decides the state

In a solid, particles are packed tightly in fixed positions and only vibrate, which is why solids keep their shape. In a liquid, particles are still close but can slide past each other, so liquids flow. In a gas, particles are far apart and move quickly in all directions, so gases spread out to fill any container.

**Key term — diffusion:** The spreading of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Spreading by Diffusion">
  Why does diffusion happen faster in hot water than in cold water?

  Particles move faster when hot, so they spread and mix more quickly.

  **Answer:** Particles move faster when hot, so they spread and mix more quickly.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying particles melt** Particles do not melt, expand or shrink — the substance does. Heating makes particles move faster and spread out; the particles themselves stay the same size.
  - **Confusing melting with dissolving** Melting is a solid becoming a liquid when heated, like ice to water. Dissolving is a solid spreading through a liquid, like salt in water — the solid is still there, just invisible.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why gases spread out to fill their container." hint="How much space is between gas particles?">
    Gas particles are far apart and move rapidly in all directions, so they spread until they fill the whole container.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Describe the arrangement and movement of particles in a solid." hint="Think about fixed positions.">
    Particles are packed tightly in fixed positions and vibrate; they cannot move around, which is why solids keep their shape.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A puddle dries up on a warm day. Explain what happens to the water particles." hint="Where do the fastest particles go?">
    The fastest water particles escape from the surface into the air as a gas; this is evaporation.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A thermometer in melting ice reads 0 °C the whole time. Explain why the temperature does not rise." hint="Where is the heat energy going?">
    The energy goes into loosening the particles from their fixed positions rather than raising their speed, so the temperature stays constant until all the ice has melted.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
