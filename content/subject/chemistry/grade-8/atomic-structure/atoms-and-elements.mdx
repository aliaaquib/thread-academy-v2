## The smallest building blocks

An **atom** is the smallest particle of an **element** that still has that element's properties. Atoms are unimaginably small — about ten million would span a millimetre — but they have internal structure.

<Definition term="atom">
  An **atom** consists of a central **nucleus** (protons and neutrons) with **electrons**
  arranged in shells around it. Atoms are electrically neutral overall.
</Definition>

## The three particles

| Particle | Charge | Mass | Location |
|----------|--------|------|----------|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | ~0 | Shells |

Protons and neutrons have nearly all the mass; electrons are ~1/2000th of a proton's mass — negligible for mass calculations, but they control **chemistry**: it's electrons that form bonds.

<ImportantNote title="The numbers that matter">
  - **Atomic (proton) number** = number of protons. This *defines* the element: 6 protons is always carbon.
  - **Mass number** = protons + neutrons. Neutrons can vary — same element, different mass.
  - In a neutral atom, **electrons = protons**.
</ImportantNote>

<WorkedExample title="Worked example">
  An atom has 8 protons and 10 neutrons. Identify it and count its electrons.

  8 protons → atomic number 8 → **oxygen**. Mass number = 8 + 10 = **18**.
  Neutral atom → **8 electrons**.
</WorkedExample>

## Isotopes

Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are **isotopes**: carbon-12 (6 neutrons) and carbon-14 (8 neutrons) are both carbon — same protons, same chemistry, different mass. Some isotopes are unstable and radioactive.

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="An atom has 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 11 electrons. Give its atomic number, mass number and element." hint="Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.">
    Atomic number 11 → sodium. Mass number = 11 + 12 = 23.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why atoms have no overall charge." hint="Compare positive and negative particles.">
    The number of positive protons equals the number of negative electrons, so the charges cancel.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
