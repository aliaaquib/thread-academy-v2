</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Properties you can observe

You can find out a lot about a material using your senses: sight, touch and sometimes sound. Is it shiny or dull, smooth or rough, bendy or stiff, heavy or light? Scientists record these observations carefully before they sort anything, because good sorting starts with careful looking. Try describing five objects in the room and compare your words with a friend.

### Sorting by one property

When you sort materials, pick just one property at a time, for example magnetic or not magnetic. A fridge magnet sticks to steel but not to plastic, wood or glass, so steel goes in one group and the others go in another. Using one rule keeps the groups fair and makes the pattern easy to see.

**Key term — property:** Something you can observe about a material, for example whether it is hard, shiny, rough or stretchy.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Grouping Materials">
  You sort buttons into shiny and dull. Why is this a fair sorting rule?

  It is fair because it uses one clear property, and every button fits into exactly one of the two groups.

  **Answer:** It is fair because it uses one clear property, and every button fits into exactly one of the two groups.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Putting an object in two groups at once** When you sort by one rule, each object belongs in exactly one group. If an object seems to fit both, your rule may need to be clearer — for example rough or smooth, not a bit of both.
  - **Sorting by colour alone** Colour is a poor sorting rule because many different materials can be the same colour. Sort by a property that tells you something useful, like hardness or waterproofness.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A raincoat is made from a special fabric. Which property makes it good for a raincoat?" hint="What must a raincoat stop?">
    It is waterproof, so rain cannot soak through it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Suggest a material for a playground slide and explain your choice using two properties." hint="Think about safety and weather.">
    Plastic is a good choice because it is smooth, so children slide easily, and waterproof, so rain does not damage it. Other sensible answers with two properties are acceptable.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which material would be best for a window: wood, glass or fabric? Give a reason." hint="Think about what a window needs to do.">
    Glass, because it is transparent so light can pass through and you can see through it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two properties you could test to sort a pile of different spoons." hint="Think about what a magnet or water could tell you.">
    Any two sensible properties, for example whether they are magnetic and whether they are waterproof (metal versus wood).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
