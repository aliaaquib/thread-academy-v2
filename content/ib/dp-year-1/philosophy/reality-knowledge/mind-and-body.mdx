## Mind and Body

Descartes thought mind and body were different substances. Modern neuroscience finds mind and brain inseparable. The puzzle — the ‘hard problem’ — remains.

<Definition term="Hard problem of consciousness">
  The **hard problem** (Chalmers): why do physical brain processes feel like anything at all? Science can map which neurons fire when you see red — but not why there’s an inner experience of redness.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Dualism: mind and matter are distinct (Descartes) — but how do they interact?
- Physicalism: mind is what the brain does — but the feeling remains unexplained.
- Thought experiments: the philosophical zombie (acts conscious, feels nothing), Mary’s room (knows all colour science, never seen red).
- No consensus after 400 years — a live frontier.

<Example title="Mary’s room">
  Mary knows every physical fact about colour but has only seen black and white. When she first sees red, does she learn something new? If yes, physical facts weren’t the whole story.
</Example>
