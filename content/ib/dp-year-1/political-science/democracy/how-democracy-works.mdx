## How Democracy Works

Ancient Athens voted — but only free men, and it executed Socrates. Modern democracy means much more than majority rule.

<Definition term="Rule of law">
  The **rule of law** means everyone — including rulers — is subject to publicly known laws, applied equally and independently judged. Without it, elections are theatre.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Free, fair, regular elections with universal suffrage.
- Rights: speech, press, assembly — so opposition can organise.
- Rule of law: independent courts, no one above the law.
- Separation of powers: legislatures, executives and judiciaries checking each other.

<Example title="Why courts matter as much as ballots">
  When a government loses an election challenge in court and accepts it, that’s the rule of law working — power changing hands because institutions, not force, decide.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why isn’t majority voting alone enough for democracy?" hint="51% vs 49%.">
    Without rights and the rule of law, a majority could vote away minorities’ freedoms — the ‘tyranny of the majority’.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two institutions that check executive power." hint="Lawmakers and judges.">
    An independent judiciary and a legislature (parliament/congress) with real oversight powers.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Democracy = elections + rights + rule of law + checks.
  - Rights protect minorities from majorities.
  - Independent courts make power transfers peaceful.
  - Each institution guards the others.
</Summary>
