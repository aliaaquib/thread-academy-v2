## Why People Migrate

Humans have always moved. What’s new is the scale — and the politics.

<Definition term="Refugee">
  A **refugee** is someone forced to flee by war, persecution or disaster who cannot safely return — with specific protections under the 1951 Refugee Convention. An economic migrant chooses to move for better opportunities; an asylum seeker is awaiting refugee status.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Push factors: war, persecution, poverty, climate disaster.
- Pull factors: jobs, safety, family reunion, education.
- Most migration is within regions — e.g. within Africa — not to rich countries.
- Remittances (money sent home) exceed all foreign aid several times over.

<Example title="Remittances vs aid">
  Migrants sent home about $860 billion in 2023 — over three times all official development aid. Migration is the world’s biggest anti-poverty programme.
</Example>

<Summary>
  - 281 million international migrants and counting.
  - Push factors repel; pull factors attract.
  - Refugees have legal protections economic migrants don’t.
  - Remittances dwarf foreign aid.
