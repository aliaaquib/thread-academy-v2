## What Is Globalisation?

In 1990, calling abroad cost a fortune; today video calls are free. Container ships, the internet and open markets stitched the world into one system.

<Definition term="Globalisation">
  **Globalisation** is the growing interconnectedness of the world — economic (trade, supply chains), cultural (media, migration of ideas), political (international institutions) and technological.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Drivers: containerisation, the internet, trade liberalisation, cheaper flights.
- Economic: supply chains span continents; a chip shortage in Taiwan stalls car plants in Germany.
- Cultural: K-pop in Kansas, tacos in Tokyo — flows go every direction now.
- Political: the UN, WTO, Paris Agreement — cooperation infrastructure for a connected age.

<Example title="The life of a t-shirt">
  Cotton from Uzbekistan, spun in India, sewn in Bangladesh, sold in London, donated, resold in Kenya. One t-shirt, five countries — globalisation you can wear.
</Example>

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish economic from cultural globalisation." hint="Supply chains vs styles.">
    Economic: integrated production and trade. Cultural: the spread and mixing of ideas, media, food and fashion across borders.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Globalisation = deepening worldwide interconnectedness.
  - Driven by technology and open markets.
  - It has economic, cultural, political and technological faces.
  - Distance shrank — but didn’t disappear.
</Summary>
