## Mechanisms

A car engine’s pistons hammer up and down — but the wheels turn smoothly round. The crankshaft mechanism translates between the two.

<Definition term="Gear ratio">
  The **gear ratio** is teeth(driven) ÷ teeth(driver). A 3:1 ratio triples torque but the output spins 3× slower — the trade behind every bicycle gear.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Crank + slider: rotation → reciprocating motion (engines, pumps).
- Cams: rotation → programmed up-and-down motion.
- Belts/chains: transfer rotation between shafts.
- Linkages: steer, fold, grip — from car suspensions to robot arms.

<Example title="Bicycle gears">
  Climbing: small chainring, big sprocket — high ratio, easy pedalling, slow wheels. Descending: reverse it. You choose the force–speed trade every shift.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A driver gear has 12 teeth, the driven gear 36. What’s the ratio and effect?" hint="36 ÷ 12.">
    3:1 — the output turns 3× slower with 3× the torque.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does a crank convert rotation to linear motion?" hint="Think piston.">
    The crank pin circles; the connecting rod pushes the slider (piston) back and forth — circular motion becomes reciprocating motion.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Mechanisms transform motion: type, speed, direction.
  - Gear ratio = torque up, speed down (or vice versa).
  - Cranks, cams, belts and linkages are the building blocks.
  - Every machine is simple machines + mechanisms combined.
</Summary>
