## Oceans

Stand on any beach and you’re at the edge of the system that makes Earth habitable: oceans store heat, move it around the globe, and breathe CO₂ in and out.

<Definition term="Thermohaline circulation">
  **Thermohaline circulation** is the deep, global conveyor of ocean water driven by differences in temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline) — cold, salty water sinks and creeps along the seafloor for centuries before resurfacing.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Surface currents are driven mainly by wind; deep currents by density.
- The Gulf Stream carries tropical warmth to northern Europe.
- Oceans absorb about 90% of excess heat from global warming and about 30% of CO₂.
- Threats: overfishing, plastic, acidification, warming.

<Example title="El Niño">
  Every few years, Pacific trade winds falter and warm water sloshes east. Peruvian fisheries collapse, Australian droughts follow, global weather shifts — one current, worldwide consequences.
</Example>

<Summary>
  - Wind drives surface currents; density drives the deep conveyor.
  - Oceans redistribute heat and regulate climate.
  - They buffer climate change by absorbing heat and CO₂.
  - Overfishing, plastic and acidification threaten ocean systems.
</Summary>
