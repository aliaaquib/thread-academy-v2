## The Water Cycle

Earth’s water is a closed system: the total amount barely changes. What changes is where it is — ocean, cloud, glacier, river, aquifer — and the Sun keeps it moving.

<Definition term="Water cycle">
  The **water cycle** is the continuous movement of water between oceans, atmosphere and land through evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, runoff and infiltration — powered by solar energy.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Evaporation + transpiration (together: evapotranspiration) lift water into the air.
- Condensation forms clouds; precipitation returns water to the surface.
- Infiltration feeds groundwater; runoff feeds rivers.
- Humans alter it: dams, irrigation, deforestation and urban runoff.

<Example title="The Aral Sea">
  Soviet irrigation diverted its feeder rivers; the sea shrank to a tenth of its size, leaving fishing boats stranded in desert. A whole sea, undone by rerouting rivers.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What powers the water cycle?" hint="It rises as vapour — what heats it?">
    Solar energy drives evaporation and transpiration, lifting water into the atmosphere.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one human activity that disrupts the water cycle and its effect." hint="Think concrete cities.">
    Urbanisation covers ground in concrete, so less water infiltrates and more runs off — causing floods and depleting groundwater.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - The Sun powers a closed loop of evaporation, condensation and precipitation.
  - Transpiration from plants is a huge part of the cycle.
  - Water is finite in total but constantly redistributed.
  - Dams, irrigation and cities all reshape the cycle.
</Summary>
