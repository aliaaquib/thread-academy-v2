## Arguments for God

Can reason reach God? The ontological, cosmological and teleological arguments each try — and each has famous critics.

<Definition term="Teleological argument">
  The **teleological argument** (design argument): the universe’s order and complexity — like a watch implying a watchmaker (Paley) — suggest an intelligent designer.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Ontological (Anselm): God as the greatest conceivable being must exist — existence is part of greatness.
- Cosmological (Aquinas): everything has a cause; the chain needs a first, uncaused cause.
- Teleological (Paley): design implies a designer — fine-tuning of physical constants.
- Objections: Gaunilo’s perfect island (ontological), ‘what caused God?’ (cosmological), evolution explains apparent design (teleological).

<Example title="Paley’s watch">
  Find a watch on a heath and you infer a watchmaker. Paley argued nature’s far greater complexity likewise infers a designer. Hume replied: the universe isn’t much like a watch — and order can emerge without a maker.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="State the cosmological argument in two sentences." hint="Causes need a first cause.">
    Everything that begins has a cause; an infinite regress of causes is impossible, so there must be a first, uncaused cause — God.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does evolution challenge the design argument?" hint="Design without a designer.">
    Natural selection produces the appearance of design — complex adaptation — through blind, gradual processes, no designer required.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Three classic arguments: ontological, cosmological, teleological.
  - Each has endured centuries of criticism.
  - Science reshaped — but didn’t end — the debate.
  - Evaluate arguments by premises AND logic.
</Summary>
