## Major World Religions

Religion answers the questions science can’t: why are we here, what matters, what happens when we die? Different traditions answer differently — and understanding them matters in a connected world.

<Definition term="Monotheism">
  **Monotheism** is belief in one God (Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Sikhism). Contrast polytheism (many gods, e.g. Hinduism as commonly practised) and non-theistic traditions focused on practice and insight (Buddhism).
</Definition>

## Key points

- Christianity (~2.4bn): Jesus as Son of God; love of God and neighbour.
- Islam (~2bn): one God (Allah); Muhammad the final prophet; the Five Pillars.
- Hinduism (~1.2bn): dharma, karma, rebirth; many deities, one ultimate reality (Brahman).
- Buddhism (~500m): the Four Noble Truths; the Eightfold Path; end of suffering. Judaism: covenant with one God; Torah; ethical life. Sikhism: one God; equality; service.

<Example title="The Five Pillars of Islam">
  Shahada (declaration of faith), Salah (prayer five times daily), Zakat (charity), Sawm (fasting in Ramadan), Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca). Belief made concrete in daily practice.
</Example>

    1) Life involves suffering. 2) Suffering comes from craving. 3) Craving can end. 4) The Eightfold Path ends it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What do Judaism, Christianity and Islam share?" hint="One God, common roots.">
    Monotheism, Abrahamic roots, shared prophets/figures, sacred scriptures, and ethical commandments.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Six major traditions shape billions of lives.
  - Monotheism, polytheism and non-theism describe different God-concepts.
  - Practice matters as much as belief in most traditions.
  - Respectful comparison builds understanding.
</Summary>
