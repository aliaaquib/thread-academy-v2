## Katakana and Kanji

Three scripts, three jobs: hiragana for grammar, katakana for foreign words, kanji for meaning-roots. Together they make Japanese text instantly scannable.

<Definition term="Kanji">
  **Kanji** are characters borrowed from Chinese, each carrying meaning: 日 (sun/day), 本 (book/origin) — together 日本 (nihon) = Japan, ‘sun-origin’, the land of the rising sun.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Katakana: コーヒー (kōhī = coffee), コンピュータ (computer) — foreign words, angular style.
- Kanji: ~2,000 jōyō kanji for literacy; each has meaning + one or more readings.
- Mixed script is efficient: kanji show word boundaries and meaning at a glance.
- Start with: 日 本 人 大 小 中 国 語.

<Example title="Decoding 日本語">
  日本語 (nihongo): 日 sun + 本 origin + 語 language = ‘the language of the land of the rising sun’. Three characters, one story.
</Example>
