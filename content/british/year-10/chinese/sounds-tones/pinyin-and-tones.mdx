## Pinyin and Tones

Mandarin is tonal: the pitch pattern on a syllable changes its meaning. Master the four tones (+ neutral) and you’ll be understood; ignore them and ‘mother’ becomes ‘horse’.

<Definition term="Tone">
  A **tone** is the pitch contour of a syllable. Mandarin has four: 1st high-flat (mā), 2nd rising (má), 3rd dipping (mǎ), 4th falling (mà) — plus a neutral tone. Same syllable + different tone = different word.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Pinyin is the romanisation: nǐ hǎo (hello) shows pronunciation, not meaning.
- 1st tone: high and flat, like singing a held note.
- 2nd: rises, like asking ‘what?’ 3rd: dips down then up. 4th: falls sharply, like a command.
- Tone pairs matter more than single tones — practise words, not isolated syllables.

<Example title="Mā má mǎ mà">
  妈 (mā) mother, 麻 (má) hemp, 马 (mǎ) horse, 骂 (mà) scold. One syllable, four words — tone is everything.
</Example>
