## Key ideas

### Your voice is an instrument

Pace, pause and emphasis change how words land. Slow down for important points, pause before a key idea, and stress the words that matter. A monotone voice sends listeners to sleep, however good the ideas.

### Plan before you speak

Even a short talk needs a plan: one clear opening line, two or three main points in order, and a strong final sentence. A plan stops you rambling and gives you confidence when nerves strike.

**Key term — discourse marker:** A word or phrase that organises speech, such as firstly, however and in conclusion. Markers act as signposts for your listener.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Using Your Voice">
  Why should you slow down for your most important point?

  So the listener has time to absorb it and recognises that it matters.

  **Answer:** So the listener has time to absorb it and recognises that it matters.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Speaking too fast when nervous** Nerves speed everyone up. Practise pausing — it feels long to you but sounds confident to listeners.
  - **Reading every word from notes** Notes should be bullet points, not a script. Reading aloud sounds flat and stops you looking at your audience.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is your audience?" hint="Who is listening?">
    The people you are speaking to.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two discourse markers you could use in a talk." hint="Think firstly...">
    Any two of: firstly, in addition, however, in conclusion.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you disagree politely in a discussion?" hint="Attack the idea, not the person.">
    Disagree with the idea, not the person — for example, I see it differently because….
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is wrong with reading a talk word for word from a script?" hint="Think about your voice.">
    It sounds flat and stops you engaging with your audience.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
