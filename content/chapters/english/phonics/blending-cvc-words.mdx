<Definition term="Blending Simple Words">
  Push letter sounds together to read three-letter words like c-a-t.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Sounds before letter names

Always learn the sound a letter makes, not its name. The letter s says sss, not ess. Sounds can be pushed together into words; letter names get in the way of blending.

### Blend from left to right

Point under each letter and say the sounds in order, then say them faster until they join into the word. Stretching sss-aaa-t until it becomes sat is the skill that turns decoding into real reading.

**Key term — blending:** Pushing individual sounds together to read a word: s-a-t becomes sat. Blending turns letter sounds into real words.

<WorkedExample title="Blend and read: cat">
  Read the word c a t by blending its sounds.

  Say each sound separately, pointing at each letter: c, a, t.
  Say the sounds again, a little closer together: c-a-t.
  Speed up until the sounds join: cccaaat.
  Say the whole word smoothly: cat.
  Check it makes sense: a cat is a small furry pet.

  **Answer:** cat.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Guessing from the first letter** Always read every sound in the word. Guessing from the first letter causes mistakes like reading horse as house.
  - **Saying letter names instead of sounds** Say sss, a, t — not ess, ay, tee. Letter names cannot be blended into words.
</ImportantNote>
