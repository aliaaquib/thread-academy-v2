<Definition term="Joining Letters">
  Connect letters smoothly to build fluent, joined-up writing.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Start at the top

Almost every letter starts at the top and travels down or around. Starting letters in the right place keeps them neat and makes joining them later much easier.

### Spaces matter as much as letters

Leave a finger-width space between each word so your writing can be read easily. Words that run together are hard to read, even when every letter is perfect.

**Key term — baseline:** The line that letters sit on. Round letters like a, c and o rest on the baseline.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Joining Letters">
  Which of these letters have ascenders: b, a, h, g?

  b and h.

  **Answer:** b and h.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Writing letters in different sizes** Use the lines to guide you: tall letters reach the top line, small letters stay between the lines.
  - **Starting letters at the bottom** Nearly all letters start at the top. Bottom-up letters are slow and hard to join later.
</ImportantNote>
