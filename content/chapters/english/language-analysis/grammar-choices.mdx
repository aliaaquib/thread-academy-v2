## Key ideas

### Syntax controls pace

Short sentences punch; long, subordinated sentences flow and build. A writer describing panic often switches to fragments — breathless, broken. Matching sentence shape to content is one of the most powerful tools a writer has, and one of the easiest to analyse.

### Lexical fields build worlds

Writers cluster related words — a lexical field — to create an atmosphere. A horror passage might draw on darkness, cold, silence and decay, with every word reinforcing the others. Spotting the field lets you describe a text's overall effect in one precise move.

**Key term — semantics:** The study of meaning in language — what words and sentences denote, connote and imply.

<WorkedExample title="Analyse a passage for tension">
  Analyse how the writer builds tension in this passage: The corridor stretched ahead, dark and silent. Somewhere, a door creaked. Maya froze. Her breath came fast. Behind her — footsteps.

  Lexis: identify the lexical field of fear — dark, silent, creaked, froze.
  Syntax: note the short sentences and fragments, which quicken the pace like a racing heartbeat.
  Sound: the hard consonants in creaked and footsteps feel sharp and threatening.
  Structure: the final fragment withholds information, leaving the reader in suspense.
  Conclude: every linguistic level pulls in the same direction.

  **Answer:** The writer builds tension through a lexical field of fear, short fragmented syntax that mimics panic, harsh consonant sounds, and a closing fragment that withholds information — every level of language works together to unsettle the reader.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Ignoring the bigger picture** Individual word choices matter, but check how they combine: a full commentary links lexis, grammar and pragmatics into one argument.
  - **Labelling without explaining** Naming a semantic field or low modality is not analysis. Always connect the label to its effect on the reader.
</ImportantNote>
