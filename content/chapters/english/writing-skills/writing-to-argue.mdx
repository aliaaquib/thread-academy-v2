<Definition term="Writing to Argue">
  Persuade readers with structured arguments and rhetorical devices.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Plan every answer

Spend five minutes planning before writing a single sentence. For an essay, turn the question into three or four points; for a story, map the beginning, middle and end. A plan prevents rambling and guarantees you answer the actual question.

### Show, don't just tell

In descriptive writing, she was sad tells; tears slid down her cheeks as she stared at the empty chair shows. Use the five senses — what can be seen, heard, smelled, touched and tasted — and choose precise nouns and verbs rather than piling on adjectives.

**Key term — discourse marker:** A connective that organises writing: however, furthermore, consequently, on the other hand. They guide the reader through your argument.

<WorkedExample title="Plan a PEED paragraph">
  Essay question: How does the writer present fear? Plan one PEED paragraph using the quotation 'her feet refused to move'.

  Point: the writer presents fear as something that freezes the body.
  Evidence: choose the short quotation 'her feet refused to move'.
  Explain: the verb refused personifies her feet, as if fear has taken control of them.
  Develop: this suggests fear is more powerful than willpower — a message that helps readers recognise their own fear.
  Write the paragraph in full, linking each sentence back to the question.

  **Answer:** The writer presents fear as a force that paralyses the body. The quotation 'her feet refused to move' personifies her feet, suggesting fear has seized control of them. This develops into a wider message: fear can overpower willpower itself, which helps readers recognise their own experiences of being frozen by fright.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Writing everything you know about a text** Only include points that answer the question. Irrelevant knowledge, however accurate, earns no marks.
  - **Repeating the same discourse marker** Vary however, furthermore and in contrast. Repeating also in every paragraph makes writing sound immature.
</ImportantNote>
