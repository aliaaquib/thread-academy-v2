<Definition term="Making Images">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Respect other people's work

Pictures and music you find online belong to someone. Only use images you made yourself, or ones your teacher says are free to use — and always say where a borrowed image came from. This habit is called giving credit, and it is the law as well as good manners.

### Save and name like a pro

Save your work often, and give files names that describe them, like 'volcano-poster-final', instead of 'untitled1'. Future you will be very grateful when it is time to find the file again.

**Key term — file:** A single saved piece of work on a computer, such as one picture or one slideshow. Files live in folders so you can find them again.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Making Images">
  Your slideshow file will not open and you last saved it an hour ago. What habit would have saved your work?

  Saving often — every few minutes — so only a few minutes of work could ever be lost.

  **Answer:** Saving often — every few minutes — so only a few minutes of work could ever be lost.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using a picture from the internet with no credit** Correction: use your own drawings or teacher-approved images, and write down where any borrowed image came from.
  - **Cramming every word onto one slide** Correction: keep slides visual — a picture plus a short caption beats a wall of text every time.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="You want music for your slideshow. What is the safe choice?" hint="Remember who owns music you find online.">
    Use music your teacher says is free to use, or record your own — and always credit the creator.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is the better file name: 'untitled2' or 'dolphin-poster-final'? Why?" hint="Imagine finding it again in a month.">
    'dolphin-poster-final' — it describes the file's contents, so you can find it without opening it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does an animation make still pictures look as if they move?" hint="Think about flipping the corner of a notebook.">
    It shows many slightly different pictures one after another very quickly, and your eyes blend them into smooth movement.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a storyboard, and why do film-makers use one?" hint="Think about planning scenes in order.">
    A storyboard is a set of sketches showing each scene in order; it lets film-makers plan and fix problems on paper before expensive filming starts.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
