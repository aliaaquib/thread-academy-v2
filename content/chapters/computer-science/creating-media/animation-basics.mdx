This lesson focuses on Animation Basics: make simple animations and slideshows that tell a story in order.

<Definition term="Animation Basics">
  Make simple animations and slideshows that tell a story in order.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Plan before you make

A storyboard saves time and arguments: sketch each slide or scene, write what the words will say, and agree who does what. Changing a sketch takes seconds; rebuilding a finished slideshow takes ages. Professionals in film and games always storyboard first.

### Respect other people's work

Pictures and music you find online belong to someone. Only use images you made yourself, or ones your teacher says are free to use — and always say where a borrowed image came from. This habit is called giving credit, and it is the law as well as good manners.

**Key term — digital media:** Anything you create on a computer that others can see or hear: images, slideshows, animations, video and sound recordings.

<WorkedExample title="Storyboarding a Slideshow">
  Plan a 4-slide show called 'My Favourite Animal' before touching the computer.

  Draw four empty boxes in a row on paper — one box per slide, in order.
  In box 1, sketch a title page and write the animal's name as the heading.
  In boxes 2 and 3, sketch one picture each and note one fascinating fact beneath it.
  In box 4, sketch a closing slide that says what you like most about the animal.
  Show the storyboard to your partner — if they understand the whole show from the sketches, the plan is ready.

  **Answer:** A finished storyboard with four ordered sketches, headings and facts means the slideshow can now be built slide by slide with no wasted effort.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Starting on the computer with no plan** Correction: spend five minutes on a storyboard first — it makes the building stage twice as fast.
  - **Using a picture from the internet with no credit** Correction: use your own drawings or teacher-approved images, and write down where any borrowed image came from.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a storyboard, and why do film-makers use one?" hint="Think about planning scenes in order.">
    A storyboard is a set of sketches showing each scene in order; it lets film-makers plan and fix problems on paper before expensive filming starts.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does an animation make still pictures look as if they move?" hint="Think about flipping the corner of a notebook.">
    It shows many slightly different pictures one after another very quickly, and your eyes blend them into smooth movement.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="True or false: it is fine to copy a whole story from a website into your slideshow as long as it looks nice." hint="Think about credit and ownership.">
    False — the story belongs to its author; summarise it in your own words and say where you found it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You want music for your slideshow. What is the safe choice?" hint="Remember who owns music you find online.">
    Use music your teacher says is free to use, or record your own — and always credit the creator.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
