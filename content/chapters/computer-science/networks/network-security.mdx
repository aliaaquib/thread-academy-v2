## Key ideas

### The internet is a network of networks

Your home network connects to your internet provider's network, which connects to other providers' networks across undersea cables and satellites. No single company owns the internet — it is millions of networks agreeing to exchange packets with each other.

### Data travels in packets

A photo is too big to send in one go, so it is split into numbered packets that may take different routes. Each packet carries the sender's and receiver's IP addresses, and the receiving device reassembles them in order — requesting any that go missing. This is why a video can keep playing even on a wobbly connection.

**Key term — network:** Two or more devices connected so they can share data and resources, from a home Wi-Fi to the entire internet.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Network Security">

</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking the internet and the World Wide Web are the same thing** Correction: the internet is the network of connected devices; the Web is just one service — pages and links — that runs on top of it, like email and streaming do.
  - **Assuming packets arrive in order** Correction: packets take different routes and arrive jumbled — numbering lets the receiver sort them out.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Your friend says Wi-Fi and the internet are the same thing. How do you correct them?" hint="One is how you connect; the other is what you connect to.">
    Wi-Fi is just the wireless link between your device and the router; the internet is the global network beyond the router that your data travels across.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="True or false: every device on a network must use a different IP address." hint="What would happen if two devices shared one postal address?">
    True — addresses must be unique so packets reach the right device, just as post needs a unique address per house.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a LAN and a WAN?" hint="Think about size: one room versus one country.">
    A LAN connects devices in a small area like a home or school; a WAN spans large distances — the internet is the biggest WAN of all.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is a long video split into packets instead of being sent whole?" hint="What happens if one tiny piece goes missing?">
    Only the missing packets need re-sending instead of the whole video, and packets can travel different routes to avoid congestion.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
