The internet is full of brilliant things to explore, but it has rules — just like crossing the road. A few simple habits keep your accounts, your personal details and your feelings safe. This chapter gives you the safety rules every young internet user should know by heart.

This lesson focuses on Strong Passwords: learn what makes a password hard to guess and why you must never share it.

<Definition term="Strong Passwords">
  Learn what makes a password hard to guess and why you must never share it.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Passwords are like toothbrushes

Never share them with anyone, not even your best friend. A strong password is long — three random words such as 'tiger-pencil-moon' are far harder to guess than your pet's name plus your birthday. Use a different password for each account so one leak does not open every door.

### Think before you share

Ask yourself: would I be happy if this photo or message was shown to my whole school? Personal information — your address, school name or location — should never be posted publicly, because you cannot control who sees it or saves it.

**Key term — password:** A secret word or phrase that proves you are you when you log in. A strong password is long and hard to guess.

<WorkedExample title="Building a Strong Password">
  Mia's password is 'mia123'. Show why it is weak and build a better one.

  'mia123' uses her name, which anyone who knows her can guess — never use your name, pet or birthday.
  It is only 6 characters long — short passwords are quick for attackers to try out.
  Pick three random words instead, for example 'tiger', 'pencil' and 'moon' — long but easy to remember.
  Join them with dashes: 'tiger-pencil-moon' — 17 characters that no one could guess from knowing Mia.

  **Answer:** 'tiger-pencil-moon' is strong because it is long, random and personal to nobody; 'mia123' is weak because it is short and built from Mia's own name.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Sharing a password with a friend** Correction: keep every password secret, even from friends — friendships change, but a shared password stays shared.
  - **Posting photos in school uniform with the school name visible** Correction: check photos for clues like badges and street signs before posting, and keep accounts private.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which password is strongest: 'fred', 'fred2001', or 'cloud-drum-apple'?" hint="Longer and more random beats short and personal.">
    'cloud-drum-apple' — it is long, random and gives no clues about its owner.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you log out of accounts on a shared school computer?" hint="What could the next user do with your logged-in account?">
    Anyone using the computer next could read your messages or pretend to be you, so always log out.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="True or false: deleting a photo from your phone removes it from the internet." hint="Think about your digital footprint.">
    False — if the photo was posted or sent, other people may already have saved or shared it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A player you met in a game asks which school you go to. What should you do?" hint="Remember what counts as personal information.">
    Do not answer — your school is personal information. Stop chatting and tell a trusted adult.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
