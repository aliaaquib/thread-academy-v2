</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Stop, block and tell

If a message upsets you, a stranger asks for personal details, or something feels wrong, stop replying, block the person and tell a trusted adult straight away. You will never be in trouble for reporting something that worries you — telling is always the brave choice.

### Think before you share

Ask yourself: would I be happy if this photo or message was shown to my whole school? Personal information — your address, school name or location — should never be posted publicly, because you cannot control who sees it or saves it.

**Key term — digital footprint:** The trail of posts, photos and messages you leave behind online. Once something is posted, it can be very hard to remove.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Spotting Online Tricks">
  Why should you log out of accounts on a shared school computer?

  Anyone using the computer next could read your messages or pretend to be you, so always log out.

  **Answer:** Anyone using the computer next could read your messages or pretend to be you, so always log out.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Clicking a pop-up that says you have won a prize** Correction: prizes that appear from nowhere are tricks — close the pop-up and tell an adult instead of clicking.
  - **Sharing a password with a friend** Correction: keep every password secret, even from friends — friendships change, but a shared password stays shared.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A message says 'Click here to claim your free tablet!' What should you do?" hint="Do unexpected prizes arrive by message?">
    Do not click — it is almost certainly a trick to steal details or install something nasty. Delete it and tell an adult.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which password is strongest: 'fred', 'fred2001', or 'cloud-drum-apple'?" hint="Longer and more random beats short and personal.">
    'cloud-drum-apple' — it is long, random and gives no clues about its owner.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A player you met in a game asks which school you go to. What should you do?" hint="Remember what counts as personal information.">
    Do not answer — your school is personal information. Stop chatting and tell a trusted adult.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your friend posts an embarrassing photo of you without asking. What is the kind thing to do if the roles were reversed?" hint="Think about permission.">
    Ask permission before posting photos of other people, and remove a photo straight away if the person in it asks you to.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
