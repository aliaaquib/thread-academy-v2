<Definition term="Right Material for the Job">
  Choosing materials for everyday objects based on their properties.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Materials have properties we can feel and see

Run your hand over sandpaper and silk and the difference is obvious: rough versus smooth. Properties like hardness, shininess and flexibility help us describe materials precisely. Scientists group materials by these properties to decide what each one is good for.

### Some materials let water or light through, others block them

A sponge soaks up water while a plastic bag keeps it out — one is absorbent and the other is waterproof. Clear glass is transparent, frosted glass is translucent, and wood is opaque. These blocking and letting-through properties decide how we use each material.

**Key term — material:** What something is made from, such as wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric or rock.

<WorkedExample title="Which material for a raincoat?">
  Priya needs a material for a raincoat. She tests three: cotton fabric, plastic sheet and paper.

  The raincoat must keep water out, so the material must be waterproof.
  Cotton soaks up water — it is absorbent, not waterproof.
  Paper tears and soaks through — no good either.
  The plastic sheet keeps water out and bends easily.

  **Answer:** The plastic sheet — it is waterproof and flexible, the two properties a raincoat needs.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying 'material' when you mean 'object'** The object is the chair; the material is the wood it is made from.
  - **Thinking glass is always transparent** Frosted or stained glass is translucent — it lets some light through but you cannot see clearly through it.
</ImportantNote>

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one transparent material and one opaque material." hint="Think of windows and walls.">
    Transparent: glass or clear plastic. Opaque: wood, metal or brick.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A material lets some light through but you cannot see through it clearly. What word describes it?" hint="It sits between transparent and opaque.">
    Translucent.
  </PracticeItem>

  </PracticeItem>
