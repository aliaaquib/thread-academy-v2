## Key ideas

### SHM describes smooth back-and-forth oscillation

In simple harmonic motion the restoring force grows with displacement: a = −ω²x, the minus sign showing acceleration always points back to equilibrium. A mass on a spring has period T = 2π√(m/k); a pendulum has T = 2π√(l/g). Maximum speed occurs at the centre of the swing, maximum acceleration at the extremes.

### Circular motion needs a constant centre-seeking force

An object circling at steady speed is accelerating because its velocity direction keeps changing. The centripetal acceleration is v²/r towards the centre, so the required force is F = mv²/r. Remove the force — cut the string — and the object flies off along a tangent, which is why mud flings off a spinning wheel.

**Key term — simple harmonic motion:** Oscillation where acceleration is proportional to displacement and always towards equilibrium: a = −ω²x.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Simple Harmonic Motion">
  A 0.2 kg mass on a spring (k = 80 N/m) oscillates. Calculate the period.

  √(0.2 ÷ 80) = √0.0025 = 0.05, so T = 2π × 0.05 ≈ 0.31 s.

  **Answer:** √(0.2 ÷ 80) = √0.0025 = 0.05, so T = 2π × 0.05 ≈ 0.31 s.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using T = 2π√(m/k) for a pendulum** That formula is for a mass on a spring; a pendulum uses T = 2π√(l/g).
  - **Inventing an outward 'centrifugal force'** In an inertial frame there is only the inward centripetal force — the outward feeling is just inertia resisting the turn.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A 1200 kg car rounds a bend of radius 50 m at 20 m/s. Find the centripetal force." hint="F = mv²/r.">
    (1200 × 400) ÷ 50 = 480,000 ÷ 50 = 9600 N.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 0.5 kg ball moving at 6 m/s is stopped in 0.2 s. Find the average force." hint="Impulse = change in momentum.">
    Δp = 0.5 × 6 = 3 kg m/s; F = 3 ÷ 0.2 = 15 N.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Two trolleys (2 kg at 3 m/s, 1 kg stationary) stick together after colliding. Find their combined speed." hint="Conserve total momentum.">
    Before: 2 × 3 = 6 kg m/s. After: 3 kg × v = 6, so v = 2 m/s.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do airbags reduce injury in a crash?" hint="Think about F = Δp/Δt.">
    They increase the time over which momentum changes, so the average force on the passenger is smaller.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
