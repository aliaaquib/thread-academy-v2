</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Forces change how things move

A stationary object stays still until a push or a pull acts on it. A moving object keeps going in a straight line until a force changes its speed or direction. The bigger the push, the bigger the change — a gentle tap nudges a ball, while a hard kick sends it flying.

### Gravity always pulls down

Drop a ball and it falls to the ground because Earth's gravity pulls it downwards. Gravity acts on everything, which is why what goes up must come down. It even keeps the Moon circling the Earth instead of drifting away.

**Key term — force:** A push or a pull. Forces can make things move, stop moving, change direction or change shape.

<WorkedExample title="Which way will it move?">
  A toy boat floats in a paddling pool. Mia pushes the back of the boat gently with her hand. What happens?

  A push acts on the boat in the direction Mia's hand moves.
  The water pushes back only a little, because water is smooth and slippery.
  The boat moves forwards, away from Mia's hand.
  If she pushed harder, the boat would move faster.

  **Answer:** The boat moves away from Mia, in the direction of the push.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying gravity pushes things down** Gravity pulls things towards the Earth — the Earth attracts the object, so 'pull' is the correct word.
  - **Believing a push has to touch something** Magnets can pull metal objects without touching them, and gravity pulls without touching too.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the force that pulls a dropped apple to the ground." hint="It comes from the Earth.">
    Gravity.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Does a bigger push make a toy car travel further?" hint="Think about what you feel when you push harder.">
    Yes — a bigger push gives the car more speed, so it travels further before friction stops it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A marble rolls across a smooth table and falls off the edge. Which force makes it fall?" hint="It acts downwards everywhere on Earth.">
    Gravity pulls the marble downwards once it is no longer supported by the table.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example of a pull you do every day." hint="Think about opening something.">
    Answers vary: opening a door or drawer, pulling on socks, tugging a zip. A pull moves something towards you.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
