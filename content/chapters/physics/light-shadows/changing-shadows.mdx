<Definition term="Changing Shadows">
  Investigating how moving a light or object makes shadows grow, shrink and move.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Moving the light or object changes the shadow

Hold a puppet close to a lamp and its shadow on the wall is huge; move it away and the shadow shrinks. Bringing the object nearer the light blocks a wider spread of rays, so the shadow grows. This is how shadow puppet shows make giant characters from small hands.

### Light travels in straight lines

Light cannot bend around corners — it always moves in straight lines called rays. That is why you cannot see around a wall but you can see down a straight corridor. Shadows have sharp edges because light cannot curve around the object making them.

**Key term — shadow:** The dark shape formed when an object blocks light from reaching a surface.

<WorkedExample title="Predicting a shadow">
  A torch shines at a wooden block standing on a table. Where will the shadow appear, and what happens if the torch is moved closer to the block?

  The block is opaque, so it blocks the torch's straight-travelling rays.
  The shadow forms on the table and wall on the far side from the torch.
  Moving the torch closer means the block blocks a wider spread of rays.
  The shadow grows larger.

  **Answer:** The shadow appears on the opposite side from the torch, and moving the torch closer makes the shadow bigger.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing light bends around objects** Light travels in straight lines, which is exactly why shadows exist.
  - **Thinking shadows can appear on the same side as the light** A shadow always falls opposite the light source, where the light is blocked.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does a clear glass of water make almost no shadow?" hint="What does glass do to light?">
    Glass is transparent, so light passes straight through instead of being blocked.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You move a toy further from a lamp. What happens to its shadow on the wall?" hint="Think about how many rays the toy blocks.">
    The shadow gets smaller, because the toy blocks a narrower spread of rays.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="At midday your shadow is short; in the evening it is long. Why?" hint="Where is the Sun in the sky at each time?">
    At midday the Sun is high overhead so light comes almost straight down; in the evening the Sun is low, so your body blocks rays at a shallow angle and the shadow stretches out.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two natural light sources." hint="One is in the sky; think of living things too.">
    The Sun and stars (also acceptable: lightning, fireflies, glow-worms).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
