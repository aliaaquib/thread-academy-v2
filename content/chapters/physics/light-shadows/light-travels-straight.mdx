<Definition term="Light Travels Straight">
  Learning that light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around corners.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Light travels in straight lines

Light cannot bend around corners — it always moves in straight lines called rays. That is why you cannot see around a wall but you can see down a straight corridor. Shadows have sharp edges because light cannot curve around the object making them.

### Shadows form when light is blocked

Stand in sunshine and your body blocks the Sun's rays, leaving a dark patch — your shadow — on the ground behind you. Only opaque objects make proper shadows; transparent glass lets the light straight through. The shadow always falls on the opposite side from the light source.

**Key term — opaque:** A material that light cannot pass through, so it makes a clear dark shadow.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Light Travels Straight">
  Why can't you see someone standing around a corner?

  Light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around the corner to reach your eyes.

  **Answer:** Light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around the corner to reach your eyes.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing light bends around objects** Light travels in straight lines, which is exactly why shadows exist.
  - **Saying the Moon is a light source** The Moon does not make its own light — it reflects sunlight towards us.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does a clear glass of water make almost no shadow?" hint="What does glass do to light?">
    Glass is transparent, so light passes straight through instead of being blocked.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="At midday your shadow is short; in the evening it is long. Why?" hint="Where is the Sun in the sky at each time?">
    At midday the Sun is high overhead so light comes almost straight down; in the evening the Sun is low, so your body blocks rays at a shallow angle and the shadow stretches out.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two natural light sources." hint="One is in the sky; think of living things too.">
    The Sun and stars (also acceptable: lightning, fireflies, glow-worms).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is a mirror a light source? Explain." hint="Does it make its own light?">
    No — a mirror reflects light from a real source; it does not produce light itself.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
