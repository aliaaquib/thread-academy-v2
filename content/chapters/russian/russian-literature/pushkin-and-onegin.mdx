## Key ideas

### The Onegin stanza

Eugene Onegin is written in a unique 14-line stanza rhyming ABABCCDD EFFEGG, which Pushkin uses for everything from landscape to irony to direct addresses to the reader. The stanza's conversational ease lets Pushkin slip between narrator and character — a technique to name in essays as authorial intrusion.

### Chekhov and the subtext

Chekhov's characters rarely say what they mean: the real drama happens in the подтекст (podtékst — subtext), in pauses and trivial remarks. In 'The Lady with the Dog' — Дама с собачкой (Dáma s sobáchkoy) — love is confessed sideways, through talk of the weather. Chekhov's gun — the rule that every detail must matter — means you should analyse objects, not just dialogue.

**Key term — стихотворение (stikhotvoréniye) — poem:** Pushkin's natural habitat. Russian poetry's music depends on stress patterns, so reading aloud is part of analysis.

<WorkedExample title="Opening Onegin">
  Analyse the famous opening line: «Мой дядя самых честных правил…».

  Translate literally: 'My uncle, of the most honest principles…' — мой дядя (moy dyádya — my uncle), честных правил (chéstnykh právil — of honest rules).
  Note the irony: praising an uncle's 'honest principles' in the very first line already sounds like a dutiful nephew's cliché — Onegin is performing respect.
  Note the stanza: this is line 1 of the 14-line Onegin stanza, so Pushkin signals that high poetic form will carry deliberately ordinary, even comic content.
  Conclude: in a single line Pushkin establishes voice (ironic, conversational), character (Onegin the bored poseur) and method (form versus content).

  **Answer:** The line introduces Onegin through ironic praise of his uncle, establishing the novel's conversational irony and the productive tension between lofty form and mundane content.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling Onegin a romantic hero** Onegin is the original лишний человек (líshniy chelovék — superfluous man): bored, clever and useless. Admiring him uncritically misses Pushkin's irony.
  - **Summarising Chekhov's plots instead of analysing** Examiners want the подтекст (podtékst — subtext): what is not said. Writing only what happens in 'The Lady with the Dog' earns description marks, not analysis marks.
</ImportantNote>
