## Key ideas

### Хлебосольство: the cult of hospitality

The traditional welcome offers хлеб-соль (khleb-sol' — bread and salt), and the virtue of хлебосольство (khlebosól'stvo — generous hospitality) still rules: guests must eat, refusing is almost rude, and the table groans under salads like оливье (oliv'yé — Olivier salad). Tea — чай (chai) — is served constantly; 'have some tea' really means 'stay a while'.

### New Year, not Christmas

The Soviet era moved the gift-giving magic from Christmas to Новый год (Nóvyy god — New Year): Дед Мороз (Ded Moróz — Father Frost) and his granddaughter Снегурочка (Snegúrochka — the Snow Maiden) bring presents on 31 December. Orthodox Christmas — Рождество (Rozhdestvó) — follows on 7 January and is a quieter, religious holiday, reversing the Western order of importance.

**Key term — праздник (prázdnik) — holiday, celebration:** Any festive day. Note the silent д: it is pronounced 'práznik', much like the silent в in здравствуйте (zdrávstvuyte).

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Bringing an even number of flowers** Two or four roses suggest a funeral. Always bring an odd number — 3, 5 or 7 — for a celebration.
  - **Wishing 'Merry Christmas' as the main greeting** On 31 December Russians say С Новым годом! (s Nóvym gódom — Happy New Year!). Christmas — Рождество (Rozhdestvó) — on 7 January is secondary.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="When is Russia's main winter holiday, and what is it called?" hint="It falls on 31 December, not 25 December.">
    Новый год (Nóvyy god — New Year), celebrated on the night of 31 December.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Who brings Russian children their New Year presents?" hint="He is accompanied by his granddaughter the Snow Maiden.">
    Дед Мороз (Ded Moróz — Father Frost), with Снегурочка (Snegúrochka — the Snow Maiden).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How many time zones does Russia span?" hint="More than ten.">
    Eleven time zones, from Kaliningrad to Kamchatka.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What should you do with your shoes when entering a Russian home?" hint="The host will offer you something.">
    Remove them and accept the offered тапочки (tápochki — slippers).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
