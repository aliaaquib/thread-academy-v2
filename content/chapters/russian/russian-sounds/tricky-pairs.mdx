<Definition term="Tricky Letter Pairs">
  Spot the difference between look-alike letters such as В (v) and Б (b), and Р (r) and П (p).
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Hard and soft consonants

Many Russian consonants come in pairs: a hard version and a soft, smiley version. Compare мат (mat — checkmate), said with a firm T, with мать (mat' — mother), where the soft sign Ь makes the T soft and gentle. For the soft sound your tongue moves a little forward — try saying 'tya' versus 'ta' and feel the difference.

### One letter, one sound

Russian is very honest: each letter usually keeps the same sound wherever it appears. Once you learn that М says 'mmm' and А says 'ah', you can read МА anywhere — in мама (máma — mum), in машина (mashína — car) and in марка (márka — stamp). Reading Russian feels like cracking a code, and the code barely changes.

**Key term — буква (búkva) — letter:** A single written symbol of the alphabet. The Russian alphabet, called the алфавит (alfavít), contains 33 буквы (búkvy — letters).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Tricky Letter Pairs">
  How many letters are in the Russian alphabet?

  33 letters.

  **Answer:** 33 letters.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying В like the English B** Cyrillic В sounds like V, not B — ваза (váza) is read 'VA-za' and means 'vase'. The letter Б is the one that sounds like B.
  - **Mixing up Р and P** Cyrillic Р is a rolled R, not P — рыба (rýba) is read 'RY-ba' and means 'fish'. Cyrillic П is the letter that sounds like P.
</ImportantNote>

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How many syllables does машина have?" hint="Count the vowel letters.">
