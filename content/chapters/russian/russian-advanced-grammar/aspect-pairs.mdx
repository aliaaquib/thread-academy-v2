<Definition term="Aspect Pairs">
  Choose between imperfective and perfective with pairs like делать (délat' — to do) and сделать (sdélat' — to have done).
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Choosing aspect

Use the imperfective for processes, habits and repeated actions: Я читал книгу вчера (ya chitál knígu vcherá — I was reading the book yesterday). Use the perfective for single completed results: Я прочитал книгу (ya prochitál knígu — I have finished the book). After уже (uzhé — already) the perfective is almost always right: Я уже сделал домашнее задание (ya uzhé sdélal domáshneye zadániye — I have already done my homework).

### Unidirectional versus multidirectional

Идти (idtí) and ехать (yékhat') describe a single journey in one direction happening now; ходить (khodít') and ездить (yézdit') describe going and returning, habitual trips, or movement with no fixed direction. Prefixes then add direction and completion: прийти (priytí — to arrive on foot), уйти (uytí — to leave on foot), приехать (priyékhat' — to arrive by transport), уехать (uyékhat' — to leave by transport). Note that prefixed motion verbs become perfective.

**Key term — вид (vid) — aspect:** Every Russian verb is imperfective (несовершенный вид — ongoing or repeated action) or perfective (совершенный вид — completed action): делать (délat' — to do, imperfective) versus сделать (sdélat' — to do, perfective).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Aspect Pairs">
  Choose: Я (читал / прочитал) книгу вчера вечером и закончил её.

  Я прочитал книгу вчера вечером (ya prochitál knígu vcherá vécherom — I finished reading the book yesterday evening).

  **Answer:** Я прочитал книгу вчера вечером (ya prochitál knígu vcherá vécherom — I finished reading the book yesterday evening).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using the imperfective after уже** Уже (uzhé — already) signals completion: Я уже прочитал (ya uzhé prochitál — I have already read), not Я уже читал (which suggests 'I used to read').
  - **Treating прийти as imperfective** Прийти (priytí — to arrive on foot) is perfective: Я приду завтра (ya pridú závtra — I will come tomorrow) is future, not present. Its imperfective partner is приходить (prikhodít').
</ImportantNote>

  - идти (idtí) / ходить (khodít') — to go (on foot): A motion pair: идти is one journey in one direction — Я иду в школу (ya idú f shkólu — I am going to school); ходить is repeated or multidirectional — Я хожу в школу каждый день (ya khozhú f shkólu kázhdyy den' — I go to school every day).
