## Key ideas

### Finding noun gender from endings

Masculine nouns usually end in a consonant — дом (dom — house), стол (stol — table) — or in -й, like музей (muzéy — museum). Feminine nouns end in -а or -я: книга (kníga — book), земля (zemlyá — land). Neuter nouns end in -о or -е: окно (oknó — window), море (móre — sea). Nouns in -ь can be either — словарь (slovár' — dictionary) is masculine but ночь (noch' — night) is feminine — so learn those together with the noun.

### Present tense endings

First-conjugation verbs like делать (délat' — to do) take -ю, -ешь, -ет, -ем, -ете, -ют: я делаю (ya délayu — I do), ты делаешь (ty délayesh), он делает (on délayet). Second-conjugation verbs like говорить take -ю, -ишь, -ит, -им, -ите, -ят. Learn the six endings once and you can conjugate hundreds of verbs.

**Key term — род (rod) — gender:** Every Russian noun has a grammatical gender: masculine, feminine or neuter. Gender decides adjective and possessive endings, so identifying it is step one.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Noun Genders">
  What gender is книга?

  Feminine — it ends in -а: книга (kníga — book).

  **Answer:** Feminine — it ends in -а: книга (kníga — book).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating папа as feminine** Папа (pápa — dad) ends in -а but is masculine because it names a man: мой папа (moy pápa — my dad), not моя папа. With people, meaning beats the ending.
  - **Saying ты to a teacher** Ты (ty) to a teacher sounds disrespectful. Use вы (vy): Вы учитель (vy uchítel' — you are the teacher).
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="Do you use ты or вы with your teacher?" hint="Teachers get respect.">

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Conjugate: он ___ (делать)." hint="First conjugation, third person singular: -ет.">

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Conjugate: они ___ (говорить)." hint="Second conjugation, third person plural: -ят.">
