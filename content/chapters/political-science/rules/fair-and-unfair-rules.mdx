## Key ideas

### Fair rules, unfair rules

A fair rule applies to everyone equally and has a good reason behind it, such as everyone tidying up so the classroom stays pleasant. An unfair rule picks on one person or group for no good reason, like only letting some children use the best toys. If a rule seems unfair, it is worth asking politely why it exists and whether it treats everyone the same.

### Rules need consequences

A rule without a consequence is just a suggestion, because nothing happens when it is broken. Fair consequences match the rule-breaking — for example, losing five minutes of playtime after pushing in line — and they apply to everyone equally. Consequences work best when they are known in advance and given calmly.

**Key term — fair:** Something is fair when it treats people equally and gives everyone the same chances, without favouring one person over another.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Fair and Unfair Rules">
  What is the main reason most rules exist?

  To keep people safe and to make things fair for everyone.

  **Answer:** To keep people safe and to make things fair for everyone.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **'Rules are just there to spoil our fun'** Most rules exist to keep people safe or to make things fair for everyone. Try asking what the rule protects before deciding it is pointless.
  - **'Unfair rules should just be ignored'** Ignoring an unfair rule can cause trouble for you and others. A better approach is to raise it calmly with a teacher or parent and explain why it seems unfair.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Is this rule fair or unfair: 'Everyone in the class must queue for lunch in the same line'?" hint="Does it treat everyone the same?">
    Fair — it treats every pupil equally and has a clear reason.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a rule and a law?" hint="Who makes each one?">
    Rules are made by families or schools, while laws are made by a country's government and enforced by the courts.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is this rule fair or unfair: 'Only the tallest children may use the football pitch'?" hint="Does it favour one group for no good reason?">
    Unfair — it favours one group for no good reason.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Imagine your friend suggests the rule 'no homework ever'. Give one reason why your teacher might disagree." hint="Think about what homework is for.">
    Homework helps pupils practise what they learned, so banning it would make learning harder for everyone.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
