## What Is Politics?

A family arguing over dinner, a union negotiating pay, nations signing treaties — all politics. Lasswell’s definition endures: who gets what, when, how.

<Definition term="Legitimacy">
  **Legitimacy** is the recognised right to rule. Power forces obedience; authority — power seen as legitimate — gets voluntary compliance. Weber named three types: traditional, charismatic and rational-legal.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Power: the ability to get others to do what you want (Weber).
- Authority: power accepted as legitimate — cheaper and more stable than force.
- The state: the institution claiming a monopoly on legitimate force in a territory.
- Politics happens everywhere power is contested — workplaces, families, friendships.

<Example title="Why do people obey speed limits?">
  Some fear fines (coercion). Most accept the law’s right to set them (rational-legal legitimacy) — and many believe limits are right (shared values). Three kinds of compliance at one road sign.
</Example>

## Practice

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give Weber’s three types of legitimate authority with an example of each." hint="Tradition, charisma, rules.">
    Traditional (monarchy), charismatic (revolutionary leader), rational-legal (elected president, bureaucracy).
  </PracticeItem>

<Summary>
  - Politics = the struggle over binding decisions.
  - Authority is power plus legitimacy.
  - The state monopolises legitimate force in a territory.
  - Politics exists wherever power is shared and contested.
</Summary>
