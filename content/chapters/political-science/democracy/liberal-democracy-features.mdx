## Key ideas

### The pillars of liberal democracy

Free and fair elections are only the start. Liberal democracy also needs the rule of law, an independent judiciary, a free press that can criticise the government, and protection for minority rights so that a majority cannot crush dissenters. Montesquieu's separation of powers — dividing authority between legislature, executive and judiciary — supplies the checks and balances that stop any one branch dominating. Without these liberal safeguards, elections alone can produce what scholars call an 'illiberal democracy' or competitive authoritarianism.

### Threats: apathy, misinformation, backsliding

Democracies decay from within as well as from coups. Voter apathy — low turnout and disengagement — weakens the claim that governments speak for the people. Misinformation, spread rapidly online, distorts the informed debate democracy needs. Democratic backsliding describes the gradual erosion of checks and freedoms by elected leaders themselves, for example by capturing courts or intimidating the press. Each threat is hard to measure precisely, but political scientists track them through election data, media-freedom rankings and the health of institutions.

**Key term — liberal democracy:** A liberal democracy combines free and fair elections with protected individual freedoms, the rule of law and limits on government power.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Features of Liberal Democracy">
  Name three features of a liberal democracy beyond holding elections.

  The rule of law, a free press and protected minority rights (also acceptable: independent judiciary, separation of powers).

  **Answer:** The rule of law, a free press and protected minority rights (also acceptable: independent judiciary, separation of powers).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **'Democracy just means elections'** Elections are necessary but not sufficient. Without the rule of law, a free press and minority rights, elections can be won by leaders who then dismantle democracy itself.
  - **'Athens was a perfect democracy'** Athenian direct democracy excluded women, enslaved people and resident foreigners from citizenship. It is a useful historical example, not a model to romanticise.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A country holds regular elections but jails opposition journalists. Is it a liberal democracy? Explain." hint="Elections plus what else?">
    No — jailing journalists violates press freedom and the rule of law, so it lacks the liberal safeguards elections alone cannot provide.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What did Montesquieu contribute to democratic theory?" hint="Think about dividing government.">
    The separation of powers — dividing government into legislature, executive and judiciary to prevent unchecked power.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How did Athenian democracy differ from modern representative democracy?" hint="Who voted, and on what?">
    In Athens eligible citizens voted directly on laws themselves; in modern systems citizens elect representatives to decide on their behalf.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Define democratic backsliding." hint="Erosion from within.">
    The gradual weakening of democratic checks and freedoms by elected leaders themselves, rather than by coup.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
