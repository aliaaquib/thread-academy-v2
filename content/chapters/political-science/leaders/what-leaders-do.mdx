Leaders are the people who help groups make decisions and get things done. From the class monitor to the prime minister, leaders guide, organise and take responsibility for others. In this chapter you will meet different kinds of leaders, see how they are chosen, and learn what makes a leader truly good.

This lesson focuses on What Leaders Do: describes how leaders make decisions, look after people and take responsibility.

<Definition term="What Leaders Do">
  Describes how leaders make decisions, look after people and take responsibility.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### What leaders do

Leaders make plans, solve disagreements and look after the people they lead. A headteacher organises the school, a team captain encourages players during a match, and a prime minister leads the country's government. Good leaders also listen, because decisions work best when they reflect what the group needs.

### Leaders come in many sizes

Leadership exists at every level of life. A class rep speaks for thirty pupils, a mayor looks after a town or city, and a prime minister leads the whole country. Each leader has different responsibilities, but all of them are trusted to make fair choices for the people they serve.

**Key term — leader:** A leader is someone who guides a group, makes decisions on its behalf and takes responsibility when things go well or badly.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Leaders Do">
  What is the main job of a leader?

  To guide a group, make decisions for it and take responsibility.

  **Answer:** To guide a group, make decisions for it and take responsibility.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **'The loudest person should lead'** Being loud is not the same as being a good leader. The best leaders listen carefully and think before they act.
  - **'Leaders get to do whatever they want'** Real leaders serve the group, not themselves. A leader who only does what they want is not leading well.
</ImportantNote>
