When people disagree, voting is one of the fairest ways to decide together. Instead of shouting or fighting, everyone gets a say and the choice with the most support wins. In this chapter you will learn how voting works, why every vote counts equally, and why accepting the result matters.

This lesson focuses on What Voting Is: explains how voting lets a group choose fairly when people disagree.

<Definition term="What Voting Is">
  Explains how voting lets a group choose fairly when people disagree.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### One person, one vote

Fair voting gives every person exactly one vote, and every vote counts the same. It would not be fair if the teacher's vote counted ten times more than yours, or if some people got to vote twice. Equal votes mean the result truly reflects what the group wants.

### Secret ballots

In a secret ballot, nobody can see how you voted. This protects people from being bullied or pressured into voting a certain way. Marking a paper in private and folding it before posting it in the box is a simple example of a secret ballot.

**Key term — majority:** The majority is the larger group — when more than half of the voters agree, their choice wins.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Voting Is">
  In a vote of 25 people, 13 choose red and 12 choose blue. Which colour wins?

  Red wins, with 13 votes to 12.

  **Answer:** Red wins, with 13 votes to 12.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **'The losers should keep arguing'** Once a fair vote is decided, the group needs to move on together. Endless arguing after a fair result stops the group getting anything done.
  - **'It is fine to peek at someone's ballot'** Peeking breaks the secrecy that keeps voting free from pressure. Everyone deserves to vote without being watched.
</ImportantNote>
