## Key ideas

### Accepting the result

Every vote has a winner and a loser, and accepting the result gracefully is part of the deal. If the count was fair, the losing side should congratulate the winners and try again next time. Refusing to accept a fair result breaks the trust that makes voting work.

### One person, one vote

Fair voting gives every person exactly one vote, and every vote counts the same. It would not be fair if the teacher's vote counted ten times more than yours, or if some people got to vote twice. Equal votes mean the result truly reflects what the group wants.

**Key term — fair:** A vote is fair when everyone gets one equal vote, nobody is pressured, and the counting is honest.

<WorkedExample title="Voting for a Class Book">
  Imagine your class of 30 must choose between two books for story time: a book about dragons or a book about space. Sixteen pupils vote for dragons and fourteen vote for space. What is the result, and how should everyone behave?

  Step 1: Check the vote was fair — one vote each, counted honestly.
  Step 2: Find the majority. Sixteen is more than half of thirty, so the dragon book wins.
  Step 3: Announce the result clearly so everyone knows it was counted properly.
  Step 4: Be gracious. Space-book fans congratulate the winners, and the class enjoys the story together.

  **Answer:** The dragon book wins with sixteen votes to fourteen. Everyone should accept the fair result gracefully, knowing they will get another vote next time.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **'My vote does not matter'** Every vote counts exactly the same, and close results are decided by just a few votes. Staying out of a vote means letting others decide for you.
  - **'The losers should keep arguing'** Once a fair vote is decided, the group needs to move on together. Endless arguing after a fair result stops the group getting anything done.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should the losing side accept a fair result?" hint="Think about trust in the system.">
    Because the vote was fair, and refusing to accept it breaks trust and stops the group moving on.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your family votes on a weekend activity and your choice loses. What is the gracious thing to do?" hint="Think about next time.">
    Accept the result cheerfully and enjoy the activity, knowing there will be another vote next time.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is a secret ballot fairer than voting with a show of hands?" hint="Think about pressure from others.">
    Because nobody can be bullied or pressured when their choice is private.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one thing that would make a vote unfair." hint="Think about cheating.">
    For example, letting some people vote twice, or pressuring others to vote a certain way.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
