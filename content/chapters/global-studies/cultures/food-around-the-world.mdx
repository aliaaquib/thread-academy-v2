Culture is the way a group of people lives — their food, clothes, languages and celebrations. Around the world there are thousands of different cultures, and each one has something interesting to teach us. This chapter takes you on a tasty, colourful journey through them.

This lesson focuses on Food Around the World: explore how what people eat depends on climate, land and tradition.

<Definition term="Food Around the World">
  Explore how what people eat depends on climate, land and tradition.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Food tells us about a place

What people eat often depends on what grows nearby and the climate they live in. People in hot countries may eat lots of fruit and rice, while people in cold countries may eat warming soups and bread. Trying new foods is a way of learning about another culture.

### Clothes can show identity

Traditional clothes often show which community or country someone belongs to. Bright colours, special patterns and certain styles of hat or robe can all have meaning. Today, many people wear modern clothes every day and special clothes for celebrations.

**Key term — Culture:** The shared way of life of a group of people, including their food, clothes, language, music and traditions. Culture is passed down through families and communities.

<WorkedExample title="Connecting food to a place">
  Rice is a staple food in Japan, and bread is a staple food in France. Suggest one reason why the two countries eat different staple foods.

  Think about what 'staple food' means: the food people eat most often.
  Remember that people grow what suits their land and climate.
  Japan has lots of watery lowland fields that are perfect for growing rice.
  France has wide plains with a climate that suits growing wheat, which is made into bread.

  **Answer:** People eat what grows well in their land and climate — rice suits Japan's wet fields, while wheat for bread suits France's plains.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking everyone in a country is exactly the same** Every country has many different cultures, foods and languages inside it. People in one city can live very differently from people in another city in the same country.
  - **Believing traditional dress is all anyone wears** Many people wear modern everyday clothes and only wear traditional dress for festivals, weddings or ceremonies.
</ImportantNote>
