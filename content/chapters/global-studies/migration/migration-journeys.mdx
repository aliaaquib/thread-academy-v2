## Key ideas

### Migration changes both ends of the journey

Places that receive migrants can gain workers, new businesses, tax income and cultural richness, but may face pressure on housing and services. Places that lose people can suffer a 'brain drain' of skilled workers, yet benefit from money migrants send home, called remittances. Judging whether migration is 'good' or 'bad' depends on whose view you take.

### Push and pull explain most moves

Geographers use push factors (bad conditions at home) and pull factors (good conditions elsewhere) to explain migration. A farmer might be pushed by drought and pulled by factory jobs in a city. Real decisions are rarely simple — most migrants weigh several push and pull factors at once, plus obstacles such as cost, borders and distance.

**Key term — Migration:** The movement of people from one place to another to live, usually for a long period. Moving between countries is international migration; moving within a country is internal migration.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Migration Journeys">
  What are remittances?

  Remittances are money that migrants send home to their families in their country of origin.

  **Answer:** Remittances are money that migrants send home to their families in their country of origin.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking migration only affects the destination country** The place people leave is affected too — through lost workers, but also through remittances sent home. Always consider both ends of the journey.
  - **Using 'refugee' for every migrant** A refugee is someone fleeing war or persecution with legal protection; an economic migrant moves for better opportunities. The words describe different situations and should not be swapped.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one positive and one negative impact of migration on a receiving country." hint="Think about workers and services.">
    Positive: new workers, businesses, tax income and cultural richness. Negative: pressure on housing and public services.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a push factor and a pull factor?" hint="One drives people away, the other attracts them.">
    A push factor drives people away from a place (e.g. war, unemployment); a pull factor attracts them to a new place (e.g. jobs, safety).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a refugee?" hint="Think about fleeing danger rather than choosing to move.">
    A refugee is a person who has fled their country because of war, persecution or violence and cannot safely return.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one pull factor and one push factor." hint="Jobs and conflict are classic examples.">
    Push factor: e.g. war, unemployment, drought. Pull factor: e.g. jobs, safety, better schools.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
