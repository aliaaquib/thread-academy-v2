## Being a Global Citizen

You’re a citizen of a country — but also, unavoidably, a resident of one planet. Global citizenship is taking the second identity seriously.

<Definition term="Global citizenship">
  **Global citizenship** is the idea that our responsibilities extend beyond national borders — to human rights everywhere, to future generations, and to the planet’s shared systems.
</Definition>

## Key points

- It’s an ethical identity, not a passport — no world government issues it.
- In practice: fair-trade choices, climate action, supporting human rights, global awareness.
- Critics: it can be vague, elite, or a cover for Western agendas.
- Defence: shared problems need shared responsibility — borders don’t contain CO₂.

<Example title="Fridays for Future">
  One Swedish teenager’s school strike became a global movement of millions. No organisation, no budget — just a shared planet and social media.
</Example>
