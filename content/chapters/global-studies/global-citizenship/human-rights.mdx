## Key ideas

### Rights come with universality

Human rights are universal: they apply to everyone, everywhere, regardless of nationality, religion or gender. They were written after the Second World War to prevent the horrors of that conflict from happening again. Critics debate whether rights are truly applied equally, but the principle remains the foundation of international law on dignity and freedom.

### Some problems need every country at once

Climate change cannot be solved by one country cutting emissions while others increase theirs — greenhouse gases mix in one shared atmosphere. This is a collective action problem: everyone benefits if all cooperate, but each is tempted to let others do the work. International agreements, such as the Paris Agreement on climate, are attempts to solve it through shared commitments.

**Key term — Human rights:** Basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person simply because they are human, such as the right to life, liberty, education and freedom of speech. They were set out in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted by the United Nations in 1948.

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking global citizenship replaces national citizenship** Global citizenship is an outlook and a set of responsibilities, not a passport. People remain citizens of their countries while also acting as members of a wider human community.
  - **Treating the SDGs as legally binding law** The Sustainable Development Goals are political commitments agreed at the UN, not enforceable laws. Countries report on progress, but no court punishes failure to meet them.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="How many Sustainable Development Goals are there, and when were they adopted?" hint="The number is in the teens; the year was 2015.">
    There are 17 SDGs, adopted by the UN in 2015.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a global citizen?" hint="It is about seeing yourself as part of a worldwide community.">
    A global citizen is someone who sees themselves as part of a worldwide community and takes responsibility for global issues through their actions.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is climate change a collective action problem?" hint="Think about one shared atmosphere.">
    Because greenhouse gases mix in one shared atmosphere, no single country's cuts are enough — every country must act, but each is tempted to let others do the work.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two ways an individual can act as a global citizen." hint="Think about campaigns, volunteering or informed choices.">
    Any two of: campaigning, volunteering, fundraising, ethical consumption, informed voting, staying informed about world events.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
