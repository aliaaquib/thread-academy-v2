## Key ideas

### Help can travel far

People in one country can help people in another. After a flood or earthquake, charities and governments send food, water and medicine to the people affected. This is called international aid, and it shows that the world can work together in hard times.

### Giving helps both sides

When you help someone, they get the help they need — and you feel good too. Scientists have found that doing kind acts can make people happier. Giving your time can be just as valuable as giving money.

**Key term — Aid:** Help given to people in serious need, especially after a disaster. Aid can be food, clean water, shelter or medicine.

<WorkedExample title="Planning a charity collection">
  Your class wants to collect food for a local food bank. List the steps you would take to organise it well.

  Find out which foods the food bank needs most, such as tins, rice and pasta.
  Ask your teacher's permission and agree on a collection week.
  Make posters telling everyone what to bring and where to leave it.
  Collect the food, check it is in date, and pack it into boxes.
  Deliver the boxes to the food bank or arrange for them to be collected.

  **Answer:** A good plan is: find out what is needed, get permission, tell people clearly, collect and check the food, then deliver it to the food bank.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming people who need help are all the same** People need help for many different reasons — a disaster, illness or difficult circumstances. Everyone deserves respect and dignity when they are helped.
  - **Thinking only money counts as helping** Giving your time, skills or even a kind word can help just as much as money. Volunteering is a powerful form of help.
</ImportantNote>
