## Key ideas

### Technology made distance smaller

Container ships, cheap air travel and the internet made it fast and cheap to move goods, people and ideas around the world. A message that once took weeks by ship now arrives instantly. This shrinking of distance is often called time–space compression, and it is one of the main causes of globalisation.

### Cultures mix — and sometimes resist

Films, music, brands and food now travel the world, so teenagers in different countries often share tastes. Some people welcome this sharing; others worry it weakens local traditions and languages. In response, many communities actively protect and celebrate their own culture, a trend sometimes called glocalisation — global ideas adapted to local tastes.

**Key term — Globalisation:** The process by which businesses, ideas, cultures and people become increasingly connected across the world, mainly through trade, technology and communication.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Technology and Connection">
  Name two technologies that helped globalisation speed up.

  Container shipping (and cheap air travel) and the internet (and mobile phones).

  **Answer:** Container shipping (and cheap air travel) and the internet (and mobile phones).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying globalisation is just about trade** Trade is a big part, but globalisation also covers culture, technology, migration and politics. Limiting it to trade misses most of the story.
  - **Assuming globalisation helps everyone equally** Globalisation creates winners and losers: consumers may get cheaper goods while some workers lose jobs or face poor conditions. Always ask 'who benefits and who loses?'.
</ImportantNote>
