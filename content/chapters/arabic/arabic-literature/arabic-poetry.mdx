## Key ideas

### Literature as social witness

Modern Arabic literature repeatedly holds a mirror to society. Mahfouz's Cairo Trilogy (الثلاثية) traces an Egyptian family across three generations of political upheaval. Palestinian poet Mahmoud Darwish (محمود درويش, 1941–2008) made exile and identity his great subjects. At A-level, always ask of a text: what does this reveal about its society — and what does it demand of its reader?

### The qasida: architecture in verse

The classical qaṣīda is an engineering marvel: dozens of lines, one rhyme (قافية, qāfiya) running through the entire poem, and a strict metre (بحر, baḥr) counted in syllable patterns. Imru' al-Qais (امرؤ القيس), author of the most celebrated Muʿallaqa, opens standing at the ruins of his beloved's campsite — the وقوف على الأطلال (wuqūf ʿalā al-aṭlāl, 'halting at the ruins'), a scene poets imitated for a thousand years. When you analyse a qaṣīda, always name the rhyme and metre first: form is meaning.

**Key term — قصيدة (qaṣīda) — ode / poem:** The classical Arabic poem: a long, single-rhyme composition. The pre-Islamic المعلقات (al-muʿallaqāt, the 'Suspended Odes') are the most famous early examples.

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating the Nights as one author's book** ألف ليلة وليلة grew over centuries from Persian, Indian and Arabic story traditions — it has compilers and storytellers, not a single author.
  - **Assuming film dialogue equals Modern Standard Arabic** Egyptian films speak Egyptian dialect, not MSA. Enjoy the cinema, but hear the difference: film dialogue uses مش (mish, not) where MSA uses ليس.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Who tells the nightly stories in the Thousand and One Nights, and to whom?" hint="Her name begins with Sh; he is King Shahryar.">
    Shahrazad (شهرزاد) tells them to King Shahryar (شهريار), to delay her execution.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which Arab writer won the Nobel Prize in Literature, and in which year?" hint="An Egyptian novelist, author of the Cairo Trilogy.">
    Naguib Mahfouz (نجيب محفوظ), in 1988 — the first Arab laureate.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the two formal features you should identify first when analysing a qaṣīda." hint="Rhyme and metre.">
    The rhyme (قافية, qāfiya) and the metre (بحر, baḥr).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does Shahrazad always stop her story at dawn?" hint="Think about what the king would do if the story ended.">
    To leave him in suspense so he spares her for one more night — storytelling as survival.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
