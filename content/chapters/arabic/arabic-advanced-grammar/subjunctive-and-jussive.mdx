<Definition term="Subjunctive and Jussive">
  What أن، لن and لم do to a verb's ending.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Moods: the verb's final vowel tells a story

After أن (an, that / to) and لن (lan, will not), the present verb takes the subjunctive with fatḥa: أريد أن أذهبَ (urīdu an adhaba, I want to go), لن يذهبَ (lan yadhaba, he will not go). After لم (lam, did not), it takes the jussive with sukūn: لم يذهبْ (lam yadhhab, he did not go). Using the indicative يذهبُ after أن or لم is one of the most penalised errors at A-level — the particle dictates the ending, always.

### The three cases

Arabic nouns wear their grammatical role as a vowel ending. Rafʿ (nominative, ـُ) marks the subject: الطالبُ يكتب (aṭ-ṭālibu yaktubu, the student writes). Naṣb (accusative, ـَ) marks the object: رأيت الطالبَ (raʾaytu aṭ-ṭāliba, I saw the student). Jarr (genitive, ـِ) follows prepositions and the second term of an iḍāfa: في البيتِ (fī al-bayti, in the house), باب البيتِ (bāb al-bayti, the door of the house). Duals and sound plurals change letters instead of vowels: الطالبان (aṭ-ṭālibān) in rafʿ becomes الطالبين (aṭ-ṭālibayn) in naṣb and jarr. In speech the endings often vanish, but in formal writing and exams they are the whole game.

**Key term — الجزم (al-jazm) — the jussive mood:** The shortened verb form triggered by لم and similar particles: لم يذهب (lam yadhhab, he did not go) — note the sukūn, not the usual ḍamma.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Subjunctive and Jussive">
  Put لم يذهب into English and name the mood of يذهب.

  'He did not go'; يذهبْ is jussive (مجزوم), marked by the sukūn.

  **Answer:** 'He did not go'; يذهبْ is jussive (مجزوم), marked by the sukūn.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using the indicative after أن** أريد أن يذهبُ is wrong; أن demands the subjunctive: أريد أن يذهبَ (urīdu an yadhaba, I want him to go).
  - **Putting tanwīn on a definite noun** الكتابٌ is impossible — ال and tanwīn never co-exist. It is either كتابٌ (kitābun, a book) or الكتابُ (al-kitābu, the book).
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Correct the sentence: أريد أن يذهبُ إلى المدرسة." hint="أن demands which mood?">
    أريد أن يذهبَ إلى المدرسة (urīdu an yadhaba ilā al-madrasa) — subjunctive with fatḥa.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="In رأيت المعلمَ, why does المعلم carry a fatḥa?" hint="What role does the teacher play — doer or receiver?">
    It is the direct object (مفعول به), in the naṣb (accusative) case — hence المعلمَ (raʾaytu al-muʿallima, I saw the teacher).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the passive of كتب الدرس (he wrote the lesson)?" hint="كُتب + nominative subject of the passive.">
    كُتب الدرسُ (kutiba ad-darsu) — 'the lesson was written'.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Translate 'the door of my friend's house' using an iḍāfa chain." hint="باب + بيت + صديقي.">
    باب بيت صديقي (bāb bayt ṣadīqī).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
