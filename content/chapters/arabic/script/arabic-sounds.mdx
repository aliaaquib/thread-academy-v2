## Arabic Sounds

Arabic has sounds English lacks — the ‘ayn (ع) from deep in the throat, emphatic consonants (ص ض ط ظ), the qaf (ق). They’re physical skills: train them like muscles.

<Definition term="Emphatic consonants">
  **Emphatic** consonants (ص ض ط ظ) are pronounced with the tongue root pulled back, darkening neighbouring vowels. س (seen) vs ص (sad): same tongue tip, totally different resonance.
</Definition>

## Key points

- ع (‘ayn): a voiced constriction deep in the throat — practice with ‘ah’ squeezed.
- ح (ha): breathy, like fogging a mirror.
- ق (qaf): a ‘k’ made far back at the uvula.
- ض (dad): the language’s namesake — لغة الضاد, ‘the language of the dad’.

<Example title="Minimal pairs">
  سين (seen = China/porcelain?) vs صين (ṣeen): emphatic vs plain changes meaning. Getting these right isn’t accent — it’s meaning.
</Example>

## Practice

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is Arabic called ‘the language of the dad’?" hint="ض.">

  </PracticeItem>
