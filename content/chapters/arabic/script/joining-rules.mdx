## Key ideas

### Four shapes, one letter

Every Arabic letter has up to four forms: isolated, initial (joined on the right), medial (joined on both sides) and final (joined on the right). The letter ع, for example, is a neat loop on its own, grows a tooth at the start of a word, stretches both ways in the middle and curls off at the end, as in علم (ʿilm, knowledge). It is always the same letter with the same sound — only its outfit changes. Handwriting links letters with a smooth baseline stroke, while print builds the joins into the typeface.

### Sun letters swallow the ل

The 14 'sun' letters — ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن — assimilate the ل of ال: it is written but not pronounced, and the sun letter is doubled. So 'the sun' is الشمس, pronounced 'ash-shams' (never 'al-shams'), and 'the light' is النور, pronounced 'an-nūr'. The 14 'moon' letters — ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي — leave the ل alone: القمر stays 'al-qamar' (the moon) and الكتاب stays 'al-kitāb' (the book).

**Key term — همزة (hamza) — glottal stop:** The letter ء, a catch in the throat like the pause in 'uh-oh'. It rides on different seats — أ، إ، ؤ، ئ — chosen by the vowels around it.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Joining Rules">
  Why is there a gap in the middle of the word أولاد?

  و is one of the six non-joiners, so it never connects to the ل after it: أولاد (awlād, boys).

  **Answer:** و is one of the six non-joiners, so it never connects to the ل after it: أولاد (awlād, boys).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Joining letters after the six non-joiners** In وردة (warda, rose), none of و، ر or د join to the letter after them, so every letter stands separate. Forcing joins produces an unreadable scribble.
  - **Pronouncing ال before sun letters** الشاي is 'ash-shāy' (the tea), not 'al-shāy'. Before ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن, drop the 'l' and double the next letter.
</ImportantNote>
