## Key ideas

### Greetings for the time of day

In the morning say صباح الخير (ṣabāḥ al-khayr, good morning) and in the evening مساء الخير (masāʾ al-khayr, good evening). The replies are just as pretty: صباح النور (ṣabāḥ an-nūr, morning of light) and مساء النور (masāʾ an-nūr, evening of light). Listen for which one people use and echo the pattern.

### Talking to boys and girls

Arabic often changes its endings for males and females. 'How are you?' is كيف حالك؟ (kayfa ḥāluk?) to a boy but كيف حالكِ؟ (kayfa ḥāluki?) to a girl. The same happens with 'what is your name?': ما اسمك؟ (mā ismuk?) for a boy, ما اسمكِ؟ (mā ismuki?) for a girl. Matching the ending shows real care.

**Key term — مع السلامة (maʿa as-salāma) — goodbye:** Literally 'with safety' — the kind way to say farewell, wishing the other person a safe journey.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Saying Goodbye">
  How do you say goodbye to a friend who is going home?

  مع السلامة (maʿa as-salāma).

  **Answer:** مع السلامة (maʿa as-salāma).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying مع السلامة when you arrive** مع السلامة is only for leaving. When you arrive, say السلام عليكم or مرحبا.
  - **Replying to السلام عليكم with السلام عليكم** The matching reply is وعليكم السلام (wa ʿalaykum as-salām) — 'and peace be upon you too'. Repeating the greeting back sounds unfinished.
</ImportantNote>
