## Key ideas

### The guest is sacred

Hospitality (ḍiyāfa) is a point of honour: a guest is offered Arabic coffee (قهوة عربية) in small cups, with sweet dates, before any business is discussed — refusing outright can offend. Receive and give with the right hand; the left is considered unclean. Greetings are physical and warm: handshakes, and among friends a cheek-to-cheek greeting. These rituals are not extras — they are the grammar of social life.

### The year turns on Ramadan

During Ramadan, Muslims fast from the dawn prayer (الفجر) to the sunset prayer (المغرب), then break the fast with إفطار (ifṭār), traditionally beginning with dates. Nights come alive with family gatherings and the تراويح (tarāwīḥ) prayers. The month ends with عيد الفطر, and about two months later comes عيد الأضحى (ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā), the Festival of Sacrifice coinciding with the Hajj pilgrimage. Wish people رمضان كريم (Ramaḍān karīm, generous Ramadan) during the month and عيد مبارك (ʿĪd mubārak, blessed Eid) at the festivals — never the reverse.

**Key term — ضيافة (ḍiyāfa) — hospitality:** The celebrated Arab virtue of honouring guests. Offering قهوة (qahwa, coffee) and تمر (tamr, dates) to a visitor is its classic expression.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Hospitality Customs">
  Your host offers you coffee. Which hand do you use to accept, and why?

  The right hand — using the left is considered disrespectful in Arab etiquette.

  **Answer:** The right hand — using the left is considered disrespectful in Arab etiquette.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying رمضان كريم on Eid day** رمضان كريم belongs to Ramadan itself. On Eid, say عيد مبارك.
  - **Offering or taking with the left hand** Always use the right hand for giving, receiving and eating — the left hand is considered unclean in Arab etiquette.
</ImportantNote>
