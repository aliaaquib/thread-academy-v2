## What Is an Ecosystem?

Every living thing exists in context. A fox is shaped by the rabbits it hunts, the winter cold it endures, and the soil its den is dug into. An ecosystem captures all of this at once: the community of organisms plus the air, water, soil and sunlight they depend on.

<Definition term="Ecosystem">
  An **ecosystem** is a community of living organisms (biotic factors) interacting with the non-living (abiotic) components of their environment — air, water, soil, light and temperature — as a functioning unit.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Energy flows one way through ecosystems: sunlight → producers → consumers → decomposers.
- Matter cycles: carbon, nitrogen and water are reused endlessly.
- Abiotic factors like temperature and rainfall decide which organisms can survive where.
- Ecosystems can be tiny (a puddle) or vast (the Amazon basin).

<Example title="A rock pool at low tide">
  Algae (producers) use sunlight to grow. Periwinkles graze the algae, crabs eat the periwinkles. When organisms die, bacteria decompose them, returning nutrients to the water. Sunlight, salinity and temperature — the abiotic factors — set the rules for who can live there.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two biotic and two abiotic factors in a forest ecosystem." hint="Biotic = living; abiotic = non-living.">
    Biotic: trees, deer (or fungi, insects). Abiotic: sunlight, rainfall (or soil pH, temperature).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does energy flow but matter cycle in an ecosystem?" hint="Think about what happens to energy at each feeding level.">
    Energy is lost as heat at each transfer and can’t be reused; atoms of carbon, nitrogen and water are passed on and recycled by decomposers.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - An ecosystem = a community of organisms + their non-living environment.
  - Biotic factors are living; abiotic factors are physical/chemical.
  - Energy flows through ecosystems; matter cycles within them.
  - Abiotic conditions determine which organisms can survive.
</Summary>
