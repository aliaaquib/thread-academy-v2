## Key ideas

### Renewable does not mean unlimited

Timber is renewable only if trees are replanted and felling stays within regrowth rates; overfished seas collapse because fish are taken faster than they breed. Fossil fuels, by contrast, took millions of years to form and are non-renewable on any human timescale.

### The circular economy designs out waste

In a linear economy we take resources, make products and throw them away. A circular economy designs products for repair, reuse and recycling, so materials loop round again. A phone built for easy repair, for example, stays in use for years instead of becoming e-waste.

**Key term — Renewable resource:** A resource that regrows or refills on a human timescale, such as timber, fresh water or solar energy, provided it is not used faster than it renews.

<WorkedExample title="Classifying resources">
  Classify each as renewable or non-renewable, with reasoning: sunlight, coal, timber, natural gas, wind, fish stocks.

  Sunlight and wind: renewed continuously by nature, so renewable.
  Coal and natural gas: formed over millions of years and being used far faster than they form, so non-renewable.
  Timber: renewable only if forests are replanted and harvested within regrowth rates.
  Fish stocks: renewable only if fishing stays within breeding rates; overfishing makes them collapse.

  **Answer:** Renewable: sunlight, wind, and timber and fish stocks when harvested sustainably. Non-renewable: coal and natural gas. Renewables still need managing within their renewal rates.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling all natural resources renewable** Fossil fuels and minerals do not renew on human timescales. Only resources that regrow or refill, and are harvested within those rates, count as renewable.
  - **Thinking sustainability is only about the environment** Sustainability has three pillars: environmental, social and economic. A solution must work for people and livelihoods as well as nature.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why fish stocks can be renewable yet still collapse." hint="Compare the rate of catching with the rate of breeding.">
    Fish breed and replace themselves, so they are renewable. But if fishing removes them faster than they breed, the stock shrinks each year and can collapse.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does the circular economy differ from the linear economy?" hint="Follow a product from raw material to the end of its life in each model.">
    The linear economy takes resources, makes products and throws them away. The circular economy designs products for repair, reuse and recycling so materials keep circulating and waste is designed out.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A company calls its plastic bottle sustainable because it is recyclable. Give one reason this claim may be weak." hint="Think about what happens before and after the bottle is recycled.">
    Recycling still uses energy and transport, and many bottles are never actually recycled. Reducing single-use bottles or refilling would be more sustainable than relying on recycling alone.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Define sustainability in your own words." hint="Think about the needs of people living a hundred years from now.">
    Meeting our own needs today without damaging the ability of future generations to meet theirs, balancing environmental, social and economic goals.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
