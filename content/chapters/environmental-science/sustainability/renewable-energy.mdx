## Renewable Energy

Every joule of fossil fuel is ancient sunlight, spent once. Renewables harvest today’s sunlight, wind and water instead.

<Definition term="Renewable energy">
  **Renewable energy** comes from sources that are naturally replenished — sunlight, wind, flowing water, Earth’s heat and biomass — unlike fossil fuels, which take millions of years to form.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Solar: photovoltaic cells convert light directly to electricity.
- Wind: turbines turn moving air into rotation, then electricity.
- Hydro: falling water spins turbines — reliable but site-limited.
- Challenge: intermittency — the sun and wind don’t follow demand, so storage matters.

<Example title="A windy night problem">
  A wind farm produces most at 3am when demand is lowest. Solutions: batteries, pumped-hydro storage, interconnectors to other grids, and demand that shifts to match supply.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is hydroelectric power reliable but hard to expand?" hint="Think about what a dam needs.">
    It works day and night, but needs suitable valleys/rivers — most good sites are already used and dams flood land.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage and one disadvantage of solar power." hint="Fuel vs weather.">
    Advantage: no fuel costs or CO₂ while operating. Disadvantage: only generates in daylight and varies with weather.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Renewables harvest ongoing natural flows, not finite stocks.
  - Solar, wind and hydro dominate new electricity worldwide.
  - Intermittency is the main engineering challenge.
  - Storage and smarter grids are the fix.
</Summary>
