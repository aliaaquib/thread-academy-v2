## Key ideas

### International climate action built up in stages

The 1992 Rio Earth Summit created the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. The 1997 Kyoto Protocol then set binding targets, but only for developed countries. The 2015 Paris Agreement brought nearly all nations together under self-set pledges, aiming to hold warming well below 2 degrees Celsius and pursue 1.5 degrees.

### Paris relies on pledges plus peer pressure

Each country submits a Nationally Determined Contribution and must report its progress transparently. Every few years a global stocktake reviews collective progress and countries are expected to raise their ambition. The system has no world police force; its power comes from transparency, diplomacy and public pressure.

**Key term — Nationally Determined Contribution:** A country's self-set climate pledge under the Paris Agreement, stating how much it will cut emissions. Countries are expected to strengthen these pledges over time.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Paris Agreement">

  To hold warming well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees.

  **Answer:** To hold warming well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking the Paris Agreement sets one binding target for every country** Paris uses self-set Nationally Determined Contributions, not identical binding targets. Countries choose their pledges and are expected to strengthen them over time.
  - **Confusing mitigation with adaptation** Mitigation tackles the cause by cutting emissions; adaptation tackles the consequences by coping with change. Most national plans need both.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain the difference between a carbon tax and cap-and-trade." hint="One fixes the price, the other fixes the quantity.">
    A carbon tax sets a fixed price per tonne of emissions and lets the quantity of emissions vary. Cap-and-trade sets a fixed total limit on emissions and lets the permit price vary through trading.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two reasons why international cooperation is essential for climate policy." hint="Think about where emissions go and where clean technology comes from.">
    Greenhouse gases mix globally, so one country's cuts are undermined if others keep polluting. Also, poorer countries need finance and technology from richer ones to develop cleanly.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between mitigation and adaptation? Give one example of each." hint="One attacks the cause, the other copes with the effects.">
    Mitigation reduces emissions or removes carbon, for example switching to wind power. Adaptation adjusts to change already happening, for example building flood defences.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why did the Kyoto Protocol only set binding targets for developed countries?" hint="Think about which countries had produced most historical emissions.">
    Developed countries had produced the bulk of historical emissions and had greater wealth and technology, so the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities placed the first binding duties on them.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
