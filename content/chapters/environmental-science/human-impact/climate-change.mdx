## Climate Change

Earth’s climate has always changed — but never this fast, and never with a single species as the cause.

<Definition term="Greenhouse effect">
  The **greenhouse effect** is the warming of Earth’s surface by gases (CO₂, methane, water vapour) that let sunlight in but trap outgoing heat — like glass in a greenhouse. Humans have intensified it by burning fossil fuels.
</Definition>

## Key points

- CO₂ levels are higher than at any point in 800,000+ years of ice-core records.
- Global average temperature has risen about 1.2°C since pre-industrial times.
- Consequences: sea-level rise, extreme weather, ocean acidification, shifting habitats.
- Feedback loops (melting ice, thawing permafrost) can accelerate warming.

<Example title="The Keeling Curve">
  Since 1958, measurements at Mauna Loa show CO₂ rising year after year, with a seasonal “breathing” as northern forests grow and shed leaves. It’s the clearest fingerprint of human influence.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish the natural greenhouse effect from the enhanced greenhouse effect." hint="One keeps Earth habitable; the other is our doing.">
    The natural effect keeps Earth about 33°C warmer than it would be; the enhanced effect is the extra warming from human-added greenhouse gases.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two pieces of evidence that the climate is changing." hint="Think ice, seas, thermometers.">
    Any two: rising global temperatures, shrinking glaciers/ice sheets, sea-level rise, ocean acidification, shifting species ranges.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Greenhouse gases trap heat; humans have added far more of them.
  - Ice cores, thermometers and satellites all show rapid warming.
  - Impacts include sea-level rise and extreme weather.
  - Feedback loops make early action important.
</Summary>
