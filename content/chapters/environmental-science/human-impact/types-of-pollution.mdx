## Key ideas

### Pollution takes three main forms

Air pollution comes from vehicles, power stations and industry; water pollution from sewage, farm fertilisers and oil spills; land pollution from litter, landfill and chemicals. All three can travel far from their source: winds carry smog across countries and rivers carry waste to the sea.

### Wildlife faces multiple pressures at once

Habitat destruction removes the places species live, pollution poisons them, overhunting and overfishing reduce their numbers, and climate change shifts the conditions they are adapted to. Species under several pressures at once are the most likely to decline.

**Key term — Pollutant:** A substance released into the environment that causes harm, such as smoke, sewage, pesticides or plastic waste.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Types of Pollution">
  Give one example each of air, water and land pollution, naming the source.

  Air: exhaust fumes from vehicles. Water: fertiliser runoff from farms. Land: chemicals leaking from landfill sites.

  **Answer:** Air: exhaust fumes from vehicles. Water: fertiliser runoff from farms. Land: chemicals leaking from landfill sites.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking pollution only harms the place it is released** Pollutants travel. Acid rain can fall far from the power station that caused it, and ocean currents spread plastic worldwide.
  - **Mixing up the greenhouse effect and the ozone hole** They are different problems. The greenhouse effect traps heat; the ozone hole, caused by CFC chemicals, lets in more ultraviolet light. Fixing one does not fix the other.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A new road is planned through a woodland. List two likely environmental impacts." hint="Think about the trees removed and the vehicles that will use the road.">
    The woodland habitat is fragmented or destroyed, harming wildlife. Vehicle exhaust adds air pollution and noise, and runoff can pollute nearby streams.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does acid rain form, and what damage does it cause?" hint="Start with burning fossil fuels.">
    Burning fossil fuels releases sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which dissolve in rainwater making it acidic. Acid rain damages tree leaves, acidifies lakes killing fish, and erodes stone buildings.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two greenhouse gases and one human source of each." hint="One comes from burning fuels; the other from farming and waste.">
    Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels such as coal and petrol. Methane from cattle farming, rice paddies and rotting waste in landfill.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is habitat destruction especially dangerous when combined with pollution?" hint="Think about how a species copes when it faces two threats at once.">
    Habitat loss shrinks populations and pollution weakens the survivors. A small, stressed population is far less able to recover, so the two threats multiply each other's harm.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
