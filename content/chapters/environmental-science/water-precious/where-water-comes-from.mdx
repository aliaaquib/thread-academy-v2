Turn on a tap and clean water flows out as if by magic, but that water has travelled a long way. It may have fallen as rain on hills, flowed down rivers and been cleaned before reaching you. In this chapter you will discover why water is precious and how to look after it.

This lesson focuses on Where Water Comes From: trace your tap water back to rivers, reservoirs and rain.

<Definition term="Where Water Comes From">
  Trace your tap water back to rivers, reservoirs and rain.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Water is always on the move

The Sun heats seas and lakes, water evaporates and rises, cools into clouds, and falls as rain. Rivers carry it back to the sea and the cycle begins again. No new water is being made, so we share the same water the dinosaurs drank.

### Clean water needs protection

Litter, oil, chemicals and sewage can all pollute rivers and seas, harming fish and making water unsafe. Never pour paint or oil down drains, and always put rubbish in bins so it cannot wash into waterways.

**Key term — Condensation:** When water vapour cools and turns back into tiny liquid droplets, forming clouds and mist. This is the opposite of evaporation.

<WorkedExample title="Ordering the water cycle">
  Put these events in order: rain falls on hills, water vapour cools into clouds, rivers carry water to the sea, the Sun warms the sea.

  Start with the energy source: the Sun warms the sea.
  Warm water evaporates into invisible water vapour that rises.
  High up, the vapour cools and condenses into clouds.
  Water falls as rain on the hills, then rivers carry it back to the sea.

  **Answer:** The Sun warms the sea → water evaporates and rises → vapour cools and condenses into clouds → rain falls on hills → rivers carry water back to the sea.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing all water is safe to drink** Only treated tap water is reliably safe. River, pond and sea water can contain germs and pollution, so never drink them.
  - **Thinking water is used up when we drink it** Water is never used up; it moves through the water cycle and is reused endlessly. What matters is keeping it clean and not wasting the clean supply.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Where does your tap water come from? Describe its journey in two or three steps." hint="Start with rain and end at your tap.">
    Rain falls on hills and gathers in rivers or reservoirs. It is piped to a treatment works where it is cleaned, then travels through underground pipes to homes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the four main stages of the water cycle in order." hint="Start with the Sun heating water.">
    Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection as rivers carry water back to the sea.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is only a small share of Earth's water available for us to drink?" hint="Think about what most of Earth's water is like.">
    About 97 percent is salty seawater, and much fresh water is frozen in ice. Only a tiny share sits in rivers and lakes where we can easily reach it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How does litter in the street end up harming river wildlife?" hint="Think about what rain does to rubbish on pavements.">
    Rain washes litter into drains and streams, and it flows into rivers. Plastic and chemicals there can poison or trap fish, birds and other wildlife.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
