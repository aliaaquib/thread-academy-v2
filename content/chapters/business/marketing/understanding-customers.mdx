## Key ideas

### The customer is king

Marketing starts from customer needs, not from the product: identify the target market, understand its wants, then design the offer around them. Businesses that ignore customers — making what they want to make — usually fail.

### Research reduces risk

Primary research (questionnaires, focus groups) gives fresh, specific data but costs time and money; secondary research (government statistics, competitor websites) is cheap but may be outdated. Samples must be representative or results mislead.

**Key term — market research:** Gathering information about customers and competitors, through primary methods (surveys, interviews) or secondary sources (existing data).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Understanding Customers">
  Why must a sample be representative?

  Because an unrepresentative sample gives results that do not reflect the true target market, leading to bad decisions.

  **Answer:** Because an unrepresentative sample gives results that do not reflect the true target market, leading to bad decisions.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Starting with the product, not the customer** Build what customers want, not what you want to make — research first.
  - **Trusting a tiny survey** Asking ten friends is not representative; biased samples produce misleading conclusions.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How does a strong brand add value?" hint="Think about price.">
    Loyal customers pay premium prices, so the brand lets the firm charge more than production cost alone would justify.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A firm targets wealthy over-50s with a luxury watch. Suggest a suitable 'promotion' method." hint="Think about where they look.">
    Any suitable method, e.g. adverts in quality newspapers or sponsorship of golf events — it must reach wealthy over-50s.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the 4Ps of the marketing mix." hint="Product…">
    Product, price, place, promotion.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between primary and secondary research?" hint="New data vs existing data.">
    Primary research collects new data first-hand (surveys, interviews); secondary research uses existing data (statistics, reports).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
