</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Spotting opportunities

Good ideas solve problems: classmates forget pencils, neighbours' cars get dirty, parents want birthday cakes. Entrepreneurs notice frustrations and imagine a product or service that fixes them — then check whether people will actually pay.

### Profit keeps you going

Revenue is exciting, but profit is what matters. If each cake costs 40p to make and sells for £1, profit is 60p per cake. Track every cost — ingredients, cups, even your time — or a 'successful' stall can secretly lose money.

**Key term — customer:** A person who buys your product or service. Without customers there is no business — only a hobby that costs money.

<WorkedExample title="Lila's friendship bracelets">
  Lila sells bracelets at £2 each. Thread costs £8 for enough to make 20 bracelets. She sells 15 bracelets. What is her profit?

  Revenue: 15 × £2 = £30.
  Cost per bracelet: £8 ÷ 20 = 40p.
  Total cost for 15 sold: 15 × 40p = £6.
  Profit: £30 − £6 = £24.

  **Answer:** Lila makes £24 profit. The 5 unsold bracelets are stock she can sell later.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting hidden costs** Your time, travel and packaging are real costs — include them or your profit is exaggerated.
  - **Pricing too low to be popular** Very low prices attract buyers but may leave no profit; the price must cover costs with something left over.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A stall makes £40 revenue and £25 profit. What were its costs?" hint="Rearrange profit = revenue − costs.">
    £15 (£40 − £25).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between revenue and profit?" hint="Think about costs.">
    Revenue is total sales income; profit is revenue minus costs.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Tom sells 10 cakes at £1.50 each. Ingredients cost £5. What is his profit?" hint="Revenue first, then subtract.">
    Revenue £15, profit £15 − £5 = £10.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one quality of a good entrepreneur." hint="Think about spotting chances.">
    Any one of: spots opportunities, works hard, takes calculated risks, is creative, persists after setbacks.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
