## Key ideas

### Ownership and liability

A sole trader is simple to set up but the owner is personally liable for debts — their house could be at risk. A private limited company (Ltd) is a separate legal person, so shareholders' liability is limited to their investment, making it easier to raise finance but costlier to administer.

### Sectors of industry

Primary businesses extract raw materials (farming, mining), secondary businesses manufacture goods, and tertiary businesses provide services. The UK economy is dominated by the tertiary sector, and many firms span sectors — a bakery buys primary wheat and sells tertiary café service.

**Key term — added value:** The difference between the selling price and the cost of bought-in materials. Businesses aim to add value at each stage.

<WorkedExample title="Sole trader or Ltd?">
  Amara is starting a jewellery business, risking £5,000 of savings on stock and equipment. Advise her on the best ownership type.

  Sole trader: simple, cheap, full control — but unlimited liability puts her £5,000 and more at risk.
  Ltd: limited liability protects her personal assets, but costs more to set up and run.
  The financial risk is significant for one person.
  Recommendation: form a Ltd company for protection, despite the extra paperwork.

  **Answer:** A private limited company is safer given the £5,000 at risk — limited liability outweighs the extra admin cost.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking profit is the only objective** Survival, growth and social aims often dominate — especially for new firms and social enterprises.
  - **Believing limited liability means no risk** Shareholders can still lose their whole investment, and lenders may demand personal guarantees.
</ImportantNote>
