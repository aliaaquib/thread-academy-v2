Enterprise is the spark of the economy: someone spots an opportunity and takes the risk to pursue it. This chapter explores how opportunities are found, what risk really means, which qualities help, and how business plans organise it all.

This lesson focuses on Spotting Opportunities: learn to see gaps in the market.

<Definition term="Spotting Opportunities">
  Learn to see gaps in the market.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Where opportunities hide

Opportunities come from changing tastes, new technology, gaps in service, or personal frustration. Observing customers, brainstorming and studying competitors reveal them — but an idea is only an opportunity if customers will pay.

### Planning for success

A business plan covers the idea, market research, marketing mix, operations and financial forecasts such as cash-flow and break-even. Writing it forces hard thinking; banks and investors demand it before lending.

**Key term — opportunity:** A gap in the market — an unmet need or a better way of meeting one — that a business could profitably fill.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Spotting Opportunities">
  Name two sections of a business plan.

  Any two of: the idea, market research, marketing mix, operations, financial forecasts.

  **Answer:** Any two of: the idea, market research, marketing mix, operations, financial forecasts.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing an idea with an opportunity** An idea is only an opportunity if enough customers will pay enough to cover costs — test willingness to pay.
  - **Thinking entrepreneurs love risk** Good entrepreneurs minimise risk through research and planning; they take calculated risks, not reckless ones.
</ImportantNote>
