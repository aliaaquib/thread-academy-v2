## Key ideas

### Risk versus reward

Entrepreneurs risk savings, income security and reputation; rewards include profit, independence and job satisfaction. Calculated risk-takers research and plan to reduce risk — they do not gamble blindly. Limited liability and insurance further contain the downside.

### Where opportunities hide

Opportunities come from changing tastes, new technology, gaps in service, or personal frustration. Observing customers, brainstorming and studying competitors reveal them — but an idea is only an opportunity if customers will pay.

**Key term — risk:** The chance of loss or failure. Entrepreneurs risk time and money, unlike employees with secure wages.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Risk and Reward">
  Give one risk and one reward of starting a business.

  Risk: losing savings / unstable income. Reward: profit / independence / job satisfaction.

  **Answer:** Risk: losing savings / unstable income. Reward: profit / independence / job satisfaction.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking entrepreneurs love risk** Good entrepreneurs minimise risk through research and planning; they take calculated risks, not reckless ones.
  - **Confusing an idea with an opportunity** An idea is only an opportunity if enough customers will pay enough to cover costs — test willingness to pay.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How can an entrepreneur reduce risk?" hint="Think research and legal form.">
    Any two of: market research, financial planning, starting small, limited liability, insurance.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do banks ask to see a business plan?" hint="Think about risk.">
    To judge whether the business is viable and the loan will be repaid — it reduces the bank's risk.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What turns a business idea into a business opportunity?" hint="Think about paying customers.">
    Evidence that enough customers will pay enough to cover costs and leave a profit.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a unique selling point?" hint="Why choose you?">
    The feature that differentiates a product from rivals and gives customers a reason to choose it.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
