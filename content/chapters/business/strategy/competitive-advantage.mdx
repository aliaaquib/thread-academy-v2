Strategy is about winning over the long term: choosing where to compete and how to win. This chapter covers competitive advantage, growth options, strategic analysis tools and the challenges of going global.

This lesson focuses on Competitive Advantage: explore Porter's routes to outperforming rivals.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Porter's generic strategies

Porter argues firms win through cost leadership (being the cheapest, like Aldi), differentiation (unique products commanding premiums, like Apple), or focus (serving a niche superbly). Being 'stuck in the middle' — neither cheapest nor different — is dangerous.

### Ansoff and growth risk

Market penetration (more of existing products to existing markets) is safest; diversification (new products, new markets) is riskiest. Firms balance organic growth against mergers and takeovers, which are fast but bring integration risk.

**Key term — competitive advantage:** An edge over rivals — lower costs or differentiated products — that is hard to copy.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Competitive Advantage">
  Name Porter's three generic strategies.

  Cost leadership, differentiation, focus.

  **Answer:** Cost leadership, differentiation, focus.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Copying rivals' strategies** Advantage must be hard to copy and fit your resources — imitation invites direct retaliation.
  - **Treating SWOT as the strategy** SWOT is an audit, not a decision — it must feed into objectives and actions.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is being 'stuck in the middle' dangerous?" hint="Think about Porter.">
    The firm is neither the cheapest nor differentiated, so rivals beat it on both price and uniqueness.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the riskiest option on Ansoff's matrix?" hint="New product, new market.">
    Diversification.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A strength is internal or external in SWOT?" hint="Inside the firm.">
    Internal — strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one benefit of economies of scale." hint="Think unit costs.">
    Lower average costs, allowing lower prices or higher margins.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
