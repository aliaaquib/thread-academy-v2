## Key ideas

### Strategy in a global world

Going global offers huge markets and lower costs but brings exchange-rate risk, cultural differences and political instability. Entry modes range from exporting (low risk, low control) to foreign direct investment (high risk, high control).

### Ansoff and growth risk

Market penetration (more of existing products to existing markets) is safest; diversification (new products, new markets) is riskiest. Firms balance organic growth against mergers and takeovers, which are fast but bring integration risk.

**Key term — Ansoff's matrix:** A growth framework: market penetration, market development, product development and diversification, in rising order of risk.

<WorkedExample title="Ansoff for a coffee chain">
  A UK coffee chain considers opening in Spain (existing drinks) versus launching a food range at home. Classify each move and compare the risks.

  Spain with existing drinks = market development.
  Food range at home = product development.
  Both are medium risk on Ansoff's matrix.
  Spain adds currency and cultural risk; the food range adds operational complexity.

  **Answer:** Both are medium-risk moves; Spain brings currency and cultural risk, the food range brings operational risk — research both before choosing.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating SWOT as the strategy** SWOT is an audit, not a decision — it must feed into objectives and actions.
  - **Pursuing growth for its own sake** Growth that destroys profit or overstretches resources (overtrading) harms shareholders.
</ImportantNote>
