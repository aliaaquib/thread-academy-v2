## Key ideas

### A simple budget

A child's budget has three jars: save, spend, share. Decide percentages — for example 40% save, 50% spend, 10% share — and split every pound that arrives. Tracking where money goes is the first step to controlling it.

### Pay yourself first

Most people spend first and save what is left — which is usually nothing. The golden habit is reversed: move savings aside the moment money arrives, then spend the rest guilt-free. Even £1 a week builds the habit and the pot.

**Key term — income:** Money coming in, such as pocket money, earnings from chores or wages from a job.

<WorkedExample title="Sam's £10 budget">
  Sam gets £10 pocket money and uses the 40/50/10 jars for save, spend and share. How much goes in each jar?

  Save: 40% of £10 = £4.
  Spend: 50% of £10 = £5.
  Share: 10% of £10 = £1.
  Check: £4 + £5 + £1 = £10 ✓.

  **Answer:** £4 to save, £5 to spend, £1 to share — every pound has a job.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saving 'what is left'** There is rarely anything left — move savings first, the moment money arrives.
  - **Borrowing for wants** Borrowed money costs extra in interest — never borrow for things you merely want.
</ImportantNote>
