## Key ideas

### Quality as strategy

Quality control inspects finished output — costly and late. Quality assurance designs quality in at each stage. Total quality management makes everyone responsible for quality, aiming for zero defects; strong quality supports premium prices and brand reputation.

### Choosing how to produce

Job production makes one-offs (a wedding cake); batch makes groups (a bakery's morning rolls); flow makes identical items continuously (cars). Flow brings huge economies of scale but needs massive demand; job offers customisation at high unit cost.

**Key term — quality assurance:** Building quality into every stage of production, as opposed to merely inspecting finished goods.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Quality Management">
  What is the difference between quality control and quality assurance?

  Control inspects finished goods; assurance builds quality into every production stage.

  **Answer:** Control inspects finished goods; assurance builds quality into every production stage.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing inspection guarantees quality** Inspection finds defects after the cost is incurred; assurance prevents them.
  - **Thinking lean means cutting staff** Lean cuts waste, not necessarily people — dismissed workers cannot suggest kaizen improvements.
</ImportantNote>
