This lesson focuses on Lean Production: eliminate waste with just-in-time and kaizen.

<Definition term="Lean Production">
  Eliminate waste with just-in-time and kaizen.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Lean thinking

Lean treats inventory, overproduction, waiting and defects as waste. Just-in-time slashes stock; kaizen empowers workers to suggest endless small improvements; cell production organises workers into teams owning a whole process. Lean cuts costs but is vulnerable to supply disruptions.

### Choosing how to produce

Job production makes one-offs (a wedding cake); batch makes groups (a bakery's morning rolls); flow makes identical items continuously (cars). Flow brings huge economies of scale but needs massive demand; job offers customisation at high unit cost.

**Key term — kaizen:** Continuous small improvements suggested by workers — a pillar of lean production pioneered by Toyota.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Lean Production">
  Which production method suits one-off luxury yachts?

  Job production.

  **Answer:** Job production.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking lean means cutting staff** Lean cuts waste, not necessarily people — dismissed workers cannot suggest kaizen improvements.
  - **Believing inspection guarantees quality** Inspection finds defects after the cost is incurred; assurance prevents them.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage and one disadvantage of just-in-time." hint="Stock costs vs reliability.">
    Advantage: lower inventory holding costs. Disadvantage: vulnerable to supply disruptions.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between quality control and quality assurance?" hint="Inspect after vs build in.">
    Control inspects finished goods; assurance builds quality into every production stage.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does TQM stand for and what does it involve?" hint="Everyone responsible.">
    Total quality management — everyone in the firm takes responsibility for quality, aiming for zero defects.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one factor, besides rent, affecting location." hint="Think workers or customers.">
    Any one of: skilled labour supply, proximity to customers or suppliers, transport links, government incentives.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
