## Key ideas

### Mitosis and the cell cycle

In mitosis, one cell divides to form two genetically identical daughter cells. Before division, the chromosomes are copied so each new cell receives a full set. Mitosis is needed for growth, for replacing worn-out cells, and for repair. Because the chromosome copies are exact, the daughter cells are clones of the parent cell.

### Microscopes and magnification

A light microscope uses light and glass lenses to magnify specimens up to about 1500 times. Electron microscopes reach far higher magnifications and resolution but cannot view living cells. Magnification is calculated with: magnification = image size ÷ real size. Always convert both measurements to the same units before dividing, for example millimetres to micrometres (1 mm = 1000 μm).

**Key term — stem cell:** An unspecialised cell that can divide to produce more stem cells or differentiate into specialised cell types.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Cell Division: Mitosis">
  Give two reasons why multicellular organisms need mitosis.

  Any two of: growth (increasing cell number); repair of damaged tissue; replacement of worn-out cells.

  **Answer:** Any two of: growth (increasing cell number); repair of damaged tissue; replacement of worn-out cells.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying mitosis produces different cells** Mitosis produces two genetically identical cells. It is meiosis, used to make gametes, that produces variation.
  - **Confusing magnification with resolution** Magnification makes things bigger; resolution makes them clearer. A blurry image magnified 1000 times is still blurry.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain why a sperm cell has many mitochondria." hint="What do mitochondria release?">
    Mitochondria release energy by respiration. The sperm needs large amounts of energy to swim towards the egg, so many mitochondria supply it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do red blood cells have no nucleus?" hint="What do they carry?">
    Losing the nucleus leaves more space for haemoglobin, so each cell can carry as much oxygen as possible.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A cell image is 40 mm wide at ×200 magnification. What is the real width in mm?" hint="real size = image size ÷ magnification">
    40 ÷ 200 = 0.2 mm.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does a root hair cell have a long hair-like extension?" hint="What does it absorb, and from where?">
    The hair increases the cell's surface area, so it can absorb water and mineral ions from the soil more quickly.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
