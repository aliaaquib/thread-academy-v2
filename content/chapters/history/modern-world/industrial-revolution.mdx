## A world transformed

Around 1750, most people farmed, made things by hand, and rarely travelled further than the next town. By 1900, Britain had factories, railways, steamships and cities of over a million people. This transformation — from hand production to machine production — is the **Industrial Revolution**.

<Definition term="Industrial Revolution">
  The **Industrial Revolution** (c. 1750–1900) was the shift from agricultural, hand-made production
  to industrial, machine-based manufacturing, powered first by coal and steam.
</Definition>

## Why Britain first?

Historians debate this, but the key factors:

- **Coal and iron** — abundant, close together, and near rivers and coasts for transport.
- **Capital** — profits from trade and farming funded investment in machines.
- **Ideas** — the Scientific Revolution encouraged experiment; inventors like Watt and Arkwright turned ideas into engines and mills.
- **Workers** — enclosure pushed labourers off the land and into towns, creating a workforce.
- **Markets** — a growing population and empire to sell to.

<ImportantNote title="No single cause">
  Strong answers never give one reason. "Coal alone caused it" is weak; "coal, capital, ideas and
  labour combined — with coal making steam power possible" shows historical thinking.
</ImportantNote>

## What changed — and for whom

**For factory owners and merchants:** wealth on a new scale. Britain became "the workshop of the world."

**For workers:** a harder story. Factory shifts lasted 12–14 hours; child labour was common; fast-growing cities like Manchester had slums, disease and pollution. Workers eventually organised — trade unions, and later laws limiting hours and child labour.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: using evidence">
  *Source: a factory inspector's report, 1833, describes children working 13-hour days.*

  A historian asks: how reliable is this for working conditions? Strong answer: it's **useful** —
  an official inspection, close in time — but note **limitations**: one district, and inspectors
  saw the worst cases. Always weigh provenance *and* limits.
</WorkedExample>

  <PracticeItem question="Give three reasons the Industrial Revolution began in Britain." hint="Think resources, money and people.">
    Any three of: abundant coal and iron; capital from trade; scientific ideas and inventors; available workforce from enclosure; growing markets at home and in the empire.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Was the Industrial Revolution 'progress'? Give both sides." hint="Who benefited, and who paid?">

  </PracticeItem>

<Summary>
  - The Industrial Revolution (c. 1750–1900) shifted production from hand to machine, powered by coal and steam.
  - Britain led because of coal, iron, capital, ideas, labour and markets — combined.
  - It created vast wealth and transformed cities, but at a heavy human cost for workers.
  - Always assess both sides, and weigh sources for reliability and limits.
