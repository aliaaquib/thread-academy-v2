## Key ideas

### Industry needed the world

Lancashire spun cotton it could not grow; the cotton came from plantations, including those worked by enslaved people before abolition. Finished cloth was then sold back across the globe. The factory system was never purely British — it was a world system with Britain at its centre.

### Reform followed suffering

Reports of children working long hours in mills shocked the public. Parliament passed Factory Acts, starting in 1833, limiting children's hours and later setting safety rules. Change came because evidence of suffering forced politicians to act.

**Key term — Raw materials:** Natural substances used to make goods, such as raw cotton for cloth or iron ore for machines.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Cotton and the World">
  Why did Britain's mills depend on the wider world?

  They needed imported raw cotton and overseas markets to sell finished cloth.

  **Answer:** They needed imported raw cotton and overseas markets to sell finished cloth.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting the global side** Britain's industry depended on raw materials from around the world and markets overseas. The story is global, not just a British one.
  - **Saying steam power started the Industrial Revolution** Water power drove the first factories; steam became dominant later. The revolution had several stages, not one starting moment.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What powered the earliest cotton mills?" hint="Think of rivers.">
    Water wheels, using fast-flowing rivers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What did the Factory Act of 1833 do?" hint="It limited children's work.">
    It limited the working hours of children in textile mills and introduced inspectors.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two inventions of the Industrial Revolution." hint="One spins, one weaves.">
    The spinning jenny and the power loom (or the steam engine).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one reason factory work was hard for children." hint="Think about hours.">
    Children worked very long hours, often twelve or more a day, in dangerous conditions.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
