## Key ideas

### Independence was won, not granted

Nationalist leaders — Gandhi and Nehru in India, Nkrumah in Ghana, and many others — organised mass movements, boycotts and negotiations over decades. Britain and France sometimes conceded gracefully and sometimes fought bitter wars, as in Kenya and Algeria. Independence movements, not imperial generosity, were the driving force.

### Freedom brought new challenges

New nations inherited borders drawn by empires, dividing peoples and sowing conflict — partition in 1947 caused massive displacement and violence. Yet independence also brought pride, democracy movements and new voices in world affairs. Historians debate whether decolonisation was a triumph, a tragedy, or — most often — both.

**Key term — Partition:** The division of British India in 1947 into the independent states of India and Pakistan.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: India: Independence 1947">
  In which year did India gain independence, and what was partition?

  1947; partition was the division of British India into independent India and Pakistan.

  **Answer:** 1947; partition was the division of British India into independent India and Pakistan.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating partition as an afterthought** Partition was central to 1947: it created two states and caused enormous displacement and violence. Any account of Indian independence must confront it.
  - **Saying Britain simply 'gave' independence** Independence was won through sustained pressure and struggle. 'Granting' independence misdescribes who drove the process.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is decolonisation?" hint="Think colonies becoming independent.">
    The process by which colonies gained independence from imperial rule.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Who was Kwame Nkrumah?" hint="Think Ghana, 1957.">
    The nationalist leader who led Ghana (the Gold Coast) to independence in 1957, the first sub-Saharan African colony to do so.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why did imperial borders cause problems after independence?" hint="Empires drew lines ignoring local peoples.">
    Borders divided ethnic groups and forced rival peoples together, sowing conflict in new states.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the historians' debate about decolonisation?" hint="Triumph, tragedy, or both?">
    Whether it was mainly a triumph of freedom, a tragedy of violence and division (e.g. partition), or — as most argue — a mixture of both.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
