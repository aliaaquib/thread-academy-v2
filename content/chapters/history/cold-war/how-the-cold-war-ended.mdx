## Key ideas

### The Cold War ended from within and below

Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms of glasnost and perestroika aimed to save the Soviet system but unleashed demands he could not control. In 1989 mass protests swept Eastern Europe and the Berlin Wall fell; the USSR itself dissolved in 1991. Historians debate how much credit belongs to Gorbachev, to Western pressure, or to ordinary people's courage.

### Rivals, not warriors

The superpowers never fought each other directly because nuclear weapons made total war suicidal. Instead they competed through alliances (NATO founded 1949, the Warsaw Pact 1955), arms races, propaganda and proxy wars. The Cold War was a struggle for influence fought everywhere except the direct battlefield.

**Key term — Superpower:** A state with global military, economic and political influence. After 1945 only the USA and USSR qualified.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: How the Cold War Ended">

  Gorbachev's reforms destabilised Soviet control, and mass protests in Eastern Europe (e.g. the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989) ended communist rule; the USSR dissolved in 1991.

  **Answer:** Gorbachev's reforms destabilised Soviet control, and mass protests in Eastern Europe (e.g. the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989) ended communist rule; the USSR dissolved in 1991.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying the Cold War was only about the USA and USSR** It drew in the whole world: Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan and many decolonising states became Cold War battlegrounds, often at terrible local cost.
  - **Treating 1989 as a purely Western victory** The end owed much to internal Soviet failure and to Eastern Europeans themselves. Reducing it to one side's triumph misses the debate historians actually have.
</ImportantNote>
