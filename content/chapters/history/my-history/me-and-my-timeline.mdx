History is not only about kings and queens. It is the story of everyone, and your own life is part of it. In this chapter you will build timelines, explore family stories, and learn the difference between the past, the present and the future.

This lesson focuses on Me and My Timeline: make a timeline of your own life, from birth to today.

<Definition term="Me and My Timeline">
  Make a timeline of your own life, from birth to today.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Your life is history too

When you were born, what your first word was, the day you started school — these are all events in history because they happened in the past. Future historians could learn about the world today by studying the lives of ordinary children. You do not have to be famous to be part of history.

### Order matters

History makes most sense when events are put in the right order. A baby is born before it learns to walk, and a child starts school before learning to read properly. Timelines turn a jumble of events into a clear story that anyone can follow.

**Key term — Timeline:** A line that shows events in the order they happened, with earlier events on the left and later ones on the right.

<WorkedExample title="Ordering My Life Story">
  These events from Sam's life are mixed up: started school, learned to ride a bike, was born, lost a first tooth. Put them in chronological order.

  Look for the event that must come first: Sam had to be born before anything else.
  Next, think about ages: children usually start school before they can ride a bike alone.
  A lost first tooth usually happens in the early school years, so it comes last here.
  Write the events in order on a line: born → started school → rode a bike → lost a first tooth.

  **Answer:** The correct order is: was born, started school, learned to ride a bike, lost a first tooth.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Putting the newest event first** Always start with the oldest event. On a timeline, the past sits on the left and today sits on the right.
  - **Thinking history is only about famous people** Everyone's life is part of history. Your family stories matter just as much to you as kings and queens matter to a nation.
</ImportantNote>
