## Key ideas

### The past lives in stories

Not everything from the past is written down. Families keep history alive by telling stories: how grandparents met, where they grew up, what school was like. Asking questions and listening carefully is real historical work.

### Your life is history too

When you were born, what your first word was, the day you started school — these are all events in history because they happened in the past. Future historians could learn about the world today by studying the lives of ordinary children. You do not have to be famous to be part of history.

**Key term — Memory:** What a person remembers about their own past. Memories are precious clues about history, though two people can remember the same event differently.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Stories from Home">
  Why do families tell stories about the past?

  To remember people and events, and to pass memories down to younger family members.

  **Answer:** To remember people and events, and to pass memories down to younger family members.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking history is only about famous people** Everyone's life is part of history. Your family stories matter just as much to you as kings and queens matter to a nation.
  - **Putting the newest event first** Always start with the oldest event. On a timeline, the past sits on the left and today sits on the right.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two people in your family who belong to an older generation." hint="Think of grandparents or great-grandparents.">
    Any two older relatives, for example a grandmother and a great-uncle.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Sort these into past and present: your breakfast this morning, your third birthday." hint="The present is happening now.">
    Past: your third birthday. Present: your breakfast this morning.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a timeline?" hint="Think about how events are laid out.">
    A line that shows events in the order they happened, oldest first.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which event comes first in your life: starting school or being born?" hint="Nothing can happen before you are born.">
    Being born comes first.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
