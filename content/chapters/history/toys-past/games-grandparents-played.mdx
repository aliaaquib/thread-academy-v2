## Key ideas

### The past was not all the same

Children long ago did not all play with the same toys. Rich children might own painted tin soldiers, while poorer children played with marbles or homemade dolls. Toys can show us that life was different for different people.

### Objects tell stories

You can learn about the past without reading a single word. An old toy shows what materials people had, what games they enjoyed and how much money families could spend. Looking closely at objects and asking questions is called studying artefacts.

**Key term — Evidence:** Anything that helps us learn about the past. An old teddy bear is evidence of how children played.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Games Grandparents Played">
  Why might a poor Victorian child not own a shop-bought toy?

  The family could not afford it, so the child would make toys or play free games instead.

  **Answer:** The family could not afford it, so the child would make toys or play free games instead.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting that rich and poor children played differently** Expensive shop-bought toys belonged to wealthier families. Poorer children often made their own toys or played outdoor games that cost nothing.
  - **Thinking everything changed** Some toys, like balls, dolls and teddy bears, have been played with for hundreds of years. Always check what stayed the same too.
</ImportantNote>
