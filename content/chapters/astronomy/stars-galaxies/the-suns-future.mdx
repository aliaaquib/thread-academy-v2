This lesson focuses on The Sun's Future: what will happen to our Sun when its fuel runs out.

<Definition term="The Sun's Future">
  What will happen to our Sun when its fuel runs out.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### A star's mass decides its fate

Low-mass stars like the Sun end as white dwarfs. Stars more than about 8 times the Sun's mass die violently as supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes. Massive stars burn their fuel fast and live only millions of years, while small red dwarfs can shine for trillions.

### We are made of stardust

Elements heavier than hydrogen and helium, including the carbon in your body and the iron in your blood, were forged inside stars and scattered by supernovae. Every atom heavier than helium in your body was once inside a star.

**Key term — Nuclear fusion:** The process in a star's core where hydrogen nuclei join to form helium, releasing huge amounts of energy. Fusion is the power source of every shining star.

<WorkedExample title="How long will the Sun live?">
  The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old and is expected to shine as a normal star for about 10 billion years in total. How much longer will it keep shining, and what happens next?

  Subtract its current age from its total main-sequence lifetime: 10 − 4.6 = 5.4 billion years remaining.
  The Sun fuses hydrogen into helium in its core; when the core hydrogen runs out, fusion moves outward.
  The outer layers swell and cool: the Sun becomes a red giant, likely engulfing Mercury and Venus.
  Finally the outer layers drift away, leaving a hot white dwarf that slowly cools.
  Earth will long since have become uninhabitable, but that is about 5 billion years away.

  **Answer:** The Sun will shine for about another 5 billion years, then become a red giant and end as a white dwarf.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking all stars die as black holes** Only stars more than about 20 times the Sun's mass can become black holes. Sun-like stars end as white dwarfs.
  - **Saying the Sun will explode as a supernova** The Sun is far too small for a supernova; it will swell into a red giant and shed its layers gently instead.
</ImportantNote>

    The Sun will become a red giant, then shed its outer layers, leaving a white dwarf that slowly cools.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do massive stars live shorter lives than small stars, even though they have more fuel?" hint="Think about how fast they burn it.">
    Massive stars burn their fuel enormously faster because of the extreme pressure and temperature in their cores, so they exhaust it in millions rather than billions of years.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What powers a star like the Sun?" hint="It happens in the core and turns hydrogen into helium.">
    Nuclear fusion: hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium, releasing energy.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What determines whether a star ends as a white dwarf, neutron star or black hole?" hint="It is about how much material the star has.">
    Its mass: low-mass stars become white dwarfs, stars above about 8 solar masses explode as supernovae leaving neutron stars or black holes.
  </PracticeItem>
