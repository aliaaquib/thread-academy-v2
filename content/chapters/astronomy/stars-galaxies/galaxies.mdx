## Galaxies

On a dark night you can see the Andromeda Galaxy with the naked eye — 2.5 million light-years away, its light left when early humans were evolving.

<Definition term="Light-year">
  A **light-year** is the distance light travels in one year — about 9.5 trillion km. It’s a distance, not a time: saying a star is 100 light-years away means its light took 100 years to reach us.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Galaxy types: spiral (Milky Way), elliptical, irregular.
- The Milky Way is ~100,000 light-years across with 100–400 billion stars.
- The universe is expanding — distant galaxies recede faster (Hubble’s law).
- The Big Bang: about 13.8 billion years ago, everything was hot and dense.

<Example title="Looking back in time">
  Telescopes are time machines: the James Webb Space Telescope sees galaxies as they were 13+ billion years ago — baby pictures of the universe.
</Example>

  - A light-year is a distance: ~9.5 trillion km.

  - We see the distant universe as it was long ago.
</Summary>
