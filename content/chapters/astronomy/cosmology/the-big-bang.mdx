Cosmology is the science of everything: the origin, structure and ultimate fate of the entire universe. A century ago we did not know other galaxies existed; today we can describe the universe's first fraction of a second and predict its far future. The story begins with the Big Bang and is still being written.

This lesson focuses on The Big Bang: the evidence that the universe began hot, dense and expanding 13.8 billion years ago.

<Definition term="The Big Bang">
  The evidence that the universe began hot, dense and expanding 13.8 billion years ago.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Three lines of evidence for the Big Bang

First, galaxies show redshift proportional to distance: the universe is expanding, so it was smaller in the past. Second, the cosmic microwave background is the cooled remnant heat of the early universe, matching predictions precisely. Third, the observed amounts of hydrogen and helium match what Big Bang nuclear fusion predicts. Together they make the case overwhelming.

### Possible fates

If dark energy behaves as a cosmological constant, the universe faces a Big Freeze: endless expansion until stars burn out and the cosmos grows cold and dark. Alternative theories allow a Big Crunch (recollapse) or a Big Rip (expansion tearing everything apart), but current evidence favours the Big Freeze.

**Key term — Big Bang:** The hot, dense beginning of the universe about 13.8 billion years ago, from which space itself has been expanding ever since. It was not an explosion in space, but an expansion of space.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Big Bang">
  State two pieces of evidence for the Big Bang.

  The redshift of distant galaxies shows the universe is expanding from a smaller past state, and the cosmic microwave background is the cooled afterglow predicted by the Big Bang.

  **Answer:** The redshift of distant galaxies shows the universe is expanding from a smaller past state, and the cosmic microwave background is the cooled afterglow predicted by the Big Bang.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying the universe is 13.8 billion light years across** 13.8 billion years is its age. Because space has expanded while light travelled, the observable universe is about 93 billion light years across.
  - **Describing the Big Bang as an explosion in space** The Big Bang was the expansion of space itself from an extremely hot, dense state; there was no pre-existing space for anything to explode into.
</ImportantNote>
