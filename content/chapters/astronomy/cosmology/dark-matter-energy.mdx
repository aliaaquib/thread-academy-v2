## Key ideas

### The universe is accelerating

In 1998 astronomers studying distant supernovae found the expansion of the universe is speeding up, not slowing down. Something dubbed dark energy is driving this acceleration. Its nature is one of the biggest unsolved problems in physics.

### Possible fates

If dark energy behaves as a cosmological constant, the universe faces a Big Freeze: endless expansion until stars burn out and the cosmos grows cold and dark. Alternative theories allow a Big Crunch (recollapse) or a Big Rip (expansion tearing everything apart), but current evidence favours the Big Freeze.

**Key term — Dark matter:** Invisible matter detected only through its gravity, which holds galaxies and clusters together. It makes up about 27 percent of the universe's mass-energy content.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Dark Matter and Dark Energy">

  The Big Freeze: accelerating expansion driven by dark energy means the universe expands forever, stars burn out, and the cosmos grows cold and dark.

  **Answer:** The Big Freeze: accelerating expansion driven by dark energy means the universe expands forever, stars burn out, and the cosmos grows cold and dark.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating dark matter and dark energy as the same thing** Dark matter is invisible mass whose gravity holds galaxies together; dark energy drives the accelerating expansion of space. They are entirely different mysteries.
  - **Describing the Big Bang as an explosion in space** The Big Bang was the expansion of space itself from an extremely hot, dense state; there was no pre-existing space for anything to explode into.
</ImportantNote>
