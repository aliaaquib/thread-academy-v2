Every morning the Sun appears in the east and every evening it sets in the west, but the Sun is not moving at all. It is Earth that spins like a top, carrying us into sunlight and back into darkness. Understanding this spin explains day and night, time zones and the seasons.

This lesson focuses on Earth Spins: how Earth's daily rotation gives us sunrise and sunset.

<Definition term="Earth Spins">
  How Earth's daily rotation gives us sunrise and sunset.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Time zones

Because Earth spins, when it is noon in London the Sun has not yet risen in New York. The world is divided into time zones so that midday roughly matches the middle of the day everywhere. When you travel east, your watch moves forward.

### Day and night

Earth rotates once every 24 hours. When your part of Earth turns to face the Sun, you have daytime; when it turns away, you have night. The Sun only appears to rise and set because we are moving with Earth.

**Key term — Rotation:** Earth spinning on its own axis, completing one full turn every 24 hours. Rotation causes day and night.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Earth Spins">
  A sundial's shadow moves during the day. What makes it move?

  Earth's rotation carries the sundial around, so the Sun appears to move across the sky and the shadow shifts with it.

  **Answer:** Earth's rotation carries the sundial around, so the Sun appears to move across the sky and the shadow shifts with it.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking the Sun moves around Earth each day** The Sun appears to move because Earth rotates; the Sun itself stays still while Earth spins.
  - **Saying summer happens because Earth is closer to the Sun** Earth is actually slightly closer to the Sun in January. Seasons are caused by Earth's tilt, not by distance.
</ImportantNote>
