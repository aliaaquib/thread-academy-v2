## Key ideas

### The solar system formed from a cloud

About 4.6 billion years ago, a giant cloud of gas and dust collapsed under gravity. Most of it became the Sun; the rest flattened into a disc where planets grew by clumping together. This is why all the planets orbit in the same direction and nearly the same plane.

### Small bodies matter

Asteroids, comets and dwarf planets are leftovers from the solar system's formation. Comets delivered water and organic material to the early Earth, and asteroid impacts have shaped life's history, including the extinction of the dinosaurs 66 million years ago.

**Key term — Asteroid belt:** A ring of rocky fragments orbiting the Sun between Mars and Jupiter. These are leftovers from the formation of the solar system that never became a planet.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Rocky and Giant Planets">
  Where is the asteroid belt, and what is it made of?

  It orbits between Mars and Jupiter and is made of rocky fragments left over from the solar system's formation.

  **Answer:** It orbits between Mars and Jupiter and is made of rocky fragments left over from the solar system's formation.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Placing the planets in the wrong order** A common slip is swapping Uranus and Neptune. Remember: My Very Excellent Mother Just Served Us Noodles, so Uranus comes before Neptune.
  - **Mixing up a light year (distance) with a year (time)** A light year is a distance, the distance light travels in a year. Saying a star is '5 light years away' describes how far it is, not how long ago something happened.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do all the planets orbit the Sun in the same direction?" hint="They formed from a spinning disc.">
    The planets formed from a rotating disc of gas and dust around the young Sun, so they inherited the disc's direction of rotation.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How old is the solar system, approximately?" hint="It formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.">
    About 4.6 billion years.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is one astronomical unit (AU)?" hint="It is defined by Earth's orbit.">
    The average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 150 million km.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Explain what a comet's tail is and which way it points." hint="Think about what happens to ice near the Sun, and what pushes the tail.">
    The tail is gas and dust released as the comet's ice vaporises near the Sun; the solar wind pushes it so it always points away from the Sun.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
