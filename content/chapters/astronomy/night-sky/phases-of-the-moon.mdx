<Definition term="Phases of the Moon">
  Why the Moon's shape appears to change over about four weeks.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### The Moon changes shape

The Moon does not make its own light; we see it because sunlight bounces off it. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half, from a thin crescent to a full disc. The full cycle of phases takes just under a month.

### Why stars seem to move

Stars appear to move across the sky during the night, but it is actually Earth that is turning. Earth spins once every 24 hours, so the whole sky appears to rotate around a point near the pole star. This is the same reason the Sun rises and sets.

**Key term — Waxing and waning:** Waxing describes the Moon growing from a thin crescent to full, while waning describes it shrinking from full back to a crescent. One full cycle takes about 29.5 days.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Phases of the Moon">
  How long does it take for the Moon to go through all its phases once?

  About 29.5 days, roughly one month.

  **Answer:** About 29.5 days, roughly one month.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying the Moon changes shape because of Earth's shadow** The phases of the Moon are caused by the viewing angle as the Moon orbits Earth, not by Earth's shadow. Earth's shadow only falls on the Moon during a rare lunar eclipse.
  - **Thinking stars in a constellation are close together in space** The stars in one constellation can be hundreds of light years apart; the pattern is a trick of perspective from Earth.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="Explain why stars appear to move across the sky during the night." hint="It is Earth doing the moving, not the stars.">
    Earth spins on its axis once every 24 hours, so the sky appears to rotate overhead while the stars stay fixed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Does the Moon make its own light? Explain your answer." hint="Compare it with the Sun, which does.">
