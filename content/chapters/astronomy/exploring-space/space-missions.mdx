## Space Missions

In 1957 a 58 cm metal sphere beeped from orbit. Twelve years later, humans walked on the Moon. Today, robots roam Mars while the ISS circles overhead.

<Definition term="Space probe">
  A **space probe** is an uncrewed spacecraft that travels beyond Earth orbit to study planets, moons, asteroids or the space between — carrying cameras and instruments, phoning results home by radio.
</Definition>

## Key points

- 1957 Sputnik 1; 1961 Gagarin first human in space; 1969 Apollo 11 Moon landing.
- Voyager 1 (1977) is now in interstellar space, 24+ billion km away.
- Mars rovers (Curiosity, Perseverance) are rolling geology labs hunting signs of past life.
- The ISS has been continuously crewed since 2000 — a lab the size of a football field.

<Example title="The Golden Record">
  Voyager carries a gold-plated record with greetings in 55 languages, whale songs and Chuck Berry — a message in a bottle thrown into the cosmic ocean.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why send probes instead of people to the outer planets?" hint="Think distance, time, danger.">
    Journeys take years, radiation and cold are lethal, and life support for crews is enormously expensive — robots do the science without the risk.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two achievements of the International Space Station." hint="Continuous presence; science.">
    Continuously crewed since 2000; thousands of experiments in microgravity, from protein crystals to human physiology.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Spaceflight went from Sputnik (1957) to the Moon (1969) in 12 years.
  - Probes like Voyager explore where humans can’t yet go.
  - Rovers are mobile labs searching Mars for signs of life.
  - The ISS proves long-term human presence in orbit is possible.
</Summary>
