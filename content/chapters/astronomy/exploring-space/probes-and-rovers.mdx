## Key ideas

### Spacecraft visit the planets for us

Probes have flown past every planet: Voyager 1 and 2 toured the outer planets in the 1970s and 1980s, Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years, and rovers like Curiosity and Perseverance are exploring Mars right now. In 2015 New Horizons flew past Pluto, giving us our first close-up of the dwarf planet.

### Telescopes are light buckets

A telescope's power comes mainly from the size of its mirror or lens: bigger means more light collected and fainter objects visible. Space telescopes like Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope sit above Earth's blurring atmosphere, giving far sharper views. Webb observes infrared light, letting it see the first galaxies and peer inside dusty star-forming clouds.

**Key term — Rover:** A robotic vehicle that drives across the surface of another world, such as the Mars rovers, carrying out experiments far from Earth.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Probes and Rovers">
  Name two robotic missions and what they explored.

  Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years; the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers are exploring the surface of Mars.

  **Answer:** Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years; the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers are exploring the surface of Mars.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying astronauts have visited other planets** No human has travelled beyond the Moon. All our visits to other planets have been by robotic probes, landers and rovers.
  - **Assuming the James Webb Space Telescope takes ordinary photos** Webb observes mainly infrared light, invisible to our eyes; its famous images are translated into visible colours so we can see them.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one advantage of putting a telescope in space rather than on the ground." hint="Think about what sits between a ground telescope and the stars.">
    It sits above Earth's atmosphere, which blurs and absorbs light, so images are much sharper.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why has no astronaut visited Mars yet?" hint="Consider distance, time and life support.">
    Mars is hundreds of times farther than the Moon, so the journey takes months each way and needs far more food, water, oxygen and radiation protection than we have yet managed for a crew.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do astronomers build telescopes with very large mirrors?" hint="Think about what a telescope collects.">
    Larger mirrors collect more light, so fainter and more distant objects become visible, and detail improves.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does redshift tell an astronomer about a galaxy?" hint="It relates to motion and the expanding universe.">
    Its light is stretched towards the red, showing the galaxy is moving away from us; greater redshift means greater distance.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
