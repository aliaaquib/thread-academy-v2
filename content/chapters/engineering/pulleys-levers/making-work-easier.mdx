This lesson focuses on Making Work Easier: simple machines trade a longer pull for a smaller effort.

<Definition term="Making Work Easier">
  Simple machines trade a longer pull for a smaller effort.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### A lever trades distance for force

Push down on the long end of a seesaw and the short end rises with a much bigger force. Your hand travels a long way while the load moves only a little, so the machine multiplies your force by making you move farther. Nothing is free: the total work stays the same.

### Pulleys redirect effort

A single fixed pulley does not make a load lighter, but it lets you pull downwards using your body weight instead of lifting upwards with your arms. Using two or more pulleys together shares the load between several rope sections, so each section carries only part of the weight and the pull feels lighter.

**Key term — Effort:** The push or pull you apply to a machine to make it work.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Making Work Easier">
  What does a single fixed pulley change about lifting a bucket from a well?

  Only the direction: you pull down instead of lifting up. The effort needed stays the same.

  **Answer:** Only the direction: you pull down instead of lifting up. The effort needed stays the same.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking a pulley makes the load weigh less** A single fixed pulley changes the direction of the pull but the load weighs exactly the same. Only extra pulleys sharing the rope reduce the effort.
  - **Pushing close to the fulcrum and wondering why it is hard** Effort near the pivot has little turning effect. Move your push farther from the fulcrum to make it easier.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is it easier to open a door by pushing the handle than by pushing near the hinges?" hint="Which push is farther from the pivot?">
    The handle is farther from the hinges, so the same push has a bigger turning effect.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the fulcrum, effort and load in a pair of scissors." hint="Where do the blades cross?">
    Fulcrum: the screw where the blades cross; effort: your fingers on the handles; load: the paper between the blades.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A crowbar lifts a 600 N rock with an effort of 150 N. How many times has the force been multiplied?" hint="Divide the load by the effort.">
    4 times, because 600 divided by 150 = 4.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A wheelbarrow is a lever. Where is its fulcrum?" hint="Which part stays still while the handles rise?">
    The wheel axle: the barrow pivots around the wheel when you lift the handles.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
