Open up a wind-up toy or watch a bicycle and you will find small parts working together to turn one kind of movement into another. These clever arrangements are called mechanisms, and engineers have used them for centuries. In this chapter you will explore gears, cams and linkages, and spot the mechanisms hiding in everyday objects.

This lesson focuses on Turning Gears: meshed gears change the speed and direction of rotation.

<Definition term="Turning Gears">
  Meshed gears change the speed and direction of rotation.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Gears trade speed for force

When a small gear drives a large gear, the large gear turns more slowly but with a bigger turning force. Bicycles use this: a small rear sprocket gives speed on the flat, while a large sprocket gives the force needed to climb hills. The gear ratio is simply the number of teeth on each wheel compared.

### Cams turn round-and-round into up-and-down

A cam is a wheel that is not perfectly round. As it spins, its high spots push a follower rod upwards and its low spots let it fall back, converting rotation into repeated up-and-down motion. Car engines use cams to open and close valves at exactly the right moment.

**Key term — Gear:** A toothed wheel that meshes with another toothed wheel to pass on rotation.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Turning Gears">
  Do two meshed gears rotate in the same direction?

  No, meshed gears always rotate in opposite directions.

  **Answer:** No, meshed gears always rotate in opposite directions.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking meshed gears turn the same way** Meshed gears always turn in opposite directions. Add a third gear between them and the first and last turn the same way again.
  - **Expecting more speed and more force at once** Gears trade one for the other: faster output means weaker turning force, and stronger output means slower turning.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a cam do in a car engine?" hint="Think about valves opening and closing.">
    It converts the camshaft's rotation into the up-and-down motion that opens and closes the engine's valves.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might an engineer choose a belt drive instead of gears?" hint="Think about noise and distance.">
    Belts run quietly, need no lubrication, and can connect shafts that are far apart, though they can slip under heavy load.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 10-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear. How many times does the driver turn for one turn of the driven gear?" hint="Divide the driven teeth by the driver teeth.">
    4 times, because 40 divided by 10 = 4.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A bicycle's large chainring drives a small rear sprocket. Does the rear wheel spin faster or slower than the pedals?" hint="Small driven gear, large driver.">
    Faster: the small sprocket must turn many times for each turn of the large chainring.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
