## Key ideas

### Cams turn round-and-round into up-and-down

A cam is a wheel that is not perfectly round. As it spins, its high spots push a follower rod upwards and its low spots let it fall back, converting rotation into repeated up-and-down motion. Car engines use cams to open and close valves at exactly the right moment.

### Linkages keep motion in step

In a linkage, bars joined at pivots force each other to move in a fixed pattern. Windscreen wipers use a linkage so one motor sweeps both blades together, and the folding arms of an ironing board keep the top level as it opens. Change one bar's length and the whole pattern of movement changes.

**Key term — Cam:** A shaped wheel that pushes a follower up and down as it turns.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Cams Change Motion">
  What does a cam do in a car engine?

  It converts the camshaft's rotation into the up-and-down motion that opens and closes the engine's valves.

  **Answer:** It converts the camshaft's rotation into the up-and-down motion that opens and closes the engine's valves.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Counting turns instead of teeth for the ratio** The ratio comes from the numbers of teeth, not from guessing. Divide the driven teeth by the driving teeth.
  - **Thinking meshed gears turn the same way** Meshed gears always turn in opposite directions. Add a third gear between them and the first and last turn the same way again.
</ImportantNote>

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A bicycle's large chainring drives a small rear sprocket. Does the rear wheel spin faster or slower than the pedals?" hint="Small driven gear, large driver.">
    Faster: the small sprocket must turn many times for each turn of the large chainring.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might an engineer choose a belt drive instead of gears?" hint="Think about noise and distance.">
    Belts run quietly, need no lubrication, and can connect shafts that are far apart, though they can slip under heavy load.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 10-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear. How many times does the driver turn for one turn of the driven gear?" hint="Divide the driven teeth by the driver teeth.">
    4 times, because 40 divided by 10 = 4.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Do two meshed gears rotate in the same direction?" hint="Watch where their teeth meet.">
    No, meshed gears always rotate in opposite directions.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
