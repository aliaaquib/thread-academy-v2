<Definition term="Joining Things Together">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Wide bases stop toppling

A structure stays upright while its centre of mass hangs over its base. Making the base wider, or keeping heavy parts low down, gives the structure more room to lean before that point moves past the edge and it tips over. That is why towers are built broad at the bottom and narrow at the top.

### Test, fail and improve

No engineer's first design is perfect, and real engineers expect things to break during testing. When a part fails, look closely at where it failed: that is exactly where the design needs to be stronger. Each round of testing makes the structure better.

**Key term — Joint:** The place where two parts of a structure are fastened together, for example with tape, glue or a slot.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Joining Things Together">
  Name two ways of joining cardboard parts together.

  Any two of: tape, glue, slots and tabs, staples, string lashings.

  **Answer:** Any two of: tape, glue, slots and tabs, staples, string lashings.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Building a tall, narrow tower** A tower that is tall and narrow topples easily because its centre of mass is high and its base is small. Widen the base and keep the heavy parts low.
  - **Covering up a failure with extra tape** Hiding the failure teaches you nothing. Study where it bent or broke and strengthen that spot properly.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="You build a square frame from four straws and it wobbles. What one change will stiffen it?" hint="Which shape cannot be squashed sideways?">
    Add a diagonal straw to make triangles; triangles cannot change shape without stretching a side.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Where should you put the heaviest parts of a tower, and why?" hint="A high centre of mass makes toppling easier.">
    Low down, because that keeps the centre of mass near the ground and the tower harder to tip over.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does a wide base make a tower more stable?" hint="Think about where the tower's centre of mass sits.">
    A wide base keeps the tower's centre of mass over the base even when it leans, so it does not topple.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your tower's middle joint keeps bending when loaded. What does this tell you?" hint="Failure points show you something useful.">
    The middle joint is the weakest point, so the design needs strengthening there, for example with an extra brace or a doubled tube.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
