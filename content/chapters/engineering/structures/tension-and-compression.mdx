This lesson focuses on Tension and Compression: tension pulls materials apart; compression squashes them together.

<Definition term="Tension and Compression">
  Tension pulls materials apart; compression squashes them together.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Tension and compression act in every member

Stretch a rope and it is in tension; stand on a drinks can and it is in compression. In a bridge, the top of a sagging beam is squashed in compression while the bottom is stretched in tension. Materials behave differently under each: concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension, which is why it is reinforced with steel bars.

### Every structure carries two kinds of load

Dead loads are always there: the weight of the beams, walls and roof. Live loads change: crowds on a stadium stand, cars on a bridge, snow on a roof, wind pushing a tower. Engineers design for the worst combination, because a bridge that holds cars but fails in a gale is a failed bridge.

**Key term — Load:** Any force acting on a structure, including its own weight, people, wind and snow.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Tension and Compression">
  A rope in a tug of war is in tension or compression?

  Tension, because each team pulls the rope apart.

  **Answer:** Tension, because each team pulls the rope apart.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming concrete is strong in every direction** Concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension, so it cracks when bent. Steel reinforcement bars carry the tension part.
  - **Forgetting the structure's own weight** Dead load is always present and can be the biggest load of all. Always include the weight of the structure itself.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="In a sagging beam, which part is in tension: the top or the bottom?" hint="The beam bends into a smile shape.">
    The bottom, because the underside of the sag stretches while the top is squashed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is steel used to reinforce concrete beams?" hint="Think about what concrete does poorly.">
    Concrete is weak in tension, so steel bars are placed where tension occurs to carry the pulling forces.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is a parked car on a bridge a dead load or a live load?" hint="Does it stay there permanently?">
    A live load, because it comes and goes; only the bridge's own weight is dead load.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 90 000 N roof is carried by 6 identical columns. What load does each column take?" hint="Share the total equally.">
    15 000 N each, because 90 000 divided by 6 = 15 000.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
