## Simple Machines

Archimedes said: give me a lever long enough and I’ll move the Earth. He was describing mechanical advantage — trading distance for force.

<Definition term="Mechanical advantage">
  **Mechanical advantage** is the factor by which a machine multiplies force: output force ÷ input force. You always pay with distance — move the input further than the output moves.
</Definition>

## Key points

- The six: lever, wheel-and-axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw.
- Levers: effort × effort arm = load × load arm.
- Pulleys trade rope pulled for weight lifted.
- Gears trade speed for torque (and reverse direction).

<Example title="Moving a 1,000 kg stone">
  With a lever giving 10× advantage, you push down with about 1,000 N (100 kg of effort) — but your end moves 10× further than the stone. Egypt’s builders knew this math.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A lever has effort arm 2 m and load arm 0.5 m. What force lifts a 400 N load?" hint="Effort × 2 = 400 × 0.5.">
    Effort = (400 × 0.5) / 2 = 100 N — a 4× mechanical advantage.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why can’t a machine give you free energy?" hint="Force × distance.">
    Work in = work out (minus friction): multiplying force means moving through proportionally less distance.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Six simple machines combine into all complex ones.
  - Mechanical advantage = force multiplied, paid for in distance.
  - Levers, pulleys and gears trade force against movement.
  - No machine creates energy — it transforms it.
</Summary>
