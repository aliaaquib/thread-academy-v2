Numbers and colours are the describing words of everyday life — your age, the time, your favourite red bicycle. Japanese numbers build logically from just a few pieces, and colour words slot neatly in front of nouns. By the end of this chapter you will count to 1,000 and paint the world in Japanese.

This lesson focuses on Counting to Ten: master the numbers 1 to 10, the foundation of all Japanese counting.

## Key ideas

### Two ways to say some numbers

The numbers 4, 7 and 9 each have two readings: し/よん (shi/yon), しち/なな (shichi/nana), きゅう/く (kyū/ku). し sounds like the word for death, so よん is often preferred in everyday counting. In set phrases the traditional reading wins — July is しちがつ (shichigatsu). When in doubt while counting, use よん, なな, きゅう.

### Building bigger numbers

Japanese numbers stack like building blocks: 11 is じゅういち (10 + 1), 20 is にじゅう (2 × 10), 100 is ひゃく. So 25 is にじゅうご (nijūgo) and 300 is さんびゃく (sanbyaku). Learn 1–10 and the pattern does the rest, all the way to せん (sen, 1,000).

**Key term — いち (ichi):** One. The starting point of counting; the numbers 1–10 are いち, に, さん, し/よん, ご, ろく, しち/なな, はち, きゅう/く, じゅう.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Counting to Ten">
  How do you say 7 in Japanese when counting?

  なな (nana).

  **Answer:** なな (nana).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Putting あか directly before a noun** あか りんご ✗ → あかい りんご ✓ (akai ringo, a red apple). Colour words need their い adjective ending before nouns.
  - **Reading きいろ without the long vowel** きいろ (kiiro, yellow) has a long 'ii'. Shortening it to きろ changes the word entirely — stretch that vowel.
</ImportantNote>
