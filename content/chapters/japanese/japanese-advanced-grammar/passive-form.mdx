Advanced Japanese is about nuance: who acts on whom, how politely, and under what conditions. The passive and causative reshape who-does-what, keigo reshapes social distance, and four conditionals carve up 'if' with precision. These structures separate A-level competence from GCSE fluency — master them and your Japanese gains real sophistication.

This lesson focuses on The Passive Form: form and use the passive (〜られる), including the suffering passive.

<Definition term="The Passive Form">
  Form and use the passive (〜られる), including the suffering passive.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Forming the passive

Group 2 (ichidan) verbs: drop る, add られる — たべる → たべられる (taberareru, to be eaten). Group 1 (godan) verbs: change the final -u sound to -a and add れる — かく → かかれる (kakareru, to be written), のむ → のまれる (nomareru, to be drunk). The agent takes に: 母にしかられた (haha ni shikarareta, I was scolded by my mother). Japanese also has the 'suffering passive' for unwelcome events: 雨に降られた (ame ni furareta, I was rained on — and suffered for it).

### The causative and keigo

Causative: Group 2 verbs drop る and add させる (たべる → たべさせる); Group 1 verbs change -u to -a and add せる (かく → かかせる). It means 'make' or 'let' depending on context. Keigo has two directions: 尊敬語 (sonkeigo) raises the other person — いく → いらっしゃる (irassharu); 謙譲語 (kenjōgo) lowers yourself — いく → まいる (mairu). Never use humble forms for the other person's actions — that reverses the respect.

**Key term — うけみ (ukemi):** The passive voice. The doer is marked with に and the verb takes 〜られる: 先生にほめられた (sensei ni homerareta, I was praised by the teacher).

<WorkedExample title="Making the passive of たべる">
  Convert たべる (to eat) into the passive form.

  Identify the verb group: たべる is Group 2 (ichidan) — it ends in える.
  Group 2 rule: drop る and add られる.
  たべる → たべ + られる = たべられる.
  In a sentence, the doer takes に: ケーキが食べられた (kēki ga taberareta, the cake was eaten).

  **Answer:** たべられる (taberareru) — 'to be eaten'.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forming the Group 1 passive like Group 2** かく → かくられる ✗. Change the final -u to -a first: かく → かかれる ✓ (kakareru, to be written).
  - **Using ら抜き言葉 (ranuki)** たべれる ✗ is non-standard slang for the potential form. In exams write たべられる ✓ (taberareru, can eat).
</ImportantNote>
