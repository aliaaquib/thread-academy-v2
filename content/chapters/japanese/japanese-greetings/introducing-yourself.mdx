## Key ideas

### The self-introduction pattern

Introduce yourself with わたしは [name] です (watashi wa [name] desu — I am [name]). So わたしは サラ です means 'I am Sara'. Note the particle は is written 'ha' but pronounced 'wa' — one of Japanese's famous little traps.

### Greetings change with the time of day

Japanese greets the morning with おはよう (ohayō), the day with こんにちは (konnichiwa) and the evening with こんばんは (konbanwa, good evening). Using こんにちは at night sounds as odd as saying 'good morning' at midnight. Listen for which greeting Japanese speakers use and copy them.

**Key term — はじめまして (hajimemashite):** Nice to meet you. Said when meeting someone for the first time, usually followed by your name and よろしくおねがいします (yoroshiku onegaishimasu, please treat me well).

<WorkedExample title="Introducing yourself">
  Introduce yourself in Japanese if your name is Leo.

  Start with はじめまして (hajimemashite) — nice to meet you.
  Say your name with the pattern わたしは [name] です: わたしは レオ です.
  Finish with よろしくおねがいします (yoroshiku onegaishimasu) — please treat me well.
  Add a small bow as you speak.

  **Answer:** はじめまして。わたしは レオ です。よろしくおねがいします。(Hajimemashite. Watashi wa Reo desu. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.) — Nice to meet you. I am Leo. Please treat me well.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting はじめまして when meeting someone new** Jumping straight to your name skips the ritual. はじめまして first, then わたしは…です, then よろしくおねがいします.
  - **Saying さようなら for a quick goodbye** さようなら sounds final, like leaving for a long time. For 'see you later', use またね (mata ne) or じゃあね (jā ne) with friends.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say 'good evening' in Japanese?" hint="It starts with こん, like こんにちは.">
    こんばんは (konbanwa).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does ありがとう mean?" hint="You say it when someone gives you something.">
    Thank you.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Translate into Japanese: 'I am Mia.'" hint="Use わたしは…です. Mia in katakana is ミア.">
    わたしは ミア です。(Watashi wa Mia desu.).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your friend is leaving school and you will see them tomorrow. Should you say さようなら?" hint="さようなら is for long farewells.">
    No — use またね (mata ne) or またあした (mata ashita, see you tomorrow).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
