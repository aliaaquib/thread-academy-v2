## Key ideas

### Stroke order rules

Write top to bottom and left to right, horizontal strokes before vertical ones, and finish with the closing bottom stroke of a box. The kanji 日 (ひ, hi, sun/day) is written: left vertical, top horizontal plus right side, middle horizontal, closing bottom stroke. Good order is not decoration — it makes handwriting legible and helps you memorise characters.

### How the three scripts divide the work

Kanji carry the meaning cores of nouns and verb stems: 食べる (たべる, taberu, to eat). Hiragana supplies grammar — particles and verb endings, like the べる in 食べる. Katakana handles foreign words: コーヒー (kōhī, coffee). A single sentence like わたしはコーヒーを飲みます (watashi wa kōhī wo nomimasu, I drink coffee) shows all three working together.

**Key term — かんじ (kanji):** Characters borrowed from Chinese, each carrying meaning as well as sound. Around 2,000 are needed for everyday reading, but you start with a handful.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Where Kanji Came From">
  Read the kanji compound 日本.

  にほん (nihon) — Japan.

  **Answer:** にほん (nihon) — Japan.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Ignoring stroke order** Drawing kanji in any order produces wobbly, unbalanced characters that natives find hard to read. Learn the top-to-bottom, left-to-right rules from your very first kanji.
  - **Reading compounds with kun'yomi** 日本人 as にほんひと ✗. In multi-kanji compounds, switch to on'yomi: にほんじん ✓.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which script writes the verb ending in 食べます?" hint="Grammar endings are never kanji.">
    Hiragana: the べます part.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the reading of 山 in 富士山 (Mt Fuji)?" hint="Compounds use on'yomi.">
    さん (san) — the on'yomi. Alone it reads やま (yama).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which reading type is やま for 山?" hint="It is the native Japanese word.">
    Kun'yomi.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How should コーヒー (coffee) be written — with う or ー?" hint="Katakana long vowels use a special dash.">
    With ー: コーヒー.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
