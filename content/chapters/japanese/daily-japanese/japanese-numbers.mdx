## Numbers

1–10 then combine: 十一 (11) = ten-one, 二十 (20) = two-ten. But ask ‘how many’ and you’ll meet counters — different words for people, animals, flat things…

<Definition term="Counter">
  **Counters** are measure words used when counting: 人 (nin) for people, 匹 (hiki) for small animals, 枚 (mai) for flat things. Number + counter + noun: うさぎ三匹 (three rabbits).
</Definition>

## Key points

- 1–10: いち に さん し/よん ご ろく なな/しち はち きゅう じゅう.
- 11 = じゅういち, 20 = にじゅう, 100 = ひゃく.
- People: 一人 (hitori, 1 person — special!), 二人 (futari), then san-nin…
- Money: 円 (en/yuan sign ¥) — 三百円 (sanbyaku-en) = 300 yen.

<Example title="At the shop">
  — これはいくらですか?
— 五百円です。
(How much is this? — 500 yen.)
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say 25 in Japanese?" hint="Two-ten-five.">
    二十五 (nijūgo).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a counter? Give an example." hint="Measure words.">
    A word used with numbers when counting categories — e.g. 枚 (mai) for flat things like paper or shirts.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Numbers combine logically like Chinese.
  - Counters categorise what you count.
  - Hitori/futari are special (1/2 people).
  - 円 (en) = yen.
</Summary>
