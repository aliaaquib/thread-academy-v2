## Key ideas

### Telling the time

Hours are number + じ: いちじ (1 o'clock), にじ (2 o'clock). Watch the irregulars: 4 o'clock is よじ (yoji), 7 is しちじ (shichiji), 9 is くじ (kuji). Half past adds はん: さんじはん (sanjihan, half past three). Minutes use ふん, which changes sound after some numbers: いっぷん (1 min), さんぷん (3 min), ろっぷん (6 min), じゅっぷん (10 min). Examiners love testing these sound changes.

### Routine verbs with time phrases

Daily verbs take the polite ます form: おきます (okimasu, wake up), たべます (tabemasu, eat), ねます (nemasu, go to bed). The time of an action takes the particle に: しちじに おきます (shichiji ni okimasu, I wake up at 7). A full routine sentence chains them: まいにち しちじに おきます (mainichi shichiji ni okimasu, I wake up at 7 every day).

**Key term — じかん (jikan):** Time; hour. Attach じ to a number for o'clock: さんじ (sanji, 3 o'clock).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Telling the Time">
  How do you say '10 minutes'?

  じゅっぷん (juppun).

  **Answer:** じゅっぷん (juppun).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying 4 o'clock as しじ** The reading is よじ (yoji): しじ ✗ → よじ ✓. The irregular hours — よじ, しちじ, くじ — are exam favourites, so memorise them.
  - **Using に with relative time words** あした (ashita, tomorrow) and まいにち (mainichi, every day) never take に: あしたに ✗ → あした ✓. Only clock times and specific dates take に.
</ImportantNote>
