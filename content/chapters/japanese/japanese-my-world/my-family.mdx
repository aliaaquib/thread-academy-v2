Language is for talking about your life — your family, your school, the things you love. This chapter builds the vocabulary of your world in Japanese, from かぞく (family) to しゅみ (hobbies). You will also learn to describe people and say what you like, the two skills every conversation needs.

This lesson focuses on Talking About Family: learn family words and the humble terms for your own relatives.

<Definition term="Talking About Family">
  Learn family words and the humble terms for your own relatives.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Two sets of family words

Japanese has humble words for your own family and respectful words for others'. Your mother is はは (haha) when you talk about her, but someone else's mother is おかあさん (okāsan). Mixing them up — calling your own mother おかあさん to a stranger — is a classic error that instantly marks you as a learner.

### Saying what you like

好き works differently from the English verb 'like': the liked thing is marked with が, not を. サッカーが すきです (sakkā ga suki desu) is literally 'football is likeable to me' → 'I like football'. Remember が with すき and きらい (kirai, dislike), and the pattern will serve you for years.

**Key term — かぞく (kazoku):** Family. When speaking about your own family to outsiders, use humble terms: ちち (chichi, my father), はは (haha, my mother).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Talking About Family">
  What is the humble word for 'my father'?

  ちち (chichi).

  **Answer:** ちち (chichi).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling your own mother おかあさん to outsiders** Use the humble はは (haha) for your own family when speaking to others; save おかあさん for other people's mothers.
  - **Using を with すき** サッカーを すきです ✗ → サッカーが すきです ✓. The liked thing always takes が.
</ImportantNote>
