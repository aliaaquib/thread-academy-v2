## Conformity

Humans are social animals to the core: exclusion once meant death. So the pull to agree with the group runs deeper than logic.

<Definition term="Conformity">
  **Conformity** is changing your behaviour or beliefs to match a group — either to be liked (normative influence) or because you assume the group knows better (informational influence).
</Definition>

## Key points

- Asch (1951): 37% of answers conformed to a unanimous wrong majority on an easy line-judgement task.
- One ally breaks the spell — dissent collapses conformity.
- Normative: conforming to fit in. Informational: conforming to be right.
- Culture matters: collectivist societies conform more.

<Example title="The lone dissenter">
  When Asch added one confederate who gave the right answer, conformity fell from 37% to about 5%. It only takes one person to make disagreement thinkable.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish normative and informational social influence." hint="Liked vs right.">
    Normative: conforming to be accepted/liked. Informational: conforming because you believe the group has better information.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is Asch’s study ethically questionable?" hint="What didn’t participants know?">
    Participants were deceived about the study’s purpose and placed under social stress without full informed consent — though it was mild and debriefed.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - People conform even when they know the group is wrong.
  - Motives: fitting in (normative) or assuming the group knows (informational).
  - A single ally dramatically reduces conformity.
  - Deception in research needs ethical justification.
</Summary>
