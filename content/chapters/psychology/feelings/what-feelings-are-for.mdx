</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Feelings are messages

Every feeling is trying to tell you something. Anger can mean something feels unfair, sadness can mean you need comfort, and nervousness can mean you want to be careful. When you listen to the message, you can decide what to do next instead of just reacting.

### Big feelings are normal

Everyone has huge feelings sometimes, even grown-ups. Feelings are like waves: they rise, they crash, and then they pass. Knowing that a feeling will not last for ever makes it easier to ride it out.

**Key term — emotion:** A feeling your brain creates to tell you something important, such as happiness when something good happens or sadness when something is lost.

<WorkedExample title="Helping a friend with big feelings">
  Imagine this: your friend Sam has spent ages building a tower, and it suddenly falls down. He is shouting and crying. What could you do to help?

  Notice the clues: shouting, tears and a red face tell you Sam feels angry and upset.
  Name the feeling kindly: you could say, 'You seem really angry that your tower fell.'.
  Offer a calm-down tool: suggest taking three slow breaths together or stepping away for a moment.
  Wait and listen: let Sam talk about what happened before trying to fix it.
  Help rebuild: ask if he would like help starting again, and accept it calmly if he says not yet.

  **Answer:** Sam's anger is a message that something upsetting happened to him. By naming the feeling, offering a calm-down tool and listening first, you show empathy and give him space to settle — which works far better than telling him to stop crying.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Feelings you hide will just go away** Hidden feelings usually grow bigger and pop out later. Naming a feeling out loud or drawing it helps it shrink.
  - **Being angry is always bad** Anger itself is a normal message, like a warning light. What matters is what you do with it — words and pauses are safe, shouting and hitting are not.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three feelings and one body clue for each." hint="Think about what your body does when you are happy, nervous or angry.">
    Answers vary, but could include: happy → smiling and bouncy energy; nervous → wobbly tummy; angry → hot face and clenched fists.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might hiding your feelings make things worse?" hint="Think about what happens to a feeling you never name.">
    Hidden feelings tend to grow and burst out later, often at the wrong moment. Naming them helps them settle.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="True or false: it is okay to feel angry sometimes." hint="Think about whether anger is a message or a misbehaviour.">
    True. Anger is a normal feeling; what matters is expressing it safely.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two calm-down tools you could use when a big feeling arrives." hint="Think of things that slow your body down.">
    Any two: slow breathing, counting to ten, drawing the feeling, squeezing a soft toy, asking a trusted adult for help.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
