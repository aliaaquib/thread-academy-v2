## Child Development

Watch a toddler insist the tall thin glass holds more juice than the short wide one. They’re not being silly — their thinking genuinely works differently from yours.

<Definition term="Attachment">
  **Attachment** is the deep emotional bond between infant and caregiver (Bowlby). Secure attachment — from responsive caregiving — becomes a template for later relationships.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Piaget’s stages: sensorimotor → preoperational → concrete operational → formal operational.
- Preoperational children are egocentric and fooled by appearances (conservation tasks).
- Bowlby: attachment is an evolved survival system; Ainsworth’s ‘strange situation’ classified secure/avoidant/resistant styles.
- The prefrontal cortex — judgement, impulse control — isn’t fully wired until the mid-20s.

<Example title="The marshmallow test">
  Mischel offered children one marshmallow now or two if they waited. Waiters tended to do better later in life — though later work shows the test also measures trust and background, not just willpower.
</Example>

## Practice

  <PracticeItem question="Why does a 4-year-old fail the conservation task?" hint="Appearances dominate.">
    In the preoperational stage, children centre on one striking feature (height) and can’t mentally reverse the transformation.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is secure attachment, and why does it matter?" hint="Responsive caregiving.">
    A bond built on reliable, responsive care; it gives the child a ‘secure base’ to explore from and templates healthy later relationships.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Children’s thinking develops in stages (Piaget).
  - Attachment bonds shape emotional development (Bowlby).
  - The brain — especially judgement centres — develops into the mid-20s.
  - Development is nature and nurture intertwined.
</Summary>
