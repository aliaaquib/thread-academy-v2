Grammar unlocks fluency: this chapter systematises the present tense across all verb groups, the indispensable irregular verbs, noun gender and adjective agreement — the backbone of GCSE French.

This lesson focuses on The Present Tense: conjugate -er, -ir and -re verbs.

<Definition term="The Present Tense">
  Conjugate -er, -ir and -re verbs.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Three verb groups

-er verbs (parler) are regular; most -ir verbs (finir) add -ss- in the plural forms; -re verbs (vendre: je vends, nous vendons) follow their own pattern. Reflexive verbs add me/te/se: je me lève (I get up).

### Irregulars to memorise

Aller, avoir, être, faire, prendre (je prends), venir (je viens), pouvoir and vouloir appear constantly. Faire expressions: faire attention (to pay attention), faire la cuisine (to cook). Venir de + infinitive means 'to have just done': je viens de manger (I have just eaten).

**Key term — finir:** To finish — the model regular -ir verb: je finis, tu finis, il finit, nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent.

<WorkedExample title="Using 'venir de'">
  Translate: 'We have just finished.'.

  Take the 'nous' form of venir: nous venons.
  Add 'de'.
  'Finished' is the past participle of finir: fini.
  Assemble: Nous venons de finir.

  **Answer:** Nous venons de finir.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting the reflexive pronoun** Je me lève needs both the pronoun and the verb — 'je lève' means you lift something else!
  - **Using 'de la' with masculine nouns** Du pain (masculine), de la confiture (feminine) — match the partitive to the noun's gender.
</ImportantNote>
