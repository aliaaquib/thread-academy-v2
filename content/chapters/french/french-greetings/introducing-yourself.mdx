## Key ideas

### Introducing yourself

Je m'appelle… gives your name, J'ai… ans gives your age — note French uses 'I have' for age: J'ai dix ans (I am ten). J'habite à… says where you live: J'habite à Londres. Enchanté(e) means 'pleased to meet you' — add an extra e if you are female: enchantée.

### Bonjour and beyond

Bonjour works until evening, when bonsoir takes over — bonsoir covers both 'good evening' and 'good night' as a greeting. Salut is for peers; bonjour is the safe default with adults. Add Comment ça va? (How's it going?) to sound natural.

**Key term — je m'appelle…:** 'My name is…' — e.g. Je m'appelle Léo. The verb s'appeler is reflexive.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Introducing Yourself">
  How do you say 'my name is Emma'?

  Je m'appelle Emma.

  **Answer:** Je m'appelle Emma.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying 'je suis neuf ans'** Age uses avoir: J'ai neuf ans (I have nine years).
  - **Using 'salut' with a teacher** Salut is informal — say Bonjour, madame / monsieur to adults.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What do you say when entering a shop in the morning?" hint="Not 'salut'.">
    Bonjour !
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say you are 7 years old?" hint="Use avoir.">
    J'ai sept ans.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="When do you switch from 'bonjour' to 'bonsoir'?" hint="Evening.">
    In the evening — bonsoir is both 'good evening' and 'good night' as a greeting.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the informal way to say 'please'?" hint="S'il te…">
    S'il te plaît (to friends); s'il vous plaît to adults.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
