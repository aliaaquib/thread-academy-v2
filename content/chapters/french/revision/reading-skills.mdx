## Key ideas

### Reading exam tactics

Skim for gist, then scan for detail; do not translate everything. Cognates and context unlock unknown words. Watch for negatives (ne…pas, ne…jamais) which flip meaning — the commonest trap in the reading paper.

### Writing for marks

Plan each answer: opinion + reason + example + a future or conditional flourish. Vary tenses, add connectives (cependant, en plus, donc) and check agreements at the end. Accuracy beats showing off.

**Key term — le passé composé:** The perfect tense — j'ai mangé, je suis allé(e). Most verbs use avoir; verbs of movement and reflexives use être.

<WorkedExample title="Spotting the trap">
  A text says: 'Je ne vais jamais au cinéma.' Does the writer go to the cinema?

  Find the verb: vais (aller).
  Spot the negative: ne…jamais means 'never'.
  Combine: 'I never go to the cinema.'.
  Answer: no.

  **Answer:** No — ne…jamais means 'never', so the writer never goes to the cinema.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Listing opinions without justifying** 'J'aime le foot' alone scores little — add parce que and an example.
  - **Writing 'je suis mangé'** Manger takes avoir: j'ai mangé. Only movement and reflexive verbs take être.
</ImportantNote>

    Être: je suis allé(e).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does 'actuellement' mean?" hint="A false friend.">
    'Currently' — not 'actually'.
  </PracticeItem>
