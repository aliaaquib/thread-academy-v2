Advanced French expresses subtlety: wishes, doubts, hypotheses and elegant argument. This chapter covers the subjunctive, the conditional, the pronouns y and en, and the architecture of complex sentences.

This lesson focuses on The Subjunctive: express doubt, desire and emotion.

<Definition term="The Subjunctive">
  Express doubt, desire and emotion.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### The subjunctive in action

Triggers: wanting (vouloir que), doubt (douter que), necessity (il faut que), emotion (avoir peur que), and conjunctions (pour que, avant que, bien que). Formation: take the 'ils' present stem and add -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent (parler → que je parle); key irregulars: être → sois, avoir → aie, aller → aille.

### Conditionals and hypotheses

Present conditional: infinitive + imperfect endings (je parlerais). Si-clauses follow strict patterns: si + present → future (s'il pleut, je resterai); si + imperfect → conditional (s'il pleuvait, je resterais); si + pluperfect → past conditional (regrets). Never put the conditional after si.

**Key term — le subjonctif:** The subjunctive mood — after vouloir que, douter que, il faut que: il faut que tu viennes (you must come).

<WorkedExample title="A doubt in the subjunctive">
  Translate: 'I doubt he is coming.'.

  'I doubt' = je doute que — this triggers the subjunctive.
  There are two different subjects, so the subjunctive is required.
  Venir in the subjunctive: qu'il vienne.
  Assemble: Je doute qu'il vienne.

  **Answer:** Je doute qu'il vienne.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using the indicative after 'il faut que'** Il faut que tu fasses — necessity always takes the subjunctive.
  - **Putting the conditional after 'si'** 'Si j'aurais' is the classic error — si takes the imperfect: si j'avais.
</ImportantNote>
