## Key ideas

### Pure vowels

French vowels do not glide like English ones: é is a clean 'ay' (café), ou is 'oo' (vous), and u is the tricky tight-lipped sound (tu) — say 'ee' with rounded lips. Practise minimal pairs like dessus and dessous to hear the difference.

### The four nasal vowels

On (bon), an/en (vent), in (matin) and un (brun) are single sounds made through the nose — do not pronounce the n! In southern France un and in have merged, but standard French keeps all four distinct.

**Key term — voyelle:** Vowel. French vowels are pure and short: a, e, i, o, u each keep one clear sound, unlike English vowels which glide.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Vowel Sounds">
  How many nasal vowel sounds are there in standard French?

  Four.

  **Answer:** Four.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying nasal vowels as vowel + n** Bon is one nasal sound, not 'bone' — keep the air in your nose and skip the n.
  - **Pronouncing the final s in 'Paris'** It is 'pa-REE' — final consonants are usually silent in French.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a liaison? Give an example." hint="Think 'les amis'.">
    Pronouncing a normally silent final consonant before a vowel: les‿amis ('lay-za-MEE').
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you make the French 'u' sound in 'tu'?" hint="Lips like 'oo', tongue like 'ee'.">
    Round your lips as for 'oo' while saying 'ee'.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which letters are often pronounced at the end of French words?" hint="CaReFuL.">
    C, R, F and L — as in sac, fleur, neuf, bal.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you pronounce the letter é?" hint="Like the end of 'café'.">
    As a clear 'ay' sound.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
