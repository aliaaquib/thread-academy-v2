## Hola — say hello

Spanish greetings change with the time of day. Learn these five and you're covered everywhere:

- **¡Hola!** — Hello! (any time)
- **Buenos días** — Good morning (until midday)
- **Buenas tardes** — Good afternoon (midday to evening)
- **Buenas noches** — Good evening / good night
- **Adiós** — Goodbye

<ImportantNote title="Pronunciation is consistent">
  Spanish is wonderfully phonetic: letters (almost) always sound the same.
  **H is always silent** (hola = "ola"), **j** sounds like a strong English "h"
  (jamón = "ha-MON"), and **ñ** is "ny" (mañana = "man-YA-na").
</ImportantNote>

## Me llamo… — introductions

- **Me llamo…** — My name is… (literally "I call myself…")
- **¿Cómo te llamas?** — What's your name? (informal)
- **Soy…** — I am… (Soy Aaquib.)
- **¿Cómo estás?** — How are you? → **Estoy bien, gracias** — I'm fine, thanks.
- **Mucho gusto** — Nice to meet you.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: a first conversation">
  ```text
  A: ¡Hola! Me llamo Sara. ¿Cómo te llamas?
  B: Me llamo Tom. Mucho gusto.
  A: Igualmente. ¿Cómo estás?
  B: Estoy bien, gracias.
  ```

  *Hello! I'm Sara. What's your name? — I'm Tom. Nice to meet you. — Likewise. How are you? — I'm fine, thanks.*
</WorkedExample>

Notice **¿** and **¡** — Spanish marks questions and exclamations at the *start* too, so readers know the tone coming.

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How would you greet someone at 4pm? At 9am?" hint="Afternoon vs morning.">
    4pm → Buenas tardes. 9am → Buenos días.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Introduce yourself in two Spanish sentences." hint="Me llamo… + Soy… or ¿Cómo te llamas?">
    Example: "Me llamo Aaquib. Soy estudiante." (My name is Aaquib. I'm a student.)
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="The H in 'hola' is… pronounced?" hint="Check the pronunciation note.">
    Silent — "hola" sounds like "ola".
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
