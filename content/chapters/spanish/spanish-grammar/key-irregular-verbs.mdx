## Key ideas

### Irregulars you cannot avoid

Ser, estar, tener, ir and hacer appear constantly. Tener expressions: tener hambre (to be hungry), tener razón (to be right), tener que + infinitive (to have to). Ir a + infinitive gives the near future: Voy a viajar — I am going to travel.

### The three conjugations

-ar (hablar), -er (comer) and -ir (vivir) verbs share most endings; only the 'we' and 'you plural' forms differ. Learn one verb of each type and you can conjugate hundreds. Stem-changing verbs (pensar → pienso, pedir → pido) change in every form except nosotros and vosotros.

**Key term — tener:** To have — irregular: tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen. Used for age, hunger (tengo hambre) and obligation (tener que + infinitive).

<WorkedExample title="Using 'tener que'">
  Translate: 'I have to do my homework today.'.

  'I have to' = tengo que.
  'Do' = hacer.
  'My homework' = mis deberes.
  'Today' = hoy.
  Assemble: Tengo que hacer mis deberes hoy.

  **Answer:** Tengo que hacer mis deberes hoy.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Conjugating the second verb** With two verbs together, conjugate the first and leave the second as an infinitive: quiero comer, not 'quiero como'.
  - **Saying 'la problema'** Problema is masculine despite the -a ending: el problema.
</ImportantNote>
