This lesson focuses on Daily Life: explore routines, meals and social habits.

## Key ideas

### Rhythms of the day

Spaniards eat lunch (la comida) around 2–3pm and dinner (la cena) at 9–10pm — later than northern Europe. The sobremesa stretches meals into social events. Timetables reflect climate and history, though globalisation is shifting them.

### Fiestas and identity

Festivals blend Catholic, indigenous and local traditions: Las Fallas in Valencia, Semana Santa processions, Día de Muertos in Mexico. They are living expressions of regional and national identity — and major tourist draws.

**Key term — la sobremesa:** The leisurely chat around the table after a meal — a cherished social ritual in Spain and Latin America.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Daily Life">
  Why do Spaniards eat dinner so late?

  Long working days split by a late lunch, warm evenings, and social habits centred on late dining.

  **Answer:** Long working days split by a late lunch, warm evenings, and social habits centred on late dining.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Generalising about 'all Spaniards'** Spain and Latin America are hugely diverse — qualify claims by region.
  - **Calling the siesta laziness** It is a cultural rhythm tied to climate and long evenings, not indolence.
</ImportantNote>
