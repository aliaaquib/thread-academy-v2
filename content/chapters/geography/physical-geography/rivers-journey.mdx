## Key ideas

### A river changes along its course

In the upper course, steep slopes give the river energy to erode downwards, cutting V-shaped valleys and waterfalls. In the middle course the valley widens and the river begins to meander. In the lower course the gentle slope means deposition dominates, building floodplains and deltas at the mouth.

### Four processes of erosion

Hydraulic action is the force of the water itself; abrasion is material scraping against rock like sandpaper; attrition is pebbles knocking together and becoming smaller and rounder; solution is minerals dissolving in slightly acidic water. All four act together in rivers and at the coast.

**Key term — meander:** A bend in a river, formed where faster water on the outside of the bend erodes and slower water on the inside deposits.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: A River's Journey">
  In which course of a river would you find a waterfall?

  The upper course.

  **Answer:** The upper course.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking deposition only happens at the mouth** Deposition happens wherever a river loses energy — on the inside of bends, behind obstacles, and across floodplains in floods, not just at the mouth.
  - **Confusing erosion with weathering** Weathering breaks rock in place; erosion moves it. Freeze-thaw is weathering, but a river carrying pebbles away is erosion.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the four processes of river erosion." hint="One is the force of water, one is scraping, one is pebbles colliding, one is dissolving.">
    Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does erosion happen on the outside of a meander bend?" hint="Think about where the water flows fastest.">
    Water flows fastest on the outside of the bend, giving it more energy to erode the outer bank.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is deposited on the inside of a meander bend, and what landform does it create?" hint="Slow water drops its load here.">
    Sand and shingle are deposited, building a gently sloping slip-off slope (point bar).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one difference between weathering and erosion." hint="One moves material, the other does not.">
    Weathering breaks rock down in place; erosion wears it away and transports it.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
