## Key ideas

### Four processes of erosion

Hydraulic action is the force of the water itself; abrasion is material scraping against rock like sandpaper; attrition is pebbles knocking together and becoming smaller and rounder; solution is minerals dissolving in slightly acidic water. All four act together in rivers and at the coast.

### Erosion is not weathering

Weathering breaks rock down where it sits — for example, freeze-thaw shattering or rainwater dissolving limestone. Erosion then picks the broken material up and moves it. At the coast, both work together: weathering weakens a cliff while waves erode its base.

**Key term — erosion:** The wearing away of rock and soil by moving water, wind or ice, which then carries the material away.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Erosion Processes">
  Name the four processes of river erosion.

  Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution.

  **Answer:** Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing erosion with weathering** Weathering breaks rock in place; erosion moves it. Freeze-thaw is weathering, but a river carrying pebbles away is erosion.
  - **Saying the fastest flow is on the inside of the bend** It is the outside of the bend: faster water erodes the outer bank, while slower water on the inside deposits to form a slip-off slope.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one difference between weathering and erosion." hint="One moves material, the other does not.">
    Weathering breaks rock down in place; erosion wears it away and transports it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A cliff is being undercut by waves at its base. Which process is at work, and what might happen next?" hint="The force of the water is doing the work.">
    Hydraulic action (with abrasion). The overhang will eventually collapse, causing the cliff to retreat.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does erosion happen on the outside of a meander bend?" hint="Think about where the water flows fastest.">
    Water flows fastest on the outside of the bend, giving it more energy to erode the outer bank.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="In which course of a river would you find a waterfall?" hint="Think about where the slope is steepest.">
    The upper course.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
