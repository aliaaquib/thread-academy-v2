</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Scale turns centimetres into kilometres

On a 1:50,000 map, 1 cm represents 50,000 cm on the ground — 500 m. So 4 cm on the map is 2 km in reality. On a 1:25,000 map, 1 cm equals 250 m, which is why these maps show more detail and are popular with walkers.

### Along the corridor, then up the stairs

Grid references always give eastings first, then northings. Imagine walking along a corridor (across the map) before climbing stairs (up the map). A four-figure reference like 4738 names the 1 km square whose south-west corner is at easting 47 and northing 38.

**Key term — scale:** The ratio between a distance on the map and the same distance on the ground, for example 1:50,000.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Map Scale">
  On a 1:25,000 map, what real distance does 8 cm represent?

  8 × 250 m = 2,000 m, which is 2 km.

  **Answer:** 8 × 250 m = 2,000 m, which is 2 km.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Misreading the scale** On a 1:50,000 map, 1 cm = 500 m, not 50 m or 5 km. Always convert the ratio into a distance you can picture before calculating.
  - **Giving northings before eastings** It must be eastings first — 'along the corridor, then up the stairs'. Reversing them points to a completely different square.
</ImportantNote>
