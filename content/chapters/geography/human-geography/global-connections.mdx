## Key ideas

### Cities grow in two ways

Urban growth comes from rural–urban migration and from natural increase, when birth rates exceed death rates. In many poorer countries both are happening at once, so cities are expanding rapidly. Urbanisation is different from urban growth: it measures the rising share of people living in urban areas, not just bigger cities.

### Distribution is uneven for good reasons

People cluster where conditions suit them: fertile plains, navigable rivers, coasts and mineral resources attract settlement, while deserts, high mountains and frozen lands stay sparsely populated. In the UK, the south-east is densely settled while the Scottish Highlands are sparse — relief, climate and economic history all play a part.

**Key term — globalisation:** The growing interconnectedness of the world through trade, transport, communication and culture.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Global Connections">
  What is the difference between internal and international migration?

  Internal migration is within one country; international migration is between countries.

  **Answer:** Internal migration is within one country; international migration is between countries.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing population density with total population** A country can have a huge population but low density if it is vast, like Canada. Density is people per square kilometre, not the total headcount.
  - **Calling every move 'migration'** Moving house within the same town is mobility, not migration. Geographers usually mean a longer-distance, longer-term move, and distinguish internal migration from international migration.
</ImportantNote>
