## Key ideas

### Weather changes, seasons repeat

Weather can change from morning to afternoon, but the seasons follow the same pattern every year. In the UK, summers tend to be warmer with longer days and winters colder with shorter days. Recording the weather every day helps you spot these patterns.

### Instruments measure the weather

A thermometer measures temperature, a rain gauge collects and measures rainfall, a wind vane shows wind direction and an anemometer measures how fast the wind blows. Using instruments turns 'it feels cold' into a number you can compare.

**Key term — weather:** What the air outside is like at a particular time: its sunshine, cloud, rain, wind and temperature.

<WorkedExample title="Reading a week's weather diary">
  Your class recorded the weather each day: Mon — sunny; Tue — sunny; Wed — cloudy; Thu — rainy; Fri — rainy; Sat — windy and rainy; Sun — sunny. Which days were wet, and how many sunny days were there?

  List the days with rain: Thursday, Friday and Saturday.
  Saturday had both wind and rain, but it still counts as a wet day.
  Count the sunny days: Monday, Tuesday and Sunday.
  Wednesday was cloudy but neither sunny nor wet.

  **Answer:** Thursday, Friday and Saturday were wet. There were three sunny days: Monday, Tuesday and Sunday.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying 'the weather is climate'** Weather is what is happening today; climate is the usual pattern over many years. A cold day in summer does not change the climate.
  - **Thinking summer happens because Earth is nearer the Sun** The seasons are caused by the tilt of the Earth's axis, not by distance from the Sun — the whole planet shares the same orbit.
</ImportantNote>
