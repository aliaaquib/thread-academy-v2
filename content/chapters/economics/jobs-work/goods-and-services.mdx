</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Goods and services

A baker makes goods you can touch and eat; a doctor provides a service you experience but cannot hold. Most jobs produce one or the other, and many businesses provide both — a restaurant sells food (goods) plus cooking and serving (services).

### Why people work

Most people work to earn money for their needs and wants, but work gives more than pay. It provides purpose, lets people use their talents, builds new skills and connects them with others. Unpaid work, like caring for family or volunteering, is valuable too.

**Key term — goods:** Physical things that are made and bought, such as bread, bicycles and books.

<WorkedExample title="Sorting the school's jobs">
  List five jobs in a school and sort each into goods, services, or both.

  Teacher — provides a service (teaching).
  Cook — produces goods (meals) and a service (serving).
  Caretaker — provides a service (repairs, maintenance).
  Librarian — provides a service (helping readers).
  Cleaner — provides a service (cleaning).

  **Answer:** Most school jobs provide services; the cook also produces goods (the meals). Services dominate because schools exist to help pupils learn.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking only paid work counts** Unpaid work like parenting and volunteering is real work that creates enormous value.
  - **Believing easy-looking jobs need no skill** Every job needs skills — a cleaner needs knowledge of chemicals and safety; a delivery driver needs navigation and time management.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one reason people work apart from money." hint="Think about purpose or people.">
    Any one of: purpose, using talents, learning skills, meeting people, helping others.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a wage and a salary?" hint="Think about how often each is paid.">
    Wages are usually paid weekly or hourly; salaries are usually paid monthly.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is a haircut a good or a service?" hint="Can you hold it?">
    A service — you experience it but cannot hold or store it.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A farmer grows wheat. Is wheat a good or a service?" hint="It is physical.">
    A good — it is a physical product.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
