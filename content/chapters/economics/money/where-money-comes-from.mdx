## Key ideas

### Earning and looking after money

Money usually comes from work — people are paid wages for the jobs they do. A budget helps you plan: set aside savings first, spend on needs next, and use what is left for wants. Keep money safe by never sharing bank details and being careful with cash.

### Why money exists

Long ago people bartered, swapping goods directly, but barter needs both people to want what the other offers. Money solved this: everyone accepts it, it divides into small amounts, and it keeps its value. That is why a £5 note is useful even though the paper itself is nearly worthless.

**Key term — barter:** Exchanging goods or services directly without money, such as swapping a toy for a book. It only works if each person wants what the other offers.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Where Money Comes From">
  Why do people trust a £10 note?

  Because everyone agrees to accept it in exchange for goods and services — its value comes from shared trust.

  **Answer:** Because everyone agrees to accept it in exchange for goods and services — its value comes from shared trust.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking money appears on its own** Money comes from work or business — even savings only grow because someone earned the money first.
  - **Confusing price with value** A high price does not always mean high quality; compare before you buy.
</ImportantNote>
