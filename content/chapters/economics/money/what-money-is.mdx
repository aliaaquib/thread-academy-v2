Money is one of humanity's cleverest inventions: a small disc of metal or a number on a screen can be swapped for food, toys or a haircut. This chapter explains what money is, how we use it every day, where it comes from, and how to look after it.

This lesson focuses on What Money Is: learn what money is and why everyone agrees to accept it.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Why money exists

Long ago people bartered, swapping goods directly, but barter needs both people to want what the other offers. Money solved this: everyone accepts it, it divides into small amounts, and it keeps its value. That is why a £5 note is useful even though the paper itself is nearly worthless.

### How we pay today

Cash is notes and coins you can hold. Most adults also pay with debit cards, phones or online banking, which move money electronically. However the money moves, the idea is identical: you give up some of your money to get something you want.

**Key term — barter:** Exchanging goods or services directly without money, such as swapping a toy for a book. It only works if each person wants what the other offers.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Money Is">
  Why do people trust a £10 note?

  Because everyone agrees to accept it in exchange for goods and services — its value comes from shared trust.

  **Answer:** Because everyone agrees to accept it in exchange for goods and services — its value comes from shared trust.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking money appears on its own** Money comes from work or business — even savings only grow because someone earned the money first.
  - **Confusing price with value** A high price does not always mean high quality; compare before you buy.
</ImportantNote>

  <PracticeItem question="Why is barter inconvenient?" hint="Think about what both swappers need.">
    It needs a double coincidence of wants — each person must want exactly what the other offers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the currency of the UK?" hint="It starts with 'pound'.">
    The pound sterling (£).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A toy costs £12 and you have £10. How much more do you need?" hint="Subtract.">
    £2 more (£12 − £10).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two ways of paying without using cash." hint="Think about cards and phones.">
    Debit card and mobile phone payment (also: credit card, online bank transfer).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
