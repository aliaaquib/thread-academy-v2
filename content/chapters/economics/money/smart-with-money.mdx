## Key ideas

### Earning and looking after money

Money usually comes from work — people are paid wages for the jobs they do. A budget helps you plan: set aside savings first, spend on needs next, and use what is left for wants. Keep money safe by never sharing bank details and being careful with cash.

### Why money exists

Long ago people bartered, swapping goods directly, but barter needs both people to want what the other offers. Money solved this: everyone accepts it, it divides into small amounts, and it keeps its value. That is why a £5 note is useful even though the paper itself is nearly worthless.

**Key term — budget:** A simple plan for using your money, dividing it between spending and saving so it lasts.

<WorkedExample title="Mia's lemonade stall">
  Mia sells 20 cups of lemonade at 50p each. Lemons and sugar cost her £3. What is her profit, and how could she split it?

  Work out revenue: 20 cups × 50p = 1,000p = £10.
  Subtract costs: £10 − £3 = £7 profit.
  Split wisely: save £4 for next week's ingredients, spend £3 on something she wants.
  Check: £4 + £3 = £7, which matches the profit.

  **Answer:** Mia makes £7 profit (£10 revenue minus £3 costs). Saving £4 and spending £3 is a sensible split.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking money appears on its own** Money comes from work or business — even savings only grow because someone earned the money first.
  - **Spending before saving** Save first, then spend what is left — otherwise there is rarely anything left to save.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is barter inconvenient?" hint="Think about what both swappers need.">
    It needs a double coincidence of wants — each person must want exactly what the other offers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the currency of the UK?" hint="It starts with 'pound'.">
    The pound sterling (£).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A toy costs £12 and you have £10. How much more do you need?" hint="Subtract.">
    £2 more (£12 − £10).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two ways of paying without using cash." hint="Think about cards and phones.">
    Debit card and mobile phone payment (also: credit card, online bank transfer).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
