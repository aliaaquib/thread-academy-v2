## Key ideas

### How taxes work

Income tax takes a share of earnings, VAT adds a percentage to the price of most goods, and corporation tax falls on company profits. Progressive taxes take a bigger share from higher earners; regressive taxes weigh more heavily on poorer households as a share of income.

### Why governments intervene

Some things markets provide badly: street lights benefit everyone whether they pay or not, so private firms would not supply enough. Governments also protect the vulnerable, keep competition fair and manage the whole economy through fiscal policy.

**Key term — tax:** A compulsory payment to the government, such as income tax on earnings or VAT on shopping. Taxes fund public services.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Public Services">
  Name two public services funded by taxes.

  Any two of: schools, hospitals, roads, police, fire service, street lighting.

  **Answer:** Any two of: schools, hospitals, roads, police, fire service, street lighting.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking taxes disappear** Tax revenue is spent on public services — it is transferred to schools, hospitals and roads, not destroyed.
  - **Believing all taxes are unfair** Fairness depends on design: progressive taxes ask more of those who can afford it, while regressive taxes hit the poor harder.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is VAT?" hint="It is added at the till.">
    Value Added Tax — a tax added to the price of most goods and services when you buy them.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one argument for and one against raising taxes to fund hospitals." hint="Think benefits and costs.">
    For: better healthcare for all. Against: workers and firms keep less of their earnings, which may reduce spending and effort.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why would a private firm not provide street lighting?" hint="Think about who benefits and who pays.">
    Because everyone benefits whether they pay or not, so the firm could not charge enough users to cover its costs.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A government spends £500 billion and raises £450 billion in tax. What is the deficit?" hint="Spending minus revenue.">
    £50 billion (£500bn − £450bn).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
