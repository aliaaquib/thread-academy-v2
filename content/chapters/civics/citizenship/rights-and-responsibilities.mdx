## Rights and Responsibilities

You have the right to express opinions — and the responsibility not to silence others’. Rights and responsibilities are two sides of one coin.

<Definition term="Human rights">
  **Human rights** are entitlements held simply by being human — life, liberty, fair trial, free expression — set out in documents like the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). Citizen rights (like voting) additionally depend on membership of a state.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Rights: expression, belief, fair trial, privacy, education, voting (for citizens).
- Responsibilities: obey the law, respect others’ rights, pay taxes, serve on juries where required.
- Human rights are universal; citizen rights attach to membership.
- Rights conflict — free speech vs safety — and democracies constantly balance them.

<Example title="The UDHR, 1948">
  After WWII’s horrors, nations declared 30 rights belonging to everyone, everywhere — from free speech to education. Not legally binding everywhere, but the moral baseline of modern politics.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Distinguish a human right from a citizen right." hint="Who holds each?">
    Human rights belong to everyone by virtue of being human; citizen rights (e.g. voting, standing for office) belong to members of a particular state.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give an example of two rights in tension." hint="Speech vs safety.">
    Free expression vs protection from hate speech; press freedom vs privacy — democracies balance them through law.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Citizenship bundles rights with responsibilities.
  - Human rights are universal; citizen rights need membership.
  - The UDHR (1948) lists 30 fundamental rights.
  - Rights often conflict — balancing them is politics.
</Summary>
