This lesson focuses on Being a Helper Yourself: find simple, safe ways children can help their family, school and community.

<Definition term="Being a Helper Yourself">
  Find simple, safe ways children can help their family, school and community.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Everyone can be a helper

You do not need a uniform to help. Children can tidy shared spaces, be kind to new classmates and help carry shopping for family. Small, safe acts of help make homes, schools and neighbourhoods better for everyone.

### Helpers keep people safe

Some helpers protect us from danger. Firefighters put out fires, police officers help keep streets safe, and lifeguards watch over swimmers. Knowing who they are helps children ask the right person for help in an emergency.

**Key term — community:** A group of people who live, work or learn in the same area and share spaces like streets, parks and schools. Members of a community look out for one another.

<WorkedExample title="Who Helps After a Storm">
  A storm has blown a tree across the pavement on your street. Which community helpers might come, and what would each one do?

  Step 1: Spot the danger. The fallen tree blocks the pavement and might have damaged wires.
  Step 2: Think about who keeps people safe. Police or community officers can warn people to stay away from the area.
  Step 3: Think about who fixes things. Council workers or tree surgeons can cut up and remove the fallen tree.
  Step 4: Think about neighbours. Kind neighbours can check on elderly people nearby to make sure they are all right.
  Step 5: Put it together. Every helper has a different job, and they work as a team.

  **Answer:** The council workers clear the tree, the police keep people away from the danger, and neighbours check on anyone who might need help. Each helper does a different job, and together they make the street safe again.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing children cannot help** Children can help in safe, sensible ways, like tidying up, comforting a friend or helping with recycling. Grown-ups handle dangerous jobs, but small acts of help still make a big difference. Being a helper is about caring, not about age.
  - **Thinking helpers are only people in uniforms** Many helpers do not wear uniforms at all. Shopkeepers, bus drivers, librarians and volunteers help the community every day. A uniform can show someone's job, but kindness and useful work matter more than clothes.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="There is a small fire in a kitchen. Which community helper should be called?" hint="Who is trained to deal with fire?">
    A firefighter. Firefighters are trained to put out fires and rescue people safely.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two ways a child can be a helper at school." hint="Think about what makes a new pupil feel welcome.">
    By listening, sharing equipment and including everyone in games. These small actions help classmates feel welcome.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why is a bin collector an important community helper?" hint="What happens if rubbish is never collected?">
    Because clean streets stop rubbish from piling up, which keeps the area healthy and pleasant. Bin collectors do an important job for everyone.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one helper who looks after people's health and say what they do." hint="Think of the people who work in hospitals and clinics.">
    A nurse or doctor. They look after people who are ill or hurt.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
