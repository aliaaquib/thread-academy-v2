## Layers of the Earth

The deepest hole ever drilled reached 12 km — barely scratching a planet 6,371 km in radius. Everything we know about the deep Earth comes from seismic waves.

<Definition term="Seismic waves">
  **Seismic waves** are vibrations from earthquakes that travel through Earth. How they bend, speed up, slow down or stop reveals the density and state of the layers they pass through.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Crust: thin solid outer layer — oceanic (~7 km) and continental (~35 km).
- Mantle: thick, mostly solid rock that flows extremely slowly.
- Outer core: liquid iron-nickel; its motion generates Earth’s magnetic field.
- Inner core: solid iron-nickel, hotter than the Sun’s surface, kept solid by pressure.

<Example title="The S-wave shadow zone">
  S-waves can’t travel through liquids. After big earthquakes, there’s a zone where no S-waves arrive — proving part of the core is liquid. One missing signal revealed the outer core.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why can’t we directly observe Earth’s core?" hint="How deep have we drilled?">
    The deepest drill reached about 12 km; the core starts around 2,900 km down — far beyond any drill.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What generates Earth’s magnetic field?" hint="Think moving metal.">
    Convection of liquid iron-nickel in the outer core acts as a dynamo, generating the magnetic field.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Earth has four main layers: crust, mantle, outer core, inner core.
  - Seismic waves are our X-ray of the interior.
  - The liquid outer core generates the magnetic field.
  - Continental crust is thicker and less dense than oceanic crust.
</Summary>
