## Key ideas

### We measure weather with tools

Scientists do not guess the weather; they measure it. A thermometer measures temperature in degrees Celsius, a rain gauge collects rain so we can read it in millimetres, and a windsock or wind vane shows wind direction. Using the same tools each day gives results you can trust and compare.

### Weather is different from climate

Weather is what is happening outside today: a rainy afternoon or a frosty morning. Climate is the bigger picture: the weather a place usually gets over many years. Knowing the difference helps you understand both today's forecast and why some countries are always hot.

**Key term — Weather:** Weather is what the air outside is like right now: whether it is sunny, cloudy, rainy or windy, and how warm or cold it feels.

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting to empty the rain gauge** If you do not tip the water out after reading, the next reading will include old rain and your measurement will be wrong. Always start from empty.
  - **Reading the thermometer in direct sunshine** Sunlight warms the thermometer itself and gives a reading that is too high. Measure temperature in the shade for a true reading of the air.
</ImportantNote>
