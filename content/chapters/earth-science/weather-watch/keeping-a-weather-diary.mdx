## Key ideas

### Patterns appear in a weather diary

One day of weather tells you little, but a week of careful records can reveal patterns, such as rain arriving after the wind changes direction. Writing down temperature, cloud, wind and rain at the same time each day turns scattered observations into real data.

### We measure weather with tools

Scientists do not guess the weather; they measure it. A thermometer measures temperature in degrees Celsius, a rain gauge collects rain so we can read it in millimetres, and a windsock or wind vane shows wind direction. Using the same tools each day gives results you can trust and compare.

**Key term — Climate:** Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years. A desert has a hot, dry climate even on a rare cool day.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: A Weather Diary">
  You record the weather at the same time every day for two weeks. Why is keeping the same time important?

  Recording at the same time makes the results fair to compare, because temperature and weather change through the day.

  **Answer:** Recording at the same time makes the results fair to compare, because temperature and weather change through the day.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying weather and climate are the same thing** Weather is today's conditions; climate is the usual pattern over many years. A cold day does not change a place's climate.
  - **Reading the thermometer in direct sunshine** Sunlight warms the thermometer itself and gives a reading that is too high. Measure temperature in the shade for a true reading of the air.
</ImportantNote>
