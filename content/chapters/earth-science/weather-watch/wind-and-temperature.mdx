## Key ideas

### We measure weather with tools

Scientists do not guess the weather; they measure it. A thermometer measures temperature in degrees Celsius, a rain gauge collects rain so we can read it in millimetres, and a windsock or wind vane shows wind direction. Using the same tools each day gives results you can trust and compare.

### Patterns appear in a weather diary

One day of weather tells you little, but a week of careful records can reveal patterns, such as rain arriving after the wind changes direction. Writing down temperature, cloud, wind and rain at the same time each day turns scattered observations into real data.

**Key term — Temperature:** Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is. We measure it in degrees Celsius using a thermometer.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Wind and Temperature">
  What unit do we use to measure temperature?

  Degrees Celsius.

  **Answer:** Degrees Celsius.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Reading the thermometer in direct sunshine** Sunlight warms the thermometer itself and gives a reading that is too high. Measure temperature in the shade for a true reading of the air.
  - **Saying weather and climate are the same thing** Weather is today's conditions; climate is the usual pattern over many years. A cold day does not change a place's climate.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="You record the weather at the same time every day for two weeks. Why is keeping the same time important?" hint="Think about fair comparisons.">
    Recording at the same time makes the results fair to compare, because temperature and weather change through the day.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you read a thermometer in the shade?" hint="What would sunshine do to the thermometer itself?">
    Sunshine warms the thermometer itself, giving a reading higher than the true air temperature.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a windsock tell you?" hint="Watch how it moves.">
    It shows the wind's direction and gives a rough idea of its strength.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between weather and climate?" hint="One is about today, the other about many years.">
    Weather is what the air is like right now; climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
