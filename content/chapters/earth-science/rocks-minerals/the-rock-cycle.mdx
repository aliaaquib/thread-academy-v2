## The Rock Cycle

The granite of a mountain may once have been seafloor mud, and could one day be magma again. Rocks are not permanent — they’re a process.

<Definition term="Rock cycle">
  The **rock cycle** is the endless series of processes — melting, cooling, weathering, erosion, compaction, heat and pressure — that transform rocks between igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic types.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Igneous: cooled magma/lava — e.g. granite, basalt.
- Sedimentary: compacted layers of sediment — e.g. sandstone, limestone; often contain fossils.
- Metamorphic: existing rock transformed by heat and pressure — e.g. marble from limestone.
- Weathering and erosion break rock down; the fragments become new sedimentary rock.

<Example title="From mountain to beach and back">
  A granite peak weathers into sand, rivers carry it to the sea, layers compact into sandstone, burial and heat turn it to quartzite — which may one day melt again.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How does a sedimentary rock become a metamorphic rock?" hint="Think burial.">
    Burial subjects it to intense heat and pressure, recrystallising its minerals without melting — e.g. limestone → marble.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are fossils found in sedimentary rock but not igneous rock?" hint="What would magma do to a bone?">
    Sedimentary rock forms at low temperatures from deposited layers that can entomb organisms; magma would destroy any remains.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Three rock types: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
  - The cycle is driven by Earth’s internal heat and surface weathering.
  - Sedimentary rocks often hold fossils.
  - Any rock type can become any other given the right processes.
</Summary>
