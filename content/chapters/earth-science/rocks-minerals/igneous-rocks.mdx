## Key ideas

### Cooling speed controls crystal size

When magma cools slowly deep underground, large crystals have time to grow, giving coarse-grained rocks like granite. When lava cools quickly at the surface, only tiny crystals form, giving fine-grained rocks like basalt. Crystal size therefore tells you where an igneous rock cooled.

### Heat and pressure remake rocks

When rocks are buried deep or squeezed at plate margins, heat and pressure rearrange their minerals without melting them. Clay becomes slate, limestone becomes marble, and granite becomes gneiss. These metamorphic rocks often show bands or aligned crystals recording the direction of the squeeze.

**Key term — Magma:** Magma is molten rock beneath Earth's surface. When it erupts onto the surface it is called lava.

<WorkedExample title="Granite or basalt?">
  Two igneous rocks are described. Rock X has large visible crystals and Rock Y has crystals too small to see. Which cooled underground and which cooled as lava, and why?

  Recall that large crystals need a long time to grow, which needs slow cooling.
  Slow cooling happens underground, where surrounding rock insulates the magma.
  Rock X has large crystals, so it cooled slowly underground: it is intrusive.
  Rock Y has tiny crystals, so it cooled quickly at the surface as lava: it is extrusive.

  **Answer:** Rock X (large crystals) cooled slowly underground and is intrusive, like granite. Rock Y (tiny crystals) cooled quickly as lava and is extrusive, like basalt.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying lava and magma are the same thing** Magma is molten rock underground; once it erupts onto the surface it is called lava. The name changes at the surface.
  - **Thinking the rock cycle has a start and an end** The rock cycle is a continuous loop with no beginning or end: any rock can be weathered into sediment, melted into magma, or metamorphosed, depending on conditions.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Name the three families of rock and say how each forms." hint="Think fire, layers and pressure.">
    Igneous rocks form from cooling magma or lava; sedimentary rocks form from compacted and cemented layers of sediment; metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why does granite have larger crystals than basalt?" hint="Think about cooling speed.">
    Granite cooled slowly underground, giving crystals time to grow large; basalt cooled quickly as lava, so only tiny crystals formed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are fossils found mainly in sedimentary rocks?" hint="Think about heat.">
    Sedimentary rocks form at low temperatures that preserve fossils; igneous rocks form from molten material and metamorphic rocks from heat and pressure, both of which destroy fossils.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Put these sedimentary processes in order: cementation, deposition, weathering, compaction, erosion." hint="Breakdown comes first.">
    Weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, cementation.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
