The ground beneath your feet is far from boring. In this chapter you will handle real rocks, describe them like a scientist, dig into what soil is made of, and meet the tiny creatures that keep soil alive.

This lesson focuses on Rocks All Around Us: learn what a rock is and spot natural rocks at home, at school and outdoors.

## Key ideas

### Rocks are everywhere

Rocks are not only found in mountains and quarries. They form pavements, walls and cliffs, and even the grit on paths started life as rock. Once you know what to look for, you will spot rocks in your school grounds, your garden and the park.

### Soil is more than mud

Soil is a mixture, not just wet dirt. It contains tiny grains of weathered rock, rotted leaves and roots called humus, plus water and pockets of air. Each ingredient matters: the grains hold the soil up, humus feeds plants, water quenches roots and air lets roots breathe.

**Key term — Rock:** A rock is a solid natural material made of one or more minerals. Mountains, pebbles and the stones in your garden are all rocks.

<WorkedExample title="Sorting rocks like a scientist">
  You collect four rocks: A is smooth and grey with speckles, B is rough and red, C is smooth and grey with speckles, D is shiny and black. Which rocks belong together, and which property did you use to sort them?

  Look at each rock's properties: colour, texture and special features.
  Rocks A and C share two properties: smooth texture and grey colour with speckles.
  Rocks B and D are different from each other and from A and C: B is rough and red, D is shiny and black.
  Group A with C, and leave B and D on their own, because scientists sort by shared properties, not by where the rocks were found.

  **Answer:** Rocks A and C belong together because they are both smooth, grey and speckled. Sorting by shared properties is how scientists classify things.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking all rocks are grey** Rocks come in many colours, including red, white, black and yellow. Colour is a useful clue, so look carefully instead of assuming.
  - **Saying soil is just mud** Mud is only wet soil. Real soil is a mixture of rock grains, humus, water and air, and each part does an important job.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a rock made of?" hint="Think about minerals.">
    A rock is made of one or more minerals joined together.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name two properties you could use to describe a rock." hint="Think about what you can see and feel.">
    Any two of: colour, shape, size, texture (how it feels), or special features such as speckles or layers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is soil made from?" hint="Name at least three ingredients.">
    Tiny pieces of broken rock, dead plant and animal matter (humus), water and air.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="You find a rough red rock and a smooth red rock. Are they the same kind of rock?" hint="Colour is only one clue.">
    Not necessarily. Colour alone is not enough; you must also compare texture and other features before deciding.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
