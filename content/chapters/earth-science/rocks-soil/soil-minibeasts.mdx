## Key ideas

### Minibeasts are soil engineers

Worms, woodlice, beetles and millipedes tunnel through soil as they feed. Their tunnels let air and rain reach plant roots, and their droppings add nutrients back into the soil. Without minibeasts, soil would become hard and lifeless, and plants would struggle to grow.

### Soil is more than mud

Soil is a mixture, not just wet dirt. It contains tiny grains of weathered rock, rotted leaves and roots called humus, plus water and pockets of air. Each ingredient matters: the grains hold the soil up, humus feeds plants, water quenches roots and air lets roots breathe.

**Key term — Soil:** Soil is the loose top layer of the ground, made from tiny broken rock pieces mixed with dead plant and animal matter, water and air.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Soil Minibeasts">
  What is soil made from?

  Tiny pieces of broken rock, dead plant and animal matter (humus), water and air.

  **Answer:** Tiny pieces of broken rock, dead plant and animal matter (humus), water and air.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing worms are pests** Worms are helpers, not pests. Their tunnels let air and water reach roots, and their droppings feed the soil.
  - **Saying soil is just mud** Mud is only wet soil. Real soil is a mixture of rock grains, humus, water and air, and each part does an important job.
</ImportantNote>
