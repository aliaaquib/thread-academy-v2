## Key ideas

### Earthquakes release built-up stress

Tectonic plates move constantly but can lock together at their edges, bending the rock like a stretched elastic band. When the stress overcomes the friction, the rock snaps back in an earthquake, releasing energy as seismic waves. The point of snapping is the focus; the epicentre is directly above it.

### People can prepare, even if they cannot prevent

Scientists monitor volcanoes for warning signs such as swelling ground, gas release and small tremors, and can often evacuate people before an eruption. For earthquakes there is no reliable short-term prediction, so safety comes from preparation: earthquake-resistant buildings, drills, and emergency supplies.

**Key term — Seismometer:** A seismometer is an instrument that detects and records the vibrations of seismic waves from earthquakes.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Measuring Earthquakes">
  What does a seismometer do?

  It detects and records the seismic waves (vibrations) produced by earthquakes.

  **Answer:** It detects and records the seismic waves (vibrations) produced by earthquakes.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing earthquakes can be predicted like weather** There is no reliable way to predict exactly when an earthquake will strike. Safety depends on preparation and strong buildings, not prediction.
  - **Calling magma lava while it is still underground** It is magma underground and only becomes lava once it erupts onto the surface.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two warning signs that a volcano might erupt soon." hint="Think about swelling, gas and tremors.">
    Any two of: the ground swelling, increased gas release, small earthquakes or tremors, rising temperatures in crater lakes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why are earthquakes common at conservative plate margins?" hint="Think about plates sliding and locking.">
    The plates slide sideways and can lock together, so stress builds up until the rocks suddenly slip, releasing the energy as an earthquake.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between the focus and the epicentre of an earthquake?" hint="One is underground.">
    The focus is the point underground where the earthquake starts; the epicentre is the point on the surface directly above the focus.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name three parts of a volcano." hint="Think from the bottom up.">
    Any three of: magma chamber, vent, crater, cone, lava flow, ash cloud.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
