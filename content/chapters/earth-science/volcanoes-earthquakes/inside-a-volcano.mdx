Volcanoes and earthquakes are the most dramatic proof that Earth's surface is on the move. In this chapter you will explore what happens inside a volcano, why some eruptions are gentle and others explosive, how earthquakes are measured, and how people live safely alongside these hazards.

This lesson focuses on Inside a Volcano: name the parts of a volcano, from the magma chamber to the crater.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Gas and stickiness decide how a volcano erupts

Runny basaltic lava lets trapped gas escape easily, so eruptions are gentle and build wide, low shield volcanoes. Thick, silica-rich magma traps gas until pressure builds up, then bursts out explosively, building steep-sided composite volcanoes from alternating layers of lava and ash.

### People can prepare, even if they cannot prevent

Scientists monitor volcanoes for warning signs such as swelling ground, gas release and small tremors, and can often evacuate people before an eruption. For earthquakes there is no reliable short-term prediction, so safety comes from preparation: earthquake-resistant buildings, drills, and emergency supplies.

**Key term — Magma chamber:** A magma chamber is the underground store of molten rock that feeds a volcano. Rising pressure in the chamber eventually forces magma up the vent.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Inside a Volcano">
  Name three parts of a volcano.

  Any three of: magma chamber, vent, crater, cone, lava flow, ash cloud.

  **Answer:** Any three of: magma chamber, vent, crater, cone, lava flow, ash cloud.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking all volcanoes erupt explosively** Eruption style depends on the magma: runny basaltic magma gives gentle eruptions, while thick silica-rich magma gives explosive ones.
  - **Calling magma lava while it is still underground** It is magma underground and only becomes lava once it erupts onto the surface.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why do composite volcanoes erupt more explosively than shield volcanoes?" hint="Think about viscosity and trapped gas.">
    Composite volcanoes have thick, high-viscosity magma that traps gas until pressure builds and bursts out explosively; shield volcanoes have runny lava that lets gas escape gently.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give two warning signs that a volcano might erupt soon." hint="Think about swelling, gas and tremors.">
    Any two of: the ground swelling, increased gas release, small earthquakes or tremors, rising temperatures in crater lakes.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between the focus and the epicentre of an earthquake?" hint="One is underground.">
    The focus is the point underground where the earthquake starts; the epicentre is the point on the surface directly above the focus.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does a seismometer do?" hint="Think about vibrations.">
    It detects and records the seismic waves (vibrations) produced by earthquakes.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
