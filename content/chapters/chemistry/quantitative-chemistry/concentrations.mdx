## Key ideas

### The mole connects mass and particles

One mole of any substance contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles and has a mass in grams equal to its relative formula mass — so 18 g of water is one mole. The relationship moles = mass ÷ Mr lets you convert between the two in seconds. This is the key that unlocks every reacting-mass calculation.

### Use the equation's molar ratio

A balanced equation gives the ratio of moles that react: in 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, 2 moles of Mg make 2 moles of MgO, a 1:1 ratio. The method is always the same: convert the known mass to moles, use the ratio to find moles of the unknown, then convert back to mass. If a reactant runs out first, it is the limiting reactant and it alone sets the maximum product.

**Key term — concentration:** Moles of solute per cubic decimetre of solution, in mol/dm³.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Concentrations of Solutions">
  In the worked example, what mass of oxygen reacts with the 12.0 g of magnesium?

  Moles of Mg = 0.494; moles of O₂ = 0.494 ÷ 2 = 0.247 mol; mass = 0.247 × 32.0 = 7.90 g. (Check: 12.0 + 7.90 = 19.9 g of product.).

  **Answer:** Moles of Mg = 0.494; moles of O₂ = 0.494 ÷ 2 = 0.247 mol; mass = 0.247 × 32.0 = 7.90 g. (Check: 12.0 + 7.90 = 19.9 g of product.).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using the mass ratio instead of the mole ratio** Grams do not react in the equation's ratio — moles do. Always convert masses to moles first, apply the ratio from the balanced equation, then convert back to grams.
  - **Forgetting to double the oxygen in O₂** Mr of O₂ is 32, not 16 — the molecule has two atoms. The same trap applies to H₂, N₂ and the halogens: read the formula, not just the element.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="5.84 g of NaCl is dissolved to make 1.00 dm³ of solution. What is its concentration? (Mr of NaCl = 58.4)" hint="Convert grams to moles first.">
    Moles = 5.84 ÷ 58.4 = 0.100 mol; concentration = 0.100 ÷ 1.00 = 0.100 mol/dm³.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Calculate the number of moles in 10.0 g of sodium hydroxide, NaOH. (Ar: Na = 23.0, H = 1.0, O = 16.0)" hint="First find Mr of NaOH.">
    Mr = 23.0 + 16.0 + 1.0 = 40.0. Moles = 10.0 ÷ 40.0 = 0.250 mol.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Calculate the concentration of a solution containing 0.20 mol of solute in 0.50 dm³ of solution." hint="Concentration = moles ÷ volume.">
    0.20 ÷ 0.50 = 0.40 mol/dm³.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A reaction should theoretically make 8.40 g of product but only 6.72 g is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield." hint="(actual ÷ theoretical) × 100.">
    (6.72 ÷ 8.40) × 100 = 80.0%.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
