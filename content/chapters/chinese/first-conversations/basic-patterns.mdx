## Basic Sentence Patterns

No tenses on verbs, no plurals on nouns, no genders — Chinese grammar trades morphology for word order and particles.

<Definition term="Ma particle">
  Adding **吗 (ma)** to a statement’s end makes it a yes/no question: 你是老师 (you are a teacher) → 你是老师吗? (are you a teacher?).
</Definition>

## Key points

- Basic order: subject–verb–object, like English: 我喝茶 (I drink tea).
- Negation: 不 (bù) before the verb: 我不喝茶 (I don’t drink tea).
- Questions: …吗 for yes/no; 什么 (shénme = what) for what-questions.
- Time words go early: 我明天去北京 (I tomorrow go Beijing).

<Example title="Building up">
  我是学生。我不是老师。你是老师吗？
(I’m a student. I’m not a teacher. Are you a teacher?)
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you turn ‘ni shi xuesheng’ into a question?" hint="Add one particle.">
    Add 吗: ‘ni shi xuesheng ma?’ (are you a student?).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Where does the time word go in a Chinese sentence?" hint="Early.">
    After the subject, before the verb: ‘wo mingtian qu…’.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - SVO order, like English.
  - 不 negates; 吗 questions.
  - No conjugations, no plurals.
  - Time words sit early.
</Summary>
