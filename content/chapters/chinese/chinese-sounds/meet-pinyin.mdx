Before you can read a single character, you need to hear Chinese. This chapter turns your ears and mouth into Chinese instruments: first you meet pinyin, the friendly alphabet that spells out sounds, then you master the four tones that give every syllable its meaning.

This lesson focuses on Meet Pinyin: discover the roman-letter system that shows you how to pronounce every Chinese syllable.

## Key ideas

### What pinyin does

Pinyin breaks every syllable into an initial (the starting consonant, like m), a final (the vowel part, like a) and a tone mark. The marks sit above the vowel: ā á ǎ à. Read mā as m plus a plus first tone, and you can pronounce hundreds of new words without ever seeing their characters.

### Same sound, different tone, different word

Tones are not decoration: change the tone and you change the word entirely, so mā (mum) and mǎ (horse) are completely different words. This is why careful listening matters more in Chinese than in English, and why tone games are worth playing every day.

**Key term — 拼音 (pīnyīn):** The system that writes Chinese sounds with the Roman alphabet, so mā tells you exactly how to say 妈.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Meet Pinyin">
  How do you write thank you in pinyin, including the neutral tone?

  xièxie — the second xie carries no tone mark because it is neutral.

  **Answer:** xièxie — the second xie carries no tone mark because it is neutral.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Saying every syllable in a flat English voice** Give each syllable its tone shape: practise mā, má, mǎ, mà in order until the pitch patterns feel natural.
  - **Forgetting the neutral tone** In words like 谢谢 (xièxie, thank you), the second syllable is short and light with no tone mark. Do not give it a full fourth tone.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Break the syllable bā into initial, final and tone." hint="Initial means the starting consonant.">
    Initial b, final a, first tone.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What are the four tone marks, in order?" hint="Think of the marks above ā á ǎ à.">
    First: flat (ā), second: rising (á), third: dipping (ǎ), fourth: falling (à).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Say mā, má, mǎ, mà. What does each one mean?" hint="Mum, hemp, horse, scold.">
    mā (妈) mum, má (麻) hemp, mǎ (马) horse, mà (骂) to scold.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which tone is hǎo in 你好 (nǐ hǎo)?" hint="Look at the mark above the a.">
    Third tone: hǎo.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
