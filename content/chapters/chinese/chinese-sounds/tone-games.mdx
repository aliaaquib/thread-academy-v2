## Key ideas

### Same sound, different tone, different word

Tones are not decoration: change the tone and you change the word entirely, so mā (mum) and mǎ (horse) are completely different words. This is why careful listening matters more in Chinese than in English, and why tone games are worth playing every day.

### The four tones

First tone is high and flat (mā 妈, mum). Second tone rises, like asking what (má 麻, hemp). Third tone dips down then up (mǎ 马, horse). Fourth tone falls sharply, like a firm no (mà 骂, to scold). Say them in order — mā, má, mǎ, mà — and feel the pitch move.

**Key term — 声调 (shēngdiào):** A tone: the pitch pattern of a syllable. Mandarin has four main tones plus a light neutral tone.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Tone Games">
  What are the four tone marks, in order?

  First: flat (ā), second: rising (á), third: dipping (ǎ), fourth: falling (à).

  **Answer:** First: flat (ā), second: rising (á), third: dipping (ǎ), fourth: falling (à).
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Mixing up mā and mǎ** Exaggerate the difference while learning — sing the first tone high and flat, and scoop the third tone low before rising.
  - **Forgetting the neutral tone** In words like 谢谢 (xièxie, thank you), the second syllable is short and light with no tone mark. Do not give it a full fourth tone.
</ImportantNote>
