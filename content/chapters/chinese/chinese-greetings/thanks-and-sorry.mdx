This lesson focuses on Thanks and Sorry: use 谢谢， 对不起 and 没关系 like a polite guest.

<Definition term="Thanks and Sorry">
  Use 谢谢， 对不起 and 没关系 like a polite guest.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Polite words

请 (qǐng) means please, 对不起 (duìbuqǐ) means sorry, and 没关系 (méi guānxi) means it doesn't matter. When someone thanks you, answer 不客气 (bú kèqi, you're welcome) — notice how 不 changes to a second tone before the fourth tone of 客.

### Greetings for the time of day

In the morning say 早上好 (zǎoshang hǎo, good morning), and at night say 晚安 (wǎn'ān, good night). To show extra respect to a teacher or grandparent, use 您好 (nín hǎo) instead of 你好 — 您 (nín) is the polite form of you.

**Key term — 谢谢 (xièxie):** Thank you. The second syllable is a light neutral tone, so keep it short and soft.

<WorkedExample title="Introduce yourself in three sentences">
  Say in Chinese: Hello! My name is Xiaoming. Goodbye!

  Start with the greeting: 你好 (nǐ hǎo).
  Add your introduction with 我叫: 我叫小明 (wǒ jiào Xiǎomíng).
  Finish with the farewell: 再见 (zàijiàn).

  **Answer:** 你好！我叫小明。再见！(Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ jiào Xiǎomíng. Zàijiàn!) — Hello! My name is Xiaoming. Goodbye!
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Using 你好 with a teacher you want to honour** Choose 您好 (nín hǎo) for teachers, grandparents and strangers you wish to respect.
  - **Saying my name is in English word order** Chinese has no word for is here: 我叫小明 is literally I called Xiaoming, never 我是叫小明.
</ImportantNote>
