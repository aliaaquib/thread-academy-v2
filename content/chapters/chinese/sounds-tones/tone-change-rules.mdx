## Key ideas

### Third-tone sandhi

When two third tones meet, the first becomes a second tone: 你 (nǐ) plus 好 (hǎo) is pronounced ní hǎo. In a longer string, every third tone except the last one changes: 我很好 (wǒ hěn hǎo) is said wó hén hǎo. The tone marks never change on paper — this is a pronunciation rule only.

### The two great changers: 一 and 不

一 is yī alone, yí before a fourth tone (一个 yí gè, 一半 yí bàn) and yì before first, second and third tones (一天 yìtiān, 一年 yìnián, 一起 yìqǐ). 不 is bù normally but bú before a fourth tone (不是 búshì, 不对 búduì). Examiners listen for these, so drill them.

**Key term — 变调 (biàndiào):** Tone sandhi: a tone that changes because of its neighbours. The written tone mark stays the same; only the pronunciation shifts.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Tone Change Rules">
  Pronounce 小老虎 (little tiger) with correct sandhi.

  xiáo láo hǔ — the first two third tones become second tones.

  **Answer:** xiáo láo hǔ — the first two third tones become second tones.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pronouncing every third tone fully in a row** A string of full third tones sounds staccato and wrong. Let each third tone except the last rise to second tone.
  - **Writing ní hǎo with a second-tone mark** Tone marks record the original tone: always write 你好 as nǐ hǎo, even though you say ní hǎo.
</ImportantNote>
