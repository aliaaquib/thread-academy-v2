## First Characters

3,000 characters cover 99% of daily text — and they’re assembled from about 200 building blocks called radicals. Learn the blocks, and characters start making sense.

<Definition term="Radical">
  A **radical** is a character component that hints at meaning or sound. 水 (water) appears in 河 (river), 海 (sea), 洗 (wash) — spot the radical, guess the territory.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Pictographs: 日 (sun), 月 (moon), 人 (person) — simplified pictures.
- Most characters combine a meaning radical + a sound hint: 妈 (mā, mother) = woman (女) + horse (马, pronounced mǎ).
- Stroke order matters — there are rules (top to bottom, left to right).
- Start with: 人 日 月 水 火 大 小 中 国.

<Example title="Decoding 好">
  好 (hǎo = good/like) = 女 (woman) + 子 (child). ‘Woman + child = good’ — 3,000 years of culture in one character.
</Example>
