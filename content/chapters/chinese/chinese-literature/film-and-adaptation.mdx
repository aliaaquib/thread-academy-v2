## Key ideas

### Film and adaptation

卧虎藏龙 (Wò Hǔ Cáng Lóng, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, 2000), directed by 李安， won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and brought wuxia martial-arts storytelling to world audiences. Analysing it means asking what the camera keeps from the literary tradition — honour, restraint, the unsaid — and what it changes for the screen. Useful critical terms: 对偶 (duì'ǒu, parallelism), 夸张 (kuāzhāng, hyperbole), 象征 (xiàngzhēng, symbolism).

### Reading 静夜思

李白 (Lǐ Bái, 701–762), the great Tang poet, wrote: 床前明月光，疑是地上霜。举头望明月，低头思故乡。(Chuáng qián míng yuè guāng, yí shì dì shang shuāng. Jǔ tóu wàng míng yuè, dī tóu sī gù xiāng.) In four short lines the poet moves from moonlight mistaken for frost to looking up at the moon and bowing his head in longing for home — the whole poem turns on the contrast of 举头 (raising the head) and 低头 (lowering it).

**Key term — 导演 (dǎoyǎn):** Film director. 李安 (Lǐ Ān, Ang Lee) directed 卧虎藏龙 (Wò Hǔ Cáng Lóng, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon).

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Film and Adaptation">
  What award did 卧虎藏龙 win, and who directed it?

  Directed by 李安 (Ang Lee), it won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

  **Answer:** Directed by 李安 (Ang Lee), it won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Treating film as just entertainment** In essays, analyse directorial choices (framing, silence, pacing) with terms like 象征， just as you would a written text.
  - **Translating a poem word for word** A literal gloss is the first step, not the answer: reorganise into natural English that keeps the image and mood.
</ImportantNote>
