German is a wonderfully phonetic language: once you know the rules, you can read almost any word aloud. In this chapter you will master the alphabet, the famous umlauts ä, ö and ü, and the letter combinations that make German sound like German.

This lesson focuses on The Alphabet: learn every letter's German name, especially the five that trick English speakers.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### The alphabet song

The German alphabet has the same 26 letters as English, plus four special ones: ä, ö, ü and ß. Most letters sound similar to English, but five are very different: W sounds like English V, V sounds like F, J sounds like Y, Z sounds like TS, and Y sounds like Ü. Sing the alphabet slowly and exaggerate these five.

### Crack the letter combinations

German letter teams are wonderfully reliable. EI always says eye (mein), IE always says ee (Liebe), AU says ow (Haus), and EU says oy (heute). CH has two sounds: a soft hiss after e and i (ich) and a throaty sound after a, o and u (Bach). SCH is simply sh, as in die Schule.

**Key term — Das Alphabet:** The German alphabet: 26 letters like English, plus ä, ö, ü and ß. Five letters sound very different from English: W, V, J, Z and Y.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: The Alphabet">
  How is the German letter W pronounced?

  Like English V.

  **Answer:** Like English V.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pronouncing W like English W** German W always sounds like English V: Wasser sounds like Vasser, and wir sounds like veer.
  - **Pronouncing Z like English Z** German Z sounds like TS: Zeit (time) sounds like tsyte, and Zoo sounds like tsoh.
</ImportantNote>
