## Key ideas

### The alphabet song

The German alphabet has the same 26 letters as English, plus four special ones: ä, ö, ü and ß. Most letters sound similar to English, but five are very different: W sounds like English V, V sounds like F, J sounds like Y, Z sounds like TS, and Y sounds like Ü. Sing the alphabet slowly and exaggerate these five.

### The magic of ä, ö, ü and ß

Umlauts are vowels with two dots that change the sound: ä sounds like the e in bed, ö is like the i in bird (with rounded lips), and ü has no English equivalent — say ee with rounded lips. The ß (Eszett) is never at the start of a word and always sounds like a sharp ss, as in die Straße (street).

**Key term — Das Alphabet:** The German alphabet: 26 letters like English, plus ä, ö, ü and ß. Five letters sound very different from English: W, V, J, Z and Y.

<WorkedExample title="Saying Tschüss perfectly">
  Sound out the word Tschüss (bye!) letter by letter, then blend it together.

  Step 1: T sounds like teh in German.
  Step 2: SCH is one sound: sh, as in English shoe.
  Step 3: Ü is the tricky one — say ee while rounding your lips.
  Step 4: The double S is just a longer ss. Blend: T-sch-üss — Tschüss!

  **Answer:** T = teh, SCH = sh, Ü = ee-with-rounded-lips, SS = ss. So: teh + sh + üss = Tschüss!
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Pronouncing W like English W** German W always sounds like English V: Wasser sounds like Vasser, and wir sounds like veer.
  - **Pronouncing Z like English Z** German Z sounds like TS: Zeit (time) sounds like tsyte, and Zoo sounds like tsoh.
</ImportantNote>
