## Key ideas

### Festivals through the year

Karneval (carnival) is huge in Köln and the Rhineland in February, with costumes and parades. The Oktoberfest in München actually begins in September and is the world's largest folk festival. In December, Weihnachtsmärkte (Christmas markets) fill town squares with Glühwein and Lebkuchen. Der Tag der Deutschen Einheit on 3rd October is Germany's national day.

### Daily life and mealtimes

German daily routine has its own vocabulary: der Alltag (everyday life), der Feierabend (time after work). The traditional evening meal is das Abendbrot, literally evening bread — cold cuts, cheese and bread. Shops often close on Sundays (der Sonntag ist Ruhetag), so Samstag is the big shopping day. Ich kaufe am Samstag ein uses a separable verb: einkaufen → ich kaufe ein.

**Key term — Das Oktoberfest:** The world's largest folk festival, held in München. Despite the name it traditionally begins in September.

<WorkedExample title="A festival year">
  Write four German sentences describing a year of festivals: Karneval in Köln (February), Oktoberfest in München (September), a Weihnachtsmarkt (December), and the Tag der Deutschen Einheit (3rd October).

  Step 1: Start each sentence with the time phrase, then the verb in second position: Im Februar feiern wir.
  Step 2: Use nach for cities with no article (nach München) and in + dative for the festival city with context (in Köln).
  Step 3: Use zum (= zu + dem) for zum Oktoberfest.
  Step 4: Check every verb is in second position and every month is capitalised.

  **Answer:** Im Februar feiern wir Karneval in Köln. Im September gehen wir zum Oktoberfest nach München. Im Dezember besuchen wir einen Weihnachtsmarkt. Am 3. Oktober ist der Tag der Deutschen Einheit.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Assuming the Oktoberfest is in October** It traditionally starts in September in München — the name is historical.
  - **Thinking German is only spoken in Germany** German is an official language in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, with distinct national varieties.
</ImportantNote>
