German is spoken by around 100 million people across several countries, each with its own traditions, festivals and flavours of the language. This chapter takes you beyond the textbook into real daily life, celebrations and the rich variety of German as it is actually lived.

This lesson focuses on Daily Life: learn the vocabulary of everyday routines, mealtimes and shopping in German-speaking countries.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Daily life and mealtimes

German daily routine has its own vocabulary: der Alltag (everyday life), der Feierabend (time after work). The traditional evening meal is das Abendbrot, literally evening bread — cold cuts, cheese and bread. Shops often close on Sundays (der Sonntag ist Ruhetag), so Samstag is the big shopping day. Ich kaufe am Samstag ein uses a separable verb: einkaufen → ich kaufe ein.

### The German-speaking world and its varieties

German is official in Deutschland, Österreich and der Schweiz — the DACH countries — plus Liechtenstein. Each has its own standard variety and dialects: Bayerisch in Bavaria, and Swiss German dialects that even drop the ß in writing (using ss instead). Hochdeutsch (Standard German) is understood everywhere and is what you learn in school.

**Key term — Die DACH-Länder:** Deutschland, Österreich and die Schweiz — the three main German-speaking countries, named after their car registration codes D, A and CH.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Daily Life">
  Name the capitals of the three DACH countries in German.

  Die Hauptstädte sind Berlin, Wien und Bern.

  **Answer:** Die Hauptstädte sind Berlin, Wien und Bern.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking German is only spoken in Germany** German is an official language in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, with distinct national varieties.
  - **Forgetting the ß rule in Switzerland** Swiss Standard German writes ss instead of ß, so die Strasse not die Straße — correct in Switzerland, wrong in Germany.
</ImportantNote>
