## Key ideas

### The German-speaking world and its varieties

German is official in Deutschland, Österreich and der Schweiz — the DACH countries — plus Liechtenstein. Each has its own standard variety and dialects: Bayerisch in Bavaria, and Swiss German dialects that even drop the ß in writing (using ss instead). Hochdeutsch (Standard German) is understood everywhere and is what you learn in school.

### Festivals through the year

Karneval (carnival) is huge in Köln and the Rhineland in February, with costumes and parades. The Oktoberfest in München actually begins in September and is the world's largest folk festival. In December, Weihnachtsmärkte (Christmas markets) fill town squares with Glühwein and Lebkuchen. Der Tag der Deutschen Einheit on 3rd October is Germany's national day.

**Key term — Der Dialekt:** A regional variety of German, such as Bayerisch or Swiss German. Hochdeutsch is the standard variety used in schools and media.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Language Varieties">
  Give one way written German in Switzerland differs from German in Germany.

  In der Schweiz schreibt man ss statt ß, zum Beispiel die Strasse.

  **Answer:** In der Schweiz schreibt man ss statt ß, zum Beispiel die Strasse.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking German is only spoken in Germany** German is an official language in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, with distinct national varieties.
  - **Forgetting the ß rule in Switzerland** Swiss Standard German writes ss instead of ß, so die Strasse not die Straße — correct in Switzerland, wrong in Germany.
</ImportantNote>
