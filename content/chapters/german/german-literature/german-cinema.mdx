## Key ideas

### Talking about film

German cinema gives you listening practice with stories. Good Bye, Lenin! (2003) is a well-known German film about a family in East Berlin. Useful film vocabulary: der Regisseur (director), die Hauptfigur (main character), die Handlung (plot) and die Botschaft (message). Give opinions with Ich finde den Film spannend, weil... (I find the film exciting because...), keeping the verb last after weil.

### Reading strategies for real texts

Do not translate word by word. First skim for the Handlung (plot): who are the Hauptfiguren (main characters) and what is the Problem? Use cognates — German and English share many words, like das Haus (house) and der Garten (garden). Guess unknown words from context, then check only the key ones. Reading the same tale you already know in English makes your first German book much easier.

**Key term — Der Regisseur:** The director of a film (die Regisseurin for a woman). Good Bye, Lenin! (2003) is a famous example of modern German cinema.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: German Cinema">
  What is the German word for a film director?

  Der Regisseur / die Regisseurin.

  **Answer:** Der Regisseur / die Regisseurin.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Inventing quotes from authors** Never make up quotations. Describe plots and characters in your own German words instead.
  - **Translating every single word** Skim for the plot first using cognates and context; only look up words that block your understanding of the story.
</ImportantNote>
