## Greetings and Introductions

German distinguishes formal (Sie) from informal (du) — getting it right is social survival.

<Definition term="Sie vs du">
  **Sie** (capital S) is the formal ‘you’ — for strangers, elders, professionals. **du** is for friends, family, children and fellow students. When in doubt, use Sie.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Hallo! / Guten Tag! (hello) — Guten Morgen / Guten Abend by time of day.
- Wie geht es Ihnen? (formal) / Wie geht’s? (informal) — ‘how are you?’
- Ich heiße… (my name is…) / Ich bin… (I am…).
- Danke / Bitte (thanks / please–you’re welcome). Entschuldigung (excuse me). Tschüss! (bye!).

<Example title="First meeting">
  — Guten Tag! Ich heiße Anna. Wie heißen Sie?
— Guten Tag, Anna! Ich heiße Herr Weber. Freut mich!
(Formal: she used ‘Sie’ with an adult stranger — exactly right.)
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="When do you use ‘Sie’ instead of ‘du’?" hint="Strangers and professionals.">
    With strangers, older people, and in professional/formal contexts; ‘du’ is for friends, family and peers.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How do you say ‘My name is…’ in German?" hint="Ich…">
    ‘Ich heiße…’ (or ‘Ich bin…’).
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Sie = formal you; du = informal you.
  - Greetings change with time of day.
  - Ich heiße… introduces you.
  - Danke, bitte and Entschuldigung go far.
</Summary>
