<Definition term="Eid al-Fitr">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Light, food and togetherness

Many festivals use the same simple ingredients: lights or candles to show hope, special foods to share, and gatherings of family and friends. Diwali lamps, Christmas candles and Hanukkah candles all use light as a picture of goodness pushing back darkness. Sharing food, from Eid sweets to Christmas dinner, turns celebration into kindness.

### Respecting each other's celebrations

In a classroom or neighbourhood, friends may celebrate different festivals or none at all. Learning what a festival means to the people who keep it - rather than just admiring its decorations - shows respect. Asking curious, kind questions is better than assuming everyone's festival looks the same.

**Key term — fasting:** Going without food or drink for a set time as an act of worship; Muslims fast from dawn to sunset during Ramadan.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Eid al-Fitr">
  Which festival marks the end of Ramadan?

  Eid al-Fitr.

  **Answer:** Eid al-Fitr.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are the same festival** Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan fasting, while Eid al-Adha honours Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice and falls during the Hajj season.
  - **Everyone celebrates Christmas in the same way** Christians everywhere remember the birth of Jesus, but customs, foods and even the date differ - many Orthodox Christians celebrate on 7 January.
</ImportantNote>
