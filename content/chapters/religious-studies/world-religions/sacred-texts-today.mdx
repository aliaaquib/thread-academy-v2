## Key ideas

### Scripture in practice, not just on the shelf

Sacred texts live in worship: Muslims recite the Qur'an in Arabic during prayer, Jews read from the Torah scroll in the synagogue each week, Christians hear Bible readings in services, Sikhs keep the Guru Granth Sahib enthroned as their living Guru, and Buddhists chant from the Pali Canon. How a text is used matters as much as what it says.

### Abrahamic and Dharmic families

Scholars often group Judaism, Christianity and Islam as Abrahamic faiths because all trace themselves to Abraham and share prophets, scriptures and monotheism. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism are sometimes called Dharmic traditions, sharing ideas like dharma, karma and rebirth. The grouping helps comparison, but each religion is distinct - Sikhism, for example, is monotheistic yet rejects both the caste system and idol worship.

**Key term — scripture:** Holy writings treated as authoritative, such as the Bible, the Qur'an, the Torah, the Vedas, the Guru Granth Sahib and the Pali Canon.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Sacred Texts Today">
  Name the six traditions usually called the 'big six' world religions.

  Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism and Sikhism.

  **Answer:** Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism and Sikhism.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Buddhists worship the Buddha as a god** Buddhism is non-theistic: Buddhists honour and follow the Buddha as an enlightened teacher, not as a creator God.
  - **Sikhism is just a mixture of Hinduism and Islam** Sikhism is a distinct religion founded by Guru Nanak with its own scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib, and its own teachings, such as devotion to one God and rejection of caste.
</ImportantNote>
