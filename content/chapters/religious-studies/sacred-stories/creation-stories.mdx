Long before books were common, religions passed on their deepest ideas through stories told aloud and remembered by heart. These sacred stories explain where the world came from, which heroes to admire and how to live well. In this chapter you will meet some of the world's best-loved religious stories and discover the lessons hidden inside them.

This lesson focuses on Creation Stories: how different traditions tell the story of how the world began.

<Definition term="Creation Stories">
  How different traditions tell the story of how the world began.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Stories carry beliefs

A sacred story is like a parcel: the exciting events are the wrapping, and the belief about God, the world or human life is inside. The creation stories in Genesis, for example, teach that the world was made by God and is good. Finding the belief inside the story is the key skill in this chapter.

### The same story can be told differently

Jews, Christians and Muslims all tell the story of Noah and the flood, but each version has its own details - in the Qur'an, for instance, one of Noah's sons refuses to board the ark. Comparing versions shows how each tradition understands the story.

**Key term — sacred story:** A story that a religion treats as holy and meaningful, such as the birth of Jesus or the Buddha's enlightenment.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Creation Stories">
  What do the creation stories in Genesis teach about the world?

  That the world was made by God and that it is good.

  **Answer:** That the world was made by God and that it is good.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Every version of a story is identical** Details often differ between traditions - for example, the Qur'anic and Biblical accounts of Noah - and those differences are worth noticing.
  - **Sacred stories are 'just made up', so they do not matter** Even stories not read as literal history matter deeply, because they carry a religion's beliefs and shape how millions of people live.
</ImportantNote>
