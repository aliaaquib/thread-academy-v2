## Key ideas

### Stories carry beliefs

A sacred story is like a parcel: the exciting events are the wrapping, and the belief about God, the world or human life is inside. The creation stories in Genesis, for example, teach that the world was made by God and is good. Finding the belief inside the story is the key skill in this chapter.

### Stories teach without lecturing

Instead of listing rules, religions often teach through characters who make choices. When Rama keeps his promises even when it costs him dearly, listeners learn about duty. Stories let people feel the lesson, which makes it easier to remember.

**Key term — sacred story:** A story that a religion treats as holy and meaningful, such as the birth of Jesus or the Buddha's enlightenment.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Rama and Sita">
  In the Ramayana, who rescues Sita from the demon king Ravana?

  Rama, with the help of Hanuman and his army.

  **Answer:** Rama, with the help of Hanuman and his army.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Sacred stories are 'just made up', so they do not matter** Even stories not read as literal history matter deeply, because they carry a religion's beliefs and shape how millions of people live.
  - **Every version of a story is identical** Details often differ between traditions - for example, the Qur'anic and Biblical accounts of Noah - and those differences are worth noticing.
</ImportantNote>
