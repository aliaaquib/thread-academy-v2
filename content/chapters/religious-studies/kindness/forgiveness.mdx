## Key ideas

### Forgiveness frees both people

Many faiths teach that forgiving someone who hurt you is strong, not weak. Christianity teaches followers to forgive as God forgives them, and Buddhism teaches that holding on to anger harms the angry person most. Forgiveness can mend friendships and communities.

### One rule, many religions

Versions of the Golden Rule appear across the world: Jesus taught 'do to others what you would have them do to you', the Prophet Muhammad taught that none truly believes until they love for others what they love for themselves, and the Hindu Mahabharata says never to do to others what would hurt yourself. The wording differs, but the message is the same.

**Key term — forgiveness:** Choosing to let go of anger towards someone who has hurt you.

<WorkedExample title="Spotting the Golden Rule">
  Three quotes: (1) 'Do to others what you would have them do to you.' (2) 'None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.' (3) 'Do not do to others what would cause pain if done to you.' Which religions do they come from, and what do they share?

  Step 1: Identify quote 1 - the words of Jesus, so Christianity.
  Step 2: Identify quote 2 - a saying (hadith) of the Prophet Muhammad, so Islam.
  Step 3: Identify quote 3 - from the Hindu epic Mahabharata, so Hinduism.
  Step 4: Compare the messages - each asks you to treat others the way you wish to be treated.
  Step 5: Conclude - the Golden Rule appears in all three, showing a shared moral teaching.

  **Answer:** The quotes come from Christianity, Islam and Hinduism. All three teach the Golden Rule: treat others as you would wish to be treated yourself.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgiving means saying the wrong thing was okay** Forgiveness means letting go of anger and blame; it does not mean pretending the hurt did not happen.
  - **Charity is only giving money** Charity includes giving time, food, kindness and skills - many religions teach that a smile or a helping hand also counts.
</ImportantNote>
