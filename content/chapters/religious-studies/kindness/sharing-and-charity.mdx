## Key ideas

### Forgiveness frees both people

Many faiths teach that forgiving someone who hurt you is strong, not weak. Christianity teaches followers to forgive as God forgives them, and Buddhism teaches that holding on to anger harms the angry person most. Forgiveness can mend friendships and communities.

### Kindness as a duty, not just a feeling

Religions do not leave kindness to chance - they organise it. Muslims give zakat, a fixed share of savings to the poor; Sikhs serve free meals called langar to everyone; Christians and Jews support charities and food banks. Rules and routines turn good intentions into real help.

**Key term — charity:** Giving money, food or time to help people in need; an important duty in most religions.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Sharing and Charity">
  Give one way religions organise kindness rather than leaving it to chance.

  Examples: zakat in Islam, langar in Sikhism, tithing or charity collections in Christianity and Judaism.

  **Answer:** Examples: zakat in Islam, langar in Sikhism, tithing or charity collections in Christianity and Judaism.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Charity is only giving money** Charity includes giving time, food, kindness and skills - many religions teach that a smile or a helping hand also counts.
  - **Kindness is only for people like us** Most religions deliberately extend kindness to strangers and even enemies - the Good Samaritan parable makes exactly this point.
</ImportantNote>
