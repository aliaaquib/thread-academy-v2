## Secularisation

Sociologists once predicted religion would fade as science advanced. The world had other ideas.

<Definition term="Secularisation">
  **Secularisation** is the declining social significance of religion — fewer attend, faith shapes law and politics less. The thesis predicted modernity would kill religion; the evidence is mixed.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Western Europe: clear decline in attendance and affiliation.
- Globally: religion is growing — Islam and Pentecostal Christianity expand fast; the world is becoming MORE religious by headcount.
- The US: polarised — both devout and ‘nones’ growing.
- New forms: spirituality, fundamentalism as modern reactions — religion adapts rather than simply dying.

<Example title="Pentecostal boom">
  Pentecostalism — barely a century old — has hundreds of millions of adherents, concentrated in the Global South. Modernity didn’t kill religion; it relocated and reinvented it.
</Example>

## Practice

  <PracticeItem question="What is the secularisation thesis, and what’s the main evidence against it?" hint="Modernity was supposed to end faith.">
    Thesis: modernisation reduces religion’s significance. Against: global religious numbers are growing — decline is mainly a Western European story.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might fundamentalism be a modern phenomenon?" hint="Reaction to change.">
    It’s often a reaction AGAINST modernity — using modern media and organisation to defend tradition against perceived threats.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Secularisation = religion’s declining social power.
  - Europe secularises; the Global South religionises.
  - The world overall is getting more religious by numbers.
  - Religion adapts — fundamentalism and new spiritualities included.
</Summary>
