## Religion and Ethics

Some say morality collapses without God; others that ethics stands on reason alone. Both sides claim the evidence.

<Definition term="Divine command theory">
  **Divine command theory**: an act is right because God commands it. Plato’s Euthyphro dilemma asks: does God command it because it’s good — or is it good because God commands it? Either answer creates problems.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Euthyphro dilemma: if goodness is independent of God, God’s commands track it but don’t define it; if goodness = God’s commands, morality seems arbitrary.
- Secular ethics: reason, empathy and social contracts ground morality without God (see Philosophy: Ethics).
- Religion’s record: hospitals, abolition, civil rights — and crusades, inquisitions, sectarian wars.
- Most believers and non-believers converge on core moral norms in practice.

<Example title="Abolition and the Bible">
  Both abolitionists and slaveholders quoted scripture. The same Bible fuelled liberation and oppression — showing texts don’t settle ethics alone; interpretation and conscience do.
</Example>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="State the Euthyphro dilemma." hint="Because it’s good, or good because commanded?">
    Is conduct right because God commands it (making morality arbitrary), or does God command it because it’s right (making morality independent of God)?
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one argument that morality doesn’t need religion." hint="Atheists behave morally.">
    Non-believers act morally, and moral reasoning (harm, fairness) works without theological premises — suggesting ethics has independent grounds.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - The Euthyphro dilemma challenges divine command theory.
  - Secular ethics grounds morality in reason and empathy.
  - Religion has inspired great good and great evil.
  - In practice, believers and non-believers share most moral norms.
</Summary>
