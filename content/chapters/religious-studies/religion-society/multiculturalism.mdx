## Key ideas

### Living together, talking together

Multicultural cities bring mosques, churches, temples and gurdwaras onto the same street. Interfaith dialogue - from local councils of faiths to national initiatives - builds cooperation on issues like poverty and hate crime. It works best when each side is honest about real differences rather than pretending all religions are the same.

### The secularisation debate

Sociologists once predicted religion would fade away, and church attendance in Britain has indeed fallen sharply. But sociologist Grace Davie argued for 'believing without belonging': many people keep personal faith without attending services. Meanwhile, Islam, Pentecostal Christianity and other traditions are growing in Britain through immigration and conversion. The picture is not simple decline but change - some measures fall while others rise.

**Key term — interfaith dialogue:** Organised conversation and cooperation between different religions.

<WorkedExample title="Evaluating the secularisation thesis">
  'Religion is dying out in Britain.' Assess this claim using evidence for and against.

  Step 1: Give evidence for - church attendance and formal membership have fallen significantly over recent decades.
  Step 2: Give evidence against (1) - Davie's 'believing without belonging': surveys show many still believe in God or pray without attending worship.
  Step 3: Give evidence against (2) - some religions are growing in Britain, including Islam and Pentecostal churches.
  Step 4: Weigh up - decline is real for mainstream Christianity's public role, but religion as such is changing shape rather than disappearing.
  Step 5: Conclude with nuance - the claim is partly true but overstated; secularisation describes institutional decline, not the death of faith.

  **Answer:** The claim is partly true: institutional religion, especially mainstream churchgoing, has declined. But it overstates the case - personal belief persists ('believing without belonging'), and some faiths are growing. Sociologists now speak of religion changing rather than simply dying.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Multiculturalism means all religions agree with each other** Multiculturalism means different faiths coexist in one society; they still disagree deeply on doctrine, which is why dialogue matters.
  - **Secularisation means religion has disappeared** Secularisation theory claims religion is losing social influence; even its supporters debate how far it has gone, and critics point to persistent belief and growing faiths.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the secularisation thesis?" hint="Modernisation and religion.">
    The theory that as societies modernise, religion loses social significance and influence.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one argument for and one against state-funded faith schools." hint="Choice versus division.">
    For: they respect parents' right to choose education matching their values. Against: selecting by faith can divide communities along religious lines.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is interfaith dialogue, and what is one of its aims?" hint="Talking across faiths.">
    Organised conversation and cooperation between religions; aims include mutual understanding, reducing prejudice and joint action on social issues.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Name one sign that religion remains socially significant in modern Britain." hint="Think of schools or public life.">
    Any of: compulsory RE and collective worship in schools, state-funded faith schools, religious festivals in public life, or the growth of some faith communities.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
