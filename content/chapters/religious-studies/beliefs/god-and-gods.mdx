What do people actually believe? Beneath the festivals and rituals lie big ideas about God, death, human nature and evil - ideas that shape how billions of people live every day. This chapter explores the main beliefs found across religions, and why they matter.

This lesson focuses on God and Gods: one God, many gods or no god - how beliefs about the divine differ.

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Different pictures of the divine

Monotheists, such as Jews, Christians, Muslims and Sikhs, believe in one God. Hindus speak of one ultimate reality, Brahman, expressed through many deities. Buddhists focus on enlightenment rather than a creator God, and humanists live without belief in God at all. These are not small differences of taste - they change how followers pray, worship and understand the world.

### Beliefs shape behaviour

What you believe changes how you act. If you believe every person carries a divine spark, you treat strangers with respect; if you believe actions plant seeds (karma) that ripen in future lives, you think carefully before acting. Understanding someone's beliefs helps you understand their choices.

**Key term — theism:** Belief in the existence of God or gods.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: God and Gods">
  What is the difference between theism and atheism?

  Theism is belief that God or gods exist; atheism is belief that they do not.

  **Answer:** Theism is belief that God or gods exist; atheism is belief that they do not.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Hindus worship many completely separate gods** Many Hindus understand the deities as different expressions of one ultimate reality, Brahman.
  - **Beliefs are private and change nothing** Beliefs shape daily choices - diet, dress, charity, work and family life are all influenced by what people believe.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one similarity between Christian and Muslim beliefs about God." hint="Count.">
    Both are strictly monotheistic - they believe in one God (Allah is the Arabic word for God).
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How might belief in karma affect someone's behaviour?" hint="Think before acting.">
    It encourages careful, kind behaviour, because harmful actions are believed to bring bad consequences.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What does an agnostic claim about God?" hint="It is about knowledge.">
    That we cannot know whether God exists.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is karma?" hint="Think of actions and consequences.">
    The teaching, found in Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and Jainism, that good and bad actions have consequences, including in future lives.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
