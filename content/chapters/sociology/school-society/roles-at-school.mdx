## Key ideas

### Every role has jobs to do

A headteacher leads the school, a teacher plans lessons and keeps order, and a pupil learns and follows the rules. When everyone plays their role well, the school runs smoothly. Roles also shape behaviour: you act differently as a pupil in assembly than as a friend in the playground.

### Belonging means everyone counts

Belonging to a class, a team or a school gives us friendship, pride and support. But belonging also brings responsibility: a group works best when every member is included and respected. Sociologists study how groups make us feel, because belonging is one of the strongest forces in human life.

**Key term — Role:** The part a person plays in a group, such as pupil, teacher or headteacher. Each role comes with its own jobs and expectations.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Roles at School">
  Name two roles in a school and one job each role does.

  For example: teacher, plans lessons and keeps order; pupil, learns and follows rules; headteacher, leads the school.

  **Answer:** For example: teacher, plans lessons and keeps order; pupil, learns and follows rules; headteacher, leads the school.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Believing only teachers have roles** Pupils, prefects and even visitors all have roles with expectations attached.
  - **Thinking rules only exist to spoil fun** Most rules protect people or keep things fair; ask who benefits before you judge a rule.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one reason schools have rules." hint="Think about what rules stop from happening.">
    To keep people safe, or to make things fair for everyone.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A pupil says: I am just a pupil, I have no role in how the school runs. Use this chapter to disagree." hint="Remember that every role has jobs to do.">
    Pupils have the role of learners and school citizens, with responsibilities like following rules, looking after property and helping others, so they shape the school too.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Your class must choose one new playground rule. Suggest a fair rule and say who it protects." hint="Think like a sociologist: who benefits?">
    Any sensible rule with a clear beneficiary, e.g. take turns on the climbing frame so everyone gets a fair go and nobody gets hurt.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between a rule and a responsibility?" hint="One is an instruction; the other is a duty that comes with your part.">
    A rule is an agreed instruction about behaviour; a responsibility is a duty attached to a role.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
