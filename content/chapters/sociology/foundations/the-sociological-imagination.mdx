## The Sociological Imagination

C. Wright Mills argued that the sociological imagination lets us connect biography to history: your life story is also the story of your society.

<Definition term="Sociological imagination">
  The **sociological imagination** is the ability to see the link between personal experiences (‘troubles’) and wider social forces (‘issues’) — understanding that private lives are shaped by public structures.
</Definition>

## Key points

- One person unemployed: personal trouble. Ten million: public issue — economic structure.
- It turns ‘what’s wrong with me?’ into ‘what’s going on around me?’
- Founders: Durkheim (social facts), Marx (class conflict), Weber (rationalisation, status).
- It doesn’t excuse personal responsibility — it completes the picture.

<Example title="Why do exam results differ by postcode?">
  Individual effort matters — but so do school funding, tutoring, home stability and networks. The sociological imagination sees both the student and the system.
</Example>
