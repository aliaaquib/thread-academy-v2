## Socialisation

Feral children — raised without human contact — struggle to learn language or social behaviour later. We become human by being socialised.

<Definition term="Socialisation">
  **Socialisation** is the lifelong process by which we learn our society’s norms, values and roles — becoming functioning members. Primary socialisation (family, early childhood) lays the foundations; secondary socialisation (school, peers, media, work) builds on it.
</Definition>

## Key points

- Family: first and deepest — language, basic values, class position.
- School: the hidden curriculum — punctuality, obedience, competition — alongside the formal one.
- Peers and media: powerful in adolescence; shape identity, tastes, slang.
- It never stops: new jobs, parenthood, migration all re-socialise us.

<Example title="The hidden curriculum">
  Schools officially teach maths; unofficially they teach sitting still, raising hands, accepting authority and ranking — lessons about how institutions work.
</Example>

## Practice

  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give an example of the hidden curriculum." hint="What’s taught without being on the syllabus?">
    Punctuality, deference to authority, competition for grades — institutional habits learned alongside subjects.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Socialisation makes us members of society — it’s learned, not innate.
  - Family, school, peers and media are key agents.
  - The hidden curriculum teaches institutional behaviour.
  - Socialisation continues across the whole life course.
</Summary>
