Have you ever asked 'why' so many times that the grown-ups ran out of answers? Philosophers do exactly that, and they keep going. Philosophy means asking the biggest, trickiest questions about the world, and thinking about them carefully together.

This lesson focuses on What Is Philosophy?: discover what philosophy means and what philosophers actually spend their time doing.

<Definition term="What Is Philosophy?">
  Discover what philosophy means and what philosophers actually spend their time doing.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Questions with no single answer

Some questions, such as 'What happens after we die?', have no answer everyone agrees on. Philosophers do not give up on these questions: they compare different answers and look for the best reasons.

### Thinking together

Philosophers in ancient Greece asked questions out loud and argued with each other in the marketplace. We can do the same: sharing ideas, listening, and building on what others say makes everyone's thinking stronger.

**Key term — Wonder:** The feeling of amazement and curiosity that starts philosophical thinking. The ancient Greek philosopher Plato said that wonder is where philosophy begins.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: What Is Philosophy?">
  Which ancient philosopher said philosophy begins in wonder?

  Plato.

  **Answer:** Plato.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking every question has one right answer** Big questions can have several thoughtful answers. The point is which answer has the best reasons behind it.
  - **Laughing at questions that sound silly** Silly-sounding questions often hide deep ideas. Asking 'why' is never stupid.
</ImportantNote>
