## Key ideas

### Rules should be fair too

A rule like 'everyone takes turns' is usually fair. But a rule like 'only tall children may play' is unfair because it treats people differently for no good reason. We can judge rules themselves by asking who they help and who they hurt.

### Fairness needs imagination

To judge fairness, imagine being the other person. How would you feel if the biggest slice always went to someone else? This habit of imagining other viewpoints is something philosophers practise all the time.

**Key term — Rule:** An instruction everyone follows, like 'take turns'. Good rules help fairness; unfair rules can harm it.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Rules and Fairness">
  Is it fair that a younger child gets a head start in a race? Explain.

  Yes, it can be fair: the head start makes the race fairer by balancing different abilities.

  **Answer:** Yes, it can be fair: the head start makes the race fairer by balancing different abilities.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Making rules that suit only you** A fair rule works for everyone, including people in a different situation from yours.
  - **Thinking fair always means the same** Same is equal, not necessarily fair. Ask what each person needs before you split.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example of an unfair rule." hint="Think of a rule that helps one group only.">
    For example, 'Only children with blue shoes may join the game' — it treats people differently for no good reason.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How can imagining yourself in someone's shoes help with fairness?" hint="How would you feel?">
    It shows you how a decision feels from their side, so you judge more fairly.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is the difference between equality and equity?" hint="Think about the children and the fence.">
    Equality gives everyone the same; equity gives people what they need for a fair chance.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Imagine you must share six apples between a hungry friend and a full friend. What is fair?" hint="Remember Sam and the biscuits.">
    Give more apples to the hungry friend, e.g. four and two, with a reason about need.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
