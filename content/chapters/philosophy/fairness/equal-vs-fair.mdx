Is it fair that everyone gets the same? Or fair that everyone gets what they need? Fairness is one of the first big ideas children argue about, and philosophers have been arguing about it for thousands of years. This chapter helps you tell the difference between equal and fair.

This lesson focuses on Equal versus Fair: see why giving everyone exactly the same is not always the fairest choice.

<Definition term="Equal versus Fair">
  See why giving everyone exactly the same is not always the fairest choice.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Equal is not always fair

Imagine three children watching a match over a fence. Giving each one the same box to stand on is equal, but the smallest child still cannot see. Giving boxes according to height is fair. Philosophers call this the difference between equality and equity.

### Rules should be fair too

A rule like 'everyone takes turns' is usually fair. But a rule like 'only tall children may play' is unfair because it treats people differently for no good reason. We can judge rules themselves by asking who they help and who they hurt.

**Key term — Equality:** Giving everyone exactly the same, whatever their needs. Equality is simple, but it is not always fair.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Equal versus Fair">
  What is the difference between equality and equity?

  Equality gives everyone the same; equity gives people what they need for a fair chance.

  **Answer:** Equality gives everyone the same; equity gives people what they need for a fair chance.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking fair always means the same** Same is equal, not necessarily fair. Ask what each person needs before you split.
  - **Making rules that suit only you** A fair rule works for everyone, including people in a different situation from yours.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Is it fair that a younger child gets a head start in a race? Explain." hint="Think about need, not just sameness.">
    Yes, it can be fair: the head start makes the race fairer by balancing different abilities.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Give one example of an unfair rule." hint="Think of a rule that helps one group only.">
    For example, 'Only children with blue shoes may join the game' — it treats people differently for no good reason.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Imagine you must share six apples between a hungry friend and a full friend. What is fair?" hint="Remember Sam and the biscuits.">
    Give more apples to the hungry friend, e.g. four and two, with a reason about need.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How can imagining yourself in someone's shoes help with fairness?" hint="How would you feel?">
    It shows you how a decision feels from their side, so you judge more fairly.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
