This lesson focuses on Listening to Reasons: practise listening carefully to other people's reasons before you reply.

<Definition term="Listening to Reasons">
  Practise listening carefully to other people's reasons before you reply.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Listen before you answer

If you reply before hearing someone's full reason, you might argue against something they never said. Good thinkers listen first, then respond.

### A reason is not just a feeling

'Because I feel like it' is a feeling, not a reason. A real reason gives someone else a way to check whether the belief makes sense.

**Key term — Belief:** Something you think is true. Beliefs can be strong or weak depending on the reasons behind them.

<WorkedExample title="Should the class get a pet hamster?">
  Your class wants a hamster. The teacher asks: 'Give me your best reasons.'.

  State your belief clearly: 'I think our class should get a hamster.'.
  Give your reasons: hamsters teach responsibility, they are quiet, and everyone could help care for it.
  Check the other side: who feeds it in the holidays? What if someone is allergic?
  Weigh the reasons: if the holiday problem has no answer, the reasons against are stronger.
  Make your final call and explain it using your reasons.

  **Answer:** A thoughtful answer might be: 'We should not get one yet, because nobody has solved the holiday feeding problem.' Notice the answer follows from the reasons, not from who shouted loudest.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Listening only to reply** If you plan your answer while someone talks, you miss their reasons. Listen to understand, then answer.
  - **Saying 'because I said so' or 'because I want to'** These are not reasons: they give nobody any way to check your thinking. Try adding evidence someone else could look at.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is stronger: a belief with reasons or a belief without reasons? Why?" hint="Which one can be checked?">
    A belief with reasons, because the reasons can be checked and discussed.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is a reason?" hint="Finish the sentence: 'I believe it because...'">
    A reason is a fact or idea that supports a belief, often given after the word 'because'.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Is 'because I want to' a good reason? Why not?" hint="Can someone else check it?">
    No: it is a feeling, not a reason others can check or test.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Why should you listen to someone's whole reason before answering?" hint="Think about arguing against the wrong thing.">
    So you respond to what they actually said, not to something you imagined.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
